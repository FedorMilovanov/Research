# Отступничество — карта греческих лексем apostasy / perseverance

**Status:** ACTIVE / SUPPORTING DOSSIER  
**Purpose:** не строить доктрину на одном русском слове «отступить» или «пребывать», а проследить разные новозаветные глаголы/существительные, их диапазон и богословскую функцию.

---

# 1. `ἀφίστημι` — уйти, отойти, отпасть

Lexical range: physically depart / withdraw; abandon; in religious contexts fall away.

Reference lexicon: https://biblehub.com/greek/868.htm

## Critical theological occurrences

### Luke 8:13

`πρὸς καιρὸν πιστεύουσιν καὶ ἐν καιρῷ πειρασμοῦ ἀφίστανται`

«до времени веруют и во время испытания отпадают».

Primary Greek: https://www.biblehub.com/text/luke/8-13.htm

This is one of the most important lexical anchors in the entire project because **the same sentence predicates both `πιστεύουσιν` and `ἀφίστανται` of the same persons**, while explicitly saying they «have no root».

Therefore:

- do not deny the text’s own faith-language;
- do not infer from the bare verb `πιστεύω` that saving union was present;
- the controlling explanation is absence of root + temporary duration + falling away under testing.

### Hebrews 3:12

`καρδία πονηρὰ ἀπιστίας ἐν τῷ ἀποστῆναι ἀπὸ θεοῦ ζῶντος`

= evil heart of unbelief **in falling/departing from the living God**.

The author defines apostasy here through **unbelief**. This is crucial for Hebrews 6/10: the larger warning structure begins not with «a regenerate heart losing regeneration» but with the wilderness paradigm of an unbelieving heart departing from the living God.

### 1 Timothy 4:1

`ἀποστήσονταί τινες τῆς πίστεως`

Some will depart from «the faith». Here the genitive can readily denote the Christian faith/doctrinal confession as an objective body of truth. This warns against reading every `ἀφίστημι` occurrence as a direct statement about loss of inward saving faith.

## Canonical bridge

Luke 8:13 + Hebrews 3:12 gives an important combined definition:

> Temporary belief can end in `ἀφίστημι`; Hebrews locates final departure in an **unbelieving heart**.

That makes unbelief/rootlessness, not merely the chronology «believed then stopped», decisive for soteriological classification.

# 2. `ἀποστασία` — defection, rebellion, apostasy

Reference: https://biblehub.com/strongs/greek/646.htm

Only two NT occurrences:

- Acts 21:21 — accusation that Paul teaches Jews `ἀποστασίαν ἀπὸ Μωϋσέως`, forsaking/defection from Moses;
- 2 Thess 2:3 — `ἡ ἀποστασία`, the apostasy/rebellion before the man of lawlessness.

### Important limitation

The noun itself does **not** carry a built-in systematic statement about the prior regenerate status of apostates. It names defection/rebellion from a prior allegiance/order.

Therefore «apostasy» should be defined narratively and covenantally, not as a synonym for «loss of justification».

# 3. `παραπίπτω` — fall away / deviate

NT occurrence: Hebrews 6:6 only.

Abbott-Smith/Tyndale lexical data notes LXX use in Ezek 14:13; 15:8; **18:24**; 20:27 for covenantal unfaithfulness and gives Heb 6:6 the sense «fall away».

Reference: https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/%CF%80%CE%B1%CF%81%CE%B1%CF%80%CE%AF%CF%80%CF%84%CF%89

## Major intertextual gold

The same Greek lemma occurs in the LXX at **Ezekiel 18:24** for the righteous person turning from righteousness.

That does not prove Hebrews quotes Ezekiel 18, but it creates a lexical/covenantal bridge worth investigating:

- Ezekiel: a covenant member turns from the righteous path and comes under judgment;
- Hebrews: extraordinarily privileged persons `παραπεσόντας` and cannot be renewed while repudiating the Son.

This strengthens the case that the verb belongs to covenant-treachery vocabulary rather than requiring a technical definition «regenerate person becomes unregenerate».

### Publication caution

Do not use Strong’s-style etymological claims such as «para means beside, so they were only beside salvation». Etymology does not determine the theology. The meaningful evidence is attested usage and context.

# 4. `ὑποστέλλω / ὑποστολή` — shrink/draw back

Hebrews 10:38–39 deliberately sets these words against faith:

- v. 38: if one `ὑποστείληται` — shrinks/draws back, God has no pleasure in him;
- v. 39: `οὐκ ἐσμὲν ὑποστολῆς εἰς ἀπώλειαν ἀλλὰ πίστεως εἰς περιποίησιν ψυχῆς`

= «we are not of shrinking back unto destruction, but of faith unto preserving/gaining the soul».

Lexical sources:
- https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/%E1%BD%91%CF%80%CE%BF%CF%83%CF%84%CE%AD%CE%BB%CE%BB%CF%89
- https://biblehub.com/greek/5289.htm

## Theological significance

Hebrews does not merely contrast «Christian who loses salvation» and «Christian who keeps it». It explicitly defines **two trajectories/classes by outcome**:

- shrinking back → destruction (`ἀπώλεια`);
- faith → preservation/acquisition of the soul.

Then 11:1 immediately begins the faith chapter. Thus Heb 10:39–11 is a canonical answer to Heb 6/10 warnings: saving faith is the faith that endures by trusting God’s promise.

This makes perseverance intrinsic to the author’s account of faith, not an optional post-conversion supplement.

# 5. `κατέχω` — hold fast / retain

Important occurrences:

- Luke 8:15: good soil **holds fast** the word and bears fruit with `ὑπομονή`;
- 1 Cor 15:2: by the gospel you are being/saved **if you hold fast** the word preached, unless you believed `εἰκῇ`;
- Heb 3:6,14; 10:23: hold fast confidence/hope.

Lexical data: https://biblehub.com/lexicon/1_corinthians/15-2.htm

## Strong canonical connection

Luke’s contrast is especially elegant:

- rocky soil: believes for a time → falls away;
- good soil: hears → **holds fast** → bears fruit **with endurance**.

The distinction is not merely initial emotion or intellectual assent; it is root-bearing persistence.

1 Cor 15:2 then uses the same hold-fast logic in explicitly gospel/salvation language.

### On `εἰκῇ ἐπιστεύσατε`

Do not translate the theology too quickly as «unless your original faith was fake». `εἰκῇ` can carry ideas such as in vain/without effect/without cause depending context. The safer claim is: Paul refuses to detach saving gospel reception from continued holding of the apostolic word.

# 6. `ὑπομονή` — endurance / steadfastness

Luke 8:15: good soil bears fruit `ἐν ὑπομονῇ`.

Lexical anchor: https://biblehub.com/lexicon/luke/8-15.htm

Other key texts:

- Rom 5:3–4 — suffering produces endurance;
- Rom 8:25 — hope waits with endurance;
- Heb 10:36 — «you have need of endurance» after the apostasy warning;
- Heb 12:1 — run the race with endurance, fixing eyes on Jesus;
- James 1:3–4 — testing of faith produces endurance;
- Rev 13:10; 14:12 — endurance of saints amid beastly pressure.

## Theological significance

In the NT, endurance is not passive chronology. It is active faithfulness under the very pressures that expose temporary faith. Therefore the language of perseverance should be framed not as «remaining technically saved long enough» but as **faith continuing to cling to Christ under trial**.

# 7. `μένω` — remain / abide

This is a major Johannine control term.

### John 8:31

«If you `μείνητε` in my word, you are truly my disciples.»

### John 15

The vine discourse repeatedly commands/defines `μένω` in Christ and His word.

### 1 John 2:19

Those who left showed that they were not truly «of us», because if they had been, `μεμενήκεισαν` — they **would have remained**.

### 1 John 2:24–28

What was heard from the beginning must **remain** in believers; then they remain in Son and Father; the promise is eternal life.

## Theological significance

John’s logic is deeply relational:

> abiding does not merit union; abiding manifests the reality of the relationship and is itself sustained within Christ’s preserving work.

This helps explain John 15 without turning `μένω` into either a meritorious condition or a meaningless test.

# 8. Lexical synthesis: Scripture describes apostasy with trajectory words

The striking result of the lexical survey is that NT apostasy vocabulary is mostly **relational and directional**:

- `ἀφίστημι` — depart from;
- `ἀποστασία` — defection/rebellion;
- `παραπίπτω` — fall away/turn aside;
- `ὑποστέλλω` — draw/shrink back;

while perseverance vocabulary is likewise directional:

- `κατέχω` — hold fast;
- `μένω` — remain/abide;
- `ὑπομονή` — endure steadfastly.

The Bible’s concern is therefore not merely a static label at one past moment («was he ever a believer?») but a **trajectory under revelation, trial and time**.

Yet trajectory is not independent of nature/root:

- Luke 8 explains falling by absence of root;
- John explains final departure by non-belonging (1 John 2:19);
- Hebrews explains departing through an evil **unbelieving heart** (3:12);
- John 10 grounds perseverance of sheep in Christ’s giving and keeping eternal life.

Thus final apostasy both **reveals** an underlying absence of saving root and is a **real culpable act** committed after genuine exposure/participation in divine privilege.

# 9. Article vocabulary recommendation

Use several Russian terms instead of flattening all Greek words into one:

- **отступничество** — umbrella theological term for decisive defection from confessed covenant/gospel allegiance;
- **отпадение** — especially Luke 8:13 / Heb 6:6 trajectory;
- **отход / оставление** — where narrative rather than technical theology is primary;
- **уклонение / отступление назад** — Heb 10:38–39;
- **пребывание** — Johannine `μένω`;
- **удерживание / держаться** — `κατέχω`;
- **стойкость / терпение** — `ὑπομονή`.

Avoid making «апостасия» sound like one technical NT label applied everywhere. The noun `ἀποστασία` itself occurs only twice; the doctrine is built from a much broader family of texts and concepts.
