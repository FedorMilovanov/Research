# 1. 2 Петра 2:1 ↔ Иуда 4: кто такой `δεσπότης`?

## Greek anchors

2 Пет. 2:1 (SBLGNT):

`... καὶ τὸν ἀγοράσαντα αὐτοὺς δεσπότην ἀρνούμενοι ...`

= «... и отрекаясь от Владыки (`δεσπότης`), купившего (`ἀγοράζω`) их ...».

Primary text: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Peter+2%3A1&version=SBLGNT

Иуд. 4 (SBLGNT):

`... καὶ τὸν μόνον δεσπότην καὶ κύριον ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦν Χριστὸν ἀρνούμενοι.`

= «... и отвергаясь единственного нашего Владыки и Господа Иисуса Христа».

Primary text: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jude+4&version=SBLGNT

NET textual note explicitly observes that Jude and 2 Peter use the same rare noun `δεσπότης` and the same verb of denial (`ἀρνέομαι`) in corresponding false-teacher passages; it also treats Jude 4’s `δεσπότης` + `κύριος` as one referent, Jesus Christ, under the Granville Sharp construction.

Source: https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version%3DNET2full%40reference%3DJude.1.4

## Correction to an older Reformed shortcut

Gill’s classic reading takes `δεσπότης` in 2 Pet 2:1 as the Father and appeals to Jude 4 as distinguishing God from Christ. That argument is materially weakened by the better-attested shorter text of Jude 4, which lacks `θεόν` after `δεσπότην`. SBLGNT notes the variant; the reading without θεόν is supported by the earlier critical tradition and is the basis of modern translations that render one person: «our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ».

Therefore **do not repeat Gill’s Jude-4 argument as settled textual evidence**. His wider Deut-32/covenant-purchase proposal remains independently discussable, but Jude 4 now pushes the identity of `δεσπότης` in 2 Pet 2:1 toward Christ.

### Working adjudication

- **High confidence:** `δεσπότης` in Jude 4 refers to Jesus Christ.
- **Medium/high confidence:** the close Jude 4 / 2 Pet 2:1 literary parallel makes Christ the probable `δεσπότης` in 2 Pet 2:1 as well.
- **Not resolved by this:** what kind of «purchase» `ἀγοράσαντα` denotes in 2 Pet 2:1.

# 2. 2 Петра 2:1 ↔ Второзаконие 32:6: real intertext, but not exact lexical quotation

Deut 32:6 is often used in Reformed exegesis to argue that Peter speaks of covenantal acquisition/ownership rather than individual saving redemption.

The thematic parallel is real:

- Deut 32 addresses Israel, a people acquired/formed by Yahweh, who respond corruptly;
- 2 Pet 2:1 begins, «false prophets were among **the people**, just as false teachers will be among **you**»;
- both contexts aggravate rebellion by prior divine ownership/benefaction.

But the lexical relationship must be stated accurately:

- 2 Pet 2:1: `ἀγοράζω` — buy/purchase;
- LXX Deut 32:6 uses a form of `κτάομαι` — acquire/possess, not `ἀγοράζω`.

Therefore the proposal is **thematic/intertextual**, not «Peter quotes the same Greek words from Deut 32:6».

### Consequence

The Deut 32 reading is a serious explanatory model, but it cannot by itself cancel the normal purchase sense of `ἀγοράζω`. The strongest formulation presently available is:

> Peter may deliberately cast the false teachers as a new-covenant analogue of rebellious Israel—owned/acquired by their Lord and yet denying Him—but the precise relation between that covenant ownership and Christ’s atoning purchase remains an exegetical question.

This formulation preserves both pieces of evidence: the Israel typology and the actual Greek verb.

### Publication HOLD

Do not publish: «2 Pet 2:1 plainly means only providential creation» or «clearly the Father, not Christ». Both are too strong.

# 3. `ἀγοράζω` — what the verb itself can and cannot prove

2 Pet 2:1: `τὸν ἀγοράσαντα αὐτοὺς`.

Primary Greek: https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/grc/2%20Peter%202%3A1

The ordinary meaning is buy/purchase/acquire. NT soteriological uses include language of persons bought at a price (1 Cor 6:20; 7:23) and the Lamb purchasing people for God (Rev 5:9; cf. Rev 14:3–4).

Therefore:

1. It is unsafe to redefine the verb itself as «merely created».
2. The **object and sphere of purchase** must be decided from context/intertext, not lexicon alone.
3. Even if the text speaks of Christ’s purchase, a further question remains: does Peter assert that these false teachers possessed the saving benefits of that purchase, or speak from covenant/professional/visible-community relation?

That final question cannot be answered by `ἀγοράζω` alone.

# 4. Hebrews 10:29 ↔ Exodus 24:8: “blood of the covenant” is a direct covenantal control

The phrase in Heb 10:29 does not float free from Hebrews’ own argument.

Heb 9:18–22 has already rehearsed Sinai and explicitly quotes the covenant-ratification scene. Exodus 24:8 says Moses sprinkled the people and declared: **«This is the blood of the covenant»**. Hebrews then uses the same covenant-blood idiom for Christ.

Primary cross-text:
- Exodus 24:8 / Hebrews 10:29: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+24%3A8%2CHebrews+10%3A29&version=NRSVUE
- Hebrews commentary noting Sinai blood-ratification: https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/hebrews/

## Why this matters

At Sinai the blood is applied to **the people corporately**. Yet the later wilderness story—Psalm 95, Hebrews 3–4, Jude 5—shows that corporate covenant consecration did not entail the final salvation of every individual sprinkled member.

This supplies a real conceptual category for Heb 10:29: a person may be truly set apart within the covenant people and stand under the holy claims/privileges of covenant blood, yet finally repudiate the covenant Lord.

This is stronger than saying «sanctified only in appearance». The old-covenant type itself supplies a category of **real covenantal sanctification without guaranteed final salvation of every participant**.

## But the new covenant raises the difficulty

Hebrews insists that Christ’s blood is superior to the old sacrifices and actually perfects/cleanses in a way they could not (9:11–15; 10:10–18). So we must not simply transfer Old Covenant externality unchanged into the New.

The exact question is:

> Does Heb 10:29 apply `ἡγιάσθη` to the apostate in the corporate-covenantal sense established by the blood-of-covenant typology, while reserving definitive saving perfection for those who persevere; or does the author describe a formerly regenerate person who loses salvation?

That is the real dispute. The Exodus 24 background makes the former coherent; it does not, by itself, prove it.

# 5. Hebrews’ own `ἁγιάζω` matrix

| Text | Subject/object | Force relevant to apostasy question |
|---|---|---|
| Heb 2:11 | sanctifier + sanctified | strong identity/solidarity language |
| Heb 9:13 | animal blood sanctifies «for purification of flesh» | proves the verb can denote real consecratory cleansing short of final inward salvation |
| Heb 10:10 | «we have been sanctified» through Christ’s body once for all | strong definitive saving use |
| Heb 10:14 | one offering perfects forever «those being sanctified» | saving/perfecting use; must control any reading of 10:29 |
| Heb 10:29 | apostate(?) sanctified by covenant blood | disputed; cannot decide from lexeme alone |
| Heb 13:12 | Jesus suffered to sanctify the people through his own blood | strongly Christological/new-covenant use |

### Critical implication

No honest synthesis can say either:

- «`ἁγιάζω` always means regeneration/final salvation» — Heb 9:13 blocks that;
- «`ἁγιάζω` here is obviously only external» — 10:10,14 and 13:12 block casual weakening.

The writer deliberately uses one sanctification vocabulary across typological, covenantal and saving contexts. The article must explain **which level 10:29 occupies and why**.

### Preferred research direction

Read 10:29 from three concentric contexts:

1. **Immediate:** deliberate rejection of Son/blood/Spirit after knowledge of truth;
2. **Hebrews 9–10:** old and new covenant blood, access, cleansing, perfection;
3. **Hebrews 3–4 / 6:** covenant people may receive extraordinary privileges yet reveal unbelief through final apostasy.

# 6. John: a controlled dataset of “belief”, discipleship and abiding

The Fourth Gospel is unusually important because it contains both:

- language that sounds very strong for people who later prove false;
- some of the strongest preservation promises in Scripture.

## John 2:23–25

Many **believed in His name** after seeing signs, yet Jesus did not entrust Himself to them because He knew what was in man.

D. A. Carson emphasizes that even «believed in his name» is not a magic technical formula that guarantees genuine saving faith; context decides. His discussion explicitly links this with John 8:31 and persevering discipleship.

Sources:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/sermon/the-spirituality-of-the-gospel-of-john-part-2-john-2-4/
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/carson-article/the-sbjt-forum-what-does-the-gospel-of-john-tell-us-about-the-doctrine-of-assurance/

### Gold for the larger thesis

The New Testament can describe a real act of believing with conventional faith language without thereby telling us that the person has the persevering faith that receives eternal life. This is exactly why Luke 8:13 («believe for a time») must be allowed to retain its ordinary force.

## John 6:36–40 vs 6:64–66

This chapter intentionally places preservation and apostasy phenomena side by side.

Positive promise:

- all whom the Father gives the Son will come;
- Christ will lose none of them;
- He will raise them at the last day.

Negative phenomenon:

- Jesus says some do not believe and knows this «from the beginning», including the betrayer;
- many called His disciples turn back and no longer walk with Him.

Carson correctly notes the force of 6:37–40: the preservation of those given by the Father is grounded in the Son’s accomplishing the Father’s will, not in autonomous human resolve.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/sermon/the-spirituality-of-the-gospel-of-john-part-4-john-6/
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/sermon/part-5-jesus-the-bread-of-god-john-6-25-71/

### Synthesis

John himself therefore gives categories for:

1. disciples who walk away;
2. unbelief hidden within discipleship;
3. persons given by the Father whom the Son will not lose.

The article should not collapse these three categories.

## John 8:30–31: abiding as disclosure of genuine discipleship

John says many believed; Jesus addresses those who had believed and says: **«If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples.»**

Carson and Piper both use this as a test/disclosure structure: abiding does not upgrade a merely saved person into an elite disciple; it demonstrates that discipleship is true.

Sources:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/carson-article/the-sbjt-forum-what-does-the-gospel-of-john-tell-us-about-the-doctrine-of-assurance/
- https://www.desiringgod.org/messages/if-you-abide-in-my-word-you-are-truly-my-disciples

### Caution still open

The exact discourse continuity of John 8:30–47 remains debated. Before publication, inspect major technical commentaries on whether vv. 33ff addresses precisely the same subset called believers in vv. 30–31. The v.31 conditional itself, however, clearly makes abiding a criterion of «truly» being Jesus’ disciples.

## John 15: branch «in me»

Piper’s synthesis is useful but not sufficient as final exegesis: he argues John already has «believers not true believers» (2:23), «disciples not true disciples» (6:66), and Judas; therefore a branch can be «in me» at the level of visible attachment without saving union. He further points to 15:8 («so prove to be my disciples») as parallel to 8:31.

Source: https://www.desiringgod.org/messages/glorifying-god-by-bearing-fruit-in-union-with-christ

This is a coherent intra-Johannine argument. Still, because `ἐν ἐμοί` is strong language, final publication must show rather than assume that John permits a representational/covenantal disciple relation distinct from saving union.

# 7. John 10:27–30 as non-negotiable preservation control

Primary Greek (John 10:28):

`κἀγὼ δίδωμι αὐτοῖς ζωὴν αἰώνιον, καὶ οὐ μὴ ἀπόλωνται εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα, καὶ οὐχ ἁρπάσει τις αὐτὰ ἐκ τῆς χειρός μου.`

Primary text:
- https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+10%3A28-30&version=SBLGNT

The construction `οὐ μή` + subjunctive is emphatic negation: Christ’s sheep **certainly will not / shall never perish**, reinforced by `εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα` and then by the double-hand image: no one can snatch them from Son or Father.

Modern grammatical discussion of Johannine emphatic negation includes John 10:28 among its clear examples:
- https://media.thegospelcoalition.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/28120922/Themelios.50.1-1.pdf

### Important nuance

The promise is not spoken about an undefined person who once made any profession. The subjects are Christ’s **sheep**, characterized in 10:27 by hearing His voice, being known by Him, and following Him.

Thus John 10 does not answer every apparent-apostasy case by definition; it tells us what must be true of those who are genuinely Christ’s sheep.

### Canonical force

Any interpretation of Heb 6, Heb 10, 2 Pet 2 or John 15 that concludes genuine sheep can finally perish must explain, not merely cite around, the emphatic force of John 10:27–30.

# 8. John 6:37–40 as second non-negotiable preservation control

The logic is more than «God wants believers to be safe»:

1. Father gives a people to Son;
2. all given will come;
3. Son will not cast/drive them out;
4. Father’s will is that Son lose **nothing** of all given;
5. Son raises them at the last day.

Carson’s argument is strong: final loss of one of those truly given by the Father would imply failure of the Son to accomplish the Father’s will stated in the paragraph.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/sermon/the-spirituality-of-the-gospel-of-john-part-4-john-6/
- https://media.thegospelcoalition.org/documents/carson/1992_reflections_on_christian_assurance.pdf

### Why this is especially important for Judas

John 6 itself distinguishes Judas from the preserved gift-people by calling him among the unbelieving known to Jesus from the beginning. Therefore the Judas narrative should be allowed to interpret apostolic office without forcing Judas into the category «given by Father → savingly comes → later lost».

# 9. Warning + preservation is not contradiction if warning is a means

A weak Reformed model says:

> «The elect cannot fall, so the warnings describe only non-elect people and have little direct function for believers.»

That underreads Hebrews, which repeatedly says **to the church**: beware, exhort one another, hold fast, run, do not refuse Him who speaks.

A stronger model:

> God ordains both the end (final salvation of His people) and the means (persevering faith, mutual exhortation, discipline, promises and warnings). The warning is effective precisely because the elect hear it, fear unbelief, and cling to Christ.

This model allows full force to both kinds of texts:

- John 6 / John 10 / Rom 8 / 1 Pet 1:5 / Jude 24: divine preservation;
- Heb 2 / 3 / 6 / 10 / 12; Rom 11; 1 Cor 10: real warnings addressed to the covenant community.

It also matches Jude’s compact pairing:

- v. 21: **«keep yourselves in the love of God»**;
- v. 24: God **«is able to keep you from stumbling»**.

Human persevering action and divine preserving action are not rivals in the text.

# 10. Revised high-confidence propositions after this pass

1. **Do not identify Judas with a formerly justified believer.** John 6:64 is a direct narrative control: Jesus knows him within the unbelieving from the beginning.
2. **Do not identify every non-saving response with conscious hypocrisy.** John 2:23–25 and Luke 8:13 allow real «belief»/response that proves non-persevering.
3. **Do not weaken Hebrews 6 by lexical tricks.** `γεύομαι`, `μέτοχος`, `παραπεσόντας` carry real weight; the stronger argument is authorial differentiation in 6:7–9 and the epistle’s perseverance structure.
4. **Do not escape Heb 10:29 by insisting Christ must be the one sanctified.** The apostate is the natural referent and there is a coherent covenantal category for his sanctification, especially against Exodus 24 / Hebrews 9.
5. **Do not call 2 Pet 2:1 a settled “Father/creation” text.** Jude 4 strongly favors Christ as `δεσπότης`; `ἀγοράζω` is genuine purchase language. The precise soteriological force remains open.
6. **Do not weaken 2 Pet 2:20 to mere intellectual acquaintance.** `ἐπίγνωσις` and the stated escape from pollutions are strong; v.22 supplies the author’s own nature/return interpretation.
7. **John’s Gospel supplies its own false/temporary discipleship categories.** John 2, 6, 8 and Judas are essential controls for John 15.
8. **John 6:37–40 and 10:27–30 are among the strongest preservation texts in the canon and must carry their full force.**
9. **Perseverance is not an autonomous human contribution that completes deficient grace.** In the Johannine material, the Son preserves those the Father gives; in 1 Pet 1:5 believers are guarded by God’s power *through faith*.
10. **Warnings are most naturally treated as divinely appointed means of perseverance, not fake hypotheticals.** This preserves their pastoral urgency without conceding that God finally loses His true sheep.

# 11. New publication architecture refinement

The site article should distinguish three concentric levels rather than repeatedly saying «not true believers»:

### Level 1 — **Saving union / sheep / given by the Father**
Texts: John 6:37–40; 10:27–30; Rom 8:28–39; 1 Pet 1:5; Jude 24.

### Level 2 — **Real covenantal / ecclesial / charismatic participation**
Texts: Israel wilderness; 1 Cor 10; Heb 6:4–5; Heb 10:29; Matt 7:22–23; apostolic circle including Judas; 2 Pet 2:20.

This participation can be profound, real and spiritually consequential without being identical to Level 1.

### Level 3 — **Mere nominal profession / conscious hypocrisy**
This exists too, but it should **not** be used as a catch-all explanation for every warning text. Some apostates experienced far more than an empty name.

This three-level model is likely a better pedagogical frame than the crude binary «saved / fake». It explains why the biblical descriptions of apostates sound so strong without confusing common/covenantal gifts with final saving union.
