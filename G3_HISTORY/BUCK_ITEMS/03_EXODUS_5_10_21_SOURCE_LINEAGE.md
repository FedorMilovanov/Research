## 3. Important downgrades: generic material is not source-specific

### `Out of the frying pan into the fire`

The dossier flags Buck's use of this metaphor after Ryken uses Tolkien's chapter title. The phrase is an old English proverb recorded centuries before either author and is extremely widespread.

Therefore:

- use of the idiom alone is **not** meaningful evidence of dependence on Ryken;
- repeated use near other distinctive Ryken material may contribute weak cumulative context but should never be counted as a standalone strong instance.

**Class:** `C`.

### Straw reinforces clay

The observation that straw reinforces mud brick is ordinary historical/background material. Close wording may show proximate dependence, but the factual proposition itself is not proprietary or source-specific.

**Class:** `C → possible B only if extended wording/sequence proves source dependence`.

## 4. Upstream-source correction: brickworker conditions are not Ryken-original material

A major dossier row reproduces historical material about Egyptian brickworkers:

- extreme heat;
- hands `torn to ribbons by the cruel work`;
- severe labor conditions and dehydration/heat illness.

The dossier itself notes that Ryken is quoting **Howard Vos**. Independent current-source checking also finds the `hands are torn to ribbons by the cruel work` language in modern Bible manners/customs material discussing ancient Egyptian brickmaking, showing that this content circulates upstream/outside Ryken.

### Consequence

This row cannot responsibly be described as Buck appropriating **Ryken's original creative prose**.

The relevant questions are instead:

1. Did Buck derive the material via Ryken's selection/quotation of Vos?
2. Did Buck identify it vaguely as `ancient documents` while failing to credit the proximate and/or ultimate source?
3. Does the surrounding sequence uniquely track Ryken enough to establish dependence?

This is potentially an attribution/source-lineage problem, but authorship must be assigned to the correct upstream source.

**Class:** `U / source-lineage attribution issue`, not `Ryken-original A`.

## 5. Potentially strong creative-language candidate: `Hurry, hurry, hurry! Work, work, work!`

The dossier compares Ryken's imaginative expansion of Exodus 5:13 with Buck using substantially the same repeated command around ~10:18.

The words `hurry` and `work` are individually generic. Therefore the bare six-word rhythm cannot receive a high grade merely from lexical overlap.

Its probative value depends on:

- exact wording and rhythm in Buck audio;
- placement immediately after the same verse;
- surrounding Ryken-specific sequence;
- absence/presence of attribution;
- whether an upstream sermon/source used the same rhetorical expansion before Ryken.

**Current class:** `U → possible B cumulatively`, not automatically A.

## 6. Stronger cumulative block: bondage / taskmaster / escalating desire

The dossier's most important Item 03 evidence is not one isolated sentence but a sustained sequence across Ryken pp. 154–156 and Buck roughly 12–20 minutes:

- humans are sinners `to the very core`;
- people become accustomed to sin and fail to recognize bondage;
- sin is described as a terrible/harsh taskmaster that demands more while giving less;
- the lustful man becomes less satisfied while craving more;
- the selfish person gets more yet becomes less content;
- Satan tightens the chains of captivity;
- `more bricks, less straw`;
- Israelites seek easier bondage rather than deliverance;
- Israel returns to Pharaoh at the first sign of trouble;
- the workers repeatedly identify themselves as Pharaoh's servants;
- they renegotiate the terms of captivity rather than seek freedom.

### Why this block matters

Many underlying theological ideas are commonplace. But a long run of **the same illustrations, contrasts, order and close formulations** is more probative than any individual doctrinal sentence.

One independent 2023 sermon outline explicitly attributes the formulation `Sin is the harshest of taskmasters. It always demands more and more ... while giving less and less in return` to Ryken. That provides a pre-controversy secondary anchor for the source-specific phrase, although the exact Ryken book object remains the primary gate.

If Buck's original audio verifies the dossier sequence and does not attribute the material, this block is a serious `B` dependence candidate and individual sustained near-verbatim passages may grade higher.

**Current class:** `U → likely B cumulatively, with possible A rows after audio/book verification`.

## 7. Distinguish dependence from complete-sermon theft

Even if the cumulative block is verified, it would establish substantial commentary dependence/unattributed verbal borrowing in part of the sermon. It would **not** by itself establish that Buck copied or purchased a complete finished sermon.

The research corpus therefore preserves both propositions:

- significant unattributed commentary borrowing may be real;
- the stronger `complete sermon theft` formulation remains unsupported by currently acquired evidence.
