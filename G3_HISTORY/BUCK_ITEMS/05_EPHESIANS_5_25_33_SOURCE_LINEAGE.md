## 3. Prayer list is independently anchored to Hughes

Pre-controversy sermon material quotes Kent Hughes with the sequence that husbands should pray for their wives' spiritual life, obligations, pressures, friendships and dreams.

The dossier alleges Buck uses four of the five items in the same order.

A list of concerns for one's spouse is not unique at the idea level. But multiple unusual list items reproduced in order are more probative than a generic exhortation to pray for one's wife.

**Working grade:** `U → potential B` pending Buck audio and exact Hughes text.

## 4. `Is our wife more like Christ... or in spite of us?` is genuinely Hughes-linked

This rhetorical question is independently and repeatedly attributed to R. Kent Hughes years before Buck's 2022 sermon. It appears not only in Ephesians-related material but in Hughes's broader marriage teaching, including *Disciplines of a Godly Man* traditions.

This creates an important nuance:

- the formulation is strongly Hughes-linked;
- it is not necessarily unique to the specific Ephesians commentary chapter because Hughes reused his own material across books/teaching;
- if Buck reproduces it without attribution, source dependence may be strong even if the exact proximate Hughes volume differs from the dossier's assumption.

**Working grade:** `U → potential B`.

## 5. Tornado anecdote: dossier overstates literary ownership

The dossier treats the story of a farmer and wife carried away by a tornado, ending with the wife saying it is the first time they have been out together in twenty years, as Hughes's `entire anecdote` reproduced by Buck.

Current upstream search changes that interpretation.

The same core joke/punchline circulated independently before Buck and outside Hughes's Ephesians commentary context. A 2019 Chabad page uses the farmer/wife/tornado story with the same twenty-year punchline. Other published material also identifies it as an old story/joke tradition; a literary version references a Fred Allen radio joke with the same punchline.

Therefore:

- the anecdote's **idea and punchline are not demonstrated to be Hughes-original**;
- Buck's selection of the same anecdote in a sermon may still show proximate dependence on Hughes when combined with nearby Hughes-specific material;
- one cannot classify the anecdote as appropriation of Hughes's personal or original creative illustration merely from the dossier.

**Working grade:** `C as authorship claim / U → possible B as proximate-dependence evidence if exact sequencing matches`.

## 6. Closing exhortation

The dossier shows close overlap in the final sequence of sacrificial, sanctifying and self-giving love.

Those categories arise directly from Ephesians 5, so conceptual similarity is expected. Exact multi-clause wording/order may still be probative.

**Working grade:** `U → C/B depending exact wording`.

## 7. Methodological result

Item 05 separates two very different kinds of evidence:

- Hughes-specific rhetorical wording and ordered lists, which may genuinely evidence unattributed source dependence;
- a circulating humorous anecdote that the dossier cannot responsibly treat as Hughes's original literary property without an upstream-source analysis.

This is another reason not to report the packet's rows as a flat numerical count of proven plagiarism.
