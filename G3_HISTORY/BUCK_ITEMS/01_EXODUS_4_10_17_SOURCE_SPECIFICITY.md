## 2. The dossier's Item 1 parallels are not all equally specific

The dossier groups several formulations around Exodus 4:10–17. A source-provenance pass shows they should be split.

### A. `God is not looking for an orator, just a reporter`

A 2015 sermon by Kevin DeYoung / University Reformed Church material explicitly introduces this line as Philip Ryken's wording from his Exodus commentary:

> `God is not looking for an orator, just a reporter.`

This predates Buck's 2017 sermon and independently anchors the phrase to Ryken's commentary tradition.

**Current dependence classification:** `U → potential B`.

If Buck's original audio reproduces this distinctive antithetical formulation without attribution, it is materially more probative than a generic observation that Moses did not need eloquence.

### B. `speech problem` versus `obedience problem`

The underlying exegetical move is much less source-specific. Independent Exodus teaching predating or independent of the dossier argues that Moses' real issue was refusal to trust/obey rather than an actual speech deficiency. Bible.org's Exodus study, for example, argues that Moses did not have a speech problem and frames the issue in terms of unbelief/refusal to obey.

Later sermon/study material also uses the explicit `not so much a speech problem as an obedience problem` contrast.

**Current dependence classification:** `C` for the idea/contrast by itself.

A nearly identical larger sentence cluster could still show dependence, but the theological proposition itself cannot identify Ryken as the source.

### C. `mouthing off`

An accessible commentary reproduction attributed to Ryken includes the line that God's response showed Moses `was mouthing off`; this is relevant evidence that the phrase occurs in the Ryken source tradition.

However, source uniqueness is weaker than it first appears. Victor P. Hamilton's *Exodus: An Exegetical Commentary* (released 2011) titles the Exodus 4:10–12 unit **`Mouthing Off to God about Your Mouth`**.

Therefore the short phrase `mouthing off` is not uniquely diagnostic of Ryken.

**Current dependence classification:** `C` for the isolated phrase; `U → possible B` if Buck reproduces Ryken's surrounding sentence sequence or extended wording.

### D. `message more important than the man` / reporter-orator development

The general proposition that God's message matters more than the messenger is ordinary homiletical/theological material. But if Buck follows the same reporter/orator contrast and surrounding progression found in Ryken, the **cluster** may be source-specific even where each component alone is not.

**Current dependence classification:** `U`, requiring sequence-level comparison.

### E. imagined `fifteen minutes` exchange

The dossier treats an imagined-duration detail as distinctive. Current public-source searching has **not** independently recovered the exact Ryken wording or another pre-Buck source for this detail.

**Current dependence classification:** `U / HIGH-PRIORITY`.

If a controlled Ryken page and Buck audio both reproduce the same invented temporal detail in the same rhetorical context, this could be materially probative because it is not an inference supplied by the biblical text.

Do not promote it until both sides are acquired.

## 3. Why this changes the audit method

Item 1 cannot responsibly receive one global severity label. It currently contains at least three evidence types:

- **source-specific candidate wording** — reporter/orator;
- **ordinary or widely available exegesis** — speech versus obedience;
- **shared phrase with another commentary tradition** — mouthing off;
- **potentially distinctive invented detail** — fifteen minutes, still unverified.

The correct unit of analysis is therefore the **individual parallel/cluster**, not the sermon as a binary `plagiarized / not plagiarized` object.
