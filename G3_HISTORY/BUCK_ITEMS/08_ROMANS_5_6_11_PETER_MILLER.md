## 2. The Peter Miller story is not unique to Kent Hughes

This is the key correction.

### Hughes itself attributes the story upstream

An accessible reproduction of the relevant Hughes material introduces the anecdote as a story related by the devotional publication **Daily Bread**. The reproduced Hughes version includes the familiar sequence:

- Revolutionary War preacher Peter Miller;
- hostile neighbor/enemy;
- treason sentence;
- Miller walking to intercede with George Washington;
- Washington first treating the condemned man as Miller's friend;
- Miller explaining that the man is actually his enemy;
- Washington granting the pardon.

Therefore the narrative is not presented by Hughes as his own original historical discovery or autobiographical illustration.

### The same narrative predates Hughes by decades

Ray Stedman's `That You Might Be Rich`, dated 1964 on the current Stedman archive, contains the Peter Miller / Washington / enemy-pardon narrative and explicitly applies it to reconciliation with God's enemies.

This matters because Hughes' *Romans* volume dates from 1991. The illustration therefore circulated in evangelical preaching long before Hughes' book.

### The historical anecdote predates the modern sermon-illustration tradition itself

Edward A. Steiner's *The Immigrant Tide, Its Ebb and Flow* (1909) already recounts Peter Miller pleading with George Washington for a man described as his enemy and Washington granting the pardon.

The exact names, distances and narrative details vary across retellings (`Whitman`, `Wittman`, `Wildman`; 60 vs 70 miles, etc.). That variation is itself useful: the underlying story is an old circulating anecdote, while a particular bundle of wording/details may still identify a nearer textual source.

## 3. Evidentiary consequence

The following inference is now blocked:

> `Buck told the Peter Miller story; Hughes tells the Peter Miller story; therefore Buck copied Hughes.`

That does not follow. The narrative was broadly available from older sermon/devotional/historical sources.

For plagiarism/source-dependence analysis, the item must be split:

### Story selection / underlying idea

**Classification:** `C` (low-specificity / nonunique).

The mere selection of Peter Miller as an illustration of love toward/reconciliation with enemies cannot carry a major authorship conclusion.

### Distinctive Hughes wording and sequence

**Classification:** `U`, potentially `B` if independently verified.

If Buck's original audio reproduces a sustained cluster of Hughes/Daily Bread wording — especially the same unusual adjectives, sentence sequence, 60-mile detail, `worst living enemy` formulation, scaffold/revenge ending, and the same theological transition — then specific verbal dependence may still be demonstrated.

The relevant question is no longer `did both use the same story?` but:

> `Does Buck reproduce the same editorial form of the circulating story found in Hughes closely enough to identify Hughes (or Hughes' upstream source) as the immediate verbal source?`

## 4. Upstream-source problem

Even a strong Buck↔Hughes wording match may not prove Hughes was the immediate source because Hughes himself attributes the story to *Daily Bread* and similar versions circulated elsewhere.

The proper dependency graph is potentially:

`older Peter Miller tradition → devotional / sermon-illustration retellings → Daily Bread → Hughes → possible Buck use`

or

`older tradition → another shared intermediate source → Buck and Hughes independently`.

Therefore final attribution should identify the **nearest demonstrable textual source**, not automatically name Hughes merely because the dossier chose Hughes as comparator.

## 5. Historical-truth caution

The research task here is textual dependence, not adjudicating every detail of the Peter Miller legend. Older sources establish that the story circulated long before Hughes, but they also show variants in names and details. Do not convert a sermon illustration into an independently verified account of every Revolutionary-War event without a separate historical-source audit.
