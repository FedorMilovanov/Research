## 2. `God chooses his people ... out of mercy ... not mysterious holiness`

The dossier compares Hughes/Chapell's sentence explaining `elect` with Buck's close formulation.

A later independent sermon manuscript directly quotes Hughes & Chapell p. 277 with the fuller sentence:

- `the term elect here and elsewhere...`
- God chooses his people to be his own out of mercy;
- not because they achieved some mysterious level of holiness.

This confirms that the source-side wording is genuinely associated with the cited Hughes/Chapell commentary rather than being an invention of the accusation packet.

At the **idea level**, election by grace/mercy rather than human merit is standard Reformed theology and follows many biblical texts. The probative feature is therefore the close sentence construction, examples and unusual `mysterious level of holiness` language—not the doctrine itself.

If Buck audio verifies the dossier's close wording without attribution, this is a meaningful `B` dependence candidate.

**Working grade:** `U → potential B`.

## 3. `Conduct cannot be placed prior to or above grace`

The dossier alleges a two-sentence near-verbatim unit:

- conduct cannot be placed prior to/above grace in establishing relationship with God;
- this does not mean conduct is unimportant.

The theological contrast itself is common Pauline/Reformed material: works do not establish the saving relationship, yet conduct matters as fruit/evidence. The exact wording may be source-specific, but current independent web search did not surface a sufficiently early exact Hughes/Chapell quotation to close literary specificity.

**Working grade:** `U → C/B depending exact source and audio`.

Do not elevate the concept itself to a unique-author claim.

## 4. `Titus was left with a difficult mission/situation in Crete`

The dossier compares Hughes/Chapell's statement that Paul's critiques of false teachers make clear Titus was left in a difficult situation with Buck's paraphrase that Titus had been left with a difficult mission amid ungodliness.

This is low-specificity. Titus 1 itself describes serious false-teacher and cultural problems in Crete, and independent commentaries routinely describe Titus's assignment as difficult.

Therefore a sentence saying `Titus had a difficult mission in Crete` is not literary evidence by itself.

**Working grade:** `C` unless exact extended wording/order demonstrates dependence.

## 5. Methodological result

Item 09 again separates doctrine from literary formulation:

- `election is by mercy, not merit` — common theological proposition;
- `Titus faced a difficult mission` — text-driven background;
- the distinctive Hughes/Chapell sentence about God's elect and a `mysterious level of holiness` — potentially source-specific wording.

A defensible audit therefore does not count three dossier rows as three equal plagiarism events.
