## 3. Historical block is demonstrably not all Hughes-original

The dossier itself notes that Hughes cites reference works for at least part of the Roman-slavery background. Independent pre-Buck sermons and teaching material reproduce the same historical block and in some cases identify upstream sources such as Geoffrey Bromiley's *International Standard Bible Encyclopedia*.

Examples found in the current pass include pre-2022 and older sermon material containing the same one-third population estimate and broader Roman-slavery background.

Therefore the historical facts must not be described as Hughes's creative intellectual property.

### Correct question

The forensic question is whether Buck used **Hughes as his proximate compilation/source** without attribution—not whether Hughes invented the statistics.

A long cluster in the same order can support proximate dependence even where each underlying datum originates in reference literature.

**Working class for historical block:** `C individually / U → potential B cumulatively for proximate-source dependence`.

## 4. Caution: some historical claims themselves deserve source criticism

The assertion that almost half of slaves were freed before age thirty circulates widely in sermon/commentary literature, but modern scholarship warns that relevant inscription samples can be biased and should not be turned into a universal Roman-slavery statistic without qualification.

This creates a second-order journalism issue:

- verifying that Buck and Hughes used the same historical claim does not automatically verify the claim's historical accuracy;
- the final article, if it discusses this row, should distinguish **textual dependence** from **truth of the historical proposition**.

The Buck investigation need not settle Roman slave demography, but it should not silently endorse a contested statistic merely because both sermon and commentary share it.

## 5. `shaking in your boots` candidate

The dossier treats Hughes's colorful `shaking in your boots lest you make a wrong move` expansion as distinctive and alleges Buck used a very similar formulation around ~14:01.

This is more source-specific than a demographic datum because it is colloquial explanatory prose rather than a historical fact. Exact originality still requires upstream search, but if Buck audio verifies a close match without attribution this is a plausible `B` candidate.

**Working grade:** `U → potential B`.

## 6. `cheerful goodwill / pleasantness`

This language is closer to the semantic field of Ephesians 6:7 itself, so parallelism is less probative than a colorful aside. The exact phrase sequence may show commentary dependence but cannot carry a major conclusion alone.

**Working grade:** `C → possible B in sequence`.

## 7. Current item result

Item 06 demonstrates a crucial distinction between:

1. **borrowed historical data** with upstream reference sources;
2. **proximate compilation dependence** on Hughes;
3. **distinctive explanatory wording** potentially attributable to Hughes.

The dossier's flat `verbatim` labels do not by themselves answer which of these is occurring.
