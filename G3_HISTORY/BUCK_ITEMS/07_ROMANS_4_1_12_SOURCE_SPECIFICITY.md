## 2. `Abraham was justified before his great works` is low-specificity

The proposition that Abraham was justified by faith before the famous works of his later life follows directly from Paul's argument in Romans 4 and the Genesis chronology.

Close wording can contribute to a cumulative dependence analysis, but the idea and basic sentence construction are not source-specific enough to bear a strong plagiarism conclusion alone.

**Working grade:** `C` standing alone.

## 3. `logizomai appears eleven times in Romans 4` is not a distinctive Hughes discovery

The dossier labels the count of eleven occurrences and the accounting/crediting metaphor a `distinctive exegetical note from Hughes`.

That claim is too strong.

Independent lexicon, commentary and teaching materials identify the same textual fact: `logizomai` occurs eleven times in Romans 4 and naturally carries reckoning/credit/accounting semantics. This is readily derived from the Greek text and is widely repeated outside Hughes.

Therefore:

- the numerical count itself is `C` / commonplace exegetical data;
- `credit to one's account` is ordinary lexical/accounting explanation;
- only unusually close surrounding wording/order could raise a particular use to `B` as evidence of proximate dependence.

**Working grade:** `C`, with cumulative-context caveat.

## 4. `David broke three commandments` is also not Hughes-specific

The triad is old and widespread:

- coveting Bathsheba;
- adultery;
- arranging/murdering Uriah.

The formulation that David thereby broke three of the Ten Commandments appears in Christian literature long before Hughes's Romans commentary and is also independently summarized in modern study-Bible material.

Therefore the **triad itself** cannot responsibly be called Hughes's distinctive formulation.

**Working grade:** `C` for the basic triad.

## 5. `The sacrificial system made no provision for such premeditated sin` — theological framing, not necessarily literary originality

The proposition that David's deliberate adultery/murder stood outside ordinary Levitical sacrificial provision is a recognized theological reading of Psalm 51:16 and high-handed/presumptuous-sin texts. Similar reasoning exists outside Hughes.

However, the dossier alleges Buck reproduces not merely the idea but the sequence:

1. David broke three commandments;
2. the OT sacrificial system made no provision for such premeditated/high-handed sin;
3. David's case was hopeless;
4. nothing remained but to cast himself on God's mercy.

That **combined sequence** is materially more specific than any individual proposition.

**Working grade:** individual ideas `C`; full sequence `U → potential B` pending exact source/audio and attribution context.

## 6. Strongest current Hughes-specific anchor: `Paul calls David blessed...`

The dossier compares Hughes's formulation:

- Paul calls David blessed;
- David twice calls himself blessed;
- no work could possibly atone for his sins;
- he was forgiven on `sola fide`.

A pre-Buck BibleOutlines page published in 2020 explicitly quotes this full formulation as **R. Kent Hughes**. Another pre-Buck Romans study guide reproduces the same sentence and attributes it to Hughes.

This is materially stronger than the commonplace `eleven times` or `three commandments` observations because the unusual rhetorical sequence is independently anchored to Hughes before Buck's 2023 sermon.

If Buck's original audio verifies the dossier's near-verbatim sequence without attribution, this is a serious `B` dependence candidate and may grade higher depending on extent/context.

**Working grade:** `U → likely B if audio confirms`.

## 7. `David's case was hopeless`

The short phrase itself is not unique enough for an `A` grade. Similar language appears in other Christian exposition. But when it appears immediately inside the longer Hughes-linked chain above, it may contribute to cumulative source dependence.

**Working grade:** `C` alone / `B` only as part of a verified sustained sequence.

## 8. Methodological conclusion

Item 07 exposes a recurring dossier problem: **a true match is not the same as a source-specific match**.

The packet's strongest point is not that Buck and Hughes both know:

- Abraham was justified before works;
- David broke three commandments;
- `logizomai` occurs eleven times.

Those are broadly available exegetical/historical observations.

The materially probative question is whether Buck reproduces Hughes's **distinctive rhetorical synthesis and ordering** without attribution—especially the `Paul calls David blessed ... no work could atone ... sola fide` sequence.
