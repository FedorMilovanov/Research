# Tom Buck / Pray's Mill / G3 crisis — 2026 evidence dossier

**Status:** ACTIVE / PUBLICATION_HOLD

This file separates four questions that polemical coverage often merges:

1. What did Tom Buck actually do in sermon preparation and attribution?
2. What did FBC Lindale do when accusations were presented?
3. What did Pray's Mill / G3-associated leaders do after the local-church process?
4. What institutional consequences followed for Pray's Mill and G3?

Wrongdoing by one side does not erase wrongdoing by another.

## I. The accusation object

The primary accusation object is a 31-page dossier titled `Plagiarism Documentation - Tom Buck, First Baptist Lindale`, prepared in 2026 and later publicly hosted.

Direct inspection establishes that it:

- examines **17 sermons** spanning **2017–2026**;
- compares them with material from **five** Crossway *Preaching the Word* commentary volumes;
- relies in part on automated speech-to-text of public FBC Lindale sermon audio;
- uses a broad plagiarism methodology that includes different evidence types: verbatim/near-verbatim wording, paraphrase, illustration, sequencing of arguments/exegesis, observations and structural similarity.

Therefore `the dossier alleges plagiarism` and `the dossier independently proves every listed instance` are different claims.

## II. What is already established against Buck

### Real unattributed commentary dependence

Phil Johnson's first-person account says he reviewed the packet in detail and found examples where wording had been taken directly from commentaries without attribution. He advised Buck to stop the practice / improve attribution, and says Buck accepted the correction.

FBC Lindale's reported public explanation also acknowledges use of commentary material without proper citation/attribution and describes corrective action.

**Current finding:** Buck cannot responsibly be presented as wholly innocent of the underlying attribution problem.

### What has NOT been established

Johnson expressly distinguishes the documented wording problem from preaching another person's completed sermons. He says the material he reviewed did not show that Buck was taking finished sermons and preaching them as his own.

**Current finding:** the label `serial sermon thief` or a claim that Buck systematically preached complete sermons written by others is unsupported by the evidence presently acquired.

This remains the priority item because it is qualitatively stronger than generic exegetical overlap.

## IV. What FBC Lindale did

Phil Johnson's first-person chronology is highly significant:

- **March 27, 2026:** Buck himself sends Johnson the accusation packet;
- FBC Lindale elders contact Johnson the same day;
- Johnson reviews the evidence and advises correction where commentary wording lacked attribution;
- Buck agrees;
- Johnson reports that elders implemented corrective measures and that Buck addressed the matter before the church.

Other reporting of the FBC response says Buck recused himself while elders/deacons reviewed the allegations and outside advisors were consulted.

**Current conclusion:** `FBC Lindale did nothing` is not sustainable as a factual description. A critic may argue the church's remedy was insufficient, but that is different from asserting no investigation or correction occurred.

## V. The external anonymous campaign

Direct recipient testimony and later admissions establish that the dispute did not remain inside FBC Lindale.

### Established / strongly corroborated

- packets were sent anonymously to people outside FBC Lindale;
- recipients included public ministry partners / friends of Tom Buck;
- at least one recipient directly describes a Texas postmark and a false/third-party return-address presentation;
- the four Pray's Mill pastors later admitted that sending packets to Buck's public ministry partners/friends was wrong and that they should have contacted FBC Lindale directly rather than acting anonymously.

### Partially verified / unresolved

- Jon Norton is reported by witnesses/secondary investigations as the person who physically mailed material from Texas, but no direct confession or postal record has been acquired;
- reports about latex gloves remain secondary witness claims;
- exact preparation, printing and mailing chain remains unresolved;
- no direct evidence presently establishes a single motive such as revenge, destruction of Buck's ministry or institutional self-protection.

## VI. The four pastors' own admission

Multiple independent publications reproduce the repentance letter sent by **Scott Aniol, Matt Sikes, Dylan Joyner and Jon Norton** to FBC Lindale.

The letter contains direct admissions that they:

- `sinfully meddled` in the disciplinary process of an autonomous local church;
- characterized FBC Lindale's handling without knowing all the details;
- acted wrongly in sending packets to Buck's public ministry partners and friends;
- should have contacted FBC Lindale directly rather than sending packets anonymously;
- sinned against God, Tom Buck and FBC Lindale leadership;
- offered no excuse for the conduct.

This is now the strongest evidence in the 2026 case because it is an admission by the actors, not an inference from adversarial reporting.

## VIII. Legal rhetoric

Some recipients/commentators have described the mailing as federal `mail fraud` or `dozens of federal crimes`.

That characterization is **not established** by the historical evidence. Federal mail-fraud statutes require specific elements; deceptive anonymity or a misleading return address does not by itself establish a §1341 conviction or even that all elements are satisfied.

Historical article wording should distinguish:

- deceptive/anonymized mailing conduct — factual/moral question;
- false return-address evidence — evidentiary question;
- federal criminal liability — legal conclusion requiring a materially higher standard.

## IX. Institutional significance

The strongest institutional issue is not that G3 lacked a doctrine of local-church autonomy. G3 and its Church Network emphasized autonomy, plurality, discipline and local-church priority repeatedly.

The documented contradiction is therefore sharper:

> leaders who publicly taught and organized around local-church autonomy later admitted that they sinfully meddled in another autonomous local church's disciplinary process.

This supports a `profession-versus-practice` analysis. It does **not**, by itself, prove that G3 Church Network exercised denominational jurisdiction or that every member of the wider G3 organization participated.
