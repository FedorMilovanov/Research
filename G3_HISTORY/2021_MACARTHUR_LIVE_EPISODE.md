# January 2021 MacArthur `live` episode — evidence hold

**Status:** PARTIALLY_VERIFIED / ARCHIVE_HOLD  
**Purpose:** preserve an early communication-integrity lead without converting a polemical source into a final institutional verdict.

## Narrow factual chronology currently reproduced

A contemporaneous polemical compilation by Brent Detwiler reproduces screenshots/text from G3 social posts, email promotion and subsequent exchanges concerning a G3 Podcast interview with John MacArthur.

According to those reproduced artifacts:

1. **October 2020:** Josh Buice publicly posted a photograph from the MacArthur interview and said it had been recorded around the previous week / early October.
2. **January 6–7, 2021:** G3 promoted the upcoming stream using language including `will be sitting down`, `live edition of the G3 Podcast` and instructions to refresh the page if viewers did not see the `live event`.
3. During/after the stream, viewers asked whether it was live or prerecorded.
4. A G3 response said the interview had been recorded `a few weeks ago` and was premiering/streaming that day.
5. Buice likewise reportedly described it as recorded `a few weeks ago` / `a few weeks back`.
6. When confronted with the October post, Buice reportedly clarified: `It was early October.`
7. In a later explanation reproduced by the same source, Buice said G3 routinely prerecorded podcast interviews and then `streamed` those recordings as live video events for production/distribution purposes, and denied an intention to deceive.

## Two claims must remain separate

The episode contains **two analytically different questions**. They should not be compressed into one accusation.

### A. Was `live edition / live event` misleading?

Potentially, but there is a semantic defense that must be preserved.

A prerecorded program can be **live-streamed/premiered** in platform terminology. The response attributed to Buice explicitly offers that explanation: G3 recorded podcast interviews, performed post-production, then streamed the recording `live` on Facebook/YouTube.

At the same time, the reproduced promotional package did not merely say `live stream`. It reportedly used future-tense/person-to-person framing (`will be sitting down`) plus `live edition` / `live event`, language that an ordinary reader could reasonably interpret as a contemporaneous interview.

Therefore the strongest present formulation is:

`PROMOTIONAL_CLARITY_CONCERN / INTENT_UNPROVED`.

Do **not** write `G3 unquestionably lied by calling a prerecorded video a live stream`; the platform/use-of-`live` distinction is real.

### B. Was `a few weeks ago` chronologically accurate?

If the reproduced October and January posts are authentic, this problem is materially cleaner.

An interview recorded in early October and streamed on January 7 was roughly three months old, not ordinarily `a few weeks` old. The later reported clarification `It was early October` is itself inconsistent with the earlier `a few weeks ago/back` description.

This claim does **not** depend on whether a prerecorded video can technically be live-streamed.

Therefore, once original social/email artifacts are archive-pinned, the chronology discrepancy could support a narrower and substantially stronger factual claim than the disputed semantics of `live`.

## What is reasonably inferable if the artifacts are authentic

- `a few weeks ago/back` was materially inaccurate if the recording occurred in early October;
- the promotional language was capable of producing a reasonable contemporaneous-interview impression;
- G3/Buice later offered a production-language explanation for `live`, which must be included in a fair account;
- none of this, by itself, proves subjective deceptive intent.

## What is NOT established

- deliberate intent to deceive;
- a coordinated `cover-up`;
- that Buice personally authored every promotional sentence;
- that G3's use of `live` was inconsistent with every platform convention used by ministries at the time;
- any causal connection to the 2025 or 2026 anonymous-conduct scandals;
- that this incident was treated internally as an ethics concern.
