## Forensic split required in the final article

### A. Recording chronology

Potentially strong factual reconstruction if original posts are acquired:

- Oct. 4, 2020 Buice: interview occurred `last week`;
- Jan. 7, 2021 G3/Buice: `a few weeks ago/back`;
- later Jan. 7 Buice: `It was early October`.

The first and third are compatible with each other. The middle description is the narrow chronological discrepancy.

### B. `Live` semantics

Must be treated separately.

A prerecorded video can be distributed through Facebook/YouTube as a live stream or scheduled premiere. Buice's reproduced explanation explicitly appeals to that workflow. Therefore:

- `the interview itself was prerecorded` can be true;
- `the prerecorded video was streamed/premiered live` can also be true;
- marketing language such as `will be interviewing`, `live edition`, or `live event` may still have been misleading to an ordinary audience, but **intentional deception is not established merely by the word `live`**.

### C. Institutional-pattern inference

Do not use this episode as retrospective proof of the 2025 anonymous-account crisis. If included at all, it should be a narrowly bounded earlier transparency/communications episode with an explicit evidence-status box.

## Article-safe wording today

> A January 2021 G3 broadcast of a prerecorded John MacArthur interview generated questions about how the event had been marketed and when it had actually been recorded. A detailed contemporaneous compilation preserves posts in which Buice first described the interview as having occurred `a few weeks` earlier and later clarified that it was recorded in early October; the same compilation reproduces an October 2020 Buice post indicating the interview had occurred the previous week. G3's explanation was that it routinely premiered or streamed prerecorded podcast video live. Because the original social posts and subscriber email have not yet been independently archived in this research corpus, the episode remains partially verified and should not be used as proof of an organization-wide culture of deception.
