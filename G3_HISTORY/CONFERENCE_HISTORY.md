# G3 Conference — growth and historical reconstruction

**Status:** ACTIVE / PUBLICATION_HOLD

This file reconstructs the G3 Conference as a distinct institution before and during the G3 Ministries era. Attendance numbers are separated by source quality; registration and actual attendance must not be silently conflated.

## Prehistory — the project is publicly visible in 2011

An official G3 page dated **2011-08-10**, `https://g3min.org/hello-world/`, is now directly retrievable in search/crawl. Josh Buice announces a G3 Conference for January 2013, aimed at pastors, evangelists, missionaries and laypeople, and frames the purpose around God, gospel, sound doctrine and God's glory.

This closes an important chronology point:

- public conception/announcement: **2011**;
- first conference: **2013**;
- Georgia nonprofit incorporation: **2019**.

These are three different dates and must not be collapsed into a single `founded in 2013/2020` sentence without qualification.

## Phase 1 — 2013–2017: church-hosted annual conference

### 2013 — Gospel / message and mission

Official G3 conference archive route:

`https://g3min.org/archives/?conf=2013`

The surviving official archive labels the conference **`The Gospel: Message and Mission`** and preserves sessions on prayer, mission, the gospel, penal substitution and related theological themes.

Tim Challies’ 2025 first-person retrospective says:

- he was an invited speaker at the **debut event in 2013**;
- he spoke for the first eight years;
- the first couple of conferences were hosted **in and by Pray’s Mill Baptist Church**.

This is unusually useful participant evidence for both date and host-church character.

### 2014 — Doctrine of the Church

Official page, dated 2013-11-13:

`https://g3min.org/2014-g3-conference/`

The page gives:

- dates: Jan. 23–25;
- theme: **`The Doctrine of the Church`**;
- stated goal: learn about the church from Scripture rather than `latest church growth techniques`.

This directly supports the historical claim that the anti-pragmatism / Scripture / local-church emphasis was early, not a post-scandal invention.

Official session archive:

`https://g3min.org/archives/?conf=2014`

contains preaching on church, discipleship, ecclesiology, worship, mission and biblical preaching.

### 2015 — The Bible is the Word of God

Official review:

`https://g3min.org/2015-g3-conference-review/`

The page explicitly says the year’s theme was **`The Bible is the Word of God`** and frames the conference around confidence in Scripture.

### 2016 — Trinity

Official announcement:

`https://g3min.org/g3-2016/`

explicitly says the 2016 conference concerns **the doctrine of the Trinity**. The official session archive likewise preserves sessions on Trinity in salvation, worship, missions, sanctification and apologetics.

### 2017 — Reformation 500

Official archive labels surviving sessions as **`Reformation 500`**. The exact full official schedule still needs a single pinned route, but archive sessions strongly establish the theme.

## Phase 2 — 2018–2020: venue expansion and national scale

### 2018 — Knowing God / discipleship

Official session pages label the conference **`Knowing God`**, while contemporary promotional material gives the fuller theme **`Knowing God: A Biblical Understanding of Discipleship`**.

This is also the period in which G3's coalition begins to encounter the social-justice/CRT fault line; that ideological development should not be read backward into the 2013–2017 themes.

### 2019 — The Mission of God

Official archive:

`https://g3min.org/archives/?conf=2019`

preserves a heavily missions-centered program: mission, church planting, contextualization, suffering church, Islam, election and missions, and missionary theology.

G3’s own later `The Mission of God Study` explicitly says its curriculum was based on sermons preached at the **2019 G3 National Conference**.

### 2020 — Worship

Official archive:

`https://g3min.org/archives/?conf=worship`

labels the conference `Worship` and includes sessions from John MacArthur, Voddie Baucham, James White, Joel Beeke, Phil Johnson, Paul Washer, Costi Hinn and others.

Tim Challies’ Jan. 20, 2020 first-person report says he had just spoken at the annual G3 Conference in Atlanta and participated in a Q&A with MacArthur, Washer, Baucham, Lawson and Buice. This is a useful independent contemporary anchor for the broad conservative-Reformed coalition at that point.

By 2020, participant accounts put attendance around 5,000; later G3-linked historical material says 5,600. **Publication wording: roughly 5,000–5,600** until the original official attendance record is acquired.

## Phase 3 — 2021: 6,500 and formal institutional scale

Official archive:

`https://g3min.org/archives/christ/`

labels the 2021 conference **`Christ`** and preserves a predominantly Christological/theological main-session program. It also includes a session on Black Liberation Theology, demonstrating that culture/ideology disputes existed inside an otherwise strongly theological program.

FY2021 Form 990 program text states that G3:

- hired an Executive Director of Operations;
- hired an Executive Vice President;
- expanded online resources;
- increased conference attendance to **6,500**.

This is stronger than blog estimates because it is the organization’s own federal filing.

Tim Challies adds a different kind of primary evidence: before the 2021 event, Buice told him he was no longer sufficiently aligned with G3 and that renewed invitation would require signing the Dallas Statement and ceasing promotion/quotation of certain Christians. Challies declined. This is direct participant evidence of coalition narrowing after the broad first-eight-year era; it does not by itself adjudicate whether the boundary was theologically justified.

## Phase 4 — 2023: apparent attendance peak and a new right-flank dispute

Contemporary sources report:

- 8,000+ registered shortly before the conference;
- >8,200 attendees during the event;
- one exhibitor reports a crowd of over **8,400**.

Exact G3 final attendance remains `COARSE_LOCATOR_ONLY` until a primary event record is acquired.

An official G3 pre-conference schedule is especially important for ideological history. It contains:

- James White on non-compliance / civil questions;
- Owen Strachan explicitly **evaluating Christian Nationalism**;
- Scott Aniol on church/worship/kingdom;
- a panel titled as a friendly debate over **Christian Nationalism, theonomy, postmillennialism** and related matters.

This is evidence that by 2023 political theology had become an explicit G3 conference subject. Combined with Aniol’s published opposition to Christian Nationalism/theonomic prescriptions, it also blocks the opposite caricature that G3 simply became a Christian-nationalist institution.

The thematic record demonstrates a primarily theological/ecclesial national-conference identity for much of G3’s history. It does **not** mean G3’s broader media ministry was apolitical or free of culture-war content.

## Local-church motif

The local-church principle clearly predates the later crises:

- the 2011 announcement addresses pastors, missionaries, evangelists and lay church members;
- 2014 explicitly rejects church-growth technique as the answer and centers Scripture;
- early events were physically hosted in/by Pray’s Mill according to a long-term participant;
- later G3 material repeatedly describes its aim as equipping local churches.

The 2017 period also includes strong anti-conference-celebrity / ordinary-church rhetoric from speakers such as Voddie Baucham. G3’s 2025 post-Buice reset returned to similar language.

Historical implication: the local-church principle was not invented after scandal. The later governance story is therefore a question of **practice versus profession**, not absence of the doctrine.

## Important date correction: legal corporation vs ministry transition

Secondary histories often say G3 `became` or was `formed as` a 501(c)(3) ministry in **2020**. The Georgia Secretary of State Articles establish that `G3 Ministries for the Church, Inc.` was legally incorporated on **2019-06-06**.

Article wording must therefore distinguish:

- 2011 public conference conception;
- 2013 first conference;
- 2019 legal nonprofit incorporation;
- 2020-era operational/institutional transition often remembered by participants/secondary histories.

Do not replace the government filing date with the simplified 2020 retrospective.

## Research cautions

1. Debate-session audience numbers are not whole-conference attendance.
2. Pre-event registrations are not final attendance.
3. Marketing phrases such as `one of the largest` are context, not independently ranked claims.
4. Conference growth must not be treated as proof of theological correctness or institutional health.
5. Later scandal must not be projected backward as proof that early conference ministry was fraudulent.
6. Official G3 pages currently visible through search/crawl may be stale post-shutdown snapshots; use them as dated historical objects, not evidence of current operations.
7. A predominantly theological national-conference program does not prove the wider G3 media ecosystem was politically neutral.
