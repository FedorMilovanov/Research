## Phase 1 — 2013–2017: church-hosted annual conference

The first G3 Conference was held in 2013 on Pray’s Mill Baptist Church’s campus in Douglasville, Georgia. The founding rationale, as reproduced in G3 historical descriptions and by long-term participants, was to create a theology conference focused on God’s Word rather than church-growth pragmatism and technique.

Tim Challies, a speaker from the first event onward, recalls that the first couple of conferences were hosted in and by Pray’s Mill and emphasizes the local congregation’s hospitality. This is strong evidence that early G3 was not merely headquartered at PMBC; the church itself functioned as host infrastructure.

## Phase 2 — 2018–2020: venue expansion and national scale

By 2018 G3 had moved to the Georgia International Convention Center in College Park. The 2018 theme was `Knowing God: A Biblical Understanding of Discipleship`.

By 2020, contemporary participant Chris Anderson described attendance as about 5,000 and the conference as primarily a preaching event featuring major conservative/Reformed evangelical preachers. A G3 history description reproduced in contemporaneous material gives growth from 750 in 2013 to 5,600 in 2020.

**Publication wording:** use `roughly 5,000–5,600` unless the original 2020 registration/attendance record is pinned.

## Phase 3 — 2021: 6,500 and formal institutional scale

FY2021 Form 990 program text states:

- G3 hired an Executive Director of Operations;
- hired an Executive Vice President;
- expanded online resources;
- increased conference attendance to **6,500**.

This is stronger than blog estimates because it is the organization’s own federal filing.

Multiple attendees independently describe roughly 6,500 people from all 50 states and multiple countries. The 2021 national theme centered on Christ and maintained strong preaching/theological emphasis.

## Phase 4 — 2023: apparent attendance peak

Contemporary sources report:

- 8,000+ registered shortly before the conference;
- >8,200 attendees during the event;
- a Baptist Faith Missions exhibitor reports a crowd of **over 8,400**.

This is presently the best-supported peak-scale estimate, but exact G3 attendance remains `COARSE_LOCATOR_ONLY` until primary event records are acquired.

## Themes and institutional identity

The early conference topic sequence remains important evidence against retrospective caricature:

- 2013 — Gospel / message and mission
- 2014 — the church from a biblical perspective
- 2015 — Scripture / Word of God
- 2016 — Trinity
- 2017 — Reformation 500
- 2018 — knowing God / discipleship
- 2019 — mission of God
- 2020 — worship
- 2021 — Christ
- 2023 — sovereignty of God

This thematic record shows a primarily theological/ecclesial conference identity. It does **not** mean G3’s broader media ministry was apolitical or free of culture-war content.

## Local-church motif

A striking recurring theme predates the later crises. In the 2017 period, Voddie Baucham warned against becoming `conference junkies` and urged Christians to value ordinary local pastors and Lord’s Day means of grace above celebrity speakers. G3’s 2025 post-Buice reform language later returned to the same anti-celebrity/local-church concern.

Historical implication: the local-church principle was not invented after scandal; it was part of G3’s professed ecclesial identity years earlier. The later governance question is therefore about **practice versus profession**, not simple absence of the doctrine.

## Research cautions

1. Debate-session audience numbers are not whole-conference attendance.
2. Pre-event registrations are not final attendance.
3. Marketing phrases such as `one of the largest` are context, not independently ranked claims.
4. Conference growth must not be treated as proof of theological correctness or institutional health.
5. Later scandal must not be projected backward as proof that early conference ministry was fraudulent.
