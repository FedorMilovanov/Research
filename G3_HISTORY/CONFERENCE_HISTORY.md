# G3 Conference — growth and historical reconstruction

**Status:** ACTIVE / PUBLICATION_HOLD

This file reconstructs the G3 Conference as a distinct institution before and during the G3 Ministries era. Attendance numbers are separated by source quality; registration and actual attendance must not be silently conflated.

## From announcement to first meeting

The earliest invitation identified here survives not just as a later copy but in the [conference’s own RSS feed captured on 27 September 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110927042330id_/http://www.g3conference.com:80/?feed=rss2). Its second item, *Conference Announcement* (`?p=1`), carries the publisher’s date **10 August 2011**. The account is “G3 Team”; the invitation itself is signed by Pastor Josh Buice. He invites pastors, evangelists, missionaries and laypeople to metro Atlanta in January 2013 and promises registration details later. The archive establishes that the invitation was public by September. Neither the publisher’s timestamp nor the capture dates Buice’s private decision to organize it.

By the [23 September announcement about Steve Lawson](https://web.archive.org/web/20110928165117id_/http://www.g3conference.com/?p=99), the organizers could give the dates (24–26 January 2013), two planned messages and a Friday panel. Registration, they said, was **not yet open**. The [March 2012 capture of the theme page](https://web.archive.org/web/20120318150142id_/http://www.g3conference.com/?page_id=2) explains why the organizers paired *message* with *mission*: they wanted doctrinal teaching to lead to missionary work, not remain an exercise for pastors alone. Its three pillars were **Gospel** (what Christians proclaim), **Grace** (the source of salvation and perseverance) and **Glory** (the end of worship and mission). That explanation was public before the first conference; it need not be inferred from a later institutional history.

G3’s [later account](https://web.archive.org/web/20230407084022id_/https://g3min.org/about/history/) locates the first meeting on the Pray’s Mill Baptist Church campus in Douglasville, Georgia. [Tim Challies](https://www.challies.com/articles/you-me-and-g3/), writing on 23 May 2025, recalls not just a venue but a host: members of the congregation received the guests. The theological conference, the church that hosted it and the Georgia corporation formed on 6 June 2019 (filing 17370469) were not interchangeable institutions.

## Phase 1 — 2013–2017: the annual conference outgrows the church campus

### 2013 — Gospel / message and mission

G3’s [recordings index](https://web.archive.org/web/20140201124351id_/http://www.g3conference.com:80/?page_id=639), captured **1 February 2014**, is explicitly headed *2013 G3 Conference — The Gospel: Message & Mission*. It lists nine numbered main sessions **plus** a separate Q&A. The organizer identifies Pray’s Mill pastor Josh Buice as the moderator. These are credits and audio links, not ten recordings heard.

The [notes posted by Berean Wife on 30 January](https://web.archive.org/web/20130722032238id_/http://www.bereanwife.net/2013/01/g3-2013-conference-notes/) identify their author as her husband. Under Thursday, 24 January, he describes Steven Lawson expounding Galatians 1:6–10 against adding works to grace. Under Friday, 25 January, he records a different question from Tim Challies: if the Great Commission still stands, what can digital communication help Christians do, and what is lost when it replaces face-to-face contact? The [Q&A notes](https://web.archive.org/web/20130722013610id_/http://www.bereanwife.net/2013/01/qa-panel-discussion/), item 7, independently of the organizer refer back to Lawson’s exposition the night before. Both posts belong to the same blog, not two independent witnesses; neither is a transcript.

Tim Challies’ 2025 first-person retrospective says:

- he was an invited speaker at the **debut event in 2013**;
- he spoke for the first eight years;
- the first couple of conferences were hosted **in and by Pray’s Mill Baptist Church**.

This is unusually useful participant evidence for both date and host-church character.

A [schedule circulated on 25 January 2013](https://missionalevangelists.blogspot.com/2013/01/g3-conference.html) lists 24–26 January: David Miller opens, Tim Challies is listed for Friday on mission in a changing world, and Paul Washer closes Saturday on prayer. It is an unsigned relay of the programme, not a witness to each delivery.

The [recording catalogue](https://www.gnli.com/titles/2013-G3-Conference-or-G3-Ministries/2908) lists Challies’s [*Mission in a Changing World*](https://www.gnli.com/videos/Mission-in-a-Changing-World-or-Tim-Challies/337172) at 37:07. The programme gave this main session a 75-minute slot and listed a different talk about the family at noon. The slot is not a duration of speech, and the catalogue is not a transcript. Likewise, the [organizer’s speaker page captured on 4 April 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130404165821id_/http://www.g3conference.com/?page_id=30) already advertises **2014** speakers; it is not a roll of the people who preached in January 2013.

### 2014 — Doctrine of the Church

The former G3 paths `g3min.org/2014-g3-conference/` and `g3min.org/archives/?conf=2014` identify historical resources; they are not live evidence of what was said. The [2014 session catalogue](https://www.gnli.com/titles/2014-G3-Conference-or-G3-Ministries/2909) is a separate source for recorded titles, not a transcript.

A contemporary Founders Journal advertisement places the Jan. 23–25 conference at Pray's Mill Baptist Church and gives the theme as `The Church: From a Biblical Perspective`, consistent with the [Southern Equip advance notice](https://equip.sbts.edu/article/g3-conference-on-the-church/). These establish what was advertised, not independent observation of each appearance.

The [January 29 session and song list](https://web.archive.org/web/20160609231550id_/http://www.g3conference.com/g3-2014-song-list/) fixes the sequence; [Reid’s Sunday service](https://www.sermonaudio.com/sermons/126141332501/a) shows one listener turning Thursday’s second sermon into an appeal about hospitality back in Iowa. It does not record Challies on stage or the congregation’s subsequent practice.

### 2015 — The Bible is the Word of God

The organizer’s [archived blog index](https://web.archive.org/web/20160209061811id_/http://www.g3conference.com:80/category/g3-blog/) retains the entry *2015 G3 Conference Review* dated 30 January. The [recording catalogue](https://www.gnli.com/titles/2015-G3-Conference-or-G3-Ministries/2910) identifies sessions on inerrancy and exposition. The old `g3min.org/2015-g3-conference-review/` is not here treated as a retrieved full review.

#### From the 2014 pulpit to a church-planting appeal

The Southern Equip notice advertises Conrad Mbewe; G3’s later [Project Africa appeal](https://web.archive.org/web/20160130071448id_/http://www.g3conference.com/?page_id=1960), opening paragraphs, actually recalls his preaching in 2014 and a subsequent visit to Kabwata Baptist Church in Lusaka. The appeal named Lackson Mudenda as the church planter in Sesheke. Mudenda’s [own account published in April 2014](https://sola5.org/testimonies-from-our-2014-pastoral-interns-kabwata-baptist-church/) describes his internship at Kabwata. His local pastoral work did not begin with the American fundraising campaign.

The appeal distinguished three roles. Checks were to be made payable to **Pray’s Mill Baptist Church**. G3 said it would work with Mbewe and HeartCry to support the project. **Kabwata**, not the conference, was named as the church that ordained and sent Mudenda on 22 March 2015 and retained pastoral oversight. The estimate was $40,000 over eight years, through March 2023; the progress report in the January 2016 capture displayed 20%. These are a target and a fundraiser’s report, not receipts. The appeal predates the Georgia corporation: assigning the promised funds to its later Form 990 accounts would misidentify the payee. No recipient statement of disbursements or project closeout was located.

### 2016 — teaching the Trinity at Pray’s Mill

The [ticket page](https://web.archive.org/web/20151125102857id_/http://www.g3conference.com/?espresso_events=g3-conference-2016) gives 21–23 January and the Douglasville address. On 25 January, [Josh Buice](https://web.archive.org/web/20160129072313id_/http://www.deliveredbygrace.com:80/reflections-on-the-2016-g3/) described what the congregation had hosted: thirteen sessions in three days, or sixteen over five days **if** the Wednesday preconference and the Sunday sessions were included. His account puts his own address on the Great Commission first and Tim Challies’s overview of the Trinity second. The point, as Buice explained it, was to bring a doctrine often associated with seminary study into preaching, worship and congregational life. [H.B. Charles Jr.](https://hbcharlesjr.com/resource-library/articles/saturday-shout-outs-g3-_____-ministry-links/) wrote on 23 January that he himself had spoken. His testimony confirms his participation; his link to Buice does not independently confirm Buice’s session or livestream totals.

There is no defensible attendance total here. In the November 2015 ticket capture, *Total Tickets Sold* reads 437 even in the row for an **additional meal plan**. On the [18 January 2016 homepage](https://web.archive.org/web/20160118114654id_/http://www.g3conference.com/) the top link says registration is full while the lower panel says open. Neither a ticket-system field before the event nor contrary labels on one page tell us how many people came.

### 2017 — Reformation 500 at the convention center

In 2016 the [organizer’s page](https://web.archive.org/web/20160118114654id_/http://www.g3conference.com/) named Pray’s Mill as the venue. For 19–21 January 2017 its [next registration page](https://web.archive.org/web/20161202212832id_/http://www.g3conference.com:80/?) named the Georgia International Convention Center. Both versions stated the same three-part mission; the change of building did not imply a new charter. The prospectus offered a theological bookstore and meetings with other ministries. A November 2016 [AFA Journal notice](https://afajournal.org/past-issues/2016/november/g3-conference-to-celebrate-reformation/) advertised the Reformation anniversary programme. Neither notice proves that every announced speaker appeared.

The strongest evidence for **actual use** of the new venue comes from someone working there: in its [notes posted 31 January](https://ironsharpensironradio.com/2017/01/31/january-30-2017-show-with-interviews-from-the-2017-g3-conference-part-1-todd-friel-rich-pierce-and-doug-mcmasters/), Iron Sharpens Iron Radio identifies its booth in the center’s exhibition hall as the place where it interviewed Todd Friel, Rich Pierce and Doug McMasters for the 30 January programme. They are interviewees, not thereby three plenary speakers. The station described nearly 3,000 people; it gave no count method. This contemporary location evidence supports Buice’s later recollection of a 2017 move without depending on the [2025 interview transcript](https://thechristianworldview.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2025-0118-Why-Jimmy-Carters-Faith-is-Lauded-While-the-Faith-of-Others-is-Loathed.pdf). Neither the reasons and terms of the venue booking nor an audio timecode for that recollection are established here.

## Phase 2 — 2018–2020: venue expansion and national scale

### 2018 — Knowing God / discipleship

Official session pages label the conference **`Knowing God`**, while contemporary promotional material gives the fuller theme **`Knowing God: A Biblical Understanding of Discipleship`**.

G3’s [registration form captured in October 2017](https://web.archive.org/web/20171008215629id_/http://www.g3conference.com/events/2018-g3-conference/) offered a Spanish-only ticket ($119) and a combined ticket ($240). Although the generic access row repeats the main dates, the [organizer’s When/Where block](https://web.archive.org/web/20180123162725id_/http://g3conference.com/) specifies Wednesday the 17th for Spanish and Thursday–Saturday the 18th–20th for English. These are two programmes at the same venue, not a count of attendees or proof of simultaneous interpretation.

An [exhibitor’s account](https://www.missionsfirstlove.org/united-states-blog/g3-conference-in-atlanta-georgia), published 30 January, distinguishes the Spanish-language gathering on Wednesday from the main meeting of **18–20 January 2018**. FirstLove describes moving its table from the registration foyer into the exhibition hall and using a six-minute presentation slot to introduce its work. It was describing its own discount and access, not G3’s terms for every exhibitor. This is one way the convention center connected ministries beyond the main-stage addresses.

One actual address can be identified more precisely than the programme title. In his [post-conference note](https://www.challies.com/vlog/from-every-tribe-and-language-and-people-and-nation/), Tim Challies names his talk *Discipleship in Diversity*. He describes ending it with members of Grace Fellowship Church reading Revelation 5 in several languages. The linked clip was the conclusion, not the complete address or a response to a statement that had not yet been published.

The June Dallas meeting was not another national conference. In [*History and Formation*](https://web.archive.org/web/20180907001508id_/https://statementonsocialjustice.com/), ¶¶1–3, Tom Ascol distinguishes fourteen men at the meeting on 19 June from later readers who suggested edits. He attributes the first draft to himself, initial edits to Buice, and dates publication to 4 September. The separate *Initial Signers* list is not a roll call. The preamble disclaims ecclesiastical authority. That intention can coexist with Challies’s later account that signing mattered to **his** invitation; neither document establishes a rule for every speaker, let alone a cause of the later crises.

### 2019 — The Mission of God

The historical G3 route was `g3min.org/archives/?conf=2019`. The evidence below for the programme’s afterlife is the archived curriculum itself, not a present-day visit to that path.

Those sermons had a second life outside the auditorium. In the [2024 *Mission of God* study](https://web.archive.org/web/20240715000817id_/https://g3min.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/G3-The-Mission-of-God-Curriculum.pdf), editor Scott Aniol arranged material from seven contributors into ten lessons for Sunday schools, small groups and families (title page; contents, p. i; preface, p. ii). The first lesson, credited to Buice, asks readers of 2 Corinthians 5:17–6:2 what part a congregation plays in sending missionaries. The second, credited to Paul Washer, asks how the church should prepare a person before sending them (lessons 1–2, pp. 1–6). The questions and teaching outlines are evidence of editorial adaptation, not verbatim words from the 2019 stage. No record of how many churches taught the course was obtained.

### 2020 — Worship

The [2020 recording catalogue](https://www.gnli.com/titles/2020-G3-Conference-or-G3-Ministries/2905) lists sermons on worship by John MacArthur, Phil Johnson, Paul Washer and Joel Beeke. Titles identify the subject; they do not establish the words spoken.

Tim Challies’ Jan. 20, 2020 first-person report says he had just spoken at the annual G3 Conference in Atlanta and participated in a Q&A with MacArthur, Washer, Baucham, Lawson and Buice. This is a useful independent contemporary anchor for the broad conservative-Reformed coalition at that point.

[Elizabeth Prata’s Jan. 18 account](https://web.archive.org/web/20200918131906id_/https://the-end-time.org/2020/01/18/last-impressions-of-g3-conclusion/) names Derek Thomas among those she heard. His [Jan. 16 entry](https://subsplash.com/u/g3min/media/d/4q4pkdh-worshipping-our-triune-god) points to a board statement rather than presenting the sermon. On [Feb. 21](https://web.archive.org/web/20200223033728id_/https://g3conference.com/articles/statement-derek-thomas/) the board and president declined to publish this particular exposition of Luke 9:28–36 while explicitly declining to call it heresy. Their report of Thomas’s position is not a public reply from him.

The distinction between conference and ministry was already visible in the return signed on 1 April 2021. In [Part III, lines 1 and 4a](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/842403597/202110919349301551/full), G3 describes education through conferences **and online resources**, then reports online expansion alongside 5,600 attendees in January 2020. Earlier estimates around 5,000 are not a measured lower bound.

A concrete 2020 wide-audience photograph is now source-pinned in `MEDIA_LEDGER.md` to a contemporaneous Church Works Media report, but remains `PERMISSION_REQUIRED`.

## Phase 3 — 2021: 6,500 and formal institutional scale

G3’s historical conference index identified this edition as *Christ* (`g3min.org/archives/christ/`). This is a historical route, not a verified live programme. The filing establishes scale, not the content of individual sermons.

FY2021 Form 990 program text states that G3:

- hired an Executive Director of Operations;
- hired an Executive Vice President;
- expanded online resources;
- increased conference attendance to **6,500**.

This is stronger than blog estimates because it is the organization’s own federal filing.

Tim Challies adds a different kind of primary evidence: before the 2021 event, Buice told him he was no longer sufficiently aligned with G3 and that renewed invitation would require signing the Dallas Statement and ceasing promotion/quotation of certain Christians. Challies declined. This is direct participant evidence of coalition narrowing after the broad first-eight-year era; it does not by itself adjudicate whether the boundary was theologically justified.

### The church network: what a congregation was being asked to join

By **2 August 2021**, the [G3 Church Network application](https://web.archive.org/web/20210802180311id_/https://g3min.org/g3-church-network/) was public. G3 addressed a problem a conference alone could not solve: pastors could attend a national gathering and still go home isolated. It offered ongoing fellowship, access to resources and optional joint projects. It explicitly allowed churches to keep another denominational affiliation. In return it asked for doctrinal commitments and at least **$500 a year** from each member church. G3 said the gifts would support the production of teaching resources; it intended to distribute twelve-week studies without charge and give members discounts on books and commentaries. This was a funding proposal, not a statement of receipts.

The form shows how endorsement of the Dallas Statement worked differently for a church applying to the network than for a speaker invited to a conference. Pastors were asked to affirm the 1689 London Baptist Confession, even if it was not their congregation’s formal confession, and could explain reservations. G3 asked for willingness to sign the Dallas Statement or an explanation of a conscientious objection; the form distinguished agreement without a signature from having signed. Neither the form nor the [October 2025 version](https://web.archive.org/web/20251005142539id_/https://g3min.org/network/) identifies who was admitted with reservations. Questions about plural elders and church discipline also allowed applicants to say a practice was a goal rather than a current fact.

One congregation explained what the offer meant to it. On [9 February 2022](https://gardenchurchnb.com/the-g3-church-network/), Randy Benson wrote that The Garden Church, a non-denominational church plant in New Braunfels, had decided to join earlier that year. He described a desire for fellowship and training **and** alignment on contested questions such as social justice and pastoral office. He understood his church to retain its own governance. His account documents one church’s decision, not the motives or signatures of the whole membership.

## Phase 4 — 2023: attendance peak, sovereignty theme and a new right-flank dispute

### Main conference theme now primary-anchored

The main 2023 G3 National Conference theme is now directly anchored as **`The Sovereignty of God`** rather than merely inferred from associated content.

G3’s [study description captured on 14 July 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20240714222403id_/https://g3min.org/library-resources/the-sovereignty-of-god-study/) identifies the 2023 national sermons as the basis for a twelve-week course on sovereignty. This establishes what G3 offered to teachers, not the attendance at individual sessions or uptake in churches. The description was read; the linked study PDF was not obtained.

A contemporaneous official G3 article published immediately before the conference likewise states that attendees would gather under the thematic banner of **God's sovereignty**, with **13 main sessions and 15 breakout sessions**.

This matters historically: political theology had become an explicit conference subject by 2023, but the national event's main thematic frame remained a recognizably theological doctrine-of-God theme.

### Attendance

Contemporary sources report:

- 8,000+ registered shortly before the conference;
- Elizabeth Prata describes Walker welcoming **8,200** in her [21 September caption](https://the-end-time.org/2023/09/21/g3-conference-2023-day-1/); on [22 September, after the James Coates session](https://the-end-time.org/2023/09/22/g3-conference-2023-day-2/), she writes **more than 8,200**;
- another exhibitor/participant report gives a figure above 8,000.

G3 itself subsequently reported **8,300** in its [FY2023 Form 990](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/842403597/202411429349300611/full), signed 21 May 2024, p. 2, Part III.4a. That supersedes the earlier conclusion here that no organizer number was available. Prata’s caption is not a quotation from Walker, and her second-day lower bound is compatible with 8,300. No source here explains how anyone counted entrants.

That same line puts the meeting in perspective. G3 reported four pastors’ workshops, the launch of G3 Plus, twelve books, one music recording and a network that had grown to 211 churches. Compare [Aniol’s announcement of 1 September 2021](https://religiousaffections.org/update/important-announcement/), first paragraph and paragraph beginning *With my move*: he had accepted an editorial and executive role, but described books, curricula and a journal as work ahead. He mentioned possibly moving some Religious Affections titles to G3 Press, not transferring a complete catalogue. The later return is evidence of reported output, not of title-by-title rights, book sales or the number of active subscribers.

### Political-theology dispute

An official G3 pre-conference schedule is especially important for ideological history. It contains:

- James White on non-compliance / civil questions;
- Owen Strachan explicitly **evaluating Christian Nationalism**;
- Scott Aniol on church/worship/kingdom;
- a panel titled as a friendly debate over **Christian Nationalism, theonomy, postmillennialism** and related matters.

This is evidence that by 2023 political theology had become an explicit G3 conference subject. Combined with Aniol’s published opposition to Christian Nationalism/theonomic prescriptions, it also blocks the opposite caricature that G3 simply became a Christian-nationalist institution.

The resulting historical picture is not `theological conference → political conference`. A better model is **theological core + increasingly explicit culture/political boundary disputes layered around it**.

## After the national meeting: continuity and limits, 2024–2026

In 2024 the ticket desk was advertising different kinds of meetings. The [May registration](https://www.tickettailor.com/events/g3ministries/1010697) names a regional Reformation conference at Countryside Bible Church, 9–11 May. The [October publisher listing](https://www.tickettailor.com/events/g3ministries/1270768) offered exhibitors four passes at First Baptist Church of Mustang, Oklahoma, 3–5 October. These are organizer listings, not gate returns. In his [15 January 2025 newsletter](https://justinpeters.org/jpm-newsletter-2024/), under *Domestic Travels*, Justin Peters describes speaking at the Cessationist Conference. He supplies firsthand evidence of participation, not the reason for the Oklahoma venue. Neither meeting should extend the national-conference attendance series.

The cancellation of the 2025 national conference was not the end of all G3 activity. The board accepted Buice’s resignation on 8 May; its 12 May statement announced the cancellation. In June G3 announced a renewed emphasis on the local church, and in July appointed Scott Aniol president. Only in August 2026, in the separate dispute over intervention in the discipline process at FBC Lindale, did the Church Network announce its dissolution and the ministry announce a wind-down. The [timeline](TIMELINE.md) distinguishes the participants’ reproduced 25 August statement from later reports. The [subscriber letter](PRIMARY_SOURCE_G3PLUS_SUBSCRIBER_EMAIL_2026-08-27.md) described a transition still in progress. The [Georgia register](Q005_Q006_GEORGIA_FILING_DELTA_2026-11-15.md) later recorded a change of name effective 28 October, not a filing dissolving the corporation or an instrument conveying the publishing rights. In [*Our leadership*](https://lumenaministries.com/about/), read 20 November, the version read named Laramie Minga as president, formerly G3’s director of operations. A [separate reviewer](https://github.com/FedorMilovanov/Research/pull/582#issuecomment-7052347960) did not find the biography in another rendering. Neither its presence nor its absence is an officer filing. The endorsements in the first rendering anticipated carrying content forward. Neither the biography nor the endorsements identify a counterparty, catalogue of assets, or closing terms.

## Theme sequence — official/primary anchors now substantially stronger

- **2013** — The Gospel: Message and Mission — historical programme record
- **2014** — The Doctrine of the Church / Church from a Biblical Perspective — advertised programme + recording catalogue
- **2015** — The Bible is the Word of God — official review
- **2016** — The Trinity — official announcement + archive
- **2017** — Reformation 500 — historical programme record
- **2018** — Knowing God / discipleship — official archive + contemporary promotion
- **2019** — The Mission of God — 2024 curriculum preface
- **2020** — Worship — recording catalogue + Challies first-person report
- **2021** — Christ — historical title; Form 990 for scale
- **2023** — **The Sovereignty of God** — official G3 curriculum + contemporaneous official conference article

The thematic record demonstrates a primarily theological/ecclesial national-conference identity across the full pre-collapse history. It does **not** mean G3’s broader media ministry was apolitical or free of culture-war content.

## Local-church motif

The local-church principle clearly predates the later crises:

- the invitation dated 10 August 2011 in the organizer’s feed addresses pastors, missionaries, evangelists and lay church members;
- 2014 explicitly rejects church-growth technique as the answer and centers Scripture;
- early events were physically hosted in/by Pray’s Mill according to a long-term participant;
- later G3 material repeatedly describes its aim as equipping local churches.

The 2017 period also includes strong anti-conference-celebrity / ordinary-church rhetoric from speakers such as Voddie Baucham. G3’s 2025 post-Buice reset returned to similar language.

Historical implication: the local-church principle was not invented after scandal. The later governance story is therefore a question of **practice versus profession**, not absence of the doctrine.

## Important date correction: legal corporation vs ministry transition

Secondary histories often say G3 `became` or was `formed as` a 501(c)(3) ministry in **2020**. The Georgia Secretary of State Articles establish that `G3 Ministries for the Church, Inc.` was legally incorporated on **2019-06-06**.

Article wording must therefore distinguish:

- the invitation to January 2013 (publisher’s date: 10 August 2011);
- 2013 first conference;
- 2019 legal nonprofit incorporation;
- 2020-era operational/institutional transition often remembered by participants/secondary histories.

Do not replace the government filing date with the simplified 2020 retrospective.

## Research cautions

1. Debate-session audience numbers are not whole-conference attendance.
2. Pre-event registrations are not final attendance.
3. Marketing phrases such as `one of the largest` are context, not independently ranked claims.
4. Conference growth must not be treated as proof of theological correctness or institutional health.
5. Later scandal must not be projected backward as proof that early conference ministry was fraudulent.
6. The old domain can serve Lumena. Use an identified capture or attributed contemporary document; neither a successful response nor a snippet authenticates an old programme.
7. A predominantly theological national-conference program does not prove the wider G3 media ecosystem was politically neutral.
