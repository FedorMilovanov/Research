# Pray's Mill — authority, discipline, congregationalism, and 2026 dispute leads

**Status:** ACTIVE / PUBLICATION_HOLD  
**Purpose:** preserve a potentially important governance/ecclesiology line without converting one adversarial report into a verdict about an entire church.

## 1. Why this line matters to the G3 reconstruction

The 2026 Tom Buck crisis is not only a question about sermon attribution or anonymous mailings. Several of the admitted actors were Pray's Mill pastors/elders while also holding G3 leadership or ministry roles. Therefore the article may eventually need to ask whether local-church authority structures, conflict procedures, and overlapping G3/PMBC roles affected the crisis.

That question is legitimate. A conclusion that Pray's Mill had a proven `culture of spiritual abuse` is **not** presently established.

## 2. Current official Pray's Mill public doctrine — dated website-state evidence

### A. Church discipline

Current Pray's Mill page:

- https://praysmill.com/articles/why-we-practice-church-discipline/

It publicly describes discipline as restorative rather than punitive and presents Matthew 18:15–17 as the governing pattern:

1. private confrontation;
2. one or two witnesses;
3. matter brought before the church through the elders;
4. removal only after continued refusal to repent.

It says the goal at every stage is restoration and says discipline applies to those who voluntarily joined the covenant community.

**Evidence state:** official current institutional doctrine / `A2` website-state.

**Critical date guard:** the page was observed/crawled in September 2026. Unless an archived copy is acquired, it must not be silently treated as proof that this exact wording/policy page governed an April 2026 dispute.

### B. Plurality and accountability

Current leaders page:

- https://praysmill.com/about-pm/our-leaders/

states that each local congregation is shepherded by a plurality of elders and describes each elder as accountable to the other elders **and to the church**.

The same page is demonstrably stale on some personnel/status details after the August/September crisis, so it is reliable as a dated public statement of claimed ecclesiology, not as controlling evidence of current office holders.

### C. Liberty of conscience

Current Pray's Mill presentation of 1689 chapter 21:

- https://praysmill.com/about-pm/the-1689-confession/chapter-21-of-christian-liberty/

states that God alone is Lord of the conscience and that requiring implicit faith or absolute/blind obedience destroys liberty of conscience.

The broader beliefs page also repeats the `God alone is Lord of the conscience` principle:

- https://praysmill.com/about-pm/pmbc-beliefs/

**Evidence state:** strong for present public confessional position; not automatic proof of how every historical conflict was handled.

### D. Confessional identity

Current doctrine pages identify Pray's Mill as a confessional Reformed Baptist church holding the Second London Baptist Confession of 1689.

- https://praysmill.com/about-pm/our-doctrine/
- https://praysmill.com/about-pm/the-1689-confession/

This supplies a legitimate normative benchmark for later theological analysis. It does not itself adjudicate disputed facts about member discipline.

## 3. Protestia's internal-correspondence report — important B1 lead, not primary closure

On Aug. 17, 2026, David Morrill/Protestia reported that its Buck investigation also obtained internal Pray's Mill correspondence and testimony involving disputes with former members.

Relevant article:

- https://protestia.com/2026/08/17/g3-promised-cooperative-not-competitive-the-anonymous-targeting-continued/4/

The report says a dispute involving former member and former G3 board chairman Jonathan Frazier began with an **April 9** complaint regarding Pastor Matt Sikes. According to Protestia's description of the documents:

- the complaint alleged a broader pattern of harsh/spiritually harmful conduct;
- it contained both firsthand-witness references and some speculative/subjective material;
- Frazier requested witness interviews, recusal of implicated elders, and consideration of outside assistance;
- an **April 17** elders/deacons response rejected roughly 80 allegations as false/without basis, raised Matthew 18 and 1 Tim. 5:19 concerns, required repentance/recantation on the complainant-family side, and reportedly said leaving the church after discipline began was `not an option` absent repentance;
- Protestia says threatened/pending discipline was dropped by **April 30** and the family was permitted to withdraw peacefully without excommunication.

### Current evidence grade

`B1 / INTERNAL-DOCUMENT-HOLDER REPORT / PRIMARY_OBJECT_HOLD`.

Why not higher:

- the underlying Apr. 9, Apr. 17 and Apr. 30 documents are not presently acquired in this corpus;
- Protestia is an adversarial/polemical actor in the wider G3/Buck controversy;
- its theological interpretation and words such as `gaslit`, `manipulation`, or `spiritual abuse` are its assessment, not self-proving facts;
- the report itself acknowledges weaknesses/speculative elements in the original complaint.

## 4. What may be investigated if the documents are acquired

The important questions are procedural and evidentiary, not slogan-based:

1. What exactly did the Apr. 9 complaint allege, item by item?
2. Which allegations had named firsthand witnesses?
3. Were those witnesses interviewed before the Apr. 17 response?
4. Which elders were subjects of allegations and did any recuse?
5. What had already occurred under Matthew 18 before the written complaint?
6. Was formal church discipline actually opened against the Frazier family, and by what congregational/elder/deacon action?
7. What exact repentance or public recantation was required?
8. Did PMBC formally claim a member could not transfer/withdraw during discipline, and under what bylaw/confessional authority?
9. What changed between Apr. 17 and Apr. 30?
10. Was the peaceful withdrawal a reversal, negotiated resolution, clarification that discipline had not formally begun, or something else?
11. How did the operative 2026 constitution/bylaws define elder authority, congregational authority, membership withdrawal and discipline?

These questions cannot be answered by the current public doctrine page alone.

## 5. Potential contradiction to test — NOT YET A FINDING

If the reported Apr. 17 correspondence is authentic and accurately described, there may be a material tension between:

- current public language that discipline is carefully staged, restorative and tied to voluntary covenant membership;
- public confessional language that God alone is Lord of the conscience and blind obedience is forbidden;
- reported language that withdrawal was `not an option` and that disputed judgments of leaders required public repentance.

But the tension is presently a **research hypothesis**, not an article-ready accusation.

Why:

- current website wording may postdate the dispute;
- we lack the complete correspondence and surrounding communications;
- church-discipline theology can distinguish ordinary voluntary departure from attempted departure during an already pending disciplinary case;
- the status of actual congregational action is unclear;
- the complainants' and elders' factual claims need independent testing.

## 6. Relation to the Buck affair

The line becomes relevant to G3 only if role/timing evidence supports it.

What is already supportable:

- PMBC and G3 were operationally intertwined;
- multiple PMBC pastors had G3 roles;
- four pastors later admitted improper anonymous external conduct/interference in FBC Lindale's autonomous-church process;
- G3/PMBC publicly emphasized local-church autonomy and accountability.

What is **not** currently supportable:

- the Frazier dispute caused the Buck campaign;
- the same motive or strategy governed both disputes;
- every PMBC elder approved all disputed conduct;
- the church as a whole endorsed anonymous external targeting;
- Protestia's broader `spiritual abuse` diagnosis has been independently established.
