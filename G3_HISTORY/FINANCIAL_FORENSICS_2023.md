# FY2023 financial forensics — G3 Ministries

**Status:** ACTIVE / EVIDENCE_HOLD on exact Part IX rows  
**Entity:** G3 Ministries for the Church, Inc. — EIN 84-2403597

## Why FY2023 matters

FY2023 is the first large financial reversal in the reconstructed G3 series:

- FY2022 revenue: **$1,735,978**
- FY2022 expenses: **$1,010,186**
- FY2022 result: **+$725,792**

versus:

- FY2023 revenue: **$1,705,689**
- FY2023 expenses: **$2,071,986**
- FY2023 result: **−$366,297**

Revenue therefore remained almost flat while spending increased by approximately **$1.062m / 105%** year over year.

## What the filing already rules out

### Payroll alone cannot explain the spike

ProPublica’s IRS extraction reports FY2023 `Other Salaries and Wages` of **$292,784**, only **14.1%** of total expenses. Even allowing for benefits/payroll taxes elsewhere in Part IX, the roughly $1.06m increase cannot be explained primarily by the visible wage line.

### Current derivative Part IX evidence points to program allocation, not administrative expansion

The FY2024 IRS-origin parser at Philanthropy.org reports:

- FY2024 program ratio: **84%**;
- change from prior filing year: **−16 percentage points**.

This mathematically implies a prior-year program ratio of approximately **100%** for FY2023 under that parser’s reading of Part IX. Separate IRS-derived nonprofit summaries also describe FY2023 as essentially/all program-service spending.

**Evidence class:** `B1` until the raw FY2023 Part IX columns are directly acquired.

### What this does and does not mean

It supports:

> The available IRS-derived allocation data make a theory that the FY2023 spike was chiefly management, fundraising or personal administrative consumption substantially less likely.

It does **not** support:

- that every FY2023 expense was wise or necessary;
- that all expenses labeled program service were economically efficient;
- that no related-party transaction existed;
- that a program-service classification independently proves arm’s-length pricing;
- that the exact cause of the extra ~$1.06m has been identified.

A nonprofit can classify large event, production, content, publishing, venue, travel, contractor and other mission-facing costs as program services. The exact Part IX rows are still needed.

## Working explanation hierarchy

### H1 — conference / event scale-up

**Plausibility: HIGH / unproved.**

G3 returned to a very large national-conference model in 2023, with contemporary attendance estimates around 8,000–8,400. Large venue, A/V, production, travel, staging, logistics and event-service costs are a plausible major contributor.

### H2 — broader ecosystem expansion

**Plausibility: HIGH / unproved.**

By this period G3 was operating publishing, G3+, a journal, a church network, workshops and substantial content production. Scott Aniol’s 2021 announcement shows this expansion was deliberate strategy rather than accidental drift.

### H3 — payroll / executive compensation caused most of the spike

**Plausibility: LOW on current evidence.**

The visible FY2023 wage line is too small relative to the total increase. No executive compensation is shown in ProPublica’s headline expense extraction for FY2023.

### H4 — administrative/fundraising bloat caused most of the spike

**Plausibility: LOW on current evidence.**

The derivative program-ratio evidence points in the opposite direction. This hypothesis should not be used in the article absent contradictory raw Part IX data.

### H5 — fraud / diversion / personal enrichment

**Plausibility: UNSUPPORTED.**

No acquired filing, government record or audit currently supports diversion or embezzlement. FY2024 explicitly reports no material diversion of assets. Schedule L existence in FY2024/FY2025 is not evidence of misconduct.
