## Wave 1 controlling findings

### Confirmed documentary anchors

- Jules Gouffé, *Le Livre de Pâtisserie* (1873), directly documents a Saint-Honoré built from a pastry base, a crown of pâte à choux and small choux. This is an early exact architectural witness, not an origin proof.
- Gouffé 1873 directly documents `pains à la duchesse` and notes that varied small pastries called `éclairs` belong to that family. CNRTL/BHVF additionally records pastry use of `éclairs` in 1856, earlier than the 1864 Littré dictionary label.
- Louis Bailleux's *Le Pâtissier moderne* (1856) has been identified as a full historical professional object and tightly located by specialist secondary research to an `Éclairs` recipe on p. 74. Direct scan-page inspection remains a `LOCATOR_HOLD`; the book/date identity itself is no longer open.
- A New York *Vanity Fair* occurrence of `éclairs` is tightly located to 2 February 1861, p. 50 col. 1. The original issue scan is still required before A-class/quote-safe promotion, but this is now a strong early American-transmission target.
- Gouffé 1873 directly documents `flan à la religieuse` in forms that do not reduce to the later standard two-stacked-choux silhouette. Therefore the modern geometry must not be projected unchanged back to every nineteenth-century use of the name.
- Gouffé 1873 distinguishes `baba moderne glacé au rhum`, `baba polonais` and `savarin`, proving that rum baba and savarin were already distinct named professional forms by that date.
- BnF/Gallica documents the first Paris–Brest–Paris cycle race as 1891. Current Pâtisserie Durand family material dates Louis Durand's cake to 1910. A La Poste institutional recap instead says 1909, while France.fr has circulated inconsistent 1905/1891 formulations. The race date and cake date are therefore explicitly separated.
- Current Dalloyau material consistently attributes the canonical modern Opéra to the Maison in 1955. Secondary locator work now points to *Le Gaulois*, 18 March 1899, p. 3, for an older Grand Hôtel `gâteau opéra` advertisement. The primary issue page is not yet acquired, so name genealogy and canonical architecture remain separate claims.
- Comité Colbert and Pierre Hermé Japan independently preserve a 1997 Ispahan anchor. Hermé interviews preserve an earlier flavor genealogy through rose and `Paradis`; this is not to be collapsed into a single invention date.
- Pierre Hermé participant material directly documents the contemporary 2000 Feuilles composition/textural concept and signature status. Exact first launch/date/name rationale remains unresolved; `2000` must not be explained by layer count or millennium without primary support.
- Mont-Blanc **predates Angelina as a category/lineage**. Artusi 1891 directly documents an Italian chestnut-strand + whipped-cream construction; a verified-first-printing preservation source locates a dessert actually named `Mont Blanc` to Fannie Farmer's 1896 first edition, p. 357, before Angelina's 1903 founding; Escoffier 1903 independently provides French professional continuity. Angelina therefore remains a major house-signature milestone, not the inventor of the whole category.
- Région Nouvelle-Aquitaine explicitly rejects the convent-origin story of the modern canelé as established history and reports absence of a modern-canelé trace before the twentieth century in the cited 1995 research. The 1985 `canelé` collective-mark story remains a priority for exact INPI acquisition.
- Official Lamotte-Beuvron material preserves the Tatin local tradition. Secondary locator work now points to *Le Journal*, 18 December 1899, p. 1, for `la tarte de Mlle Tatin`; the exact primary issue remains to acquire. The accident story remains `LEGEND / LOCAL TRADITION` until a contemporaneous record proves it.
- Tarte Tropézienne must be split into separate chronology nodes: the current Maison dates Micka's pastry creation to 1955, while independent film records date the Saint-Tropez shoot of *Et Dieu… créa la femme* to **3 May–7 July 1956**. Therefore `Bardot named it in 1955 during filming` is rejected as current wording. Later Bardot testimony strongly supports her `marraine`/promotion role, but exact naming mechanism remains retrospective.
- Secondary legal sources converge on **18 August 1972** for a French Tropézienne process/patent filing; exact historical INPI patent number/object and corresponding historical mark record remain `LOCATOR_HOLD`. Later 2013 French marks are independently documented and must not be substituted for the 1972 origin filing.
- Élysée sources directly document the modern presidential galette ritual and its no-fève republican form; they do not prove an uninterrupted Saturnalia-to-modern-galette genealogy.

## Evidence boundary

`A3` participant or institutional pages prove what the participant/institution currently states; they do not automatically prove a historical event decades or centuries earlier. Historical event claims require contemporaneous/full-object evidence or explicit bounded wording.

`B1` can triangulate, discover locators and expose conflicts but cannot be sole quote-safe support for a disputed claim.

Search snippets and unacquired catalog records remain `C`/discovery until the full object or exact locator is verified.

Tight secondary locators such as `Bailleux 1856 p.74`, `Vanity Fair 1861 p.50 col.1`, `Le Gaulois 1899 p.3` and `Le Journal 1899 p.1` are materially better than vague discovery, but they remain `LOCATOR_HOLD` until the primary scan/object is directly inspected.

## Rights boundary

Textual historical evidence and visual publication rights are handled separately. A digitized historical object can be excellent evidence while its reproduction remains `RIGHTS_HOLD` or `PERMISSION_REQUIRED`. No visual asset is production-approved merely because it is viewable or downloadable.

Modern maison photography remains reference-only unless explicit permission/licence is obtained. The preferred future `/canon/` visual archive is genuine facsimile material with item-level rights: historical recipe pages, menus, advertisements, trademark/patent records and archival photography.
