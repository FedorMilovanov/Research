## Wave 2 controlling findings

### Confirmed / materially strengthened documentary anchors

- Jules Gouffé, *Le Livre de Pâtisserie* (1873), directly documents recognizable Saint-Honoré, Religieuse, Éclair, baba and savarin forms with exact page locators already stored in the dossiers/ledger.
- **Éclair now has an earlier French documentary target than 1856.** Specialist historical-language research locates Charles Paul de Kock's 1848 *L’Atelier de demoiselles ou l’apothicaire de Pontoise*, printed p. 4 col. 1, to an explicit cream-filled `éclairs` pastry description. Hachette BnF independently confirms the original edition date **1848** and BnF-digitized provenance. The underlying BnF/Gallica page still has to be directly acquired; 1848 is therefore the earliest **current locator**, not an absolute first-use or invention claim.
- Louis Bailleux's *Le Pâtissier moderne* (1856) is identified as a full historical professional object; specialist historical research tightly locates `Éclairs` to printed p. 74. Direct page scan remains required before quote-safe promotion.
- University of Michigan identifies the 1861 New York *Vanity Fair* volume as a public-domain U.S. periodical object; specialist research tightly locates an `éclairs` occurrence to 2 February 1861, p. 50 col. 1. Exact page inspection remains open.
- **Religieuse:** Gouffé 1873 remains the earliest directly verified professional Religieuse recipe/form in the current corpus. Modern institutional/professional sources repeatedly connect an ancestor to Frascati around 1856, and Bailleux's 1856 first-edition provenance independently confirms that he trained under M. A. Cintract of Maison Frascati. Targeted pre-1873 searches for `flan à la religieuse`, `religieuse au café/chocolat`, Frascati/Cintract and adjacent terminology have not yet produced an inspectable earlier recipe. This is negative-search coverage, not evidence of absence; Frascati 1856 remains unresolved.
- BnF/Gallica documents the Paris–Brest–Paris race in 1891. Current Durand family/regional sources date Louis Durand's cake to 1910, while La Poste gives 1909 and a direct RTL interview with Louis Durand's great-grandson also says the family has kept the same cake since 1909. A weak local Maisons-Laffitte chronology additionally preserves a 1911 date and mentions pâtissier Bauget; this is `C/DISCOVERY` only and widens the primary acquisition window to **1909–1911** rather than creating a new accepted date.
- Current Dalloyau material attributes the canonical modern Opéra to the Maison in 1955. Wave 2 resolves the exact primary route for the older name lead to *Le Gaulois*, 18 March 1899, p. 3: `https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5305801/f3.image.langFR`. Route resolved is not content verified; identity with Dalloyau 1955 is not assumed.
- Comité Colbert and Pierre Hermé Japan independently preserve a 1997 Ispahan anchor; earlier `Paradis` / rose genealogy remains separate.
- **2000 Feuilles naming context is materially strengthened.** Pierre Hermé participant material documents current composition/signature status, and a Gault&Millau article structured around Hermé's direct recollections links the `2000 Feuilles` renaming to the millennium context. Exact first sale/catalogue date remains open. A first-person Hermé author-text points to an earlier Ladurée `Millefeuille praliné` precursor, but the currently searchable copy is not on an authorized publisher surface; the Ladurée sequence remains an acquisition lead until legitimate source custody is obtained.
- Mont-Blanc now has a strong pre-Angelina chain. Artusi 1891 documents a close chestnut-strand + whipped-cream construction. Fannie Merritt Farmer's **1896 first-edition full object** is directly verified in Internet Archive (`bostoncookingsc00collgoog`, `ark:/13960/t0ns15r2w`), and printed p. **357** is directly located to a recipe headed `Mont Blanc`. This closes the bounded claim that a named Mont Blanc existed before Angelina's 1903 founding. Page-image verification remains required before quotation/facsimile use. Escoffier 1903 independently supplies French professional continuity.
- Région Nouvelle-Aquitaine rejects the convent-origin story of the modern canelé as established history. The 1985 professional institutionalization is independently strengthened by French pastry-industry material describing the Gironde pâtissiers' decision to create the Confrérie and identifying its early Grand Maître. Région and Bibliothèque de Bordeaux/Eurêkoi independently report the one-`n` spelling and collective-mark deposit. The **exact 1985 INPI mark object remains open**.

### Tatin — controlling documentary and myth-genealogy findings

Tatin now requires a layered historiography rather than a single origin anecdote.

- Exact primary route is identified for *Le Journal*, **18 December 1899, p.1**: `https://www.retronews.fr/journal/le-journal/18-decembre-1899/129/238489/1`. University of Liège historian Pierre Leclercq, following Henri Delétang, identifies Gabriel Hanotaux as the author in this research line. Direct page inspection remains open.
- A **separate contemporaneous 1903 printed witness** is now tightly located. Leclercq/Delétang identify an excursion report in `Bulletin de la Société de géographie du Cher`, `1re année, 1902-1903`, **pp.127–138**, describing Hôtel Tatin and a hot apple tart as a house specialty associated with Mlle F. Tatin. A digitized full-volume object is independently identified through Google Books / University of Michigan provenance: `https://books.google.com/books/about/Bulletin.html?id=N_efAAAAMAAJ`. Exact relevant scan page still requires direct inspection.
- **Jullemier chronology is corrected:** modern synthesis describes a meal/event around c.1903, while *Contes de Sologne* is bibliographically dated to **1915**. `c.1903` must not be presented as Jullemier's publication date.
- BnF independently identifies the 1921 periodical `Blois et le Loir-et-Cher`, ISSN **0995-8347**, and Tolbiac holding **`4-LC11-1357`**, in which later sources place Paul Besnard's `La Tarte des Demoiselles Tatin`. Exact 1921 issue/month/page remains open.
- Primary routes are identified for 1923 transmission through Gallica and *Comœdia*; exact relevant wording remains to inspect.
- BnF independently confirms Curnonsky & Marcel Rouff's **1926 `L'Orléanais`** volume in *La France gastronomique*; exact Tatin page remains open. No acquired primary Curnonsky passage currently supports the claim that he invented the accident anecdote.
- **The accident/distraction story is a late documentary layer, not early evidence in the current corpus.** Leclercq/Delétang report no Curnonsky publication carrying the alleged story; they locate a vague clumsiness precursor in *Horizons d'Argonne* **1976** (cited to p.97) and associate the developed promotional legend with the Confrérie des Lichonneux around **1979**. The original 1976 page and original 1979 prospectus remain acquisition targets.
- The current Confrérie states formal Journal officiel registration on **27 March 1979** and its own history page explicitly labels the birth narrative a `légende`, while retelling multiple mutually incompatible mechanisms. This is strong participant evidence for **modern myth transmission**, not for a nineteenth-century accident.
- Therefore safe current interpretation is: documentary evidence establishes a renowned Hôtel Tatin apple-tart specialty by 1899–1903; the famous accident story enters the located documentary tradition much later. This does not prove that no oral accident story ever existed; it prevents projecting a late legend backward as contemporaneous fact.

## Evidence boundary

`A3` participant/institutional pages prove what that participant/institution currently states; they do not automatically prove an earlier historical event.

`B1` can triangulate and discover exact locators but cannot be the sole quote-safe support for a disputed claim.

An exact archive URL is materially stronger than a vague citation, but **route resolved != content verified**. Gallica/RetroNews/Google Books routes whose relevant page/context was not directly inspected remain under locator/content-verification hold.

An authored copyrighted text found on an unauthorized user-upload surface may identify a valuable acquisition target but is not promoted to quote-safe evidence merely because wording is searchable. Obtain an authorized book/publisher/library object first.

Negative search coverage may narrow what is currently documented; it never proves historical non-existence.

OCR inside a verified historical full object can establish navigation and bounded content identity, but repository policy still requires page/context verification before a direct quotation is marked quote-safe.
