## Dates prohibited as simple origin labels at current state

- Saint-Honoré `1847` / `1863`: not primary-closed; Lacam page-level locator instead points to an 1840 Maison Chiboust account on p.44, while p.49 is a separate technical statement.
- Éclair `1856 as first known pastry use`: superseded by tightly located 1848 de Kock occurrence; exact 1848 primary page still needs direct scan inspection, and 1848 must not be called an invention date.
- Religieuse `1856 as closed invention`: multiple modern Frascati retellings exist, but Gouffé 1873 remains the earliest directly verified professional recipe in the current corpus; negative search coverage is not absence proof.
- Paris-Brest cake `1891`: race date only. Exact cake chronology remains 1909/1910 conflict; 1911 is only a weak discovery signal.
- Kouign-Amann `1860`: local tradition pending early record.
- Tatin `Jullemier published in 1903`: incorrect current wording; c.1903 is the described meal/event, while *Contes de Sologne* is bibliographically dated 1915.
- Tatin accident as nineteenth-century origin fact: unsupported by current early documentary network; located printed myth genealogy appears much later, with a 1976 vague precursor and developed Confrérie narrative around 1979.
- `Curnonsky invented the Tatin accident legend`: no acquired primary support; current historical synthesis reports no such Curnonsky passage.
- Canelé convent/18th-century origin: not established by current regional research.
- Éclair `Carême / early 1800s`: no exact primary invention locator.
- Mont-Blanc `1903 invention`: rejected; named Mont Blanc is directly present in Farmer 1896 p.357 and related construction in Artusi 1891.
- 2000 Feuilles `2000 = literal layer count`: rejected; direct-interview evidence ties renaming to millennium context, exact first catalogue/date still open.
- Tropézienne `Bardot named it in 1955 during filming`: incompatible with documented May–July 1956 filming.
