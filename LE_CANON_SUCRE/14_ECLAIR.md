# 14 — Éclair

## Проверяемый вопрос

Когда `éclair` фиксируется именно как pâtisserie; как он связан с `pains à la duchesse`; можно ли доказать популярную атрибуцию Carême; насколько быстро название выходит за пределы Франции?

## Краткий статус

`PASTRY TERM ATTESTED 1856 / PROFESSIONAL RECIPE IN BAILLEUX 1856 LOCATED TO p.74 / U.S. ENGLISH-LANGUAGE USE TIGHTLY LOCATED TO 1861 / DUCHESSE→ÉCLAIR RELATION DOCUMENTED 1873 / CAREME INVENTION UNRESOLVED`

## Lexicographic institutional evidence

CNRTL / TLFi etymology:
https://www.cnrtl.fr/etymologie/%C3%A9clair

TLFi gives **1864** (Littré) for the pastry sense.

CNRTL / BHVF attestations:
https://www.cnrtl.fr/definition/bhvf/%C3%A9clair

BHVF provides an earlier **1856** literary occurrence in H. de Kock in a clearly pastry list. Therefore 1864 should not be called the first known pastry use merely because it is the dictionary milestone.

## Early American transmission — 1861

The same specialist historical-language study provides a precise U.S. locator for the pastry word in English:
https://wordhistories.net/2025/03/25/eclair-cake/

It identifies Fitz-Hugh Ludlow's *The Primpenny Family*, chapter 4, in **Vanity Fair (New York), 2 February 1861, p. 50, col. 1**, where `éclairs` occur in an ordinary list of sweets alongside macaroons and sponge cake.

A separate OED-based food-history recap preserves the same issue/page-column locator:
https://www.hungrymonster.com/food-facts/food_facts.php?fid=9112&p=Breads

Current evidence decision:

- exact U.S. date/issue/page/column = `TIGHT SECONDARY LOCATOR`;
- original *Vanity Fair* issue scan = `NOT_ACQUIRED / LOCATOR_HOLD`;
- safe bounded inference: the French pastry name was circulating in American English by 1861, only five years after the 1856 French evidence now in the corpus;
- unsafe inference: this proves a specific France→New York transmission route or identifies the first American bakery selling it.

This is valuable for the future Canon because it measures **early international reception**, not origin.

## Historical primary evidence — Gouffé 1873

Pâte à choux for croquembouches, pains à la duchesse and éclairs:
https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AGouff%C3%A9_-_Le_Livre_de_P%C3%A2tisserie%2C_1873.djvu/121

Duchesse/éclair observation:
https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AGouff%C3%A9_-_Le_Livre_de_P%C3%A2tisserie%2C_1873.djvu/349

Gouffé directly treats varied small pastries called `éclairs` in relation to `pains à la duchesse` and specifies pâte à choux as their shared technical basis.

This is a strong professional witness to terminology/form evolution.

## Popular Carême attribution

Modern tourism/food pages frequently connect the éclair or its elongated predecessor to Antonin Carême. Wave 1 has **not** found an exact Carême page saying he invented an éclair or changed a duchesse into the named pastry.

Therefore:

`CAREME_INVENTED_ECLAIR = UNRESOLVED / EVIDENCE_HOLD`.

Carême can be discussed only after his own books or contemporaneous sources are checked with exact locators.

## Ответы, отрицания и альтернативные объяснения

- 1856: pastry word attested in BHVF literary evidence;
- 1856: Bailleux first-edition professional book now has a tight locator to an `Éclairs` recipe at p. 74;
- 1861: U.S. English-language occurrence tightly located to *Vanity Fair*, 2 Feb., p. 50 col. 1; primary issue scan pending;
- 1864: Littré lexicographic milestone, not first pastry use;
- 1873: Gouffé professional family relationship with pains à la duchesse;
- Carême: influence on choux/pastry may be historically important, but `inventor of éclair` is not closed.

The Bailleux finding means the dossier no longer needs to treat 1873 as the earliest known **professional recipe** target. The 1861 New York occurrence also shows the name had entered English-language cultural circulation unusually early.

## Анализ и границы допустимой формулировки

## Anatomy / technique

Bailleux locator indicates an already recognizable filled/glazed professional éclair family in 1856; exact wording and method await direct scan inspection.

Documented Gouffé 1873 technical family:

`pâte à choux → elongated/duchesse-related small pastry → filling/glazing variants`.

Modern fondant, cream types and standard dimensions require a later development chronology.

## Myth vs Document

**DOCUMENTARY NETWORK:** 1856 BHVF literary use + 1856 Bailleux professional recipe locator + 1861 U.S. transmission locator + 1864 lexicography + 1873 Gouffé professional description.  
**UNRESOLVED ATTRIBUTION:** Carême as inventor.  
**RESEARCH TARGET:** predecessor names such as `pain à la duchesse`, possible `cartouche` uses and exact transition.
