# 08 — Mont-Blanc / Angelina

## Проверяемый вопрос

Как отделить более раннюю европейскую историю Mont-Blanc / Monte Bianco от конкретного signature Angelina; что можно доказать о Rumpelmayer, 1903 и современной тройке meringue–cream–chestnut?

## Краткий статус

`PRE-ANGELINA CHESTNUT+CREAM LINEAGE CONFIRMED 1891 / NAMED MONT BLANC CONFIRMED IN US FIRST EDITION 1896 / ANGELINA HOUSE LINEAGE CONFIRMED / DO NOT CLAIM CATEGORY INVENTION`

## Pre-Angelina primary lineage — Italy, 1891

Pellegrino Artusi, *La scienza in cucina e l'arte di mangiar bene* (1891), recipe 653, `Dolce di marroni con panna montata`:
https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Scienza_in_cucina_e_l%27arte_di_mangiar_bene/Torte_e_dolci_al_cucchiaio/653._Dolce_di_marroni_con_panna_montata

The verified Wikisource text explicitly dates the work to **1891** and gives the construction:

- cooked chestnuts/marroni;
- powdered sugar;
- chocolate;
- cedro liqueur;
- mixture passed through a sieve into strands around an inverted small cup;
- the central void filled with whipped cream.

`evidenceClass=A1`, `FULL_OBJECT_VERIFIED`, `EXACT_LOCATOR_VERIFIED` for the bounded claim that an extremely close chestnut-strand + whipped-cream construction exists in Artusi 1891.

Important boundary: Artusi does **not** title recipe 653 `Monte Bianco`; he calls it `Dolce di marroni con panna montata`. It therefore proves the pre-Angelina construction/genealogy, not by itself the early use of the Mont-Blanc name.

## Named Mont Blanc before Angelina — United States, 1896

Fannie Merritt Farmer, *The Boston Cooking-School Cook Book*, first printing 1896.

A preservation browser based on a verified first printing records `Mont Blanc` in the 1896 first edition and locates it to **p. 357**, with original page-scan links:
https://cardmyrecipe.com/collections/fannie-farmer

Independent modern bibliographic/recipe indexing likewise identifies `Mont Blanc` in Farmer's book as a recipe from the **1896** edition:
https://app.ckbk.com/recipe/bost85438c25s001r049/mont-blanc

The first-edition recipe index places `Mont Blanc` in the cold-dessert sequence around p. 357. Current status:

- first-edition identity + page = `B1 STRONG PRESERVATION/BIBLIOGRAPHIC EVIDENCE`;
- original scan route exists but was not directly materialized through the current crawler = `LOCATOR_HOLD` for promotion to A-class;
- historical implication is already clear enough to reject any simple `Angelina invented the category in 1903` wording.

A later public-domain Project Gutenberg edition (1910 revision) preserves a `Mont Blanc` recipe and searchable full text:
https://www.gutenberg.org/files/65061/65061-h/65061-h.htm

This later edition is useful for continuity, but it is not substituted for the 1896 first-edition witness.

## Ответы, отрицания и альтернативные объяснения

Wave 1 now proves a materially stronger two-stage lineage:

1. **1891 Italy:** Artusi documents a chestnut-strand + whipped-cream dessert construction closely related to later Mont-Blanc language, but under a descriptive title rather than `Monte Bianco`.
2. **1896 United States:** Farmer's first edition contains a dessert actually named `Mont Blanc`, before Angelina's 1903 founding.
3. **1903 onward:** Angelina develops/preserves its own famous house signature and visual architecture.

Therefore the old open question is narrowed sharply. We no longer ask whether *any* Mont-Blanc lineage existed before Angelina; it did. The remaining questions are:

- earliest use of the **name** in French/Italian/Swiss sources before Farmer 1896;
- exact European transmission chain;
- when meringue becomes part of the now-familiar Angelina architecture;
- exact first Angelina menu/catalogue appearance.

## Анализ и границы допустимой формулировки

Safe:

> Chestnut-and-whipped-cream constructions closely related to Mont-Blanc are documented before Angelina: Artusi gives one in Italy in 1891, and Fannie Farmer's 1896 first-edition American cookbook already lists a dessert named `Mont Blanc`. Angelina, founded in 1903 according to its house history, should therefore be treated as the maker of a famous house signature rather than the inventor of the whole dessert category.

Unsafe:

> Anton Rumpelmayer invented the Mont-Blanc dessert in 1903.

Also unsafe:

> Artusi called his 1891 recipe `Monte Bianco`.

He did not; the title is `Dolce di marroni con panna montata`.

## Хронология

- **1891** — Artusi primary text: `Dolce di marroni con panna montata`, chestnut strands + whipped cream.
- **1896** — Farmer first edition: named `Mont Blanc`, p. 357 according to verified-first-printing preservation browser; original scan still to inspect directly.
- **1903** — Angelina house founding milestone.
- **1903** — Escoffier's *Guide culinaire* also contains `Mont-Blanc aux marrons`, useful as an independent professional French continuity witness, though it is contemporaneous with Angelina rather than pre-dating it.
- early 20th c. — current Maison associates its signature Mont-Blanc with Rumpelmayer.

## Anatomy / technique

### Artusi 1891

The dossier should therefore visualize **form evolution**, not one timeless recipe.

## Myth vs Document

- `DOCUMENTED PRIMARY 1891`: Artusi pre-Angelina chestnut/cream architecture.
- `STRONG 1896 FIRST-EDITION WITNESS`: named American `Mont Blanc` before Angelina; direct scan acquisition pending.
- `DOCUMENTED CURRENT HOUSE`: Angelina signature/status/composition and 1903 house history.
- `HOUSE TRADITION`: hairstyle inspiration.
- `UNRESOLVED`: earliest named European Mont-Blanc/Monte Bianco and exact first Angelina product appearance.

## International significance

The 1896 Boston witness matters beyond chronology: it documents that the name/form had already crossed national boundaries before Angelina's founding. The future Canon article can therefore show Mont-Blanc as a **transnational Alpine/French-Italian dessert language**, then distinguish Angelina's Parisian signature as one historically influential house realization.

This interpretation remains a research inference from the 1891 Italian and 1896 American witnesses, not a claim that the precise transmission route Italy → United States → Paris has been proven.
