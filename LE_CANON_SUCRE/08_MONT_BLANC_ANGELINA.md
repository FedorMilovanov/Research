## Ответы, отрицания и альтернативные объяснения

Wave 2 now supports a three-stage documentary sequence:

1. **1891 Italy:** Artusi documents a close chestnut-strand + whipped-cream construction under a descriptive title.
2. **1896 United States:** Farmer's first edition directly contains a dessert named `Mont Blanc` on printed p. 357.
3. **1903 France:** Escoffier contains `Mont-Blanc aux marrons`, while Angelina begins the house history that later becomes one of the category's best-known signatures.

Therefore the question is no longer whether any named Mont-Blanc lineage existed before Angelina. It did. Remaining questions are narrower:

- earliest named European `Mont-Blanc / Mont Blanc / Monte Bianco` before 1896;
- exact international transmission chain;
- when meringue becomes part of the familiar Angelina architecture;
- exact first Angelina menu/catalogue appearance.

## Анализ и границы допустимой формулировки

Safe:

> Chestnut-and-cream constructions related to Mont-Blanc are documented before Angelina: Artusi gives one in Italy in 1891, and Fannie Farmer's 1896 first edition directly contains a dessert named `Mont Blanc` on p. 357. Angelina, founded in 1903 according to its house history, should therefore be treated as a maker of a famous house signature rather than the inventor of the whole dessert category.

Unsafe:

> Anton Rumpelmayer invented the Mont-Blanc dessert in 1903.

Also unsafe:

> Artusi called his 1891 recipe `Monte Bianco`.

He did not.

## Myth vs Document

- `DOCUMENTED PRIMARY 1891`: Artusi pre-Angelina chestnut/cream architecture.
- `DOCUMENTED PRIMARY 1896`: Farmer, named `Mont Blanc`, printed p. 357.
- `DOCUMENTED HISTORICAL PROFESSIONAL 1903`: Escoffier `Mont-Blanc aux marrons`.
- `DOCUMENTED CURRENT HOUSE`: Angelina signature/status/composition and 1903 house history.
- `HOUSE TRADITION`: hairstyle inspiration.
- `UNRESOLVED`: earliest named European witness before 1896 and exact first Angelina product appearance.

## International significance

The 1896 Boston object proves that the Mont Blanc name had already crossed national boundaries before Angelina's founding. The future Canon article can therefore describe a transnational dessert language and then distinguish Angelina's Parisian signature as a historically influential house realization.

Do **not** infer a proved route Italy → Boston → Paris; the documents establish chronology and circulation, not a single transmission path.
