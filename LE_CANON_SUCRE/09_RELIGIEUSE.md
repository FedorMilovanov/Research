# 09 — Religieuse

## Проверяемый вопрос

Каково самое раннее документированное значение `religieuse` в pâtisserie; можно ли доказать Frascati/1856; когда появляется поздняя стандартная конструкция из двух stacked choux?

## New pre-Gouffé professional witness — Bailleux 1860

Saint Petersburg Polytechnic University Electronic Library (SPbPU):
https://elib.spbstu.ru/dl/2/ed-3262_0000697628bx.pdf/en/info

The institutional record identifies:

- **Louis Bailleux**;
- *Le patissier moderne ou Traité élémentaire et pratique de la patisserie française au dix-neuvième siècle*;
- imprint **Paris, 1860**;
- a digitized institutional object / DOI `10.18720/SPBPU/2/ed-3262`;
- restricted read access on the public Internet, but a machine-readable table of contents.

Critically, the institutional TOC for **Chapter V, `Traité des petits gâteaux de dessert secs et garnis`**, first part `Gâteaux garnis`, directly lists:

`... D’Artois → Fourré de Crème → Religieuse → Hérissons → Surprises ...`

The same TOC earlier lists `Eclairs`, `Choux crème`, `Pain Duchesse`, etc. This is direct institutional evidence that by the **1860 edition** Bailleux's professional pastry manual contained a separately named `Religieuse` entry in the filled-small-cake section.

Current evidence decision:

- Bailleux 1860 edition identity = `A2 INSTITUTIONAL OBJECT VERIFIED`;
- `Religieuse` as a dedicated professional TOC entry = `DIRECTLY VERIFIED AT TOC/STRUCTURE LEVEL`;
- exact page number + recipe wording + architecture = `LOCATOR_HOLD / CONTENT_HOLD`, because the SPbPU scan is restricted for anonymous Internet users;
- this **supersedes** the old statement that Gouffé 1873 was the earliest directly verified *named professional occurrence* in the corpus;
- Gouffé 1873 **still remains the earliest exact Religieuse recipe/form page directly inspected in the corpus**.

This distinction matters: a verified TOC entry proves professional named inclusion, but does not authorize us to invent the 1860 recipe or geometry.

## Edition-history problem — 1856 vs 1860

Christie's identifies Bailleux **1856** as the first edition (267 pages, 34 plates):
https://www.christies.com/en/lot/lot-6415317

A specialist rare-book record for the **1860** edition describes it as a **third edition, revised, corrected and enlarged**, with 279 pages and 38 plates, and notes that the first edition appeared in 1856 with 267 pages:
https://www.livre-rare-book.com/book/15728719/2743

Therefore a new P0 question is now explicit:

> Was `Religieuse` already present in the 1856 first edition, or was the entry added in the enlarged 1860 edition?

Google Books verifies the 1856 first-edition full object bibliographically, but its accessible web metadata does not expose `Religieuse` in the page text/TOC. Absence from the web metadata is not evidence that the recipe is absent from the book.

Until the 1856 TOC/page itself is inspected:

- `BAILLEUX_1856_CONTAINS_RELIGIEUSE` = `UNRESOLVED / LOCATOR_HOLD`;
- `BAILLEUX_1860_CONTAINS_NAMED_RELIGIEUSE_ENTRY` = `CONFIRMED AT INSTITUTIONAL TOC LEVEL`.

This also prevents circular reasoning: Christie's modern sentence that Frascati was the origin of Religieuse must not be used to infer that the 1856 first edition necessarily already printed a Religieuse recipe.

## Historical exact primary evidence — Gouffé 1873

Chocolate form:
https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AGouff%C3%A9_-_Le_Livre_de_P%C3%A2tisserie%2C_1873.djvu/345

Coffee continuation/figure:
https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AGouff%C3%A9_-_Le_Livre_de_P%C3%A2tisserie%2C_1873.djvu/346

Gouffé directly describes `flan à la religieuse` using multiple choux / pains à la duchesse. The coffee construction uses six oval choux arranged in a crown plus a round choux; the chocolate construction is also multi-piece.

This remains the earliest **exact recipe/form page directly inspected** in this Research corpus.

It also proves that the nineteenth-century professional family should not be projected backward as a timeless modern `large choux + small choux` silhouette.

## Frascati 1856 source map

### La Poste — modern institutional recap

https://www.laposte.fr/professionnel/pp/carnet-de-12-timbres-patisseries-francaises-lettre-verte/p/1124487

Attributes creation in **1856** to Signor Frascati. This remains modern retelling, not contemporaneous event proof.

### Christie's — Bailleux provenance and Frascati origin statement

https://www.christies.com/en/lot/lot-6415317

Christie's verifies that Bailleux dedicated the 1856 first edition to his master **M. A. Cintract of Maison Frascati**, where he trained, and separately states that Café Frascati was the origin of the Religieuse.

The Bailleux–Frascati professional connection is thus real and tied to the first-edition provenance. The Religieuse-origin sentence is still a modern specialist historical assertion, not a directly inspected 1856 Frascati record.

### Modern form-history sources conflict

Modern French/institutional/professional retellings do not even preserve one stable 1856 geometry. Examples variously describe:

- choux pieces arranged next to one another;
- a square/older form;
- or the familiar large-choux + small-choux silhouette.

This conflict reinforces the rule: **do not reconstruct the 1856 Frascati form as historical fact until an early recipe/image is acquired.**

## Ответы, отрицания и альтернативные объяснения

Current conclusions:

- by **1860**, Bailleux's professional manual definitely contains a dedicated named `Religieuse` entry in its `Gâteaux garnis` section;
- whether that entry was already present in the **1856 first edition** is now a precise unresolved edition-history question;
- Gouffé **1873** remains the earliest exact recipe/form page directly inspected in the corpus;
- multiple later sources repeat Frascati 1856, and Bailleux's first-edition provenance genuinely connects him to Maison Frascati through M. A. Cintract;
- none of this yet proves the exact 1856 invention event or its geometry;
- the standard modern two-choux transition remains undated.
