## Effective-candidate rule

Deep prototypes are especially sensitive to later research closures. They must resolve their source candidate in this order:

1. base candidate record;
2. candidate overrides from Waves 3e, 3g, 3k, 3l and any later wave;
3. source upgrades/quorum records;
4. then MCQ prototype;
5. prototype-level editorial overrides, if any.

Examples:

- `w3mcq_036` uses the **Wave-3l** effective `w3q_013`: project position on 4:6 is now-dead believers evangelized while alive, while neutral scholarship remains contested.
- `w3mcq_043` uses the **Wave-3e** effective `w3q_033`: Brown strengthens the meddling/interference family for `ἀλλοτριεπίσκοπος` without licensing one narrow gloss.
- `w3mcq_045–046` use the **Wave-3g** 4:16 closure: SBLGNT `ὀνόματι`, ECM/NA28 `μέρει`, and witness counting is not the whole of textual criticism.
- `w3mcq_047` uses the **Wave-3k** effective Malachi classification: serious proposed prophetic/imagery background, contested, not formal quotation.

A prototype may never restore the older superseded HOLD/text.

## Editorial quality rule inherited from Wave 3j2

Perfect position balance is necessary but insufficient.

Deep MCQs must also avoid:

- correct option as uniquely longest explanation;
- correct option as uniquely terse bare word;
- one option carrying all Greek technical detail while distractors are generic prose;
- obviously absurd theology as filler;
- distractors from completely different verses where a knowledgeable reader never has to compare claims.

Wrong options should embody a **real nearby error**:

- morphology→exegesis;
- project→consensus;
- one witness→original text;
- source proposal→formal quotation;
- historical possibility→known fact;
- lexicon gloss→complete context;
- application→harmful absolutization.
