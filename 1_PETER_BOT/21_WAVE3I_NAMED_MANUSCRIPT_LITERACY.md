# 1 Peter Wave 3.0i — Named-Manuscript Literacy

**Status:** `RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PRODUCTION / NOT RANKING`

Wave 3.0i adds a manuscript-literacy layer based on the official Codex Sinaiticus transcription for 1 Peter 4:1–5:13.

The goal is not to teach `א = original`. It is to train this distinction:

```text
WHAT ONE MANUSCRIPT READS != CRITICAL-TEXT DECISION
EARLY WITNESS != AUTOMATIC WINNER
CRITICAL EDITION != ERASURE OF MANUSCRIPT HISTORY
NAMED-WITNESS FACT != ALL-WITNESSES CLAIM
```

## Why Sinaiticus is useful here

The official Codex Sinaiticus site exposes a continuous transcription of the relevant folio. That gives first-order manuscript data for several pedagogically important units already present in Wave 3.

### 1 Peter 4:14

Sinaiticus reads an expanded clause containing:

`καὶ τῆς δυνάμεως αὐτοῦ`

within the Spirit/glory wording.

SBLGNT does not print that expansion.

Safe claim:
- this expansion is genuinely present in Sinaiticus.

Unsafe claim:
- therefore the expansion is original;
- therefore the short form is secondary;
- therefore the entire official ECM apparatus is closed.

The old 4:14 official-apparatus HOLD remains.

### 1 Peter 4:16

Sinaiticus reads:

`ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι τούτῳ`

Wave 3g already established through Kok/de Winter that ECM/NA28 adopt `ἐν τῷ μέρει τούτῳ`.

This is an excellent live example:

> a famous early manuscript can support a reading that the ECM editors did not select for the reconstructed text.

That does not make Sinaiticus “wrong”; it means manuscript evidence and editorial reconstruction are different epistemic objects.

### 1 Peter 5:2

The Sinaiticus transcription reads the shepherding sentence without `ἐπισκοποῦντες`, but it does contain `κατὰ θεόν`.

This independently illustrates the Wave-3g point that 5:2 is not one binary yes/no variant.

It also prevents a common polity shortcut:

> “the earliest manuscript either has both oversight terms or neither.”

That statement is false for Sinaiticus.

The official ECM closures for `ἐπισκοποῦντες` and `κατὰ θεόν` remain separate HOLDs.

### 1 Peter 5:10

Sinaiticus has all four restoration verbs:

- `καταρτίσει`;
- `στηρίξει`;
- `σθενώσει`;
- `θεμελιώσει`.

Secondary apparatus in Wave 3g showed shorter sequences in other witnesses.

Therefore:

```text
SINAITICUS_HAS_FOUR = VERIFIED
SBLGNT_HAS_FOUR = VERIFIED
ALL_EARLY_WITNESSES_HAVE_FOUR = FALSE/UNSAFE
EARLY_WITNESSES_NORMALLY_HAVE_SHORTER_LIST = ALSO UNSAFE
```

### 1 Peter 5:12

Sinaiticus reads `στῆτε` in the closing purpose/exhortation statement.

This is primary-witness confirmation that the imperative reading is ancient. It still does not substitute for official ECM reasoning about `στῆτε / ἑστήκατε`.

### 1 Peter 5:13

Sinaiticus reads:

`βαβυλῶνι ἐκκλησία συνεκλεκτή`

That is striking because SBLGNT has `ἡ ἐν Βαβυλῶνι συνεκλεκτή` without an explicit noun `ἐκκλησία`.

This gives an exceptionally good referent lesson:

- in Sinaiticus, `ἐκκλησία` is actually present;
- in SBLGNT, it is not;
- therefore saying “Peter literally wrote church here” requires naming the witness/textual base;
- the presence of `ἐκκλησία` in Sinaiticus is real transmission evidence but not by itself a proof of the reconstructed original wording.

## Authoring rule

Named-manuscript questions should name both the witness and the comparison text.

Good:
> What does Codex Sinaiticus read at 5:2 relative to SBLGNT?

Bad:
> What does “the ancient Greek text” read at 5:2?

`MANUSCRIPT_TRANSCRIPTION != CRITICAL_APPARATUS != CRITICAL_TEXT`.
