# 1 Peter — Textual Variant and Manuscript Curriculum

**Wave:** 2026-08-14 / Wave 2  
**Status:** `RESEARCH-CURRICULUM / NOT-PRODUCTION / FAIL-CLOSED`

## 0. Goal

This is not a list of every apparatus unit in 1 Peter. It is a curated queue of variants and witnesses that can actually teach textual criticism to ordinary users, advanced learners, and researchers.

The curriculum must distinguish:

```text
BASE_TEXT_READING
ALTERNATIVE_READING
EDITION_COMPARISON
MANUSCRIPT_ATTESTATION
EDITORIAL_REASONING
EXEGETICAL_IMPACT
DOCTRINAL_IMPACT
```

No card may jump from “a variant exists” directly to “the Bible is uncertain.”

# 1. Primary tools and their jobs

## SBLGNT

Useful for a freely redistributable Greek base and edition-comparison work. Its apparatus compares editions; it is not an ECM-style manuscript apparatus.

**Bot use:** simple/standard Greek cards, edition comparison, reproducible open text.

## MorphGNT

Morphological tagging layer over SBLGNT.

**Bot use:** form/lemma/parsing. Never identify a referent or settle theology from morphology alone.

## NA28

Current published Nestle–Aland edition as of this wave. For Catholic Letters, already reflects ECM Catholic Letters changes.

**Bot use:** current scholarly base reference; do not redistribute copyrighted edition content wholesale.

## GNT6

Current UBS Greek New Testament. Publisher states its Greek main text is identical to forthcoming NA29 and that its apparatus is selected especially for translation/exegesis relevance.

**Bot use:** highly suitable selection guide for variants worth teaching.

## NA29

Forthcoming publisher date: 2027-02-28. Do not call it the current published NA edition before publication.

## ECM Catholic Letters

High-level textual-critical basis for Catholic Letters, including 1 Peter. INTF reports 33 Catholic-Letters textual changes from NA27 adopted into NA28 and 43 split primary-line readings in the ECM Catholic Letters.

## NTVMR

Primary digital research environment for manuscript information/transcriptions/images where available.

## Houghton textual commentary

Current commentary companion to GNT6 / future NA29; publisher states every GNT6 apparatus entry is commented. Catalogued here as a high-priority current reference, not as inspected full text.

# 2. Witness curriculum

## P72 / Papyrus Bodmer VIII

Why teach it:

- early physical witness to the Petrine epistles;
- manuscript can be connected to an actual digitized object rather than only a siglum;
- useful for explaining scribal corrections, individual readings, codex context, and why manuscript age does not automatically determine the initial text.

Teaching questions:

- What does `P72` identify?
- Why is a manuscript not the same thing as a critical edition?
- What can an image prove that a modern transcription cannot?
- What can neither image nor transcription alone prove about the initial text?

## P81

Fragmentary witness useful for showing that papyri often preserve only small portions and therefore cannot vote on verses they do not contain.

## P125

Fragmentary early witness to portions of 1 Peter 1–2; useful for “coverage before authority” pedagogy.

## Major majuscules/minuscules

Introduce only when attached to an exact variant. Avoid isolated siglum memorization.

# 3. Curated variant queue

The entries below are **research candidates** until exact apparatus/witness readback is completed. Edition-comparison observations may be stronger than manuscript-distribution claims.

## V01 — 1 Peter 1:22

**Topic:** wording/intensification around love from the heart.  
**Why useful:** demonstrates how additions/word order may affect nuance more than core proposition.  
**Required closure:** GNT6/ECM apparatus + witness distribution + Houghton if included.

## V02 — 1 Peter 1:23

**Topic:** transmitted wording around incorruptible seed / living-abiding word.  
**Why useful:** vocabulary chain into Isaiah quotation at 1:24–25.  
**Required closure:** exact apparatus before any card.

## V03 — 1 Peter 2:2 — `εἰς σωτηρίαν`

SBLGNT edition-comparison apparatus makes this an excellent example because major printed traditions differ over inclusion.

**Pedagogical value:**
- omission/inclusion can sharpen the clause’s telic direction;
- do not imply that the doctrine of salvation depends on this unit;
- connect to Jobes/Psalm 33 research only at the interpretation layer.

**Status:** `EDITION_DIFFERENCE_VERIFIED / MANUSCRIPT_APPARATUS_REQUIRED`

## V04 — 1 Peter 2:25 — orthographic `ἀλλά` elision episode

INTF explicitly explains that an elision difference at 1 Pet 2:25 was originally counted among NA28 textual changes and later removed because it is only orthographic.

**Exceptional teaching value:** not every visible textual difference is a meaningful variant.

**Status:** `INTF_EXPLANATION_VERIFIED`

## V05 — 1 Peter 3:15 — `τὸν Χριστόν` / alternative Lord-referent wording

VariantApp/discovery resources flag an important variant involving Christ/God wording.

**Why useful:** intersects Isa 8:13 reuse and Christology.  
**Danger:** never use a secondary variant list as final manuscript proof.  
**Required closure:** ECM/GNT6 exact apparatus and manuscript transcription checks.

**Status:** `HIGH_PRIORITY / WITNESS_HOLD`

## V06 — 1 Peter 3:18 — `ἔπαθεν` / `ἀπέθανεν`

Current Chapter-3 research correctly protects the SBLGNT reading `ἔπαθεν` (“suffered”) from retrojecting an English “died” into the Greek wording.

Printed-edition comparison shows an important alternative `ἀπέθανεν` (“died”).

**Teaching sequence:**
1. What does the base text print?
2. What does the alternative mean?
3. Does the next participle `θανατωθείς` make death absent from the sentence? No.
4. Does the variant erase substitutionary atonement? No.
5. Which manuscript evidence supports each? Apparatus required before advanced card.

**Status:** `BASE_AND_EDITION_DIFFERENCE_VERIFIED / WITNESS_HOLD`

## V07 — 1 Peter 3:18 — pronoun variation (`ὑμᾶς` / `ἡμᾶς` etc.)

Useful for scribal-person variation and rhetorical addressee questions.

**Status:** `DISCOVERY_ONLY / ECM_HOLD`

## V08 — 1 Peter 3:21 — relative/pronominal wording around baptismal analogy

Useful because even a small form can interact with syntactic analysis.

**Status:** `DISCOVERY_ONLY / ECM_HOLD`

## V09 — 1 Peter 4:1 — expansion around Christ suffering (`ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν` in some traditions)

**Pedagogical value:** familiar doctrinal phrase can enter transmission secondarily; theological truth elsewhere cannot be used as a textual-critical shortcut.

**Status:** `EDITION_COMPARISON_CANDIDATE / MANUSCRIPT_HOLD`

## V10 — 1 Peter 4:14 — expansion around Spirit/glory wording

**Pedagogical value:** Byzantine/expanded readings and liturgical/theological harmonization possibilities.

**Status:** `EDITION_COMPARISON_CANDIDATE / MANUSCRIPT_HOLD`

## V11 — 1 Peter 4:16 — `ὀνόματι` / `μέρει`

**Pedagogical value:** alternative nouns can produce noticeably different translation sense.

**Status:** `EDITION_COMPARISON_CANDIDATE / MANUSCRIPT_HOLD`

## V12 — 1 Peter 5:2 — `ἐπισκοποῦντες`

**High product importance:** elder/overseer polity claims can become circular if an unexamined textual variant is used to prove terminology.

**Required before publication:** exact GNT6/ECM apparatus + witness distribution + editorial assessment.

**Status:** `P0_POLITY_VARIANT_HOLD`

## V13 — 1 Peter 5:2 — `κατὰ θεόν`

Same paragraph and similarly high importance for translation/exposition.

**Status:** `P0_POLITY_VARIANT_HOLD`

## V14 — 1 Peter 5:12 — `στῆτε` / `ἑστήκατε`

**Pedagogical value:** imperative vs indicative/perfect-form implications and how morphology interacts with textual criticism.

**Status:** `DISCOVERY_ONLY / APPARATUS_HOLD`

## V15 — 1 Peter 5:13 — Babylon / rare Rome-related variant traditions

The main interpretive question “Babylon = Rome?” must not be confused with a textual variant in later witnesses.

**Teaching value:**
- textual reading vs interpretation of place-name;
- later scribal gloss/substitution possibility;
- do not use rare `Rome` reading as proof that original `Babylon` must mean Rome.

**Status:** `DISCOVERY_VERIFIED / EXACT_WITNESS_HOLD`

## V16 — 1 Peter 5:14 — ending/benediction variation

Useful for endings, liturgical expansions, and manuscript completeness.

**Status:** `DISCOVERY_ONLY / APPARATUS_HOLD`

# 4. Variant admission rules

A variant can enter a learner-facing card only if:

```text
EXACT_REFERENCE = yes
BASE_TEXT_READING = pinned
ALTERNATIVE_READING = pinned
SOURCE_TYPE = declared
IF_MANUSCRIPT_CLAIM -> manuscript apparatus inspected
IF_EDITORIAL_REASON_CLAIM -> editor commentary or explicit methodology inspected
IMPACT_WORDING = calibrated
DOCTRINAL_SENSATIONALISM = none
```

## Competitive boundary

Default:

```text
TEXTUAL_CRITICISM_CARD.competitive = false
```

Possible later exception only for simple stable facts such as “which reading does the chosen base text print?”, after source review.

# 5. Question templates

### Simple
“В выбранном греческом базовом тексте 1 Пет 3:18 стоит ἔπαθεν. Что это означает?”

### Standard
“Почему наличие варианта ἀπέθανεν не позволяет сказать, что ‘рукописи не знают, страдал Христос или умер’?”

### Deep
“Какие два разных вопроса нужно разделить: wording первой клаузы и значение θανατωθείς позже в том же стихе?”

### Research
“Сравни GNT6/ECM witness data and Houghton’s explanation for this unit; list external and internal considerations without reducing the decision to oldest-manuscript voting.”

---

# 6. Anti-patterns

Reject cards like:

- “Какая рукопись самая правильная?”
- “P72 древнее, значит его чтение всегда оригинальное.”
- “Если два варианта существуют, доктрина ненадёжна.”
- “Большинство рукописей автоматически доказывает исходный текст.”
- “NA29 изменяет Библию.”
- “ECM — компьютер сам выбрал текст.”
