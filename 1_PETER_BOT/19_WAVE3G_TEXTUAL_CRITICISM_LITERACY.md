## 4:16 as a curriculum case

This is now one of the strongest textual-criticism teaching examples in the whole course because it forces edition transparency:

```text
“the Greek text says X” = unsafe shorthand when editions differ

better:
“SBLGNT prints ὀνόματι”
“ECM/NA28 prints μέρει”
```

The learner can also be taught why simple witness counting is not identical to textual criticism. Kok/de Winter report that external evidence is strong for `ὀνόματι`, while the ECM/CBGM decision gives weight to genealogical coherence and the capacity of one reading to explain the emergence of another.

No claim is made that CBGM is an automatic machine that produces the original text without editorial judgement.

## 5:2 — complex variation, not one binary switch

The open STEP VarApp for 1 Peter 5:2 shows multiple combinations:

- `ἐπισκοποῦντες ... κατὰ θεόν`;
- `ἐπισκοποῦντες` without `κατὰ θεόν`;
- `κατὰ θεόν` without `ἐπισκοποῦντες`;
- neither element.

This is sufficient for a **secondary-apparatus literacy question**:

> why is 5:2 not accurately described as one yes/no variant?

It is **not** sufficient to close the project's official ECM P0 HOLD.

Therefore old `w3q_068–069` are not deleted; their stems are overridden to say explicitly **“SBLGNT”**.

This preserves the good Greek-learning value while preventing the false claim that every manuscript has the same four-verb mnemonic.

## 5:12 — what can be taught before ECM closure

SBLGNT/MorphGNT read `στῆτε`.

The open STEP apparatus reports `στῆτε` for P72, Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus, Vaticanus and others, while `ἑστήκατε` occurs in the Byzantine line and other witnesses.

This is enough to teach:

- the variant really exists;
- SBLGNT's imperative is not an invented paraphrase;
- the two readings differ morphologically and rhetorically.

It is **not** enough to claim that the official ECM rationale has been inspected.

So the old exact-ECM question remains HOLD while a new secondary-apparatus question becomes `READY_NONCOMPETITIVE`.

## 5:13 — later Rome readings as a method lesson

The STEP apparatus reports `Βαβυλῶνι` broadly and also a few later `Ῥώμῃ` readings.

These later Rome readings are useful as transmission/reception evidence, but they do not lexically define the original `Βαβυλῶνι`.

That produces an excellent deep question:

```text
LATER EXPLANATORY VARIANT != ORIGINAL WORD SEMANTICS
```

The historical Rome-vs-Mesopotamia question remains separate.
