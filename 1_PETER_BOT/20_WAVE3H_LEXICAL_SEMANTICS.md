# 1 Peter Wave 3.0h — Lexical Semantics Without Lexical Fiat

**Status:** `RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PRODUCTION / NOT RANKING`

Wave 3.0h adds a bounded lexical-semantics layer for Chapters 4–5. Its main purpose is methodological: older open lexica are useful legal full-text controls, but a dictionary gloss must never be promoted into complete exegesis.

Core rule:

```text
LEXICON RANGE != PASSAGE EXEGESIS
ETYMOLOGY != CONTEXTUAL MEANING
DOCUMENTARY PARALLEL != IDENTICAL SCENE
PUBLIC_DOMAIN != CURRENT LEXICAL CONSENSUS
```

## Legal/open lexical stack

### Abbott-Smith

G. Abbott-Smith, *A Manual Greek Lexicon of the New Testament* (1922).

The maintained TEI project states that the lexicon and marked-up text are public domain; release 1.0 records completed markup/checking. Wave 3h uses bounded entries, not wholesale copying and not as a substitute for BDAG.

### Moulton–Milligan

J. H. Moulton & G. Milligan, *The Vocabulary of the Greek Testament Illustrated from the Papyri and Other Non-literary Sources* (1914–1929).

The public-domain scan route is independently documented. Wave 3h uses Moulton–Milligan where documentary parallels materially sharpen or restrain a claim.

## Chapter 4 lexical units

### 4:10 — οἰκονόμος

Abbott-Smith gives the concrete household/estate-manager background and explicitly applies the wider metaphorical stewardship sense to Christians generally in 1 Peter 4:10.

Safe teaching:
- stewardship imagery is real;
- the term can describe entrusted administration.

Unsafe:
- converting `οἰκονόμος` into a specific church office in 4:10;
- reconstructing one exact social status for every addressee from the metaphor.

### 4:11 — λόγιον

Abbott-Smith gives `λόγιον` as an oracle/divine utterance and includes 1 Peter 4:11.

Safe teaching:
- the phrase `ὡς λόγια θεοῦ` carries weightier God-speech language than merely “speak nicely.”

Unsafe:
- claiming every Christian sentence becomes inspired Scripture;
- deriving a complete doctrine of inspiration from the noun alone.

### 4:12 — πύρωσις

Abbott-Smith distinguishes literal burning from metaphorical refining/trial by fire and assigns the latter to 1 Peter 4:12.

Safe teaching:
- fire/testing semantics are genuinely available;
- Proverbs 27:21 is lexical/LXX background worth comparing.

Unsafe:
- declaring Malachi 3 the source merely from `πύρωσις`;
- claiming the noun itself proves one purification theology.

### 4:19 — κτίστης

Abbott-Smith gives the broader range founder/creator and specifically uses “creator” of God in 1 Peter 4:19.

Moulton–Milligan also documents non-biblical honorific use of `κτίστης` for a civic founder.

Safe teaching:
- Peter's `πιστῷ κτίστῃ` is genuine Creator/founder vocabulary.

Unsafe:
- treating the lexical range as a complete doctrine of creation;
- assuming every occurrence outside the NT refers to cosmic creation.

## Chapter 5 lexical units

### 5:5 — ἐγκομβόομαι

Abbott-Smith supports a clothing/girding image. Moulton–Milligan explicitly says they found no instance of this rare verb in their documentary sources and cites only a later lexical/comic parallel for “gird something on oneself.”

That negative evidence matters.

Safe teaching:
- Peter uses a vivid clothing/girding metaphor for humility.

Unsafe:
- competitive claim that the word itself certainly denotes one exact slave apron/uniform;
- building a detailed Roman costume reconstruction from etymology.

### 5:8 — ἀντίδικος

Abbott-Smith gives opponent in a lawsuit/adversary. Moulton–Milligan calls it a common legal word and supplies documentary lawsuits, while also noting a wider adversary use suitable to 1 Peter 5:8.

Safe teaching:
- legal-adversary background is real;
- semantic widening to “opponent/adversary” is also real.

Unsafe:
- claiming 5:8 depicts a literal earthly courtroom;
- reducing the devil image to a merely legal metaphor and ignoring the lion/predation imagery.

### 5:10 — σθενόω

Abbott-Smith gives “strengthen” and cites 1 Peter 5:10.

But Wave 3g already established a textual-transmission caveat: the four-verb sequence is edition-specific and some witnesses have shorter lists.

Therefore lexical teaching must say:

> in SBLGNT 5:10, `σθενώσει` is a future form of `σθενόω`, “strengthen.”

It must not say:

> all manuscripts contain this verb here.

### 5:13 — συνεκλεκτός

Abbott-Smith gives “chosen together with / co-elect” and cites the feminine form in 1 Peter 5:13.

Safe teaching:
- co-election/shared chosen status is lexical content.

Unsafe:
- dictionary semantics identify the historical referent as either one woman or a personified church;
- feminine morphology alone settles the referent.

## Lexicon hierarchy rule

Abbott-Smith and Moulton–Milligan are high-value open controls because they can be inspected legally in full. They do **not** outrank BDAG simply because BDAG is copyrighted.

Future production authoring should use:
- open lexica for reproducible claim nuclei and historical/documentary context;
- current copyrighted lexica by lawful consultation for modern lexical control;
- passage syntax/context and independent scholarship for exegesis.

`OPEN_LEXICON != FINAL_EXEGETICAL_AUTHORITY`.
