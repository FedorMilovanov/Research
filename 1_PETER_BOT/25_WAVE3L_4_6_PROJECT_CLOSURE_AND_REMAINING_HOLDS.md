## 1 Peter 4:6 — two layers must remain separate

### Neutral research layer

The identity of `νεκροῖς` and the time of evangelization remain **contested interpretation**.

The serious families include at least:

1. people evangelized while alive who are dead at the time of writing;
2. proclamation/evangelization to people already dead.

David G. Horrell's NTS 49 (2003) abstract is especially useful as a neutral counter-control. He notes that the “heard while alive, now dead” reading had become increasingly widely accepted in recent English commentaries, but argues that serious problems remain and that a proclamation to people already dead is more plausible than recent commentators allow.

Therefore the project must not relabel its chosen reading as neutral consensus.

### Project layer

John MacArthur's full GTY sermon on 1 Peter 4:2–6 explicitly interprets 4:6 as referring to believers who heard the gospel and are **now dead** at Peter's time of writing.

The relevant inspected passage is unusually direct:

- `νεκροῖς` means those who are now dead;
- they are believers who heard the gospel;
- persecution/death cannot defeat their final life before God.

Sam Storms/TGC independently gives the same basic reading: the dead are Christians who heard the gospel while alive and have died by the time of the letter.

The existing Forbes control also represents this reading.

This provides enough passage-level quorum for a **project-position choice**.

## What this closure does NOT mean

Do not claim:

- `νεκροῖς` lexically means “believers evangelized while alive”;
- the aorist/passive morphology establishes the chronology;
- Horrell's postmortem reading is unserious;
- the reading is universal academic consensus;
- 4:6 teaches a second chance for salvation after death;
- 4:6 is automatically the same event/address group as 3:19;
- MacArthur's pastoral speculation that some may have been martyrs is a direct fact about the audience.

Project preference is explicit precisely so neutral research does not have to pretend the dispute disappeared.
