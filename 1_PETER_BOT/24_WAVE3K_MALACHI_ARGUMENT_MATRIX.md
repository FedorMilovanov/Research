## Direct Greek comparison

The official Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft LXX web text for Malachi 3:1–5 contains:

- the Lord coming to `τὸν ναὸν ἑαυτοῦ`;
- `ὡς πῦρ χωνευτηρίου`;
- refining/purifying silver and gold;
- judgment language (`ἐν κρίσει`).

SBLGNT 1 Peter 4:12–19 contains:

- `τῇ ... πυρώσει πρὸς πειρασμόν`;
- Spirit/glory resting on sufferers;
- `τὸ κρίμα ἀπὸ τοῦ οἴκου τοῦ θεοῦ`;
- a contrast between God's people and those who disobey the gospel.

The imagery can be compared, but the passages do not form a sustained Greek quotation.

Important non-identities:

```text
Mal 3 temple = ναός
1 Pet 4:17 house = οἶκος

Mal 3 refining fire = πῦρ χωνευτηρίου
1 Pet 4:12 fiery trial = πύρωσις

Mal 3 judgment = κρίσις family
1 Pet 4:17 judgment = κρίμα
```

Therefore thematic/imagery correspondence must not be relabelled as verbatim quotation.

## Johnson thesis — bounded evidence

Dennis E. Johnson's JETS 29.3 article identity and opening methodological section are accessible through Galaxie. The accessible opening argues that exegesis must consider conscious OT allusions where context warrants them.

The detailed body remains subscription-blocked through this direct route, so Wave 3k does not pretend full Johnson inspection.

John Proctor, JETS 36.1 (1993), provides an accessible scholarly summary of Johnson's main 1-Peter/Malachi proposal. Proctor explicitly summarizes the proposed convergence of:

1. fiery testing/refining in 1 Peter;
2. `οἶκος` construed as temple/sanctuary;
3. Malachi-3 fire coming to God's temple for judgment and purification;
4. divine presence during the fiery trial;
5. judgment moving outward toward evildoers.

This makes the **content of the Johnson proposal sufficiently defined** for a source-aware question even though Johnson's complete article is not directly inspected.

## Independent alternative model — Liebengood

The University of St Andrews Research Repository openly exposes the HTML abstract and a downloadable PhD thesis by Kelly D. Liebengood, later developed into the Cambridge monograph.

The repository abstract explicitly says the study argues that Zechariah 9–14, read through the Gospel, functions as the substructure for 1 Peter's eschatology and theology of suffering. It also explicitly connects the proposal with:

- 1 Peter's fiery-trials imagery;
- 4:14;
- the transition from 4:12–19 to 5:1–4;
- `οἶκος τοῦ θεοῦ`.

That HTML abstract is sufficient to establish that a substantive alternative prophetic-substructure proposal exists.

## Why the old HOLD can be downgraded

The old HOLD conflated two different questions.

### A. Is Malachi 3 a real scholarly proposal for the background of 4:12–19?

Yes. That is verified.

### B. Has the project established Malachi 3 as Peter's uniquely intended or formal intertext?

No.

The correct teaching target is therefore a **classification**:

> Malachi 3 is a serious proposed prophetic/imagery background, particularly around fire, God's dwelling/people and judgment; the degree of dependence is contested and no formal quotation has been demonstrated.

That nucleus is safe as `READY_NONCOMPETITIVE`.
