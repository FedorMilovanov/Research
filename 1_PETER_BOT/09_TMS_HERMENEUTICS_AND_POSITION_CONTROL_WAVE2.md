The current TMS doctrinal statement explicitly teaches that:

- the church began at Pentecost;
- the church is a unique spiritual organism distinct from Israel;

# 2. TMS hermeneutical family

The TMS Journal archive and TMS educational material provide an internally coherent method family relevant to 1 Peter:

- grammatical-historical reading;
- authorial intent and textual context;
- attention to original languages;
- distinction between interpretation and application;
- resistance to making later revelation cancel the original meaning of earlier Scripture;
- dispensational reading of the Bible's storyline;
- continued future for national Israel.

Useful TMS archive controls include:

- Michael J. Vlach, “Hermeneutical Principles and the Bible’s Storyline: A Dispensational Approach” (TMSJ 34.2, 2023);
- Abner Chou, work on apostolic/Christocentric hermeneutics;
- William Barrick, “Exegetical Fallacies: Common Interpretive Mistakes Every Student Must Avoid” (2008);
- Robert L. Thomas, “The New Testament Use of the Old Testament” (2002);
- Robert L. Thomas, “The Hermeneutics of Progressive Dispensationalism” (1995);
- S. Lewis Johnson, “Paul and the Israel of God” (2009 archive issue);
- Michael Vlach, work on replacement theology / Israel / covenants.

These sources can inform project-systematic synthesis, but passage-specific claims still need passage evidence.

# 4. 1 Peter 2:4–10 — highest church/Israel sensitivity

Observable facts:

- Peter applies a cluster of Israel's scriptural titles and prophetic texts to his Christian readers;
- he uses chosen-race / royal-priesthood / holy-nation / possession language;
- he invokes Hosea-like people/not-people and mercy language;
- Christ is the cornerstone around whom the community is built.

What does **not** follow merely from those observations:

```text
CHURCH_HAS_REPLACED_ISRAEL = not direct text
NATIONAL_ISRAEL_HAS_NO_FUTURE = not direct text
ISRAEL_AND_CHURCH_HAVE_NO_CONTINUITY_AT_ALL = contradicted by shared scriptural-language phenomena
EVERY_OT_ISRAEL_PROMISE_TRANSFERS_UNCHANGED_TO_CHURCH = not established
```

TMS-compatible project synthesis:

> Gentile and Jewish believers in Christ genuinely share salvation privileges and can be addressed with Israel-shaped scriptural language, while this ecclesial participation does not by itself cancel God's distinct future commitments to national Israel.

This is a systematic synthesis and must be labeled `project`.

Alternative scholarly models should be mapped fairly:

- continuity/new-covenant people-of-God models;
- supersession/reconstitution models;
- incorporation-into-Israel models;
- typological/Christological fulfillment models;
- distinct Israel/church dispensational models with shared salvation blessings.

No ranking question should ask “Which school is biblical?” as a factual quiz item.

# 5. 1 Peter 2:21–25 — atonement

Textual/exegetical data strongly support more than a bare moral-example reading:

- Christ suffered;
- he is sinless in the Isaiah 53 cluster;
- he bears sins in 2:24;
- the result is death to sins/living to righteousness;
- healing and shepherd-return language follow.

TMS project position can teach substitutionary atonement, but it should build cumulatively from clause-level evidence rather than define `ὑπέρ` or one verb as if a lexeme alone contained the whole doctrine.

```text
SUBSTITUTIONARY_PROJECT_SYNTHESIS = supported by sentence/Isaiah-cluster + theology controls
ONE_PREPOSITION_PROVES_COMPLETE_ATONEMENT_MODEL = reject
```

# 6. 1 Peter 3:18–22 — disputed passage discipline

TMS/MacArthur is especially relevant because the project leans toward the fallen-spirit/Watchers + victory-proclamation family, but current Chapter-3 research correctly keeps the owner decision on HOLD.

Project rules:

- morphology of `πνεύμασιν` does not identify the spirits;
- aorist morphology does not date the proclamation;
- Watchers background does not prove direct literary dependence on 1 Enoch;
- project position should be taught after serious alternatives are presented;
- baptismal systematics may not be derived from `σῴζει` or a single gloss of `ἐπερώτημα`.

This passage is the model for how the entire course should treat difficult texts.

# 7. 1 Peter 4 — suffering, judgment, gifts

TMS-aligned application should preserve:

- suffering for Christ is not evidence that God's promises failed;
- believers are not promised immediate political/social triumph;
- judgment beginning with God's house does not authorize careless transfer of every OT judgment image into a church-age chronology;
- gifts/stewardship in 4:10–11 should be read textually before entering cessationist/noncessationist systematics.

TMS is cessationist; this is a project-systematic position, not the lexical meaning of `χάρισμα` in 4:10.

# 8. 1 Peter 5 — elders and polity

The TMS doctrinal statement cites 1 Pet 5:1–4 in its teaching on local-church elders.

Before polity cards rank, textual criticism at 5:2 must be closed for relevant variants (`ἐπισκοποῦντες`, `κατὰ θεόν`).

Observable pastoral leadership data include:

- elders are exhorted;
- shepherding language;
- oversight wording in the relevant textual form;
- willingness vs compulsion;
- not for shameful gain;
- not domineering over allotted charges;
- being examples;
- Chief Shepherd / crown of glory.

Project synthesis can teach plural elder leadership and pastoral oversight, but should not claim that one contested textual form by itself establishes a complete polity.

Open CC scholarship from Elliott and Breed gives independent controls on 5:1–5 leadership vocabulary; disagreement with their broader ecclesiology does not invalidate useful passage observations.

# 9. Eschatology in 1 Peter

1 Peter contains future salvation, revelation of Christ, glory, judgment, inheritance and appearing language.

TMS premillennial/pretribulational systematics can be offered as project synthesis where relevant, but the course should not turn every generic future reference into a hidden rapture/millennium proof text.

# 10. How project-position questions should look

Bad:

> “What does 1 Peter 2:9 prove? A) replacement theology is false B) dispensationalism is true ...”

Good:

> “Which statement best distinguishes the direct textual fact from the project's systematic conclusion?”

Good answer:

> “The direct text applies Israel-shaped titles to Peter's readers; the project additionally maintains, from its broader canonical theology, that this does not cancel a future for national Israel.”

This format teaches epistemic categories as well as content.
