## Chapter 4 question ladder

### 4:1–2

Ready layers:
- direct contrast: former human desires vs will of God;
- `ὁπλίσασθε` morphology;
- `πέπαυται` morphology;
- Deep guard against deriving sinless perfection from perfect morphology.

Forbes and Storms are used only as inspected evangelical interpretations. Their agreement is useful control, not neutral lexical proof or consensus.

### 4:3–4

Ready:
- former Gentile-style life and social surprise/abuse;
- history question that separates a rhetorically meaningful Gentile past from invented individual biographies;
- Atkinson's published abstract-level claim that `ἐν ᾧ` in 4:4 has causal force.

Atkinson remains abstract-bounded. No detailed article argument is attributed as inspected.

### 4:5–6 — P0 disputed passage

Ready:
- judgment of living/dead as direct text;
- morphology of `εὐηγγελίσθη`;
- Deep question: what `νεκροῖς` grammar does **not** decide;
- separation of 4:6 from an automatic identification with 3:19.

Still HOLD:
- final project/course position on who the dead are and when the gospel was preached.

Forbes/Storms prefer people evangelized while alive and dead by Peter's writing. The UBS translation handbook preserves serious interpretive complexity. That is enough to teach the dispute responsibly, not enough to erase it.

### 4:7

Ready:
- `ἤγγικεν` morphology;
- the immediate ethical result: sober-minded watchfulness/prayer;
- project-level guard that perfect morphology cannot select a complete preterist/futurist/rapture chronology.

TMS eschatology stays `project`, not lexical evidence.

### 4:8 / Proverbs 10:12

This is now a high-value OT-reuse item.

LXX Prov 10:12 uses `φιλία` and a substantially different clause, whereas 1 Pet 4:8 has `ἀγάπη καλύπτει πλῆθος ἁμαρτιῶν`.

Classification for candidate authoring:

`STRONG_PROVERBIAL_SCRIPTURAL_REUSE`, **not** `MECHANICAL_VERBATIM_LXX_QUOTATION`.

A separate application guard rejects the idea that love makes repentance, truth, protection of victims, justice, or discipline unnecessary.

### 4:9–11

Ready:
- hospitality without grumbling;
- gift-as-stewardship questions;
- `οἰκονόμοι`;
- speaking/serving as the organization of the exhortation, not a mathematically exhaustive two-gift taxonomy;
- TMS cessationism only as a broader project-systematic layer after the direct text is learned.

### 4:12–19

## Chapter 5 question ladder

Ready:

- disputed Deep question on whether `μάρτυς` by itself requires the narrow meaning “visual eyewitness of the crucifixion”.

No authorship theory is allowed to ride on one noun.

### 5:2–4 — leadership lane

Ready:
- `ποιμάνατε` as the governing shepherding command;
- three ethical contrasts: compulsion/willingness, shameful gain/eagerness, domination/example;
- anti-domination and example questions;
- Chief Shepherd and unfading crown;
- Elliott's elder/oversight/shepherd/flock observation;

No polity system may make either unclosed textual unit its single load-bearing proof.

### 5:5 / Proverbs 3:34

- 1 Pet 5:5: `ὁ θεός ...`;
- remaining line is very close.

The candidate tests the visible adaptation. It explicitly forbids inventing Peter's motive for the subject change.

### 5:6–9

Ready:

- anxiety application that refuses the harmful claim “a faithful Christian never has anxiety or needs help”;

### 5:10

Ready high-value Greek set:

`καταρτίσει / στηρίξει / σθενώσει / θεμελιώσει`

### 5:12 — Silvanus

Ready:
- author's own purpose statement: exhort/testify to God's true grace and stand in it;
- disputed carrier vs secretary/amanuensis question;
- exact scholarly attribution: Richards argues carrier, NET prefers carrier while acknowledging alternatives, Storms leaves the precise role unresolved.

HOLD:
- exact current apparatus on `στῆτε / ἑστήκατε`.

Unsafe:

> polished Greek therefore proves Silvanus composed the letter.

### 5:13 — Babylon

Three questions stay separate:

1. grammatical form/referent of `συνεκλεκτή`;
2. identity/location of `Βαβυλών`;
3. Mark as Peter's “son”.

Ready research questions distinguish:
- feminine grammatical form from the historical referent;
- written toponym from the Rome interpretation;
- Rome / Mesopotamian Babylon / Egyptian Babylon as a history-of-interpretation map without giving them equal probability;
- the methodological danger of circularly using Revelation to prove Rome in 1 Peter and then 1 Peter to prove the meaning of Revelation.

No current-location consensus is declared from the 1954 thesis; it is only a historical map.

### 5:14

Ready:
- kiss of love as the named ancient greeting;
- application question separating enduring brotherly welcome/peace from mandating one cultural form;
- closing peace to all in Christ.
