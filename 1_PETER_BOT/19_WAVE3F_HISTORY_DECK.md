# 1 Peter Wave 3.0f — Historical/Social Question Deck

**Status:** `RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PRODUCTION / NOT RANKING`

Wave 3.0f adds a deliberately small historical/social deck after the main text/Greek/interpretation/application layers were already strong.

The objective is not to increase a `history` counter. It is to train the learner to distinguish:

```text
HISTORICAL_EVIDENCE != TOTAL_RECONSTRUCTION
PREDOMINANT_BACKGROUND != EVERY_INDIVIDUAL
SOCIAL_HOSTILITY != AUTOMATIC_OFFICIAL_PROSECUTION
ANCIENT_SOCIAL_TOPOS != ONE_ROMAN_LEGAL_CHARGE
EARLY_LEADERSHIP_WITNESS != COMPLETE_DENOMINATIONAL_POLITY
LETTER_ROLE_HYPOTHESIS != GRAMMATICAL_CERTAINTY
HISTORY_OF_INTERPRETATION != EQUAL_CURRENT_PROBABILITY
RECEPTION_HISTORY != UNCHANGING_CULTURAL_FORM
```

## Eight candidates

Added `w3q_113–120`, four per chapter.

All are:
- `claim_type=history`;
- `position=neutral`;
- `READY_NONCOMPETITIVE`;
- `competitive_candidate=false`.

### `w3q_113` — 4:3 / Gentile background

Stenschke's full Tyndale Bulletin text calls 4:3 the strongest indication for a predominantly Gentile Christian audience, especially because of the reference to lawless idolatry.

The question preserves two boundaries:
- predominantly Gentile != every single addressee was Gentile;
- the vice list != a personal biography or exact cult membership for every reader.

### `w3q_114` — 4:3–4 / conversion and social rupture

Stenschke argues that conversion cost these Gentile Christians part of their previous socio-cultural identity and social fabric. 4:4 itself depicts surprise and slander when believers stop participating in former conduct.

Byrley prevents an overreach from this social hostility directly to one empire-wide official persecution regime.

### `w3q_115` — 4:15 / meddling as ancient social offense

Brown's JBL study makes the historical difference between modern “nosiness” and ancient meddling important. Crossing socially assigned boundaries could be treated as serious interference and have political/social ramifications.

This does not prove one Roman criminal statute or the narrow gloss “revolutionary.”

### `w3q_116` — 4:12–16 / persecution reconstruction

Byrley surveys competing historical reconstructions and argues for a broader cosmic-conflict reading. The candidate does not require the learner to accept his whole model; it asks why the text should not be tied automatically to one Nero/Domitian/Trajan empire-wide scheme.

### `w3q_117` — 5:1–5 / early Christian leadership

Elliott + Breed supply independent historical/exegetical controls for elder/shepherd/oversight/flock/diakonia language.

The question tests how much historical evidence the passage provides without turning one passage into the whole later polity system.

### `w3q_118` — 5:12 / Silvanus

Richards and NET make ancient epistolary practice a genuine historical question. The wording can support a carrier discussion, but does not turn the carrier/secretary question into a parsing fact.

### `w3q_119` — 5:13 / Babylon

The existing history map preserves Rome, Mesopotamian Babylon, and Egyptian Babylon as historical interpretation families. This candidate explicitly rejects “three theories exist, therefore each is 33% likely.”

### `w3q_120` — 5:14 / kiss reception

Strawbridge supplies modern peer-reviewed reception/history control. The embodied greeting had real unity/reconciliation/kinship force and later ritual restrictions.

This lets the learner distinguish historical continuity of principle from an assumption that one physical form remained identical and obligatory in every Christian culture.

## Authoring boundary

Historical distractors should be nearby overreaches:

Good:
- “predominantly Gentile” -> incorrectly “every addressee was Gentile”;
- social slander -> incorrectly “formal imperial prosecution is proven”;
- historical three-location map -> incorrectly “equal probability”;
- letter-carrier argument -> incorrectly “grammar proves secretary.”

Bad:
- unrelated emperors or cities with no connection to the argument;
- intentionally silly social customs;
- a distractor no informed reader would ever consider.

`HISTORY_READY_NONCOMPETITIVE != HISTORICAL_CERTAINTY`.
