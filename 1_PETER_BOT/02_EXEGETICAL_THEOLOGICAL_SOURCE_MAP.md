# Exegetical and Theological Source Map for 1 Peter

## 1. Position architecture

The bot should be confessional without becoming epistemically careless.

### Project anchor

**The Master's Seminary / MacArthur / GTY** controls the course's declared theological synthesis, especially where the owner has specified:

- verbal inspiration and grammatical-historical interpretation;
- substitutionary atonement;
- distinction of church and Israel;
- future fulfillment of promises to Israel;
- premillennial earthly kingdom;
- pretribulational rapture.

Store these conclusions as `position=project` when they go beyond an unambiguous statement of a single verse.

### Evangelical exegetical controls

Use at least two serious witnesses where practical:

- Thomas Schreiner
- Karen Jobes
- Peter Davids
- Wayne Grudem
- Craig Keener
- D. A. Carson
- Sam Storms

Agreement among them can strengthen confidence, but disagreement is informative rather than a bug.

### Academic/adversarial controls

Use Horrell/Williams, Achtemeier, Elliott and peer-reviewed passage studies to expose hidden assumptions, social-history overclaims, and competing readings. Their inclusion does not make their theological synthesis the project position.

## 2. Passage map

### 1:1–12 — identity, election, hope, suffering

Source stack:
- SBLGNT/MorphGNT
- Carson's free 1:1–12 exposition
- MacArthur 60-2 onward
- Schreiner / Jobes / Davids / Keener
- OT-use controls for Exodus/covenant and prophetic background

Guard: `πρόγνωσις` cannot by itself settle an entire predestination system.

### 1:13–2:3 — holiness, redemption, new birth, word

Priorities:
- imperatives and participles;
- Isaiah/Leviticus/Psalms intertext;
- `ἀναγεννάω`, `φθαρτός/ἄφθαρτος`, `λογικός`.

Guard: do not turn `λογικός` into a devotional slogan without lexical/context control.

### 2:4–10 — stone, priesthood, people of God

Highest theological-risk zone for the owner's Israel/church concern.

Separate:
1. what Peter directly applies to his audience;
2. OT quotation/allusion;
3. ecclesiological inference;
4. TMS project synthesis about Israel and the church.

Do not ask a competitive question whose "correct" answer is merely a covenantal or dispensational systematic preference.

### 2:11–3:7 — public conduct, authority, slaves, marriage

Use:
- MacArthur project exposition;
- primary Greco-Roman witnesses;
- Treggiari/Balch/Horrell;
- Jobes/Keener/Schreiner/Davids.

Guard: historical plausibility is not a direct command; household context is not a license for modern abuse.

### 3:8–17 — Psalm 34, Isaiah 8, witness/apologia

Use:
- Psalm 33 LXX / Psalm 34 MT-English;
- Green, Christensen, Gréaux;
- Van Rensburg/Moyise + independent commentaries;
- MacArthur + evangelical controls.

Guard: `ἀπολογία` requires an answer/defense in context but does not choose one modern apologetic school.

### 3:18–22 — spirits, Noah, baptism

Mandatory disputed map:
- fallen spirits / Watchers + victory/judgment proclamation;
- Christ-through-Noah;
- descensus/human-dead reception;
- `ἐπερώτημα`: appeal/request vs pledge/stipulation vs response/confession-related;
- baptismal efficacy models;
- `σαρκί ... πνεύματι` alternatives.

TMS/MacArthur can be the **project conclusion**, but competing serious readings must remain visible. No ranking by commentator preference.

### 4:1–19 — suffering, holiness, gifts, judgment

Priorities:
- `ὁπλίζω`, `πέπαυται`, `ξενίζω`, `ἀγάπη ἐκτενής`, `χάρισμα`;
- Noah/suffering continuity from 3:18–4:6;
- judgment beginning with God's household.

Guard: verb tense/aspect does not make a sinless-perfection doctrine automatically.

### 5:1–14 — elders, humility, suffering, devil, Babylon

Priorities:
- `πρεσβύτερος`, `ποιμαίνω`, textual status of `ἐπισκοπέω`, `κλῆρος`;
- humility/anxiety;
- resistance to the devil;
- Silvanus and Babylon.

Dating, Silvanus's precise role, and Babylon=Rome must be classified as reconstruction where the text does not spell them out.

## 3. Commentary-use rule

A commentary can serve four different functions:

```text
PARSE/SYNTAX CONTROL
HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
EXEGETICAL OPTION
PROJECT THEOLOGICAL SYNTHESIS
```

Do not cite a commentator for a function the inspected passage does not support.

## 4. Fee / Carson classification

- **Fee/Stuart:** method/hermeneutics and accessible pedagogy.
- **Carson:** 1 Peter 1 exposition + 1 Peter OT-use treatment, plus broader exegetical method.
- Neither is to be represented as a complete verse-by-verse 1 Peter commentary in this corpus.

## 5. Historical commentary role

Leighton, Lillie, Calvin, Ames, Brown, Bigg, Hort and Schaff are useful for:
- reception history;
- older Protestant exegesis;
- pastoral rhetoric;
- history of interpretation.

They are not current controls for:
- newly catalogued papyri;
- ECM-based textual decisions;
- modern Asia Minor archaeology/social history;
- current lexical bibliography.
