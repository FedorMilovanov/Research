## What Brown materially adds

Brown does not solve the word by pretending that a modern gloss is mechanically obvious. Her argument is stronger and more nuanced:

1. the word is a NT hapax and lexical evidence is limited;
2. “meddling/interference” is strongly supported by the compound and by the wider Greco-Roman topos;
3. ancient meddling could be socially serious rather than merely nosy or annoying;
4. it can involve inappropriate movement beyond one's assigned social sphere and therefore carry political/social ramifications;
5. Brown's conclusion places the term within that broader social-boundary frame.

This improves the question bank because it permits a **positive bounded conclusion** without false precision.

## What remains unsafe

Do not teach:
- `ἀλλοτριεπίσκοπος = revolutionary` as a lexical equation;
- `ἀλλοτριεπίσκοπος = informer/spy` as certainty;
- `ἀλλοτριεπίσκοπος = embezzler` as certainty;
- `ἀλλοτριεπίσκοπος = errant bishop` as certainty;
- “hapax means nothing can be known”; or
- “Brown settled the scholarly consensus.”

The source improves probability and contextual coherence, not omniscience.
