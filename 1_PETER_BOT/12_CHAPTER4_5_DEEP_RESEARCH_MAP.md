# CHAPTER 4

## 4:1–2 — suffering in the flesh / ceased from sin

### Direct observations

Priority forms:

- `παθόντος` / suffering;
- `ὁπλίσασθε` / arm yourselves;
- `ἔννοιαν` / mindset, intention, way of thinking;
- `πέπαυται` / perfect middle/passive form from `παύω`;
- `ἁμαρτίας`;
- `ἐπιθυμίαις` vs `θελήματι θεοῦ`.

### Dispute

`ὁ παθὼν σαρκὶ πέπαυται ἁμαρτίας` must not be simplified into “physical suffering automatically makes a person sinless.”

Interpretation families worth mapping:

1. suffering as decisive break with sinful former life;
2. death/martyrdom reading — a dead believer is finished with sin;
3. baptismal/conversion participation frameworks;
4. ethical maxim shaped by Christ's suffering.

Project content should preserve the ethical force without teaching sinless perfection from the clause.

### Bot potential

- simple: what contrast governs remaining earthly life?
- Greek: parse `πέπαυται`;
- deep: why does perfect aspect not by itself prove a doctrine of perfection?

## 4:5–6 — judgment of living/dead and gospel to the dead

This is a **P0 disputed passage** for Chapter 4.

Text sequence:

- opponents give account to the One ready to judge living and dead;
- `εἰς τοῦτο γὰρ καὶ νεκροῖς εὐηγγελίσθη` follows;
- purpose/result contrast: judged according to humans in flesh / live according to God in spirit.

### Must separate

```text
νεκροῖς = grammatical/textual referent expression
WHO_EXACTLY_THE_DEAD_ARE = interpretation
WHEN_GOSPEL_WAS_PREACHED = interpretation
WHERE_IT_WAS_PREACHED = interpretation
SECOND_CHANCE_AFTER_DEATH = systematic conclusion, not lexeme
RELATION_TO_3_19 = interpretive hypothesis
```

### Required witnesses

- modern major commentary sections;
- at least one serious reading defending “now dead, evangelized while alive”;
- serious descensus/postmortem reading;
- historical reception if course deep mode needs it.

A single recent article cannot rank the dispute.

## 4:7 — `πάντων δὲ τὸ τέλος ἤγγικεν`

High-value teaching point:

`ἤγγικεν` is perfect active indicative from `ἐγγίζω`.

Morphology itself does not solve:

- exact eschatological chronology;
- preterist/futurist system;
- rapture timing.

Immediate ethical response:

- `σωφρονήσατε`
- `νήψατε`
- `εἰς προσευχάς`.

Project premillennialism should remain a systematic layer, not be smuggled into the parse of `ἤγγικεν`.

## 4:10–11 — gifts, stewardship, speech/service

Key terms:

- `χάρισμα`
- `διακονοῦντες`
- `οἰκονόμοι`
- `ποικίλης χάριτος θεοῦ`
- `λόγια θεοῦ`
- `ἰσχύος`
- `χορηγεῖ`.

### Project boundary

TMS cessationism can be stated as project systematics **after** the passage is taught.

Unsafe:

> `χάρισμα` lexically means only non-miraculous gifts.

Unsafe:

> The two categories “speaking/serving” are a complete inspired taxonomy of exactly two gifts.

Safer:

> Peter organizes the exhortation around speaking and serving examples while emphasizing stewardship of God's varied grace and God's supply.

### Open scholarship

Breed 2016 explicitly connects 4:7–11 with elder ministry in 5:1–4. This is an interpretation to test, not a text-only fact.

## 5:10–11 — God of all grace restores after suffering

Four future/restorative verbs are excellent Greek-learning material:

- `καταρτίσει`
- `στηρίξει`
- `σθενώσει`
- `θεμελιώσει`.

Need exact current Greek text and variant check before freezing a four-verb mnemonic, because endings and lists are places where transmission can vary.

Theology:

- suffering described as `ὀλίγον` in eschatological perspective;
- God calls to eternal glory in Christ;
- restoration/strengthening is divine action.

Reject prosperity reading promising immediate removal of all hardship.

## 5:12 — Silvanus and purpose statement

`διὰ Σιλουανοῦ ... ἔγραψα` is a major authorship/composition issue.

Interpretation families:

1. Silvanus as letter carrier;
2. secretary/amanuensis involvement;
3. broader compositional assistance.

The preposition/phrase alone does not prove the maximal secretary theory.

Textual criticism:

`στῆτε / ἑστήκατε` is in the Wave-2 queue.

Purpose/theology:

`ταύτην εἶναι ἀληθῆ χάριν τοῦ θεοῦ` and stand in it — high-value whole-letter summary.

## 5:13 — Babylon, chosen woman/community, Mark

Three distinct questions:

1. Who/what is `ἡ ἐν Βαβυλῶνι συνεκλεκτή`?
2. What location does `Βαβυλών` denote?
3. What is implied by `Μᾶρκος ὁ υἱός μου`?

Do not collapse them.

### Babylon location map

Serious historical readings include:

- Rome as cryptic/apocalyptic Babylon;
- Mesopotamian Babylon;
- Egyptian Babylon (historical minority proposal).

A Concordia Seminary 1954 thesis openly maps these three theories; useful for history of interpretation but not a current scholarly arbiter.

Project can prefer Rome if evidence synthesis warrants, but location must remain interpretation, not Greek morphology.

Textual variation involving later Rome-related readings must not be used circularly to prove original semantic intent.
