## 1. Проверяемый вопрос

Что говорят сами эдикторы посмертных трудов Чарнока — Richard Adams и Edward Veal — в одном из
contemporaneous биографических нарративов, написанных людьми, знавшими его?

**Ответ:** они дают ~1 500 слов, которые являются одновременно биографией, характеристикой,
редакторским манифестом и заявлением о правах на рукописи. Ниже — полная транскрипция с
сохранением оригинальной орфографии и разбиения на страницы издания, затем построчный разбор.

## 2. Полная транскрипция (орфография оригинала сохранена)

> ### TO THE READER.
>
> Reader,
>
> THOU art here presented with a little piece, of a Great Man; Great indeed, if great Piety, great
> Parts, great Learning, and great Wisdom, may be admitted to claim that Title: And we verily believe
> that none well acquainted with him, will deny him his right; however malevolent Persons may grudge
> him the honour. It hath been expected, and de∣sired by many, that some ac∣count of his Life might
> be gi∣ven to the world; But we are not willing to offer violence to his ashes, by making him so
> publick now he is Dead, who so much affected privacy while he lived. Thou art therefore de∣sired to
> rest satisfied with this brief account of him, That being very young he went to Cambridge, where in
> Immanuel Colledge he was brought up under the Tuition of the pre∣sent Arch-Bishop of Canterbury.
> What Gracious workings and Evidences of the New-Birth ap∣peared in him while there, hath already
> been spoken of by [\* 1.1] one who was at that time his Fellow Collegiate and Intimate. Some time he
> after∣ward spent in a private Family, and a little more in the exercise of his Ministry in Southwark;
> then removed to New-Colledge in Oxon, where he was Fellow, and spent several years; being then taken
> notice of for his singular Gifts, and had in Repu∣tation by the most Learned and Godly in that
> University, and up∣on that account the more fre∣quently put upon Publick work. Being thence (the
> year after he had been Proctor) called over into Ireland to a constant pub∣lick Employment, he
> exercised his Ministry for about four or five years, not with the approba∣tion only, but to the
> admiration of the most Wise and Judicious Christians, and with the concur∣rent applause of such as
> were of very different sentiments from him in the things of Religion; Nay, even those that never
> lov∣ed his Piety, yet would com∣mend his Learning and Gifts, as being beyond exception, if not
> abve compare. About the year 1660. being discharged from the publick exercise of his Mini∣stry, he
> returned back into England, and in and about London spent the greatest part of fifteen years, without
> any call to his old work in a setled way; but for about these five years last past hath been more
> known by his constant Preach∣ing, of which we need not speak, but let them that heard him, speak for
> him; or, if they should be silent, his Works will do it. He was a Person of excellent Parts, strong
> Reason great Judg∣ment, and (which do not often go together) curious Phansie, of high Improvements,
> and ge∣neral Learning, as having been all his days a most diligent and methodical Student, and a
> great Redeemer of time, rescu∣ing not only his restless hours in the Night, but his very walk∣ing
> time in the Streets from those impertinencies and fruitless vani∣ties, which do so customarily fill
> up mens minds, and steal away their hearts from those bet∣ter and more Noble objects, which do so
> justly challenge their greatest regards: This he did by not only carefully watching (as every good
> Christian should do) but constantly writing down his Thoughts, whereby he both go∣vern'd them
> better, and furnish∣ed himself with many materials for his most elaborate Discour∣ses. His chief
> Talent was his Preaching Gift, in which, to speak modestly, he had few equals. To this therefore, as
> that for which his Lord and Master had best fitted him (neglecting the practice of Physick, in which
> he had arrived at a considera∣ble measure of knowledge) he did especially addict himself; and direct
> his Studies, and even when Providence denyed him opportunities, yet he was still lay∣ing in more
> stock, and prepa∣ring for work against he might be called to it. When he was in Employment, none
> that heard him, could justly blame his retiredness, he being even when most private, continually at
> work for the Publick; and had he been less in his Study, he would have been less liked in the Pulpit.
> His Library, furnish∣ed (tho not with a numerous, yet) a curious Collection of Books, was his
> Workhouse, in which he laboured hard all the Week, and on the Lords Day made it appear he had not
> been idle; and that tho he consulted his privacy, yet he did not in∣dulge his Sloth. He was
> some∣what reserved where he was not well acquainted; otherwise very free, affable and communicative
> where he understood and liked his company. He affected not much Acquaintance, because be would
> escape Visitants, well knowing how much the ordina∣ry sort of Friends were apt to take up of his
> time, which he could ill spare from his belov∣ed Studies, meeting with few that could give him
> better en∣tertainment vvith their compa∣ny, than he could give himself alone. They had need be
> very good, and very learned, by whose converse he could gain more than by his own Thoughts and
> Books. He was a true Son of the Church of England, in that sound Doctrine laid down in the Articles
> of Religion, and Taught by our most famous ancient Divines and Reformers; and a real follower of
> their Pie∣ty, as well as a strenuous main∣tainer of the Truth they profes∣sed. His Preaching was
> mostly practical, yet rational and argu∣mentative, to his hearers under∣standings, as well as
> affections; and where controversies came in his way, he shewed great A∣cuteness and Judgment in
> discus∣sing and determining them, and no less skill in applying them to practice: So that he was
> in∣deed a workman that needed not to be ashamed, being able by sound Doctrine, both to exhort and
> con∣vince gain-sayers. Some have thought his Preaching too high for vulgar Hearers, and it
> can∣not be denyed but his gifts were suited to the more intelligent sort of Christians; yet it
> must withal be said, that if he were sometimes deep, he was never abstruse; he handled the great
> Mysteries of the Gospel with much clearness and perspicuity; so that if in his Preaching he were
> above most, it was only because most were below him. Several considerable Treatises on some of the
> most impor∣tant points of Religion, he fin∣ished in his ordinary course, which he hath left
> behind him, in the same form he usually writ them for the Pulpit. This comes out first as a
> Prodromus to several others designed to be made publick, as soon as they can be with conveniency
> trans∣cribed, which (if the Lord will, and spare life) shall be attested with our hands; and
> whatever any else shall publish, can be but imperfect Notes (his own Co∣pies being under our
> revisal at the request of his Friends) taken from him in the Pulpit, in which what mistakes do
> often happen, every one knows, and we have found by experience in the case of this very Author,
> more than once. This was thought fit to be said, to secure the reputation of the Dead, and prevent
> the a∣buse of the Living. These Ser∣mons might have come out with the solemn ceremony of large
> recommendations, the Authors worth being so well known to, his Preaching so highly esteem∣ed by the
> most eminent Mini∣sters about the City; but it was judged needless, his own works being sufficient
> to praise him.
>
> One thing more is to be ad∣ded, That such as he is here, such he is in his other Pieces; so that
> thou hast here, Reader, a spe∣cimen of the strain and Spirit of this holy Man, this being his
> familiar and ordinary way of Preaching, and these Sermons coming out first, not as if they were the
> non-such of what he left behind him, but because they could soonest be dispatch∣ed, and to obviate
> the injuries might else be done by spurious Treatises, both to him, and thee; and likewise by this
> little tast to gratifie the Appetites of such who having been his Auditors, did long even with
> greediness to feast themselves again upon those excellent truths, which in the delivery were so
> sweet to them. Perhaps too it may quicken their appetites who never heard him, it may be never yet
> heard of him. If thou like this Cluster, Fear not but the Vintage will be an∣swerable: If this
> little earnest be good Metal, the vvhole sum vvill be no less currant. That a Blessing from Heaven
> may be upon this vvork, and upon thee in Reading, and Studying the Nature, and Beauty, and Ends of
> Divine Providence, and that the Lord of the Harvest (espe∣cially vvhen so many are dayly called
> home) vvould send forth more and more such labourers into the Harvest, is the hearty prayer of.
>
> **Thine in the Lord.**
> **Richard Adams.**
> **Edward Veal.**

### 2A. «TO THE READER» фолианта 1682 — второй редакторский предисл (добавлен 2026-10-25)

**Источник:** EEBO-TCP `A32723.0001.001` (*Several discourses upon the existence and attributes of
God*, Lond., 1682; Wing **C3711**), узел `1:2`. Подпись: **«Thine in the Lord, EDW. VEEL. RI. ADAMS.»**

Это **отдельный документ**, не перепечатка эпистола Providence. Он написан к главному труду и даёт
восемь сведений, которых нет больше нигде в отделе.

**1. Транскрибаторы названы по именам.**

> THIS long since promised, and greatly expected Vo∣lume of the Reverend Authour upon the Divine
> Attributes, being **Transcribed out of his own Manuscripts**, by the unwearied diligence of those
> worthy Persons that undertook it, [\* 1.1] is now at last come to thy hands…

Сноска `* 1.1` дословно: **«Mr. J. Wichens. Mr. Ashton.»**

До этого отдел знал только Adams'а и Veal'а как эдикторов. Теперь известны **два человека,
непосредственно переписывавшие рукописи**: **Mr. J. Wichens** и **Mr. Ashton**. Ни Calamy, ни
McCosh, ни Symington, ни DNB этих имён не называют. Это **новые лица в биографии публикации
Чарнока**, и они — первые кандидаты в круг «тех, кто знал его бумаги».

**2. Рукописи — авторские, а не стенограммы.** «Transcribed out of **his own Manuscripts**» —
прямое заявление эдикторов 1682 года. В сочетании с эпистолом 1680 («Several considerable
Treatises … he hath left behind him, **in the same form he usually Writ them for the Pulpit**»,
«constantly writing down his Thoughts») и с элегией 1680 («'Twas to pass thro' the Pulpit to the
Press») это даёт **три независимых contemporaneous свидетельства** о характере рукописей.

**3. Длительность серии атрибутов — около трёх лет.**

> How much of that beauty … mayest thou behold in this Volume, which was our Author's main
> business, **for about three years before he died**, to display before his hearers?

Уточнение: эпистол 1680 говорит о **пяти** годах проповеди («for about these five years last past
hath been more known by his constant Preaching»), а предисл 1682 — о **трёх** годах именно серии
атрибутов. Это не конфликт, а уточнение: проповедовал пять лет, серию атрибутов вёл около трёх
(~1677–1680). Рабочая формулировка отдела: **серия атрибутов — около 1677–1680**.

**4. Джон Престон умер, проповедуя о том же предмете.**

> Dr. Preston did worthily upon the Attributes in his day, but his Discourses likewise are more
> succinct, when this Authours are more full and large. But whatever were the mind of God in it, it
> was not his Will, that either of these two should live to finish what he had begun, **both being
> taken away when Preaching upon this Subject**. Happy Souls! whose last breath was spent in so noble
> a Work, [\* 1.5] praising God while they had any being.

Сноска `* 1.5` = **Пс. 146:2** («While I live will I praise the LORD»).

**John Preston (1587–1628)**, мастер Emmanuel College, величайший пуританский проповедник поколения
до Чарнока, автор *A Liveles Life of Man* и трактатов об атрибутах. Эдикторы **сами проводят
параллель**: оба умерли, не закончив серию об атрибутах. Для эпической биографии это мощнейший
сюжет: Чарнок — не одинокий прерванный труд, а вторая половина пары с человеком, который умер при
том же предмете за пятьдесят два года до него, и который, как и Чарнок, был связан с Emmanuel.

**5. Прямая речь Чарнока: «the old is better».**

> He was not indeed for that Modern Divinity which is so much in vogue with some, who would be
> counted the only sound Divines; having tasted the old, he did not desire the new, but **said, the
> old is better**.

Это **единственное в отделе reported высказывание Чарнока в прямой речи**, и оно теологически
нагружено: отказ от «Modern Divinity» (то есть от реформированной схоластики и новых богословских
мод) в пользу старого богословия. Обыгрывает Лк. 5:39.

**6. Отношение к Schoolmen и к Jackson.**

> Though he make great use of Schoolmen, yet they are certainly more beholden to him, than he to
> them; he adopts their Notions, but he refines them too, and improves them, and reforms them from
> the barbarousness in which they were expressed, and dresseth them up in his own Language…
>
> What Dr. Jackson did (**to whom our Authour gave all due respect**) was more brief, and in another
> way.

**Thomas Jackson (1579–1640)** — третий именитый богослов в ряду сравнений (после Preston). Эдикторы
подчёркивают, что Чарнок «дал ему должное уважение».

**7. Ортодоксия — заявление эдикторов.**

> though he treat of many of the most abstruse and mysterious Doctrines of Christianity, which are
> the Subjects of great Debates and Controversies in the World, yet **we find no one material thing
> in which he may justly be called Heterodox** (unless old Heresies be of late grown Orthodox, and
> his differing from them must make him faulty) but generally delivers (as in his [\* 1.4] former
> Pieces) what is most consonant to the Faith of This, and other the best Reformed Churches.

Сноска `* 1.4` = **«Treatise of Providence and of Thoughts.»** — то есть эдикторы 1682 года относят
к «former Pieces» именно Providence и «Thoughts»; это независимое подтверждение того, что
*Supplement* 1683 включает «The Sinfulness and Cure of Thoughts» в ряд «прежних» произведений.

Против социниан: «Some Errours, especially the **Socinian**, he sets himself industriously against,
and cuts the very Sinews of them, yet, sometimes, almost without naming them.»

**8. Метод и стиль — характеристика для биографии.**

> 'Tis plain, he was **no empty Preacher**, but was more for sense than sound, filled up his words
> with matter, and chose rather to inform his Hearers Minds, than to claw any itching Ears.
>
> His Method is much the same in most of these Discourses… **He rarely makes Objections, and yet
> frequently answers them**, by implying them in those Propositions he lays down for the clearing up
> the Truths he asserts.
>
> nothing is more **nervous** than his Reasonings, and nothing more **affecting** than his
> Applications.
>
> in them his main business is, as to discipline a prophane World for its neglect of God, and
> contempt of him in his most adorable and shining Perfections…
>
> Though he make great use of Schoolmen… nothing is more nervous than his Reasonings, and nothing
> more affecting than his Applications.

**Ограничение, которое эдикторы сами признают:**

> some little things, a Word, or a Phrase now and then he may have, which no doubt, **had he lived to
> Transcribe his own Sermons, he would have altered**.

То есть эдикторы 1682 года **сознают**, что перед нами транскрипция, а не авторская правка. Это
important для `locatorState` всех quote-card: текст фолианта 1682 — `A1` как печатный объект, но
**не финальная авторская воля**.

**Порядок подписей.** Эпистол 1680 подписан «Richard Adams … Edward Veal»; предисл 1682 — «EDW.
VEEL. RI. ADAMS.». Порядок имён переменен. Мелочь, но для редакционной истории существенная:
Veel в 1682 году назван первым.

### Сноска издания

## 3. Что этот текст даёт, чего не даёт ни один вторичный источник

### 3.1. Разрешение `CONFLICT-2` и `CONFLICT-3` — решающее

Эдикторы пишут: **«Being thence (the year after he had been Proctor) called over into Ireland»**.

- Если proctor назначен в **1654** (McCosh), Ирландия = **1655**. Сходится с документально
  подтверждённым прибытием Henry Cromwell в Ирландию в августе 1655.
- Если proctor назначен в **1652** (Calamy), Ирландия = **1653**. **Противоречит** 1655.
- Если proctor в 1652 и Ирландия в 1655 (CCEL), это «три года спустя», а не «the year after».

**Вывод отдела:** первичный эпистол Adams & Veal **независимо подтверждает 1654** как год
назначения proctor и **опровергает 1652**. Calamy и вслед за ним Symington ошибаются. McCosh прав.
Это первое в отделе **документальное**, а не аргументированное, разрешение конфликта.

### 3.2. Подтверждение пяти лет в Crosby Hall

«but for about these five years last past hath been more known by his constant Preaching»

Независимо от Wood («in the five last years of his life»). Два источника дают одну цифру.
1675 + 5 = 1680. Сходится.

### 3.3. Подтверждение последовательности жизни

«Some time he afterward spent in a private Family, and a little more in the exercise of his Ministry
in Southwark; then removed to New-Colledge in Oxon, where he was Fellow, and spent several years»

Последовательность: **Кембридж (Emmanuel) → частная семья → Southwark → New College, Oxford →
Ирландия**. Длительности первых двух этапов не даны вовсе. Эдикторы не называют ни дат, ни имён.

### 3.4. Johnson назван по имени в сноске

Adams & Veal ссылаются на рассказ об обращении: «hath already been spoken of by [* 1.1] one who was
at that time his Fellow Collegiate and Intimate». Сноска: **«Mr. Johnson in his Sermon on occasion of
Mr. Charnocks death.»**

**Значение:** funeral sermon John Johnson — не просто ценное свидетельство, а **тот источник, на
который ссылались сами эдикторы**. Он был бы третьим, а не первым по важности, если бы не это.
Он поднимается до `A1` не только хронологически, но и по признанию эдикторов.

### 3.5. Медицина — прямое утверждение о практике

«neglecting the practice of Physick, in which he had arrived at a considerable measure of knowledge»

Это **сильнее**, чем все вторичные формулировки. Эдикторы не говорят «он интересовался медициной»;
они говорят, что он **оставил практику врачевания**, в которой достиг значительного знания, ради
проповеди. Формулировка «practised medicine» из Book Owners Online тем самым получает `A1`-опору,
но с обязательной оговоркой: практика была **прервана/оставлена**, не сопровождала его до конца.

### 3.6. Церковная идентичность — ключевой факт для биографии

«He was a true Son of the Church of England, in that sound Doctrine laid down in the Articles of
Religion, and Taught by our most famous ancient Divines and Reformers; and a real follower of their
Piety, as well as a strenuous maintainer of the Truth they professed.»

**Это важнейшая строка всего эпистола.** Чарнока принято называть «пуританским пресвитерианином»,
и это верно по практике. Но эдикторы, знавшие его, описывают его как **истинного сына Церкви
Английской** в доктринальном смысле: 39 статей, древних богословов и реформаторов Англии.

Для эпической биографии это даёт центральное напряжение: человек, который в 1660 был
«discharged from the publick exercise of his Ministry» и который в 1675 проповедовал в
лицензированном нонконформистском зале Crosby Hall, но которого друзья описывают как сына
Церкви Английской. Не «отступник» и не «радикал», а конформист по доктрине, отделившийся по
порядку.

### 3.7. Редакторский манифест — заявление о правах на рукописи

«Several considerable Treatises … he finished in his ordinary course, which he hath left behind him,
in the same form he usually writ them for the Pulpit. This comes out first as a Prodromus … which
(if the Lord will, and spare life) shall be attested with our hands; and whatever any else shall
publish, can be but imperfect Notes (his own Co∣pies being under our revisal at the request of his
Friends) taken from him in the Pulpit … This was thought fit to be said, to secure the reputation of
the Dead, and prevent the a∣buse of the Living.»

**Значение для эпичности:** это заявление о **праве первичной публикации**, сделанное в 1684 году.
Любой другой, кто издал бы Чарнока, публиковал бы «imperfect notes». Это объясняет, почему
корпус Чарнока так однороден редакционно: он прошёл через две пары рук.

### 3.8. Почему Providence вышел первым

«these Sermons coming out first, not as if they were the non-such of what he left behind him, but
because they could soonest be dispatch∣ed, and to obviate the injuries might else be done by
spurious Treatises»

Не потому, что это лучший труд, а потому, что его можно было быстрее подготовить и чтобы
предотвратить подделки. Плюс: «by this little tast to gratifie the Appetites of such who having
been his Auditors, did long even with greediness to feast themselves again».

### 3.9. Закрывающая метафора

«If thou like this Cluster, Fear not but the Vintage will be an∣swerable: If this little earnest be
good Metal, the vvhole sum vvill be no less currant.»

«Гроздь — винтаж», «залог — валюта». Это последние слова эпистола и один из самых цитируемых
образцов пуританской риторики эдикторов.

### 4A. Первое издание 1680 получено (добавлено 2026-10-25)

**`A32726.0001.001`** — *A treatise of divine providence: I. In general. II. In particular, as
relating to the church of God in the world / by … Mr. Steph. Charnocke …* London: Printed, for
Tho. Cockeril, at the Three Legs in the Poultrey, near the Stocks-Market, **1680**. Wing **C3712**;
ESTC R13224. **Титульный лист, лист «Advertisement» и эпистол «TO THE READER» получены полностью**
(узлы `1:1`, `1:2`, `1:3`).

**Титульный лист 1680 (дословно):**

> A TREATISE OF DIVINE PROVIDENCE.
> - I. In General.
> - II. In Particular,
>
> AS Relating to the Church of God in the World.
>
> By that late Eminent Minister of Christ Mr. STEPP. CHARNOCKE, B. D. and sometimes Fellow of

**Лист «Advertisement» 1680 (узел `1:2`, дословно) — новый документ отдела:**

> **Advertisement.**
>
> WHat ever comes out in Print, of this late Reverend Mr. Steph. Char∣nock's, will be **Attested by
> Mr. Edward Veal, and Mr. Richard Adams**, which is more fully expressed in the Epistle.

**Значение.** Лист «Advertisement» — **отдельный документ**, которого нет в транскрипции тома 08
(она построена по изданию 1684, где листа нет). Он устанавливает, что **уже в 1680 году** эдикторы
заявили права на аттестацию **всего**, что будет напечатано от имени Чарнока, — не только этого
трактата. Это ранняя форма того заявления о правах на рукописи, которое в §3.7 тома разобрано по
эпистолу. Лист даёт также **порядок имён**: «Mr. Edward Veal, and Mr. Richard Adams» — Veal назван
первым, тогда как в эпистоле подпись идёт «Richard Adams … Edward Veal». Мелочь, но для
редакционной истории существенная.

**Главное: эпистол 1680 и эпистол 1684 — один и тот же текст.** Сопоставление узла `1:3` издания
1680 с разделом `1:2` издания 1684 показывает, что эдикторы **не переписывали** биографический
нарратив между изданиями. Расхождения — только капитализация и две опечатки TCP-транскрипции
(«abve compare» в 1680 против «above compare» в 1684; «be would escape Visitants» в 1680 против
«he would escape Visitants» в 1684). Следствие для отдела:

- все биографические утверждения эпистола действуют **с 1680 года**, а не с 1684;
- в частности, **Уильям Сэнкрофт назван в 1680 году** («brought up under the Tuition of the
  present Arch-Bishop of Canterbury») — за одиннадцать лет до Wood 1691 и за шесть лет до Calamy
  1727. Формула «a true Son of the Church of England…» также датируется **1680**, а не 1684;
  Wood 1691 мог взять её из эпистола либо из общего источника.

**Титульный лист 1682 (*Attributes*, `A32723.0001.001`, узел `1:1`) — дословно:**

> SEVERAL DISCOURSES UPON THE EXISTENCE AND ATTRIBUTES OF GOD.
>
> By that late Eminent Minister of Christ, Mr. STEPHEN CHARNOCKE, B. D. And sometimes Fellow of
> New-Colledge in Oxon.
>
> LONDON, Printed for D. Newman, T. Cockerill, Benj. Griffin, T. Simmons, and Benj. Alsop.
> M DC LXXXII.

**Это разрешает `CONFLICT-6`**: титульный лист первого издания датирован **M DC LXXXII** = 1682.
Диапазон «1681–82» у McCosh и DNB отражает две части с разной датой титула, а не неопределённость.
Заметьте также написание **«CHARNOCKE»** (с финальным -e) на титулах 1680 и 1682 против
«CHARNOCK» в заголовке broadside *The sayings…* 1680.

**Несоответствие статусов.** Каталог EEBO-TCP (`TCP.json`, файл `textcreationpartnership/Texts`)
помечает `A32726`, `A32722`, `A32723`, `A32724` статусом **Restricted**, тогда как страницы
Michigan (`quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo2/…`) для этих же ID демонстрируют уведомление **CC0 1.0**.
Отдел фиксирует расхождение и **не утверждает** CC0 для `A32726` как установленный факт: текст
использован как прочитанный, право на перепечатку — под вопросом. `RIGHTS_HOLD` для этих объектов
**сохраняется** до выяснения.

## 5. Границы допустимой формулировки

- Нельзя attribуить эпистол McCosh или Symington: это прямой источник 1680/1684 годов.
- Нельзя приводить цитаты из эпистола по McCosh: теперь есть EEBO-TCP для обоих изданий.
- Нельзя датировать эпистол 1684 годом, если речь идёт о биографических утверждениях: эпистол
  существует с 1680 года (издание `A32726`), и 1684-е издание его перепечатывает без изменений.
- Нельзя attribуить Сэнкрофта только Wood 1691: «the present Arch-Bishop of Canterbury» назван уже
  в эпистоле 1680 года.
- Нельзя attribуить формулу «a true Son of the Church of England» только изданию 1684 года.
- Нельзя citing титульный лист 1680 как «без эпиграфа»: на нём есть эпиграф Пс. 103:19
  («His Kingdom Ruleth over all»).
- Нельзя утверждать, что `A32726` свободен по CC0: каталог даёт Restricted, страница Michigan —
  CC0; расхождение не разрешено.
- Нельзя утверждать, что proctor был в 1652: опровергнуто.
- Нельзя называть Чарнока только «пуританским пресвитерианином» без оговорки о «true Son of the
  Church of England».
- Нельзя утверждать, что он был практикующим врачом: он **оставил** практику ради проповеди.
- Нельзя утверждать, что Providence был первым, потому что лучший: эдикторы прямо отрицают это.
