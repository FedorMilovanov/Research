## 1. Коринф — римская колония, а не греческий город

> «If we are to understand the background or cultural context of these letters we need to read them against the backdrop of a Roman colony, not a Greek city. Institutions, legal procedures, social customs, architecture, public images and to some extent language owed more to Rome than to the Greek world.»

> «At Corinth public inscriptions are predominantly in Latin rather than Greek; this is especially noticeable when identical texts are found elsewhere in Greece.»

> «The colony was in effect a new foundation, as the city had lain desolate for over a century... the public buildings and temples are essentially Italian; in layout parts of the city even recall the Forum of Augustus at Rome.»

## 2. Мужчины: capite velato — статус жреца/магистрата

> «Augustus is shown wearing a tunic and a toga, the dress of a Roman citizen, which is drawn up over his head. The right hand... would almost certainly have held a patera, or shallow dish, for pouring libations.» (статуя из Юлиевой базилики в Коринфе)

> «This feature of the so-called capite velato was "the iconographical mark of a sacrificial presiding over a specifically Roman ritual".»

> «Very few of the participants in the sacrifice have their toga pulled over their heads; this form of dress is confined to the priests, the two togate figures on the north side, and, perhaps significantly, Aeneas making his sacrifice. The rest of the people merely wear wreaths. The covering of heads is not a general form of dress adopted by people attending a sacrifice; it is specific to those who are taking an active part.»

## 3. Реконструкция ситуации: элита и статусная борьба

> «The issue which Paul is dealing with here seems to be that members of the social élite within the church—the dunatoi and the eugeneis (1:26)—were adopting a form of dress during worship which drew attention to their status in society. The dress was a result of these individuals being contentious (φιλόνεικος) (v. 16).»

> «The picture is one of rival groups, perhaps from different families, within the church who are using their dress to further their ambition to dominate and thus to be honoured by those present.»

> «If they continued to wear the toga over their heads it would indicate that there was continuing inequality in the church; the uncovered head would restate that "the head of every man is Christ" (v. 3).»

> «Although the image of the head covered with the toga conjures up the image of pagan sacrifices, this passage has probably nothing to do with shunning the worship of idols (10:14). Paul had already dealt with food offered to idols... Here he is speaking out against a form of dress which would be familiar to a Romanised or even Roman church at Corinth as placing emphasis on social status.»

## 4. Женщины: портреты и вуаль как знак брачного статуса

> «Public marble portraits of women at Corinth, presumably members of wealthy and prestigious families are most frequently shown bare-headed. This would suggest that it was socially acceptable in a Roman colony for women to be seen bare-headed in public.»

> «The 1000 hetairai linked to the cult of Aphrodite, and the corresponding notoriety of Corinth, belong to the hellenistic city swept away by Mummius in 146 BC. In contrast the Roman shrine was far more modest, and only described as a ναίδιον.» (против мифа о «городе проституток»)

> «A republican funerary relief from the Via Statilia... A husband and wife stand facing the viewer. The husband is shown bareheaded, whereas his wife has her head covered; as D. Strong has commented, she reflects "the republican ideal of dutiful womanhood".»

> «The wearing of the veil said something about the wife's position in society: the lack of it at a meeting such as this would have been a poor reflection on her husband.»

> «Paul's encouragement to cover the head should be seen as an encouragement to retain some value systems of the secular Roman society.»

## 5. Бритая/остриженная голова — знак унижения и наказание прелюбодейки

> «Plutarch tells us that "in Greece, whenever any misfortune comes, the women cut off their hair and the men let it grow..."»

> «Dio Chrysostom talks about the shaving of women's heads as a sign of humiliation in his discussion of Demonassa of Cyprus... The first [law] states "a woman guilty of adultery shall have her hair cut off and be a harlot (ἐπορνεύετο)".»

> «What Paul may be saying is that if women in the church will not wear a veil, then they will be seen as dishonouring their husbands... If the wife insists on being unveiled then she might as well wear a sign of humiliation by having her hair cut.»

## 6. Природа (φύσις) и длинные волосы

> «It should be remembered that Paul is addressing a church which included members of the social élite at Corinth. It is thus immaterial that farmers or foreigners wore their hair long if the custom in a Roman colony was for the men to wear their hair short and for women to wear their hair long.»

> «If Paul was using φύσις in the Stoic sense, in which "nature is the origin and guarantor of culture", his appeal is to the men's own Roman cultural background.»

> «Long hair, as opposed to a shaven head, was a symbol in Roman society of a wife's relationship to her husband.»

## 7. Вывод Гилла

> «The issue which is discussed in these verses seems to be a continuation of the problems faced in the early chapters of the epistle. It seems to reflect the jostling for power and authority amongst the leading families within the church at Corinth after Paul's departure... Although for men Paul counteracts their desire to apply secular models into their ekklesia, in the case of women Paul restates that they should continue to place a high value on marriage and should not flout social conventions in the church meeting purely for the sake of being contentious.»

## Значение для серии

1. Археологическая база для Q1 (что происходило в Коринфе): мужчины-элиты в capite velato = статусное поведение, а не «эллинский обычай» Златоуста; женщины — вуаль как знак брачного статуса (подтверждает Winter, Остера).
2. Ответ на контраргумент Томпсон (женские портреты с открытой головой): Гилл признаёт это, но объясняет: открытая голова допустима в портретах, однако вуаль была знаком брачного статуса и чести мужа.
3. Против мифа «Коринф = город 1000 гетер»: римский храм Афродиты был скромным ναίδιον (ср. Страбон viii.6.20–21).
4. φύσις у Гилла = стоическое «природа — источник и гарант культуры» (усиливает культурно-контекстное прочтение 11:14–15).
5. 11:16 φιλόνεικος связано Гиллом с любовью к чести (φιλοτιμία, Платон) — статусная борьба элит как фон всего отрывка.
