**Snapshot:** 2026-09-29

## 1. Executive finding

Clint Archer's Jan. 24, 2025 Cripplegate article provides the clearest public description yet of a proposed gate for Steven Lawson's renewed first-person public communication.

Archer said he would release a Lawson interview only if the following four stakeholder groups were **“all in agreement”**:

1. Lawson's family;
2. Lawson's counselors;
3. OnePassion's board;
4. the elders at Trinity Bible Church (TBC).

Primary source:
- https://thecripplegate.com/steve-lawson-interview/

This is a materially stronger statement than the generic formulation that Lawson was merely receiving advice. It describes a proposed **multi-party release condition** for a public first-person interview.

By contrast, the public corpus surrounding the 2026 book has not identified a comparable approval/review chain for the much larger public communication represented by *Mercy in the Wilderness*.

That contrast is legitimate and important.

It does **not** prove that Lawson published the book against counsel, that any of the four groups opposed it, or that Archer's January 2025 proposed release condition remained binding in September 2026.

---

## 2. What Archer actually proposed on Jan. 24, 2025

Archer's account says Lawson had wanted to speak publicly but had been counseled against rushing into a statement.

According to Archer, Lawson then asked the men counseling him whether it might be prudent to speak from the heart in an interview at Archer's church in Mobile.

Archer's publication condition was explicit: he would release the interview only if Lawson's family, counselors, OnePassion board, and TBC elders were **all in agreement**.

Source:
- https://thecripplegate.com/steve-lawson-interview/

### Evidence classification

This is:

- a first-person statement by the proposed interviewer;
- contemporaneous with the contemplated interview;
- explicit about the stakeholder groups Archer intended to treat as release gatekeepers.

It is **not** a formal TBC policy document, OnePassion board resolution, counseling contract, or statement signed by all four groups.

Therefore the precise claim is:

> **ARCHER PROPOSED A FOUR-GROUP CONSENSUS GATE FOR RELEASING HIS LAWSON INTERVIEW.**

Do not inflate that into:

> “TBC formally decreed that Lawson could never communicate publicly without unanimous written permission from these four groups.”

No such document is presently in the corpus.

The grammar is conditional, not a statement that the interview had already occurred.

This secondary-source overstatement must not enter the article as fact.

Preserved primary object:

### What is not established

The public corpus does not currently document:

- which counselors reviewed it;
- whether Anne/family approved its wording;

Therefore the statement should be treated as a primary Lawson communication, not as documentary proof of the approval process behind it.

## 8. The 2025 ↔ 2026 governance asymmetry

The narrow, defensible comparison is:

### January 2025

For a contemplated short first-person interview, Archer publicly described a process in which release would occur only after agreement from four stakeholder groups:

- family;
- counselors;
- OnePassion board;
- TBC elders.

### September 2026

A 122–124-page public testimony was released, but the public record does not identify:

- whether the same groups were consulted;
- whether an equivalent set of gatekeepers replaced them;
- whether the finished manuscript was pastorally or theologically reviewed;
- what changed in the communications-governance model between Jan. 2025 and Sep. 2026.

### Correct conclusion

> **PUBLIC GATEKEEPING WAS DESCRIBED WITH UNUSUAL SPECIFICITY IN JANUARY 2025; THE GATEKEEPING PROCESS BEHIND THE MUCH LARGER SEPTEMBER 2026 BOOK REMAINS PUBLICLY OPAQUE.**

That is a transparency finding, not proof of rebellion.

---

## 9. Why the comparison is probative

The significance is not that family members, counselors or elders own Lawson's conscience or possess permanent censorship rights.

The significance is evidentiary:

1. Lawson's post-scandal public communication was initially treated as something capable of harming others if mishandled.
2. Archer publicly presented family, counselors, OnePassion and TBC as stakeholders whose judgment mattered before public release.
3. The later book is much larger and more interpretive than the contemplated interview.
4. It narrates the meaning of Lawson's own fall and restoration and therefore re-enters public Christian influence.
5. The public corpus has not shown who exercised equivalent external judgment over that release.

Thus the strongest article question is process-oriented:

> **What changed between January 2025, when even an interview was publicly described as requiring broad agreement, and September 2026, when Lawson published an extended theological testimony through a tiny private imprint?**
