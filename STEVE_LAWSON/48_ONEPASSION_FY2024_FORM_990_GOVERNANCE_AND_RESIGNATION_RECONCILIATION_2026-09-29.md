At first glance this looks inconsistent with OnePassion's Sept. 20, 2024 public statement that Lawson had **“resigned from all his duties at OnePassion Ministries.”**

Preserved statement:
- https://ministrywatch.com/steven-lawson-removed-from-ministry-for-inappropriate-relationship/

That apparent contradiction is **not real**. IRS Form 990 instructions explicitly require an officer/director/trustee who served **at any time during the organization's tax year** to be treated as a **current** officer/director/trustee for Part VII, even if that person ceased serving before year-end.

Official IRS sources:
- https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990
- https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/exempt-organization-annual-reporting-requirements-reporting-compensation-in-part-vii-form-990-when-status-changes-during-year

Therefore:

> **The FY2024 Form 990 listing Steve Lawson as “President” does not establish that he remained president after his September 2024 resignation or on Dec. 31, 2024. It is the reporting treatment IRS instructions require for a person who served as president during any portion of FY2024.**

This is a crucial guardrail against a false scandal claim.

## 3. IRS rule: “current” means served at any time during the tax year

The current official Form 990 instructions say:

> A “current” officer, director, or trustee is a person that was an officer, director, or trustee **at any time during the organization's tax year**.

The instructions further say:

> **Don't check the “Former” box** if the person was a current officer, director, or trustee at any time during the organization's tax year.

Official instructions:
- https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990

The IRS's dedicated FAQ is even more explicit:

> A filer should list anyone who was a current officer or director **at any time during the tax year, even if the person is not an officer or director at the end of the year**.

Official FAQ:
- https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/exempt-organization-annual-reporting-requirements-reporting-compensation-in-part-vii-form-990-when-status-changes-during-year

### Application to Lawson

Lawson indisputably served as OnePassion president for most of 2024 before resigning in September.

Thus Part VII's `President` treatment is exactly what IRS rules predict even if his resignation was fully effective in September.

### Publication rule

Unsafe:

> “OnePassion's 2025-filed tax return proves Lawson remained president after resigning.”

Safe:

> “The 2024 return still labels Lawson president because IRS rules classify anyone who held the office at any point in the tax year as a current officer for Part VII; the filing therefore cannot determine his status after the September resignation.”
