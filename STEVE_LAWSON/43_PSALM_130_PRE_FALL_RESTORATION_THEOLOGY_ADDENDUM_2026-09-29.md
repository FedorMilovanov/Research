## 3. July 19, 2009 — Countryside Bible Church sermon preserves the Lawson attribution

Source:
- https://countrysidebible.org/sermons/20090719p-113832

In a sermon on the Psalms of Ascents, the preacher reaches Psalm 130:5:

> “I wait for the LORD, my soul does wait, and in His word do I hope.”

He asks what the already-forgiven, repentant psalmist is waiting for and then expressly says:

> **“Steve Lawson brings this out in his commentary as well.”**

The preacher then attributes to Lawson the answer that the psalmist is waiting for:

> **“complete restoration and peace.”**

He explains the concept as the forgiven sinner waiting for the relationship to be fully restored and for the peace of knowing that forgiveness is real.

## 4. Why Psalm 130 is especially relevant

Psalm 130 is not abstract mercy language. Its sequence is directly relevant to the categories now under debate:

1. guilt/sin;
2. confession and dependence on divine forgiveness;
3. fear/reverence flowing from forgiveness;
4. waiting in faith;
5. restoration/redemption.

According to the 2009 carrier, Lawson's commentary specifically connects the post-forgiveness waiting to restored relationship and peace.

That is materially closer to the 2026 phrase:

> “more retribution than restoration”

than merely proving Lawson had preached the doctrine of grace.

## 6. Important distinction: restoration to God/fellowship ≠ restoration to pastoral office

Psalm 130 restoration cannot be used as a proof-text for reinstating a disqualified pastor.

The categories must remain distinct:

- forgiveness before God;
- restoration of spiritual fellowship;
- restoration of interpersonal relationships where possible;
- restoration of trust;
- qualification for ecclesial office;
- return to a public teaching platform.

The current Lawson controversy repeatedly becomes confused when those are collapsed.

Even if Lawson's 2006 Psalm 130 commentary strongly teaches restored fellowship after forgiveness, that says nothing by itself about future qualification under 1 Timothy 3 / Titus 1.

### Publication guardrail

Do not use this finding to argue either:

- “Lawson's own old commentary proves he should be restored to ministry”; or
- “because he taught restoration, his current repentance is false.”

Neither follows.

This wording keeps the 2009 attribution transparent rather than laundering it into a primary Lawson quote.
