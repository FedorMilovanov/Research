## 4. Hard current-status rule

A current webpage that uses present-tense legacy copy is insufficient to establish a current ministry role.

For any claim that Lawson has returned to public teaching/pastoral ministry, require at least one of:

1. a dated 2025/2026 event or sermon object naming Lawson as current speaker;
2. current audio/video produced by the institution;
3. a current official announcement explicitly restoring/appointing Lawson;
4. a dated current schedule whose event actually occurred and is independently observable.

Without such an object, use only:

> **The old Trinity ministry page remains reachable and still contains legacy present-tense Lawson copy.**

Do not write:

> **Trinity currently has Lawson teaching The Bible Study.**

or:

> **Steadfast Hope is currently livestreaming Lawson four days a week.**

## 5. Why search-engine recency can mislead here

Search engines crawled these pages recently, which can make them look like current 2026 evidence.

But crawl freshness answers:

> `When did the crawler see the page?`

not:

> `When was the ministry claim on the page last substantively updated?`

That distinction is crucial in the Lawson corpus because multiple institutions retain historical pages/resources after changing his current status.

A `crawled today` label therefore must never be converted into `published/updated today` unless the page itself supplies reliable dated-update evidence.
