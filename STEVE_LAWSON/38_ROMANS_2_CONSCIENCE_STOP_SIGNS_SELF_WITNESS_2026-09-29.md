## 1. Why this source matters

In Lawson's teaching on Romans 2:12–16, he gave an unusually vivid explanation of how repeated sin affects the conscience. He compared conscience to:

- a smoke alarm;
- pain warning that something is broken;
- traffic stop signs;
- brakes on a car.

He warned that repeatedly overriding conscience can lead to a point where it becomes seared, moral restraint is lost, and the sinner continues down what he called a **“highway of sin.”**

That pre-scandal teaching is directly relevant to *Mercy in the Wilderness*, because current readers/reviewers report that Lawson now says he:

- ignored warning signs;
- turned a blind eye to Scripture;
- turned a deaf ear to conscience;
- presumed he would never commit the sin that eventually overtook him.

The value of the comparison is not that old teaching proves what Lawson was doing privately on the date of the Romans lesson. It is that he had publicly articulated, in precise moral categories, the process he later describes retrospectively in his own fall.

## 4. Lawson's conscience model

Lawson said every person has a conscience that signals when the line of God's law has been crossed.

He compared conscience to a smoke alarm that wakes someone when something is wrong and to bodily pain that alerts a person that something has been broken.

His central warning was that conscience can be progressively overridden.

The most distinctive wording is:

> “how many stop signs have you been running as the law of God is telling you, ‘Stop this, stop this, stop this.’”

He then warned that the conscience can become:

> “seared as with a hot iron”

until there are, in his analogy:

> “no brakes in the car”

and a person proceeds down a:

> “highway of sin.”

Later in the discussion he describes people as having:

> “worn the brakes off their car”

because they have been:

> “plowing through their conscience.”

### Core moral logic

Lawson's pre-scandal teaching is therefore clear:

1. conscience gives genuine warning;
2. repeated resistance weakens sensitivity;
3. continued violation can sear conscience;
4. seared conscience removes moral restraint;
5. rationalization follows repeated violation.

This is substantially the same causal mechanism that readers report he now applies to himself in the 2026 book.

## 5. Lawson also connected conscience to secrecy and hidden sin

The Romans material does not merely discuss public immorality. Lawson explicitly teaches that God will judge:

- secret thoughts;
- motives;
- hidden ambitions;
- lusts;
- the inner life known to God.

The Romans 2:16 conclusion emphasizes that God judges **“the secrets of men.”**

This matters because Lawson's later scandal involved a long disparity between public ministry and concealed conduct.

### Guardrail

The Romans exposition is a general exposition of Paul's doctrine, not a veiled autobiographical confession. It must never be represented as Lawson describing his own hidden relationship in 2017.

The proper use is comparative:

> he publicly taught the moral mechanics of hidden sin and violated conscience years before later describing those same dynamics in his own fall.
