**Snapshot:** 2026-09-29  
**Status:** `BOOK SELF-DIAGNOSIS CORROBORATED / PRE-FALL MERCY-PEACE-LOVE TEACHING ALSO STRONGLY PRESENT / HARD FALSE-TEACHER POLEMICS PRESENT / SOCIAL-MEDIA PROVOCATION ADMITTED BY LAWSON BUT HISTORICAL POST SET NOT YET RECONSTRUCTED`

## 1. Executive finding

One of the most important causal admissions attributed to Steven J. Lawson's 2026 book *Mercy in the Wilderness* is not merely generic pride or busyness. A reader who quotes the book at length reports Lawson's own heading/self-diagnosis as:

> **“I was more law than grace, more retribution than restoration.”**

The same reader transcribes Lawson as saying that when he posted something provocative, likes and reposts rose sharply, while pastoral/encouraging posts drew fewer responses; Lawson says the fall made him realize both how much grace he needed and how much grace he needed to extend to others.

Current public carrier:
- Goodreads, *Mercy in the Wilderness*, review by Blue Morse, Sept. 24, 2026:
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/258369757-mercy-in-the-wilderness

A separate full-book review by David Morrill independently describes Lawson as now saying he had become:

- combative;
- harsh;
- more interested in judgment than mercy.

Source:
- David Morrill, Protestia, Sept. 25, 2026:
  https://protestia.com/2026/09/25/mercy-in-the-wilderness-a-public-testimony-that-says-too-little/

### Core conclusion

This self-diagnosis is important, but the historical public corpus does **not** justify flattening Lawson's pre-fall teaching into the statement “he preached law but not grace.”

His own 2023–2024 teaching corpus includes strong and repeated public instruction on:

- mercy;
- peacemaking;
- reconciliation;
- meekness;
- mourning over sin;
- responding to hostility with grace and peace;
- leaving vengeance to God;
- truth joined to love.

At the same time, the same corpus contains forceful boundary/polemical material on:

- false teachers;
- rejecting false teachers;
- danger from divisive people inside the church;
- the damage caused by one person's sin;
- doctrinal vigilance.

Therefore the strongest research-grade formulation is:

> **Lawson's 2026 claim is best treated as a retrospective diagnosis of his own disposition, emphases, public persona, and practice — especially social-media reinforcement — rather than as a simple description of the doctrinal contents of everything he preached. His pre-fall teaching demonstrably contained both strong mercy/restoration themes and strong judgment/separation themes. The unresolved historical question is whether his actual public practice and platform incentives became disproportionately combative despite that balanced doctrinal vocabulary.**

## 9. The social-media admission is the genuinely new causal mechanism

The most analytically important part of the 2026 self-diagnosis is not the abstract law/grace phrase. It is the reported mechanism:

> provocative content received more likes/reposts; pastoral and encouraging material received less.

If the Goodreads transcription is accurate, Lawson is describing a **platform reinforcement loop**:

1. provocative or combative content;
2. larger visible engagement;
3. social reward for sharpness;
4. incentive to repeat the style;
5. gradual skewing of public posture.

This is materially different from saying:

> “Lawson believed false doctrine about grace.”

The stronger question is:

> **Did engagement metrics reward a harsher public persona than the mercy/reconciliation theology Lawson was simultaneously teaching?**

That is a serious and potentially instructive thesis.

### Current evidence limitation

The historical X/Twitter post corpus has **not yet been reconstructed sufficiently** to quantify:

- what posts Lawson himself would classify as “provocative”;
- their engagement counts;
- the comparison group of pastoral/encouraging posts;
- whether the pattern was stable across years;
- whether he intentionally optimized for outrage or simply noticed the effect retrospectively.

Therefore the book admission can be reported, but the platform-reward mechanism should not yet be independently presented as a measured empirical finding.

## 10. What the evidence DOES support

Strongly supported:

1. Lawson's book contains a retrospective self-critique centered on law/grace, retribution/restoration and combative public posture.
2. He reportedly links the problem to higher engagement for provocative posts.

5. Therefore the issue cannot responsibly be reduced to “he never preached grace.”

## 12. The sharper article argument

The most defensible argument is not:

> “Lawson was a preacher of law who knew nothing of grace.”

It is:

> **Lawson's own public record shows that he knew how to preach mercy, grace, reconciliation, meekness, peace, and love. He also preached strong biblical warnings against false teachers and divisive men. In his 2026 confession, he does not renounce those doctrines; he indicts the balance and posture of his own public life, saying he became more retributive than restorative and learned that provocative posts were rewarded more heavily than pastoral ones. If that self-assessment is accurate, the tragedy is not doctrinal ignorance but a widening gap between a theology of grace he could articulate and a public/private life he now says did not embody it adequately.**

This is stronger than the caricature because it rests on both halves of the source record.
