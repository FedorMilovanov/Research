## 1. Governing question

The corpus is not an attempt to collect scandal for its own sake and not an attempt to infer the eternal state of a man's soul from news coverage. It asks a narrower historical, pastoral, and theological question:

> What can be responsibly established about the gap between Steven Lawson's public teaching and his hidden conduct; how did those closest to his ministry interpret that gap; and how far do Lawson's later confession and book actually explain, rather than merely describe, the failure?

The resulting article may make a firm moral and ministerial argument. It may not convert uncertainties into facts.

## 2. Repository evidence policy

This authority inherits [`../data/repository-evidence-policy-v2.json`](../data/repository-evidence-policy-v2.json).

### Evidence classes used here

- `A1`: participant-created primary record.
- `A2`: official transcript, report, or primary institutional publication.
- `A3`: official event-specific institutional statement/decision.
- `B1`: high-quality secondary corroboration; not sole support for disputed quote-safe claims.
- `C`: context/discovery lead/unverified source; never quote-safe.
- `D`: excluded/unreliable/irrelevant.

### Access and locator discipline

A source may be authoritative in origin but incompletely acquired. Therefore the corpus records separately:

- origin/evidence class;
- current access state;
- locator state;
- whether a quotation is safe for public copy.

The most important example is Lawson's March 12, 2025 X statement: it is a participant-created `A1` object, but much of the durable evidence currently available to this corpus consists of full-text mirrors and screenshots in secondary publications. Its origin is primary; the current custody path is not yet ideal.

## 3. Privacy and proportionality

The woman involved is a private person for purposes of this corpus. Her name is excluded even where third-party material might permit identification.

The following are prohibited unless this authority is explicitly revised with a public-interest justification:

1. naming or doxxing the woman;
2. publishing private-address, family, employment, or contact details not necessary to the ministerial analysis;
3. speculating about her motives, conversion, mental health, or culpability beyond sourced public claims;
4. using photographs to identify her;
5. treating her as a narrative device in a story whose public subject is Lawson's ministerial conduct.

Age range, student status, and institutional connection may be discussed only because they bear on questions of power, accountability, and the accuracy of public explanations, and only with explicit attribution to the source that made the claim.

## 5. Primary interpretive propositions

### P1 — Sound doctrine is not sufficient qualification for the office

MacArthur's October 2024 comments are unusually important because he did not solve the contradiction by saying Lawson had always taught obvious doctrinal error. He explicitly acknowledged the apparent soundness of Lawson's theology while saying the behavior made his leadership intolerable and potentially corrupting.

This supports a precise thesis: **orthodoxy of propositions cannot compensate for disqualifying life.** This is not an anti-doctrinal point; it is a pastoral-qualification point grounded in 1 Timothy 3, Titus 1, James 3:1, and the New Testament's repeated union of teaching and example.

### P2 — MacArthur's "moral heretic" category is relevant but must be handled correctly

In an earlier official interview MacArthur said a true leader is judged by fidelity to the Word "both in what they say and how they live," followed by the formulation that there are two ways to be a heretic: doctrinally and morally.

That statement clarifies MacArthur's framework. It does **not** allow the article to write: "MacArthur called Lawson a moral heretic." The direct 2024 source stops short of that label.

The strongest accurate connection is:

> MacArthur's own prior category prevents us from treating sound doctrine as the whole test of a teacher; after Lawson's exposure, MacArthur then applied the life-side of that test with exceptional severity, even while acknowledging Lawson's sound theology.

### P3 — Lawson's own earlier teaching makes the case unusually sobering

The evidentiary value of Lawson's old messages is not that they magically prove hypocrisy from day one. Their value is that they show he possessed unusually explicit categories for the danger he later embodied publicly:

- the preacher's need for personal holiness;
- hypocrisy in teachers;
- a conscience rendered insensitive by persistent sin;
- the need to watch life as well as doctrine;
- the danger of judging others while failing to teach oneself.

The most striking currently verified bridge is the September 2023 Ligonier pre-conference session connected to 1 Timothy 4:2. Because the exact video transcript is not yet in custody, the article should avoid theatrical verbatim reconstruction and instead make the verified thematic point.

### P4 — Public confession is meaningful evidence, but self-report has limits

Lawson's March 2025 statement materially improved the public evidentiary picture. He stopped using merely the church's ambiguous phrase "inappropriate relationship" and called it a sinful relationship with a woman not his wife. He explicitly named deception of his wife, harm to his children, damage to Christ's name and ministries, and said he alone was responsible.

That is substantially more direct than silence or blame-shifting. It deserves to be represented fairly.

But the same source cannot independently establish that repentance is complete, that every harmed relationship is reconciled, or that all accountability questions are resolved. The correct verb is often **"said"**, not **"proved."**

### P5 — The 2026 book should be evaluated on specificity and public purpose, not on whether a fallen man is allowed to write anything

The relevant questions are:

1. Does the book supply concrete moral analysis rather than generic language about busyness, pride, temptation, and grace?
2. Does it reckon with the sustained nature of hidden conduct?
3. Does it explain how public ministry continued while private life deteriorated?
4. Does it address accountability failures, including what happened when his wife apparently sought help?
5. Does it keep the harmed parties from becoming props in Lawson's restoration narrative?
6. Does it distinguish forgiveness from return to authority?

A fair article should acknowledge genuine admissions reported in the book, especially the refused marriage counseling, while also identifying what remains missing.

## 6. Theological guardrails for the article

The article may argue firmly that:

- persistent secret sexual/marital infidelity disqualifies a pastor/elder;
- public giftedness is not evidence of private godliness;
- a teacher can say true things while failing morally;
- a seared conscience is a biblical category and a terrifying pastoral warning;
- public sin by a public teacher warrants public accountability proportionate to the public damage;
- forgiveness in Christ does not mechanically restore a man to church office;
- repentance produces fruit, but outsiders should be cautious about claiming omniscient access to the heart.

The article should not claim:

- certainty that Lawson was never converted;
- certainty that he is presently unrepentant;
- that every sermon he preached was therefore false;
- that doctrinal truth becomes false because the preacher failed to live it;
- that MacArthur explicitly called Lawson a false teacher in October 2024;
- that every disputed detail of the relationship is proven.
