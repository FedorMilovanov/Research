Date: 2026-09-29

Scope: Anne Lawson, marital reconciliation, what can and cannot be inferred from continuing separation, biblical tests of repentance, and Reformed Baptist confessional controls.

## 1. Bottom line

The strongest responsible argument is **not**:

> “If Steve Lawson were truly repentant, his godly wife would already have forgiven him and taken him back.”

That conclusion exceeds the evidence and confuses several distinct realities:

- forgiveness;
- restoration of trust;
- reconciliation as a relationship;
- cohabitation;
- continuation of marriage after adultery;
- church restoration;
- ministerial qualification.

An injured spouse can forgive in a Christian sense while remaining unable or unwilling to restore trust or resume ordinary married life. Lawson cannot unilaterally produce reconciliation; Anne's response is not under his control.

The stronger question is:

> **What concrete fruits of repentance are observable in the relationships most directly devastated by Lawson's sin, especially his marriage and family, and why does the current state of those relationships remain publicly unresolved while he has chosen to issue a public book explaining his fall and the mercy he says he found?**

That question is supported both by the public chronology and by biblical categories of repentance without claiming to read Lawson's heart or judge Anne.

## 8. Biblical control: repentance is more than emotion, but fruit is not self-salvation

### 8.1. 2 Corinthians 7:10–11

Paul distinguishes godly sorrow from worldly sorrow and then points to what godly sorrow **produced** in the Corinthians: earnestness/carefulness, eagerness to clear the matter, indignation, fear, longing, zeal, and readiness to address wrong.

The point for this case is not to force every word of 2 Cor. 7:11 into a modern scandal checklist. The passage's immediate context is the Corinthians' response to apostolic correction.

But it does establish an important principle:

> **biblical godly sorrow is productive; it issues in observable movement toward dealing with the sin rather than remaining only verbal regret.**

The 1689 Baptist Confession itself cites 2 Cor. 7:11 in its definition of saving repentance.

### 8.2. Luke 3:8 / Acts 26:20

The New Testament language of bearing fruit “in keeping with repentance” / performing deeds appropriate to repentance likewise prevents repentance from being reduced to rhetoric.

Fruit does not purchase forgiveness. It evidences turning.

### 8.3. Luke 19:8

Zacchaeus is not a universal restitution formula for every sin, but he is a useful biblical example of repentance moving outward toward concrete repair where repair is possible.

Significantly, the 1689 confession cites Luke 19:8 when saying that believers must repent of particular known sins **particularly**.

### 8.4. Matthew 5:23–24

Jesus' command to seek reconciliation with an offended brother reinforces the seriousness of addressing relational wrong rather than treating worship-language as a substitute for repair.

Guardrail: reconciliation requires another person. The offender has a duty to pursue peace/repair; he does not have the power to compel the injured party to restore the former relationship.

## 9. 1689 Second London Baptist Confession — exact confessional control

Current authoritative/standard reproductions:

- https://baptistconfession.org/
- https://rbs.org/2lbc
- https://1689confession.org/repentance-unto-life-and-salvation/

Chapter 15 contains **five paragraphs**, not six.

### 15.2

The confession explicitly allows that genuine believers may fall into “great sins and provocations” and be renewed through repentance unto salvation.

This is a vital anti-despair guardrail: Lawson's grievous sin does not itself prove that forgiveness or genuine repentance is impossible.

### 15.3

Saving repentance includes:

- Spirit-given perception of the manifold evils of one's sin;
- humility;
- godly sorrow;
- detestation of sin;
- self-abhorrence;
- prayer for pardon and strength;
- a purpose and **endeavor** to walk before God in what pleases Him.

Proof texts include Ezek. 36:31 and 2 Cor. 7:11.

The confessional category therefore supports testing repentance by direction and endeavor without pretending to infallibly inspect the heart.

### 15.4

It is every person's duty to repent of particular known sins **particularly**.

Proof texts include Luke 19:8 and 1 Tim. 1:13, 15.

Applied cautiously here: a generic narrative of overwork, lost devotional life, pride, or “wilderness” does not by itself exhaust repentance for concrete betrayal of wife, family, church, students, institutions, and people spiritually influenced during the double life.

### Important confessional correction

The Westminster Confession 15.6 expressly says a person who has scandalized a brother or church should be willing to declare repentance privately or publicly to those offended.

The **1689 Baptist Confession does not reproduce that Westminster paragraph**; its chapter 15 ends at §5.

Therefore the future article may cite Westminster 15.6 as a related Reformed formulation, but must **not** call it the wording of the 1689 Baptist Confession.

## 10. What continuing marital estrangement can legitimately mean

If continuing separation/estrangement is independently established, it is **relevant evidence**, but not a verdict by itself.

### It may be consistent with genuine repentance because:

- Anne may need prolonged physical/emotional safety and distance;
- trust after multi-year betrayal can take years and may never return fully;
- the injured spouse's choice is not controlled by the offender;
- many conservative Protestant readings recognize adultery as legitimate grounds on which the innocent spouse may divorce;
- repentance cannot demand reconciliation as proof from the victim.

### It becomes more concerning if evidence shows that Lawson:

- refused reasonable paths of repair after professing repentance;
- pressured Anne to validate his public restoration narrative;
- prioritized public rehabilitation over family repair;
- evaded concrete confession to people directly harmed;
- rejected pastoral/church requirements tied to family repair;
- portrayed an unresolved marriage as a completed redemption story.

Current corpus does **not** establish those propositions.

2. The sin continued until exposure rather than voluntary self-disclosure, according to the strongest public accounts.
3. By Dec. 2024 his own TBC elders still treated family repair as a critical missing fruit.
4. By Mar. 2025 Lawson himself still prayed for future marital restoration.
5. By Sep. 2026 the book has appeared as a public testimony of mercy/repentance, yet no primary public evidence establishes restored marriage.
6. The book, according to a reviewer who read it, gives substantially more narrative space to Lawson's losses, help, wilderness, and recovery than to the concrete state of the damaged relationships.
7. There is no public statement from Anne endorsing the book or its account.

None of these facts individually proves false repentance.

Together they justify a serious editorial question:

> **Has the public redemption narrative advanced farther than the publicly observable repair of the relationships the sin devastated?**

That is a strong question because it does not require mind-reading.

## 12. Why “his wife is godly, so she would forgive him” is not a safe argument

This line should be rejected from the article.

It risks placing the burden of proving Lawson's repentance on Anne's response to trauma.

A godly spouse may:

- forgive while requiring distance;
- forgive while refusing restored trust;
- forgive while declining cohabitation;
- forgive while pursuing a biblically permitted divorce after adultery;
- need years before deciding what reconciliation can mean.

Therefore Anne's continuing absence from a restoration narrative cannot be used to accuse **her** of hardness or to mathematically disprove **his** repentance.

The responsibility that can be evaluated is Lawson's own: confession, truthfulness, submission, restitution/repair insofar as possible, patience under consequences, and willingness to accept outcomes he cannot control.

## 13. Strongest article-ready argument

A careful formulation:

> The most revealing question is not whether Steve Lawson can say that God forgave him. The gospel answers that question for every truly repentant sinner. Nor is the question whether his wife was obligated to restore normal married life on his timetable. She was not. The harder question is what repentance has done to the concrete wreckage his sin created. Lawson now acknowledges that Anne had asked for marriage counseling before the scandal and that pride made him refuse. His own church later said that going back toward his wife and family mattered in demonstrating repentance. Six months after exposure he was still praying for future restoration of the marriage. Two years later he has published a book about mercy, yet the public record still does not establish that the marriage is restored. That does not prove his repentance false. It does mean the unresolved marriage cannot honestly be treated as a footnote while the story of his own “wilderness” becomes a public teaching vehicle.
