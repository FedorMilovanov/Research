## 2. What Jones actually adds in 2026

Jones's core argument is not that grace or forgiveness are impossible. It is that a long-running pattern of deception requires a long period of observable fruit.

He argues that:

- repentance should be accompanied by observable fruit;
- deeply embedded sinful patterns require time, discipline, accountability, and grace;
- healing of grievously damaged relationships also takes time;
- in his pastoral judgment, Lawson should remain silent and serve quietly in a local church rather than publish so soon;
- a decade of quiet service and reflection would make a later confession more persuasive than an early public book;
- Lawson's book does not convince Jones that the underlying delusion has been adequately confronted.

Jones explicitly says he read **“large portions”** of the book and adds that he rarely reads books in their entirety. This is therefore **not a full-book review authority** for page-by-page completeness.

### Publication rule

Jones may be used as a named example of a conservative Reformed pastor arguing that forgiveness and restoration to fellowship must be distinguished from rapid public re-entry or the credibility of a public testimony.

Do not present his judgment as an institutional verdict by PCA, Reformation21, Trinity, SVC, TMS, GCC, Ligonier, or OnePassion.

## 3. Continuity with Jones's Sept. 23, 2024 essay

Jones wrote about Lawson almost immediately after the scandal became public:

This continuity makes Jones useful for **reception history**, not for proving hidden facts about Lawson's private restoration process.

## 5. The critical phrase: “his former wife”

Jones writes, in the course of arguing that the book re-exposes injured people to public attention, that it brings back into public view:

> “the two women, his former wife and the young lady”

This is the first high-visibility current source located in this research pass that calls Anne Lawson **“his former wife.”**

That wording is potentially important. It is **not presently sufficient evidence that the Lawsons are divorced**.

Why not:

1. Jones provides no divorce record, family statement, church statement, interview, or source note for the phrase.
2. The essay does not say Jones has personal knowledge of the marriage's legal status.
3. Jones is not publicly established as part of Lawson's counseling/accountability structure.
4. A targeted current search did not locate a direct statement from Anne Lawson announcing divorce, separation terms, reconciliation, or the book.
5. A targeted current search did not locate a Trinity or Stephens Valley statement announcing a divorce.
6. Other current September 2026 writers still use language describing Lawson as **married**. For example, the Sept. 25 ResponsiveReiding review introduces the 2024 fall with “Lawson, married…”:
   - https://responsivereiding.com/2026/09/25/steve-lawsons-mercy-in-the-wilderness-some-thoughts/
