## 1. Executive finding

Christ Centered Reformed Ministries (CCRMin) currently promotes *Mercy in the Wilderness* with a first-person contextual note stating:

> “I have been informed by an elder involved in his restoration that he was encouraged by those walking closely with him in the church to write it.”

CCRMin continues:

> “Those personally involved in his restoration believed that putting these things into words was something he needed to do…”

Current source:
- https://www.ccrmin.com/

A targeted provenance pass now establishes that the relevant CCRMin publishing identity is **Justin Hoke**. His public LinkedIn profile links directly to `ccrmin.com`, and CCRMin articles are published under his name.

Sources:
- https://www.linkedin.com/in/justin-hoke-708431225
- https://www.ccrmin.com/christ-centeredness-and-the-covenant-of-grace-two-expressions-of-one-glorious-reality

This improves source classification: the claim is not anonymous website copy. It is a first-person report published within Hoke's ministry project.

However, the **elder himself remains unnamed**, and no public evidence currently identifies that elder as a Stephens Valley Church elder, Trinity Bible Church elder, OnePassion leader, or member of Lawson's accountability team.

## 2. What CCRMin actually claims — and what it does not

The exact public claim supports the proposition that:

1. Hoke says he received information from **an elder involved in Lawson's restoration**;
2. that elder reportedly said people walking closely with Lawson **in the church** encouraged Lawson to **write**;
3. those involved reportedly thought writing the material out was something Lawson needed to do.

The statement does **not** identify:

- the elder;
- the church;
- whether the elder personally encouraged Lawson to write;
- whether the elder read the manuscript;
- whether the elder encouraged **publication**;
- whether SVC corporately approved the book;
- whether Trinity approved it;
- whether OnePassion approved it;
- whether Lawson's two professional counselors approved it;
- whether the two unnamed pastors approved it;
- whether the accountability team approved it.

### Canonical rule

> **ENCOURAGED TO WRITE ≠ APPROVED TO PUBLISH.**

That distinction must survive every future use of the CCRMin claim.

### Correct source weighting

CCRMin/Hoke is potentially valuable for:

- direct-contact claims Hoke says he personally received;
- Hoke's own editorial judgments;
- evidence of a sympathetic Lawson-support network.

CCRMin/Hoke should be weighted more cautiously for:

- Lawson motive claims;
- evaluations of critics;
- whether the book is sufficiently repentant;
- whether publication was wise;
- whether criticism is fair.

A sympathetic source can report true first-hand information. Bias affects interpretive weight; it does not automatically nullify a direct-contact claim.

## 8. Why the CCRMin claim remains important despite the unresolved identity

The claim is still useful because it narrows one disputed question about the book's genesis.

It provides an attributed first-person report that at least some people involved in Lawson's restoration believed **writing** through the experience was beneficial/necessary.

That weakens overly categorical claims that Lawson necessarily conceived the act of writing entirely by himself with no restorative-circle encouragement.

But it does not answer the harder public-governance question:

> **Who decided that private/reflection writing should become a publicly sold book in September 2026?**

The current public record still does not identify the person or institution that accepted responsibility for that decision.
