## 1. Why this layer is needed

The September 2026 discussion around *Mercy in the Wilderness: How I Fell & What I Found* now contains four questions that are often being collapsed into one:

1. Is the book a real Lawson-authorized publication?
2. Did Lawson personally compose every sentence?
3. Was AI or a ghostwriter used in drafting/editing?
4. Who pastorally/editorially approved the decision to publish the book?

The public evidence does not give the same answer to all four.

The strongest current synthesis is:

> **The book is strongly supported as a genuine Lawson-authorized publication. The composition workflow — including possible AI assistance, ghostwriting, copyediting, or collaborative editing — remains publicly undocumented. No evidence located proves AI authorship. The pastoral/editorial approval chain remains opaque.**

That distinction should govern all future article wording.

## 6. Why stylistic AI detection is especially weak here

A text can look generic, repetitive, polished, formulaic or “AI-like” for many reasons, including:

- authorial style;
- heavy human editing;
- templated devotional prose;
- dictation/transcription cleanup;
- ghostwriting;
- AI assistance;
- ordinary copyediting;
- a mixture of several processes.

Without provenance evidence, stylistic resemblance cannot identify the production method with research-grade confidence.

That is particularly important because the article's stronger criticism does not depend on proving AI use. The documented governance question remains valid even if Lawson personally wrote every word.
