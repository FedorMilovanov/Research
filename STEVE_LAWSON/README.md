## Purpose

This directory consolidates the research on Steven J. Lawson's 2024 moral disqualification, the response of Trinity Bible Church of Dallas, OnePassion Ministries, The Master's Seminary / Grace Community Church figures, Lawson's March 2025 public confession, his earlier preaching on ministerial holiness/hypocrisy/conscience/self-deception, and the September 2026 publication of *Mercy in the Wilderness: How I Fell & What I Found*.

## Evidence semantics

This corpus follows the repository-wide policy in [`../data/repository-evidence-policy-v2.json`](../data/repository-evidence-policy-v2.json):

- `A1` — primary participant-created/legal/governmental record;
- `A2` — official transcript/report/primary institutional publication;
- `A3` — official event-specific institutional statement or decision;
- `B1` — high-quality secondary corroboration; never sole support for a disputed quote-safe claim;
- `C` — discovery/context/unverified; never quote-safe;
- `D` — excluded or unreliable.

A page being reachable is not the same thing as its content being verified. A surviving journalistic copy of a deleted social-media post is not silently upgraded into an official primary record. A public statement can be genuine without every claim inside it being independently verified.

## Interpretive boundary

The evidence supports a strong article about **moral hypocrisy, ministerial qualification, the inadequacy of doctrinal orthodoxy by itself, self-deception, false shepherds, the conscience, public repentance, consequences, and the difference between forgiveness and restoration to office**.

The evidence does **not** authorize us to pronounce infallibly on Lawson's regeneration, to diagnose a psychiatric condition, to identify the woman, or to convert reports and suspicions into facts.

The sharpest defensible formulation is therefore not "MacArthur proved Lawson was a false teacher." It is:

> MacArthur's own categories make the problem larger than merely asking whether Lawson's doctrine was orthodox. MacArthur explicitly insisted elsewhere that fidelity is measured in both teaching and life, and after Lawson's exposure he said sound theology had coexisted with conduct that disqualified him, could corrupt a church, and raised the frightening question of how a preacher could keep preaching past his conscience. Lawson's own earlier teaching likewise warned that church members and even shepherds can possess religious proximity and correct words without the spiritual reality they assume.
