# Steven J. Lawson — book approval and publishing-chain audit

**Snapshot:** 2026-09-29  
**Status:** `CONTROL AUDIT / NEW PRO-LAWSON INSIDER CLAIM ACQUIRED / PUBLICATION APPROVAL STILL UNVERIFIED`  
**Depends on:** `21_CONTROL_RESEARCH_2026-09-29.md`, `22_CLINT_ARCHER_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-09-29.md`, `23_NASHVILLE_ACCOUNTABILITY_CHAIN_AUDIT_2026-09-29.md`

## 1. Executive finding

A new public source materially changes one narrow part of the working hypothesis about *Mercy in the Wilderness*.

It is no longer responsible to imply that Steven Lawson necessarily decided entirely on his own, contrary to his recovery/accountability circle, to write the 2026 book. Christ Centered Reformed Ministries (CCRMin) currently carries an explicit first-person attribution saying its author was informed **by an elder involved in Lawson's restoration** that people walking closely with Lawson in the church **encouraged him to write** the book.

Source:  
https://www.ccrmin.com/

However, the source does **not** establish:

- the elder's identity;
- which church the elder belonged to;
- whether the elder had formal authority over Lawson;
- whether “encouraged to write” meant therapeutic/private writing or public publication;
- whether the manuscript was reviewed before release;
- whether timing was approved;
- whether the $14.99 commercial/POD format was approved;
- whether Anne/family approved or were consulted;
- whether Trinity Bible Church approved;
- whether Stephens Valley Church as a church approved;
- whether the full accountability team approved;
- whether OnePassion/TMS/GCC/Ligonier approved.

Therefore the correct evidence classification is:

> **ATTRIBUTED UNNAMED-ELDER CLAIM / PRO-LAWSON CARRIER / CORROBORATES ENCOURAGEMENT TO WRITE, NOT FORMAL APPROVAL TO PUBLISH.**

This is an important correction because it weakens the simplistic theory that the book itself proves unilateral rebellion against counsel. It does not resolve the more important governance question: **who authorized the transition from recovery writing to renewed public influence?**

## 2. Exact CCRMin claim

CCRMin's home page promotes *Mercy in the Wilderness* and adds a contextual note. In substance, the author says:

- Lawson did not undertake the book simply to thrust himself back into the public spotlight;
- the author was informed by **an elder involved in Lawson's restoration** that Lawson was encouraged by people walking closely with him in the church to write it;
- those involved believed putting the experience into words was something he needed to do.

The page does not name the elder or explain the chain of authority.

Source:  
https://www.ccrmin.com/

### Critical distinction

There are at least four different propositions, and the source supports only the first with an attributed insider claim:

1. **Someone in the restoration circle encouraged Lawson to write.** — claimed by CCRMin.
2. **That person encouraged Lawson to publish.** — not established.
3. **The responsible church/accountability authority approved publication now.** — not established.
4. **The responsible authority reviewed/approved the final text, theology, timing, pricing and publicity.** — not established.

The eventual article must not collapse these propositions.

## 4. The unanswered question is now narrower and stronger

Before acquiring CCRMin, the research question was:

> Did anyone in Lawson's accountability circle advise him to write/publish this book?

After acquiring CCRMin, the better questions are:

> **Who was the elder, and what exactly did he encourage?**

> **Did “write this down” become “publish a 124-page book for the public,” and who made that decision?**

> **Who reviewed the manuscript before release?**

> **Did the people charged with monitoring Lawson's decisions agree that public authorship was wise at this stage?**

These questions avoid motive-reading and focus on verifiable process.

Thus the evidence has moved from **complete silence** to **partial insider attribution**, not to transparent institutional authorization.

## 6. Publishing metadata: But God Press vs. Steven Lawson

ISBN: `9798996167302`.

Major international retail/catalog metadata commonly identifies:

- Publisher: **But God Press**;
- publication date around Aug. 20 / Sep. 1, 2026 depending carrier;
- paperback;
- approximately 122–124 pages.

Examples:

- Bookshop.org: `But God Press`, 124 pages;
- Booktopia: `But God Press`, Aug. 20, 2026, 124 pages;
- IBS / European sellers: `But God Press`;
- Korean YES24: same ISBN and physical POD-style metadata.

Yet AbeBooks' edition record for the same ISBN lists:

- **Publisher: Steven Lawson, 2026**.

Source:  
https://www.abebooks.com/9798996167302/Mercy-Wilderness-Fell-What-Found/plp

This is not enough to prove the legal proposition `But God Press = Steven Lawson personally`, because book-industry metadata can vary by distributor and reseller.

It does strongly support an **author-controlled/private-imprint/self-publishing structure** rather than a conventional independent Christian publisher with a visible editorial organization.

### What has not been found

Current searches have not located:

- a separate But God Press corporate website;
- a meaningful catalog of other authors/titles;
- named editors;
- an editorial board;
- submission policies;
- theological review process;
- an institutional relationship to SVC, Trinity, OnePassion, TMS or Ligonier.

### Guardrail

This evidence is relevant to **external editorial gatekeeping**.

It is not evidence that:

- Lawson published for greed;
- royalties are large;
- the book is fraudulent;
- Lawson deceived retailers;
- the book lacks all informal editorial review.

## 7. Why gatekeeping matters in this particular case

For an ordinary Christian memoir, a private imprint is not inherently troubling.

Here the context is different:

- Lawson's scandal involved a years-long gap between authoritative public teaching and private conduct;
- the book itself interprets that fall and its theological meaning;
- the book returns Lawson to public Christian influence as an author;
- it uses biblical interpretation, including the “wilderness” analogy criticized by multiple theologians;
- his March 2025 statement presented him as operating under a multi-layer accountability structure.

Therefore a reasonable research question is:

> **Was the book independently tested by the people who supposedly knew Lawson's condition best, or did an author-controlled publication pipeline allow Lawson himself to define the meaning of his restoration?**

The current evidence cannot answer that yet.

- Keep `write` / `publish` / `approve timing` / `review final manuscript` as four separate evidentiary questions.
