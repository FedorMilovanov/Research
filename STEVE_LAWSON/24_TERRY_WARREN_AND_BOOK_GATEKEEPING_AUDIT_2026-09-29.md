## 1. Executive finding

The Beverly & Terry Warren dedication in *Mercy in the Wilderness* can now be tied much more strongly to the Stephens Valley Church (SVC) elder network than the earlier `23_` audit allowed.

The strongest currently defensible classification is:

> **BEVERLY + TERRY WARREN IDENTITY LINK TO SVC ELDER NETWORK: STRONGLY IDENTIFIED / NOT FORMALLY CONFIRMED BY A DIRECT LAWSON OR WARREN STATEMENT.**

This is based not merely on a matching `Terry Warren` name. An official Stephens Valley Church dedication bulletin dated March 8, 2020 lists the exact couple **Terry and Beverly Warren** under the heading **`SVC Elders and Wives`**. The same document separately lists `Terry Warren - treasurer` on the SVC Building Committee. The current official SVC officers page also lists `Terry Warren` among **Reserve Elders**, distinct from `Elders Emeriti`.

The 2026 Lawson book is visually confirmed to be dedicated to **Beverly & Terry Warren**, described as an `oasis of mercy` in Lawson's wilderness.

The evidence therefore strongly indicates that the book's dedicatees are the same elder-couple from the congregation that became Lawson's Nashville pastoral environment.

This is important, but its implication must be kept narrow:

- it strongly connects Lawson's closest named book-support relationship to SVC's elder network;
- it does **not** prove that Terry Warren was one of the two professional counselors;
- it does **not** prove that Terry Warren sat on the separate accountability team;
- it does **not** prove that SVC as a church formally approved the book;
- it does **not** establish Anne Lawson's approval of the book.

## 5. Book gatekeeping: a stronger contradiction-in-process question

Clint Archer's Jan. 24, 2025 account described a planned public Lawson interview. According to Archer, the interview would be released only if there were agreement from:

1. Lawson's family;
2. his counselors;
3. the OnePassion board;
4. Trinity Bible Church elders.

Source:

https://thecripplegate.com/steve-lawson-interview/

Archer's narrative therefore presented **public communication itself** as something subjected to broad external review because an ill-timed statement could cause additional harm.

By September 2026 Lawson had published *Mercy in the Wilderness*, a full commercial book about his fall and post-fall spiritual experience.

Targeted searches through Sept. 29 have not located a public statement from any of the four 2025 gatekeeping groups saying that they:

- reviewed the manuscript;
- recommended publication;
- consented to publication;
- judged the timing appropriate;
- endorsed its theological framing;
- approved the book as Lawson's substitute for a separate public statement.

Nor has the search found a public statement from those groups opposing the book.

### The defensible research question

The issue is therefore not `why did they let him publish?` as though their approval were already known.

It is:

> **Was the 2025 gatekeeping arrangement still operative when the 2026 book was written and released? If yes, who approved the book and what changed in the pastoral judgment about public speech? If no, when and why did Lawson cease to be bound by that arrangement?**

That question is evidentiary, ecclesiological, and pastoral. It does not require imputing financial or comeback motives.

But the trajectory **did change materially**.

That makes the missing decision record important. A serious final article should ask what observable pastoral process explains the transition from:

`silence + canceled book contracts + possible secular employment`

to:

`commercial first-person confession book as the principal public statement`.

No public primary explanation of that transition has yet been located.

## 9. Marriage remains the most important human-fruit question — but Anne must not be blamed

Current primary evidence does not establish the present legal or relational status of the Lawson marriage.

What is established historically:

- the marriage was catastrophically injured by Lawson's conduct;
- Lawson moved away saying Anne needed space;
- he later visited Dallas seeking reconciliation;
- in March 2025 he publicly prayed for future `redemption and restoration in our marriage`, which shows he was not then claiming completed restoration;
- the 2026 book/reviews discuss attempts at family repair but do not furnish a current primary statement from Anne.

Secondary 2026 sources report continuing estrangement. Justin Peters also states that Lawson and Anne are in different states and not reconciled, but his video does not display a source for that claim.

### Guardrail

Do **not** reason:

`Anne is godly → she would forgive → therefore continued separation proves Lawson is unrepentant.`

That fails because:

- Christian forgiveness is not identical to restored trust;
- restored trust is not identical to cohabitation;
- reconciliation is bilateral;
- the injured spouse may have legitimate reasons to require long-term separation even if she forgives;
- her private judgment and pastoral counsel are not ours to invent.

The strong question is instead:

> **When Lawson now publishes a public testimony of repentance and mercy, what verifiable fruit exists in the principal relationship his sin devastated, and why is that part of the story still publicly unresolved?**

This question is fair precisely because it leaves Anne's conscience and choices alone.

## Bottom line

The strongest evidence-based criticism is no longer that Lawson had `no accountability`.

The stronger and more defensible criticism is that **a real but publicly fragmented accountability network surrounded him, while the identity, church jurisdiction, decision rights, and present authority of that network remain unclear — and a major public book appeared without any located public explanation of how the earlier multi-party gatekeeping process evaluated or authorized that decision.**

The Warren evidence now connects the book's closest named support couple directly and historically to the Stephens Valley elder network, making SVC even more central to the 2025-26 narrative. What it still does not tell us is who had final authority over Lawson, who judged the fruits of repentance, and who decided that September 2026 was the right time for a public book.
