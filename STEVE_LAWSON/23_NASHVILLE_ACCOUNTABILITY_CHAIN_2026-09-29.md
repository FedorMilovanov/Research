# Steven J. Lawson — Nashville accountability chain / jurisdiction audit

Date: 2026-09-29  
Status: CONTROL RESEARCH / MATERIAL GOVERNANCE TENSION ESTABLISHED / PRIMARY GAPS REMAIN  
Scope: Anne Lawson → Trinity Bible Church of Dallas → Nashville / Stephens Valley Church → professional counselors → OnePassion → TMS/GCC → 2026 book-publication approval chain.

## 1. Bottom line

The public record no longer supports a simple narrative in either direction.

It is **not established** that Lawson simply fled Dallas, selected friendly counselors, and evaded all church discipline. There is substantial evidence that he was counseled, attended Stephens Valley Church (SVC), met repeatedly with pastors/elders, and submitted to an accountability structure in Nashville.

But it is also **not established** that there was one coherent, transparent, single-chain ecclesial process supervised from beginning to end by Lawson's formal membership church, Trinity Bible Church of Dallas (TBC).

The strongest public evidence instead shows a **distributed and jurisdictionally opaque accountability architecture**:

- TBC was Lawson's formal membership church and claimed it had disciplined him by exposure/removal/termination of compensation;
- by Dec. 11, 2024, TBC elders publicly sounded skeptical that repentance had yet been demonstrated and said the next step had to involve wife/family;
- Lawson was physically in Nashville, attending SVC, where he was **not a member** as of Jan. 23, 2025;
- SVC pastor Jim Bachmann said SVC therefore had no disciplinary jurisdiction over him;
- Bachmann also said that, as of Jan. 23, **TBC had not contacted him or SVC** about coordinating Lawson's discipline/restoration;
- yet on Jan. 24 Clint Archer described another accountability chain in which men selected by Lawson's spiritual leaders were reportedly giving daily care and reporting to TBC elders and OnePassion;
- by March 2025 Lawson himself publicly described weekly accountability to two pastors, local-church elders, and an additional accountability team, and he identified Stephens Valley as a major pastoral refuge;
- no public primary document located so far identifies who possessed final ecclesial authority, whether membership was ever transferred to SVC, or how TBC, SVC, the professional counselors, OnePassion, and the unnamed accountability team formally coordinated.

This does **not prove bad faith**. It does establish that the public story is more fragmented than the reassuring shorthand “he was under church discipline/accountability” suggests.

## 3. Jan. 23, 2025 — Jim Bachmann / Stephens Valley Church

Source:

- https://roysreport.com/steve-lawson-attends-nashville-church-pastor-believes-he-can-be-restored/

This interview materially complicates the later Archer summary.

### 3.1. What Bachmann directly said about SVC's role

Roys Report identified Lawson as attending Stephens Valley Church in Nashville, where his brother was a member. A Dec. 19, 2024 SVC Advent Dinner photo showed Lawson with lead pastor Jim Bachmann.

Bachmann said Lawson:

- had been attending SVC for several months;
- was **not a member** of SVC;
- was not preaching, teaching, or serving in ministry there; he was essentially a quiet worshiper;
- was seeing **two professional counselors** in Nashville;
- was meeting SVC elders for friendship, support, advice, and prayer;
- had told Bachmann he moved to Nashville to give his wife space;
- had recently visited his wife in Dallas and hoped for reconciliation;
- accepted responsibility for his sin, in Bachmann's judgment.

### 3.2. The crucial jurisdiction statement

Bachmann explicitly said SVC could not discipline Lawson because Lawson was not a member there.

More importantly, Bachmann said that as of Jan. 23, 2025 **no one from Trinity Bible Church had contacted him or Stephens Valley Church** to involve them in discipline/restoration. He said SVC would be willing to coordinate with Trinity if Trinity wished.

This is one of the most important governance facts in the corpus.

It means we cannot casually describe SVC itself as an extension of a formally coordinated TBC discipline process at that date.

### 3.3. Bachmann's restoration posture

Bachmann also said he believed it was possible Lawson might one day “serve a local church in some capacity” and suggested a broken/repentant Lawson might in some sense be more effective than before.

Guardrail: this must **not** be rewritten as “Bachmann wanted Lawson restored to the pastorate.” The exact phrase was broader: “serve a local church in some capacity.” Clint Archer later emphasized that distinction.

Still, Bachmann's public posture was noticeably more restoration-optimistic than the Dec. 11 TBC snapshot.

## 4. Jan. 24, 2025 — Clint Archer's account and the structural tension

Primary article:

- https://thecripplegate.com/steve-lawson-interview/

Archer reported that:

- he spoke personally with Lawson and members of the OnePassion board;
- “all involved” agreed Nashville was the appropriate temporary arrangement;
- Lawson was to stay away until family/church judged return appropriate;
- several men experienced in counseling fallen pastors were in regular/daily contact with him;
- those men had been selected by Lawson's spiritual leaders and were reporting to his elders and OnePassion;
- Lawson remained in contact with Anne and had returned to Dallas to visit;
- Lawson and TBC elders agreed that attending TBC in person at that point would not be helpful;
- Lawson understood permanent disqualification from qualifying pastoral/elder ministry.

### 4.1. What cannot be assumed

Because Bachmann said only one day earlier that **TBC had not contacted SVC**, Archer's “several men” cannot automatically be identified with Bachmann/SVC elders.

At least five live explanations remain:

1. Archer was referring to a separate counseling/accountability group outside SVC.
2. The reporting chain ran through individual counselors rather than SVC institutionally.
3. Relevant coordination occurred after Bachmann's Jan. 23 interview.
4. Bachmann himself was not inside the TBC/OnePassion reporting chain even though he was providing pastoral care.
5. The public accounts are partially inconsistent because different insiders possessed different information or described the same process differently.

The corpus currently cannot choose among these as fact.

### 4.2. Research rule

Do not smooth this tension into “Trinity sent Lawson to Stephens Valley for discipline.” That has **not** been established.

Do not invert it into “Lawson escaped Trinity and invented a fake accountability process.” That has **not** been established either.

The supported formulation is:

> By January 2025 Lawson was receiving substantial pastoral and professional care in Nashville, but the publicly described lines of ecclesial jurisdiction and reporting were fragmented and not transparently unified.

## 13. Why the chain matters for evaluating repentance

A distributed care network can be legitimate. Professional counselors and trusted pastors can help enormously.

But in a Baptist ecclesiology, professional counseling does not itself answer the church-jurisdiction questions:

- Where was Lawson a member?
- Which elders possessed authority to shepherd/discipline him?
- Who judged credible repentance?
- Who could receive or exclude him from membership?
- Was membership transferred or released?
- Did the church he harmed remain substantively involved after the move?

Publicly, those questions are not yet cleanly answered.

This does not invalidate Lawson's repentance. It makes claims like “he fully submitted to church discipline” **too broad unless the specific structure is explained**.

## 14. Article-ready synthesis

A defensible formulation for the eventual article:

> Lawson's move to Nashville should not be caricatured as proof that he fled all accountability. By early 2025 he was demonstrably receiving professional counseling and repeated pastoral care at Stephens Valley Church. Yet the public record also shows a fractured jurisdictional picture: Trinity remained his formal membership church and claimed disciplinary responsibility, while Stephens Valley's pastor said Lawson was not a member there and that Trinity had not contacted SVC to coordinate discipline. A day later Clint Archer described a separate accountability network reporting to Trinity and OnePassion. By March Lawson himself spoke of two pastors, local elders, and an accountability team without identifying them. This may reflect a legitimate multi-layered care structure, but it is not transparent enough to let outsiders simply assume one coherent church-discipline chain.
