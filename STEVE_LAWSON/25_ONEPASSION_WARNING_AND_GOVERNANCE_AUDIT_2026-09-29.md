## 2. Phil Johnson's account of a GCC secretary concern

Main source:

- https://roysreport.com/steve-lawson-began-his-adulterous-affair-with-woman-when-she-was-a-student-at-the-masters-university-gcc-pastor-admits/

Additional carriers:

- https://www.crosswalk.com/headlines/contributors/milton-quintanilla/phil-johnson-responds-to-questions-on-steven-lawson-scandal-what-i-said-is-true.html

In an email later published by a correspondent and reproduced by these outlets, GCC elder/executive Phil Johnson said that after the scandal became public, a Grace Church secretary told him she had earlier observed behavior between Lawson and the woman that appeared “overly familiar.”

Johnson said the secretary told him she had taken her concern to an **employee of OnePassion Ministries**, not to a GCC elder.

Johnson's reported examples involved behavior such as the woman straightening Lawson's tie and combing his hair in a public setting.

Johnson therefore maintained that, as far as he could determine, no witness who had observed concerning behavior had reported it to GCC elders.

### Evidentiary limitation

This is Johnson reporting what the secretary told him after exposure. We do not currently possess:

- the secretary's original contemporaneous message;
- the recipient's response;
- a date;
- an internal OnePassion record.

Therefore it is a credible insider report, not a complete primary warning file.

## 3. OnePassion did not answer the journalistic verification request

The Roys Report asked OnePassion whether an employee had received such a concern and, if so, when and what was done.

According to the published article, OnePassion did not respond.

Silence is not proof of receipt or mishandling. But it means the central factual question was left publicly unresolved.

### Critical guardrail

Do **not** reason:

> “Only Steve and his daughter were listed employees, therefore the secretary must have told one of them.”

That inference is not valid. A ministry may have contractors, volunteers, later employees, persons not reportable in the same 990 category, or colloquially described “employees” not visible in the limited public snapshot.

Recipient identity remains **UNKNOWN**.

## 6. The FY2024 990 cannot be used as current 2026 leadership

The most recent public IRS-derived data for FY2024 still reports Lawson as president with compensation during that fiscal year.

This does not contradict the September 2024 board announcement that Lawson resigned all OnePassion duties. Form 990 reporting spans a fiscal year and may include a former officer's compensation/service during part of that year.

Therefore:

- do not cite the FY2024 990 to claim Lawson is currently president;
- do not use old officer titles to identify the post-resignation president;
- post-September-2024 leadership requires a separate current source.

## 8. Why the possible warning matters

If a concern was in fact reported within OnePassion before exposure, the central question is not whether a secretary's observation by itself proved adultery. It plainly did not.

The serious institutional questions would be:

1. What exactly was reported?
2. To whom?
3. Was it documented?
4. Did the recipient ask follow-up questions?
5. Was Lawson confronted?
6. Was the board or any elder informed?
7. Did the concern join any other warning signs?
8. Was the family-donor/personal-friend relationship already known?
9. Did OnePassion have any conflict-of-interest or whistleblower mechanism independent of Lawson?

These questions become more important because OnePassion was not merely an outside conference platform. It was Lawson's own ministry, with long-running family involvement and a board responsible for governance.
