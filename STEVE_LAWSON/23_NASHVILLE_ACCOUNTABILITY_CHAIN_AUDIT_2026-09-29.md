## 1. Executive finding

The public record does **not** support the simple claim that Steven Lawson merely fled Dallas, appointed friendly counselors, and escaped church discipline. Several sources say the initial move to Nashville was made to give Anne space, that Lawson stayed near his brother, and that other men were involved in counseling/accountability. That evidence must be retained.

However, the record also does **not** support the opposite, cleaner narrative that one clearly defined ecclesiastical authority designed, supervised, and completed a transparent discipline/restoration process from Trinity Bible Church of Dallas through Stephens Valley Church.

The strongest current conclusion is:

> **Lawson appears to have been under substantial pastoral/counseling contact in Nashville, but the public accountability architecture is jurisdictionally opaque. Trinity says it disciplined him; Stephens Valley initially said it could not discipline him because he was not its member and had received no request from Trinity to coordinate a discipline/restoration process; Clint Archer described unnamed counselors reporting to Trinity and OnePassion; Lawson later described two pastors, local elders, and a separate accountability team without naming them or identifying the final ecclesial authority.**

This is a genuine structural question, not merely hostile speculation.

## 2. Timeline: Dallas → Nashville

### September 2024 — exposure and removal

Trinity Bible Church of Dallas removed Lawson from all ministry activity after he disclosed an inappropriate/sinful relationship with a woman not his wife. OnePassion announced his resignation/removal from duties. The Master's Seminary removed him from faculty/leadership association; Austin Duncan publicly described the conduct as permanently disqualifying for pastoral ministry.

Canonical guardrail: Lawson was **Lead Preacher**, not an elder/pastor of Trinity. The Dec. 11, 2024 fellowship-meeting record nevertheless strongly establishes that Steve and Anne had completed Trinity's formal membership process.

### September 2024 — move to Nashville

Multiple later carriers say Lawson moved to Nashville shortly after the scandal. The commonly repeated explanation is that Anne needed space and that Lawson stayed near/with his brother, who attended Stephens Valley Church (SVC).

This makes the mere fact of leaving Dallas ambiguous. If Anne requested physical separation, moving away can initially be consistent with respecting the injured spouse rather than evading her.

What is *not* publicly documented is a contemporaneous primary instruction from Anne or Trinity setting out:

- who proposed the move;
- who formally approved it;
- its duration;
- conditions for returning to Dallas;
- which church retained membership jurisdiction;
- who could determine repentance/reconciliation;
- whether membership was ever transferred.

## 3. Trinity Bible Church: membership now strongly established

The former corpus treated membership as an unresolved conflict. That is no longer the best evidence classification.

The preserved reports of Trinity's Dec. 11, 2024 fellowship meeting state that Steve and Anne went through Trinity's formal membership process. This evidence is stronger than the earlier November dispute based on unnamed staff or G3's two unnamed Trinity sources.

Therefore the ecclesiological question becomes sharper, not weaker:

> If Lawson was a Trinity member when the scandal broke, what was the continuing relationship between Trinity's membership/discipline jurisdiction and the Nashville counseling structure after he moved out of state?

At the Dec. 11 meeting, Trinity elders reportedly said they had disciplined Lawson by exposing the sin to the church, immediately removing him from ministry, terminating financial compensation, and treating him as permanently disqualified from ministry. They also spoke skeptically at that historical moment about whether repentance had yet been demonstrated in relation to Anne/family.

This establishes Trinity's own claim to have acted disciplinarily. It does **not**, by itself, establish that every later step normally associated with church discipline, membership standing, restoration to fellowship, the Lord's Table, or excommunication was completed.

## 4. Stephens Valley Church in January 2025: pastoral care without membership jurisdiction

The Roys Report interviewed SVC senior pastor Jim Bachmann on Jan. 23, 2025.

Source:  
https://roysreport.com/steve-lawson-attends-nashville-church-pastor-believes-he-can-be-restored/

Bachmann said:

- Lawson had attended SVC for several months;
- Lawson's brother was a member there;
- **Lawson himself was not an SVC member** at that point;
- therefore SVC **could not discipline him**;
- Lawson was not teaching, preaching, or serving in ministry;
- he was seeing **two professional counselors**;
- he met regularly with elders for friendship, support, advice, and prayer;
- Bachmann believed Lawson was still a Trinity member;
- Bachmann did not regard Lawson as running away from discipline;
- most significantly, **no one from Trinity had contacted Bachmann or SVC to ask SVC to participate in Lawson's discipline or restoration**, although Bachmann said SVC would have been willing to coordinate.

This is one of the most important records in the corpus because it creates a direct tension with the impression of a single, coordinated Trinity→Nashville accountability system.

It does not prove there was no indirect communication through individual counselors. It does prove that, according to the senior pastor of the receiving congregation, **Trinity leadership had not directly engaged that congregation in the process as of Jan. 23, 2025**.

This account cannot simply be harmonized with Bachmann by assertion.

Possible reconciliations include:

1. **Counselors ≠ SVC elders.** Trinity may have communicated with a separate counseling team while having no direct contact with SVC as a church.
2. Archer may have had later/more complete information than Bachmann.
3. “Reporting to Trinity” may have been informal updates rather than Trinity exercising direct ecclesial supervision.
4. Different insiders may have understood the same ad hoc arrangement differently.

Current evidence cannot decide among these possibilities.

### Source-weight guardrail

Archer is valuable as an insider but is not independent. Lawson founded the Mobile church Archer now pastors; Archer had publicly described Lawson as a major mentor/titan and had close institutional/friendship proximity to OnePassion leadership. He also had some involvement in the early public-response communications. Therefore:

- do **not** use Archer's conclusion that Lawson was demonstrably repentant as though it were neutral verification.

## 6. Lawson's own March 12, 2025 statement: three accountability layers

Lawson's first public confession contains the clearest first-person description of his then-current structure.

Primary preserved statement:  
https://thechristianworldview.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Steven-Lawson-statement.pdf

Lawson said he had been in extensive counseling for five months and had submitted himself to:

1. **weekly accountability to two pastors**;
2. **the elders of a local congregation**, who shepherded his soul;
3. **an accountability team**, which monitored progress and counseled decisions.

He also said he was participating in prayer meetings, Sunday school, worship, weekly communion, and mid-week Bible study.

This statement is important both positively and negatively.

### Positive evidence

It is inconsistent with the crude claim that Lawson simply vanished with no accountability. He publicly claimed a multi-layer structure and active church participation.

### Unresolved structural problem

He did **not** identify:

- the two pastors;
- the members of the accountability team;
- whether the two pastors were also SVC elders;
- whether the professional counselors Bachmann mentioned were the same people;
- who appointed the accountability team;
- to whom that team reported;
- whether Trinity remained the church of membership;
- whether Trinity had delegated any authority;
- whether SVC had since received him into membership;
- who had authority to determine standing at the Lord's Table.

Thus the public record contains at least four categories that may or may not overlap:

- two professional counselors (Bachmann);
- a few experienced men selected by prior spiritual leaders (Archer);
- two pastors (Lawson);
- local elders + an accountability team (Lawson).

Calling this “accountability” is justified. Calling it a transparent, clearly governed **church-discipline process** is not yet justified.

## 8. Marriage: what the record does and does not show

### What Lawson himself said in March 2025

He said he had confessed to his wife and family, took sole responsibility, and asked prayer that God would bring “redemption and restoration in our marriage.”

That wording itself indicates that, as of March 12, 2025, he was **not publicly claiming that marital restoration had already occurred**.

### January evidence

Bachmann told TRR Lawson had moved to give his wife space and had visited her in Dallas in early January hoping for reconciliation. Archer likewise described visits/continued contact.

### September 2026

Justin Peters states in the Didaché video that Lawson still lives in a different state and that the couple has not reconciled. The video displays no source for this claim.

Recent reviewers similarly describe continuing estrangement, but the corpus still lacks a current primary statement from Anne, Lawson, a family representative, a church, or a verified legal record establishing present marital status.

### Correct inference

The absence of publicly demonstrated reconciliation after two years is a **legitimate question about unresolved consequences and observable fruit**, especially because marriage was among the relationships most directly harmed.

It is **not proof by itself that Lawson has not repented**. Reconciliation is bilateral. A wronged spouse may forgive and still require long separation, may not yet trust, or may decline resumed cohabitation. The public has no right to presume Anne's internal state or impose a timetable on her.

The strongest research question is therefore:

> **What concrete fruits of repentance are visible in the relationship against which Lawson most directly sinned, and why does the public narrative of “mercy in the wilderness” still lack a clear, primary account of marital restoration?**

That question can be asked without blaming Anne and without pretending to know Lawson's heart.

## 9. Counseling does not answer the central conscience problem by itself

A weak objection says: “Lawson already knows theology, so why would he need counseling?”

The stronger formulation is almost the reverse.

Lawson's old purity teaching proves that doctrinal ignorance was not the central problem. By 2019 he could articulate:

- radical mortification of lust;
- fear of God;
- the shame sexual sin brings to family;
- the certainty hidden sin will be exposed;
- the destruction sexual immorality brings to ministry/family;
- the distinction between forgiveness and forfeiting ministry.

Therefore competent counseling would not primarily exist to teach him that adultery is wrong. Its proper targets would include:

- self-deception;
- conscience and compartmentalization;
- patterns of lying/concealment;
- pride and celebrity identity;
- marriage/family repair where possible;
- the relationship between giftedness, power, access, and accountability;
- habits that allowed public orthodoxy to coexist with private contradiction.

This makes the **identity and authority of the counselors** more important, not less.

January 2025: Bachmann explicitly said Lawson was **not a member**.

March 2025: Lawson said “elders of a local congregation” shepherded him and that he took communion weekly.

Current search through Sept. 29, 2026 has not located a first-party SVC statement saying:

- membership was transferred from Trinity;
- SVC formally assumed discipline jurisdiction;
- Lawson was restored to ordinary membership after a defined process.

This matters ecclesiologically. Pastoral care, counseling, attendance, and communion can be substantial, but they are not automatically identical to the church's formal membership/discipline authority.

## 12. Who approved the 2026 book? Public record is silent

This has become one of the most important unanswered questions.

In January 2025 Archer described a planned public interview whose release would depend on agreement from:

- Lawson's family;
- counselors;
- OnePassion board;
- Trinity elders.

Archer's account portrayed public communication as something being tightly restrained to avoid further harm.

By September 2026 Lawson had instead released a 122-page commercial book about the fall.

Current searches have found **no public statement** from:

- Anne Lawson;
- Trinity Bible Church elders;
- Stephens Valley Church / Jim Bachmann;
- the identified Nashville accountability team;
- OnePassion board;
- The Master's Seminary;
- Grace Community Church;
- Ligonier;

saying that they reviewed, advised, endorsed, or approved publication of *Mercy in the Wilderness*.

Likewise, no public source currently establishes that they opposed it.

Therefore the defensible question is:

> **Did the same accountability structure that was said to govern Lawson's public decisions approve this book? If so, who approved it and why was publication now judged wise? If not, what does that say about the practical authority of the accountability structure?**

This is stronger than speculating that the book is a “money grab.”

## 13. Publishing structure adds to the transparency question

The book lists **But God Press** as publisher. Current searches do not reveal a normal established publisher footprint for that imprint.

Major retail metadata confirms the imprint, while at least one bookseller metadata record has listed Steven Lawson himself in the publisher field. This supports, but does not conclusively prove, a self-publishing/private-imprint structure.

The cover design is credited to Caleb Faires, a real Middle Tennessee designer/artist.

No current evidence ties But God Press institutionally to Stephens Valley Church, OnePassion, or Trinity. The fact that SVC happens to feature a sermon titled “But God” is not evidence of a publisher connection and should not be used.

### Guardrail

Private/self-publication can explain higher author control and less institutional gatekeeping. It does **not** establish financial motive, profit level, or deceptive intent.

## 17. The strongest argument now

The strongest case is **not**:

> “He moved away, therefore he was running from repentance.”

That is too strong and contradicted by evidence that his wife wanted space and that real counseling occurred.

The stronger case is:

> **Lawson's post-scandal life contains real evidence of counseling, pastoral contact, confession, and church attendance. But the public record never presents a clear ecclesiological chain from the church in which he was a member and sinned publicly to the Nashville structure that later evaluated his repentance. The receiving church initially said it lacked membership jurisdiction and had no direct Trinity coordination. Lawson later described multiple unnamed accountability layers. Two years later, he re-entered public influence through a self/private-imprint book, while no church, family representative, accountability team, or former institution has publicly said it approved that step. That opacity does not prove false repentance; it does make claims of mature, tested repentance harder for outsiders to verify.**

This formulation is stronger because it does not need motive-reading.

2. If Trinity retained it, **who exercised the church's authority after he moved 650+ miles away?**
3. If SVC later assumed it, **when did he become a member and when was jurisdiction transferred?**

6. How did Trinity's Dec. 2024 statement that it had disciplined him relate to Bachmann's Jan. 2025 statement that Lawson had not gone through a discipline process?

8. What measurable relational repair occurred with Anne and the family, insofar as they wish any of it to be public?
9. Did Lawson personally seek reconciliation/restitution with TMS, GCC, Ligonier, OnePassion staff, students, and churches/conferences that lent him public trust?
10. **Who advised or approved publication of the 2026 book?**
11. If nobody in the accountability structure approved it, how binding was that accountability?
12. If they did approve it, what changed from the 2025 judgment that even a short public interview should wait for broad agreement?
13. Is the “wilderness” narrative primarily a confession of consequences, or does it subtly construct a new platform from the experience of those consequences?

- Do **not** say Lawson “ran away from discipline” as established fact.
- Do **not** say Nashville counseling was fake; substantial counseling/pastoral involvement is well supported.
- Do **not** say SVC restored him to membership unless acquired.

## 21. Bottom line for the next article draft

The case is becoming more precise.

The strongest criticism is no longer the sensational version that Lawson disappeared, did nothing, and now came back for money. That version is too crude and is contradicted by real evidence of counseling, pastoral care, and public confession.

The deeper problem is this:

**A man who taught church accountability, conscience, mortification, and permanent ministerial consequences lived for years against those teachings. When exposed, he entered a complex Nashville recovery structure whose counseling appears real but whose church jurisdiction, decision rights, and membership status remain publicly unclear. He now tells the church what he learned from that process through a commercial book, while the people/institutions said to be overseeing him have not publicly identified themselves as having tested or approved that new public teaching act.**

That is a stronger, fairer, and harder-to-dismiss argument than speculation about motives.
