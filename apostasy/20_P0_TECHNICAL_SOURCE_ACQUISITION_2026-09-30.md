**Claim:** Bock treats second/third soils as not genuinely saved; temporary belief is evaluated in light of its non-persevering end.  
**Current access:** `LOCATOR_LEAD / SECONDARY QUOTATION`, not direct edition object.  
**Discovery witnesses:** Bible.org reproduces the citation with pp.148–149; Precept Austin independently identifies the same page range and interpretation.

### L8-T02 — Robert H. Stein, *Luke* (NAC)

**Locator lead:** p. 246.  
**Claim:** the problem is not that the second-soil hearers have no phenomenon called faith, but that the faith lasts only “for a while” and is not saving faith in the canonical sense.  
**Current access:** `LOCATOR_LEAD / SECONDARY QUOTATION` through direct Free Grace interaction.  
**Publication status:** `NOT QUOTE_SAFE` until original NAC page acquired.

Research value: Stein is particularly useful because his formulation does **not** require saying `πιστεύουσιν = they did not believe`; he distinguishes temporary belief from saving faith by its outcome.

## 2. Hebrews 10:29 — referent and sanctification

### H10-T01 — recent open scholarly article on priestly Christology and sanctification

**Source:** “Priestly Christology and Holiness: Christ’s Priesthood and the Sanctification of Believers,” *Religions* 17.1 (2026), article 71.  
**Access:** `FULL_OBJECT_VERIFIED` through publisher/open PDF.  
**Role:** strong recent scholarly representative of the reading that 10:29 describes the apostate/believer as genuinely sanctified by covenant blood and treats apostasy as interrupting that sanctification.  
**Value:** provides a technically serious **opposing reading** that the Reformed article must answer, not caricature.  
**Publication status:** `B1 / NOT YET QUOTE_SAFE` until exact PDF page/paragraph locator is recorded in the final source registry.

Important: this source should not be treated as neutral consensus. It is valuable precisely because it develops one strong interpretation from Hebrews’ own sanctification/new-covenant language.

### H10-T02 — Calvin, Commentary on Hebrews 10:28–31

**Direct object:** CCEL section `calcom44.xvi.vi`.  
**Access:** `FULL_OBJECT_VERIFIED`.  
**Exact structural locator:** Hebrews 10:28–31, comment on v.29 / “blood of the covenant.”  
**Observed reading:** Calvin speaks of the blood by which “we are sanctified,” connects sanctification with expiation/sprinkling and ancient covenant imagery, and treats the passage as apostasy from professed faith.  
**Critical caution:** the English editor’s footnote adds its own interpretive gloss; final article must distinguish **Calvin’s words** from **editorial annotation**.

This is important because “classical Reformed” is not one uniform solution where every author makes Christ the referent.

### H10-T03 — Owen, *The Death of Death*, treatment of Hebrews 10:29

**Direct object:** CCEL `deathofdeath.i.x.v`.  
**Access:** `FULL_OBJECT_VERIFIED`.  
**Role:** exact primary-source locus where Owen argues that the verse does not require truly justified/regenerate apostates and develops external/consecratory sanctification possibilities.  
**Publication status:** strong primary candidate; exact edition/page lock still needed if a printed-page citation is required.

### H10-T04 — Orthodox Presbyterian Church Q&A

**Direct object:** OPC Q&A, 2005-03-06, “Hebrews 10:29 and the Blood of the Covenant.”  
**Access:** `FULL_OBJECT_VERIFIED / exact dated page`.  
**Role:** useful confessional-exegetical witness that **grammar can accommodate either apostate or Christ as referent**, while the answer itself prefers apostate/covenant-community reading.  
**Policy:** institutional theological answer, not a substitute for technical syntax commentary.

### H10-T05 — Society of Evangelical Arminians direct counter-argument

**Access:** full online article.  
**Role:** direct Arminian critique of Christ-referent, especially using Heb 13:12 and the dominant pattern of Christ as sanctifier / people as sanctified.  
**Policy:** direct school-representative source, not neutral B1 scholarship.  
**Use:** strongest-alternative section only.

### Hebrews 10 remediation judgment

The corpus can now sharpen the model matrix:

1. **apostate genuinely sanctified** — represented by recent scholarly argument + Arminian school argument;
2. **apostate covenantally/consecratorily sanctified** — represented in Reformed tradition;
3. **Christ-referent** — historical Owen/Gill option;
4. **grammar itself is not decisive** — supported by OPC institutional analysis, but still requires a technical modern grammar/commentary witness.

Remaining P0 is therefore **not source diversity**. It is technical weighting: which referent is syntactically/contextually most probable in major modern Hebrews scholarship, and how `ἁγιάζω` behaves across 2:11; 9:13; 10:10,14,29; 13:12.

## 3. 2 Peter 2:1 — bought / Master

### P2-T01 — Thomas R. Schreiner, *1, 2 Peter, Jude* (NAC)

**Locator lead:** pp. 330–332.  
**Current access:** exact-page citation is available through a secondary quotation of the commentary; original edition not yet acquired in this pass.  
**Observed position:** Schreiner takes Christ as likely referent, recognizes `ἀγοράζω` as redemption-language, but finally uses phenomenological/professional language to deny that the false teachers were truly redeemed.  
**Publication status:** `LOCATOR LEAD / NOT QUOTE_SAFE` until original pages are read.

This is a particularly important Reformed source because it **does not** use the simpler Deut 32 “non-redemptive purchase” solution; it preserves the redemptive force more strongly and solves the problem at the level of phenomenological description.

### P2-T02 — Schreiner, “Problematic Texts for Definite Atonement”

**Access:** full-text public mirror/reprint.  
**Role:** extended direct argument for phenomenological language in 2 Pet 2:1 and 2:20: false teachers appeared to be bought/know Christ, while final dog/sow outcome discloses their true nature.  
**Policy:** provenance/version must be locked before quote-safe use; useful now as argument map.

### P2-T03 — non-redemptive / sovereignty reading

**Representative lead:** detailed Reformed argument emphasizing `δεσπότης`, absence of an explicit purchase price in the immediate context, and possible Deut 32:5–6 background.  
**Current access:** direct apologetic article and older theological discussions; **not enough yet** for technical publication ownership.  
**Remediation need:** acquire a serious commentary/monograph defending the Deuteronomic/covenantal-purchase reading, not just apologetics.

### P2-T04 — older grammatical/exegetical tradition

Public-domain technical commentaries (including the older Expositor’s Greek Testament tradition) explicitly take `δεσπότης` as Christ and `ἀγοράζω` as purchase by Christ’s blood.  
**Use:** historical reception / lexical pressure, not final modern adjudication.

### P2-T05 — 2 Peter 2:20 `ἐπίγνωσις` / subject chain

Older technical commentary material makes two useful points requiring modern verification:

- `ἐπίγνωσις` is treated as strong/full knowledge and is compared with 1:2,3,8;
- the repetition of “overcome” across vv.19–20 is used to argue that v.20 continues describing the false teachers themselves rather than abruptly switching to their victims.

This directly matters because some Free Grace readings shift the referent in vv.18–22 to deceived believers. The final article must therefore include a **pronoun/participant-chain table** rather than resolving the issue by theology alone.
