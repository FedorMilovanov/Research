# X. Evidence gaps surfaced by canon-wide audit

1. **Genesis 3 / Adam-Eve final-state question:** avoid claiming explicit narrated conversion. Need patristic/Reformed history only as reception, not as replacement for textual silence.
2. **Saul:** need high-quality Samuel commentary distinguishing royal Spirit/anointing from regeneration claims.
3. **Solomon:** need Kings + Chronicles + Ecclesiastes reception audit; do not claim final repentance from Ecclesiastes without authorship/autobiography argument.
4. **Joash:** need technical Chronicles commentary on role of Jehoiada and borrowed/mediated fidelity.
5. **2 Kings 17 syncretism:** add exact modern commentary on vv. 33, 41.
6. **Jer 3 / Hos 14 lexical return:** HALOT/BDB and LXX exact-locator pass.
7. **Ezek 18/33:** retain dedicated hard-text treatment; life/death and righteousness language must not be reduced to NT justification categories.
8. **Gal 5:4:** currently underrepresented relative to Hebrews; needs standalone lexical/context audit.
9. **2 Thess 2 `apostasia`:** needs eschatological meaning/history-of-interpretation file.
10. **Rev 22:19 textual/translation issue:** do not use as simple “saved person erased from book of life” proof without text-critical pass.

# XI. High-confidence source-backed conclusions from this supplement

1. Judges presents apostasy as a **repeating and worsening corporate pattern**, not isolated anecdotes.
2. Jeremiah and Hosea confirm that covenant apostasy is relational treachery, but prophetic language repeatedly addresses the unfaithful with a real summons to return.
3. Chronicles is indispensable for differentiating **late decline**, **discipline**, and **late repentance**.
4. Hebrews 10 is deeply Deuteronomic: its apostasy warning deliberately invokes Israel’s covenant legislation and Song of Moses.
5. Hebrews 12’s «root» should be read against Deut 29’s hidden covenant defector/idolater, not reduced to the modern sense «a bitter person».
6. Revelation 2–3 gives mature NT corporate theology: a church may combine genuine strengths with lethal compromise and is commanded to repent/hold fast/conquer.
7. Warnings and preservation should not be set against each other a priori; at least one major Reformed model explicitly treats warnings as divine means, while McKnight provides a serious genuine-believer-apostasy alternative that must be represented fairly.
