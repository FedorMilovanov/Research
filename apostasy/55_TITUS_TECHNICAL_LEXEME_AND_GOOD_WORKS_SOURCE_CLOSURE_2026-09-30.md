# 2. Titus 3:10 — `αἱρετικός`

Mounce Greek Dictionary:

- lemma: `αἱρετικός, ή, όν`;
- NT frequency: one occurrence;
- gloss: **divisive**;
- definition: **one who creates, or fosters factions**;
- Titus 3:10 translated in MOUNCE as `the factious person`.

Source:
https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/hairetikos

Reverse interlinear:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Titus+3%3A10-12&version=MOUNCE

## Exegetical result

The immediate lexical evidence strongly supports:

> **factious / divisive person**

rather than importing without explanation the later technical ecclesiastical sense of English/Russian “heretic.”

This does **not** mean doctrine is irrelevant:

- Titus 1 already deals with false teachers;
- Titus 3:9 mentions law controversies;
- a divisive person may be doctrinally corrupt.

But the word itself foregrounds **faction-producing division**.

### Publication-safe wording

> «В Тит. 3:10 Павел употребляет редкое `αἱρετικός`, которое здесь лучше передавать как “разделяющий / создающий фракцию человек”. Позднейшее техническое слово “еретик” исторически родственно, но не должно автоматически вкладываться в единственное новозаветное употребление всего своего позднего догматического объёма.»

Status: **GREEN**.

# 3. Titus 3:11 — `ἐξέστραπται`

Mounce Greek Dictionary for `ἐκστρέφω`:

- principal perfect passive form is represented by `ἐξέστραμμαι`;
- NT occurrence: Titus 3:11 only;
- gloss: **(pass.) to be warped, perverted**;
- semantic description: to turn out / turn inside out; hence to change entirely; in NT passive, be perverted.

Source:
https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/ekstrepho

MOUNCE Titus 3:11:

> `has been warped`

and identifies `ἐξέστραπται` as perfect passive indicative 3sg.

## What the perfect contributes

The perfect presents a **resultant established condition**: the person is not merely having one momentary bad impulse; persistent divisiveness after admonition has exposed a warped/turned condition.

## What the perfect does not prove

Verbal aspect does not by itself prove:

- metaphysical impossibility of later repentance;
- a hidden decree of final reprobation;
- omniscient certainty about the person’s eternal destiny.

Paul tells Titus what judgment to make for **present ecclesial handling** after repeated correction.

### Cross-control

2 Tim. 2:24–26 still commands gentle correction of opponents in hope that God may grant repentance. Therefore:

> **an established sinful condition can justify a real ecclesial boundary without granting pastors omniscience over the person’s future.**

Status: **GREEN lexical/aspect; eternal-state inference prohibited**.

# 4. Titus 3:11 — `αὐτοκατάκριτος`

Mounce Greek Dictionary:

- lemma: `αὐτοκατάκριτος`;
- NT frequency: one;
- gloss/definition: **self-condemned**;
- Titus 3:11 only.

Source:
https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/autokatakritos

## Interpretive precision

“Self-condemned” need not mean the person inwardly admits:

> «I know the church is right and I know I am condemned.»

The context supports an objective sense:

- he has received admonition;
- receives a second admonition;
- continues in the divisive sinful pattern;
- his persistence supplies condemning evidence against his own profession/position.

Thus self-condemnation is **behaviorally manifested after correction**, not necessarily an audible confession of guilt.

### Pastoral importance

The church does not set a boundary because leaders have read secret motives infallibly.

It acts on:

- observable teaching/conduct;
- clear admonition;
- persistent response.

Status: **GREEN lexical; interpretive wording remains context-controlled**.

# 5. Titus 1:16 — profession and deeds

MOUNCE translation:

> `They profess to know God, but by their deeds they deny him... worthless for any good work.`

Source:
https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Titus%201%3A16

Bill Mounce’s Pastoral Epistles teaching makes the letter-level point explicit:

- the Pastorals closely join false teaching with corrupt behavior;
- Titus 1:16 is a primary example;
- the teachers’ works expose the falsity of their claim to know God.

Source:
https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/academy/nt120-survey-of-the-new-testament/nt120-25-pastoral-epistles

## Result

This confirms that the project’s phrase **practical denial** is not merely a Puritan construction imposed on Titus.

Paul himself establishes the contrast:

`profession to know God ↔ deeds that deny God`.

### Guardrail

Mounce’s statement that in this situation their behavior shows they are not Christians is a theological inference from the total profile, not a dictionary definition of `ἀρνέομαι`.

The project can adopt the conclusion within its Reformed synthesis while still distinguishing:

- settled false-teacher pattern;
- temporary grievous falls of true believers.

Status: **GREEN**.

# 6. Whole-Pastorals “good works” network

Bill Mounce’s 2024 technical/translation discussion notes the repeated Pastoral Epistles formula `ἔργον ἀγαθόν`:

- 1 Tim. 2:10 — godly women and good works/deeds;
- 1 Tim. 5:10 — widow known for good works;
- 2 Tim. 2:21 — cleansed vessel prepared for every good work;
- 2 Tim. 3:17 — Scripture equips for every good work;
- Titus 1:16 — false teachers unfit for every good work;
- Titus 3:1 — believers ready for every good work.

Source:
https://www.billmounce.com/blog/mwm/paul-good-deed-doer

Titus itself adds:

- 2:14 — Christ purifies a people zealous for good works;
- 3:8 — believers devote themselves to good works;
- 3:14 — learn to devote themselves to good works.

## Theological result

The contrast in 1:16 is deliberately integrated into the ethical architecture of the letter:

> false teachers: profession contradicted by works, unfit for good work;
>
> Christ’s people: redeemed and purified to become zealous for good works.

Yet Titus 3:5 explicitly prevents works-righteousness:

> salvation is not because of righteous works done by us, but according to God’s mercy.

Therefore the letter’s order is:

`mercy/saving grace → purification/training → good works as fruit/evidence`

not:

`good works → meritorious cause of salvation`.

Status: **GREEN canonical/letter-level structure**.

# 7. Charnock — practical atheism after exegesis

Stephen Charnock’s use of Titus 1:16 in *A Discourse on Practical Atheism* remains a high-value Puritan pastoral-theological control.

His practical logic:

- the mouth may acknowledge God;
- conduct can deny His authority;
- life may function as though the confessed God were morally irrelevant.

Primary-access source:
https://www.monergism.com/discourse-practical-atheism

### Correct order of use

1. Paul establishes profession/deeds contradiction.
2. Modern Greek/exegetical sources establish syntax/lexical force.
3. Charnock develops the spiritual anatomy and pastoral implications.

Not the reverse.

Status: **GREEN as practical theology; exact quotation locator required if verbatim quotation is used**.

# 8. Owen — warning before boundary

Owen’s *Eshcol* remains especially useful for Titus 3:9–11 because he does not make separation the first pastoral move.

His practical pattern is consistent with Paul:

`dangerous contention → attempts at conviction/admonition → obstinacy → separation/boundary`.

Primary-access source:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/eshcol.i.vi.x.html

This is exactly the kind of Puritan practical theology the project should retain:

- serious about error;
- serious about restoration;
- serious about limits;
- not impulsively condemnatory.

Status: **GREEN pastoral witness**.

# 11. Publication wording upgrade

> **Тит. 1:16 описывает не единичную непоследовательность верующего, а устойчивое практическое отрицание: люди исповедуют знание Бога, но их характерные дела отрицают исповедуемого Господа. В Тит. 3 Павел столь же далёк и от бесконечной терпимости, и от мгновенного клеймения. `αἱρετικός` в единственном новозаветном употреблении обозначает человека, создающего разделение; ему даётся первое и второе увещание, и только после упорного продолжения Титу велено установить границу. `ἐξέστραπται` показывает уже сложившееся извращённое состояние, а `αὐτοκατάκριτος` — что его собственное поведение после ясных предупреждений становится свидетельством против него. Но текст не даёт пастырю всеведения: церковная обязанность прекратить бесплодное общение с упорным разделителем не равна декларации, будто Бог никогда впоследствии не сможет дать ему покаяние.**

**Publication status remains `HOLD` only for exact modern print locators, not because the lexical core remains unresolved.**
