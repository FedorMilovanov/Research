## 1. Главный вывод

Лк. 11:24–26 / Мф. 12:43–45 действительно описывают реальное освобождение от нечистого духа, последующее возвращение демона и состояние **хуже первого**.

Но текст **не говорит**:

1. что этот человек был возрождён;
2. что в «дом» вошёл Святой Дух;
3. что Святой Дух затем покинул человека;
4. что оправдание было получено и потеряно;
5. что речь является прямым описанием потери спасения истинным верующим.

Наиболее надёжная синоптическая логика:

> **избавление / внешнее очищение без положительной принадлежности Христу оставляет человека или общину духовно пустыми; нейтральности нет, поэтому последующий возврат зла может сделать итог хуже первоначального состояния.**

Матфей сам прямо применяет образ:

> `οὕτως ἔσται καὶ τῇ γενεᾷ ταύτῃ τῇ πονηρᾷ`
>
> «так будет и с этим злым поколением».

Следовательно, ближайший Matthean referent — не абстрактный regenerate believer, а **поколение, видящее мессианское действие Иисуса и всё же отвергающее Его**.

# 2. Матфея 12:43–45 — exact Greek

## 2.1. Spirit leaves

`Ὅταν δὲ τὸ ἀκάθαρτον πνεῦμα ἐξέλθῃ ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου...`

- `τὸ ἀκάθαρτον πνεῦμα` — нечистый дух;
- `ἐξέλθῃ` — выйдет;
- `ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου` — из человека.

Text:
https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/NA28%2CVUL%2CESV/MAT.12

Nothing here identifies the departing spirit as the Holy Spirit. The grammatical subject is explicitly **unclean spirit**.

---

# 3. The decisive Matthew phrase — `σχολάζοντα`

Matt 12:44:

`εὑρίσκει σχολάζοντα σεσαρωμένον καὶ κεκοσμημένον`

- `σχολάζοντα` — vacant / unoccupied / idle;
- `σεσαρωμένον` — swept;
- `κεκοσμημένον` — ordered/adorned.

Morphology:
https://biblehub.com/text/matthew/12-44.htm

Greek:
https://www.greekbible.com/matthew/12/44

### The point is not dirtiness alone

The house is:

- cleaner;
- ordered;
- yet **unoccupied**.

This is why the passage functions powerfully against mere moral reform.

The problem is not:

> “the person did not clean enough.”

The problem is:

> **the house has no new rightful occupant/allegiance.**

---

# 4. Important Synoptic textual control — Luke does not securely contain “empty”

Luke 11:25 in the main modern Greek line reads:

`καὶ ἐλθὸν εὑρίσκει σεσαρωμένον καὶ κεκοσμημένον`

— “having returned, it finds [it] swept and put in order.”

SBL/Nestle/major current line omits `σχολάζοντα`; Westcott-Hort and some apparatus traditions show the word as a variant.

Comparison:
https://www.biblehub.com/texts/luke/11-25.htm

Morphology/edition comparison:
https://biblehub.com/text/luke/11-25.htm

### Editorial rule

Do not quote Luke as if its uncontested text says:

> “empty, swept, and garnished.”

That wording belongs securely to **Matthew**.

Luke contributes the same broad theological pattern through context, not necessarily through `σχολάζοντα`.

---

# 5. Luke’s immediate context: no spiritual neutrality

Luke 11:20–23 is crucial.

Jesus has just said:

- if He casts out demons by the finger of God, the kingdom of God has come upon them (11:20);
- the stronger one overcomes the strong man (11:21–22);
- then:

`ὁ μὴ ὢν μετ’ ἐμοῦ κατ’ ἐμοῦ ἐστιν`

> “the one who is not with Me is against Me.”

Greek:
https://www.greekbible.com/luke/11/23

### Therefore

11:24–26 cannot be isolated as a demonological curiosity.

The preceding thesis is:

> **there is no neutral middle ground between allegiance to Christ and opposition to Him.**

An externally improved house that is not positively Christ’s remains exposed.

---

# 6. Luke’s following context: hear and keep the Word

Immediately after the returned-spirit image, Jesus corrects a woman’s beatitude:

`μακάριοι οἱ ἀκούοντες τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ φυλάσσοντες`

> blessed are those hearing the word of God and keeping it.

Greek:
https://www.greekbible.com/luke/11/28

Then Luke moves to the sign-of-Jonah discourse and calls the contemporaneous hearers:

> “this generation” / a wicked generation (11:29ff.).

### Luke’s positive alternative

Not:

> “get tidier.”

But:

> **be with Christ; hear and keep God’s word.**

---

# 7. Matthew’s context — explicit generational application

Matthew leaves no doubt that the illustration has a corporate-historical target.

Matt 12:45 ends:

`οὕτως ἔσται καὶ τῇ γενεᾷ ταύτῃ τῇ πονηρᾷ`

Greek:
https://greekbible.com/matthew/12/45

NA28 display:
https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/NA28%2CVUL%2CESV/MAT.12

The same chapter repeatedly addresses the generation that:

- witnesses Jesus’ works;
- attributes Spirit-wrought exorcism to Beelzebul/Satan;
- demands a further sign;
- is compared unfavorably with Nineveh and the queen of the South;
- refuses the One greater than Jonah/Solomon.

### Hence

The primary Matthean lesson is not:

> “a Christian can be Spirit-indwelt, then lose the Spirit.”

It is:

> **this generation can experience extraordinary kingdom visitation and real relief from demonic oppression, yet through rejection of Messiah end in deeper evil and judgment.**

---

# 8. The unclean spirit still calls it “my house”

Both parallels preserve the demon’s language:

> `εἰς τὸν οἶκόν μου ἐπιστρέψω`
>
> “I will return to **my house**.”

That possessive language is rhetorically significant.

The story does not narrate a new owner taking possession.

It narrates:

- departure;
- vacancy/order;
- return;
- expanded occupation.

This is compatible with **reformation without regeneration**, not evidence of regeneration lost.

---

# 9. “Seven other spirits” — do not force arithmetic

The spirit brings:

`ἑπτὰ ἕτερα πνεύματα πονηρότερα ἑαυτοῦ`

— seven other spirits more evil than itself.

The result:

`τὰ ἔσχατα ... χείρονα τῶν πρώτων`

— the last state is worse than the first.

### Guardrail

The point is intensification/completeness of evil, not a doctrine that precisely eight demons are the universal pattern of relapse.

Do not build demonology arithmetic from the illustration.

---

# 10. What “worse than the first” establishes

It establishes that **greater light / relief followed by rejection can produce a more dangerous final condition**.

This concept appears elsewhere:

- Matt 11:20–24 — greater revelation increases accountability;
- John 5:14 — “sin no more, lest something worse happen”; context differs, so use cautiously;
- 2 Pet 2:20–22 — knowledge/escape followed by re-entanglement leads to worse last state;
- Heb 6 / 10 — high privilege followed by decisive rejection intensifies judgment.

### But categories must remain distinct

Parallel severity does not make these texts grammatically identical.

Matt 12 / Luke 11 is **not** a hidden technical statement about losing justification.

# 12. MacArthur / GTY — moral reformation without transformation

John MacArthur’s exposition “The Danger of Moral Reformation” reads Luke 11:24–26 as a contrast between:

- external moral/religious cleanup;
- actual transformation in Christ.

Primary GTY source:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/42-161/the-danger-of-moral-reformation

MacArthur explicitly stresses Matthew’s added “unoccupied” language and argues that the cleaned but vacant life remains more exposed because self-reformation can create false security.

A separate GTY sermon links Matthew 12:43–45 with 2 Pet 2’s “last state worse than first” and explicitly says no salvation terms are present in the superficial-reformation condition being described there:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/61-22/creatures-born-to-be-killed-part-3

### Value

This is highly compatible with the grammar/context:

> **external change ≠ regeneration.**

### Guardrail

Some of MacArthur’s historical reconstruction of exactly what Jewish exorcists were doing is more inferential than the parable itself. The final argument should rest first on Luke/Matthew, not on a speculative reconstruction.

# 13. Matthew-specific generational reading — strong contextual control

A conservative/common Synoptic reading emphasizes that Matt 12:45 interprets the illustration for us:

> “so also will it be with this evil generation.”

The IVP Matthew commentary correctly notes that Matthew places the paragraph inside the conflict with Jesus’ opponents and explicitly turns the image toward the generation rejecting Jesus’ reign.

Author/platform rule applies: use as secondary corroboration only.

Accessible source:
https://www.biblegateway.com/resources/ivp-nt/Jesus-Opponents-End-Up-Worse-Than-They

https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/236/cambridge-greek-testament-for-schools-and-colleges-commentary/matthew/12/43-45

### Conclusion

Any individual application must remain **secondary to Matthew’s explicit corporate-generational application**.

# 14. Does the passage teach loss of the indwelling Holy Spirit?

No textual basis establishes that.

To prove a loss-of-indwelling argument one would need the text to show:

1. the person received the Holy Spirit;
2. the Holy Spirit occupied the “house”;
3. the Holy Spirit departed;
4. the unclean spirit then returned.

None of these statements occurs.

Instead:

- an **unclean** spirit leaves;
- in Matthew the house remains **vacant**;
- it is cleaned/ordered;
- the unclean spirit returns with others.

### Therefore

Using this text as a direct proof of:

> “a regenerate believer can lose the Holy Spirit and become demon-possessed again”

is **exegetically unsupported**.

---

# 15. Can genuine believers be demon-possessed? — keep outside this dossier

This text does not require settling the entire systematic demon-possession question.

The narrower claim is enough:

> **the person in this illustration is never said to be regenerate or Spirit-indwelt.**

Do not create an unnecessary doctrinal dependency.

# 16. Puritan practical layer — John Owen on false mortification

Owen’s *Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers* is an unusually precise practical-theology parallel.

He argues:

- true mortification is the Spirit’s work;
- an unregenerate person may achieve outward relinquishment/change;
- such efforts can change life-patterns without changing heart/condition;
- external self-reformation can make self-righteous people/hypocrites rather than Christians.

Primary CCEL, ch. VII:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/mort.i.x.html

Owen’s statement is not an exegesis of Matt 12/Luke 11, so it must **not** be cited as if he establishes their lexical meaning.

Its legitimate role is practical synthesis after the Synoptic exegesis:

> the “swept and ordered” life is a vivid biblical warning against confusing suppression/reformation with Spirit-wrought mortification and union with Christ.

Owen also explicitly grounds true mortification in the Spirit rather than self-strength:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/mort.i.iv.html

---

# 17. A crucial distinction — absence of gross sin vs presence of Christ

The passage’s deepest practical contrast is not:

`dirty person → clean person`.

It is:

`demonic domination → external relief/order → still no positive Christ-allegiance → deeper domination`.

Luke 11:23 supplies the interpretive binary:

> with Christ / against Christ.

Luke 11:28 supplies the positive response:

> hear and keep God’s word.

Matthew supplies the corporate verdict:

> this evil generation.

### Therefore

Christian sanctification is not merely **subtraction** of vice.

It is life under Christ’s reign by His Word and Spirit.

# 18. Relation to temporary faith / apostasy taxonomy

This passage belongs in the apostasy corpus, but carefully.

### High-confidence categories

- `external moral/religious reformation` — HIGH;
- `real relief/change without proven regeneration` — HIGH;
- `greater exposure after greater light` — HIGH;
- `worse final condition after return to evil` — HIGH;
- `corporate rejection of Messiah / evil generation` — HIGH in Matthew;
- `no spiritual neutrality` — HIGH in Luke.

### Not established

- prior regeneration — NOT ESTABLISHED;
- prior justification — NOT ESTABLISHED;
- Holy Spirit indwelling then leaving — NOT ESTABLISHED;
- loss of eternal election — NOT ESTABLISHED;
- restoration/regeneration after the worse state — NOT DISCUSSED.

# 19. Relation to 2 Peter 2

2 Pet 2:20–22 and Matt 12:45 share the “last state worse than first” logic.

MacArthur explicitly uses Matt 12 to illuminate 2 Pet 2:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/61-22/creatures-born-to-be-killed-part-3

But the corpus must maintain distinctions:

- Matt/Luke: demon departure/return, house imagery, explicit evil-generation application in Matthew;
- 2 Peter: knowledge of Christ, escape from pollutions, re-entanglement, way of righteousness, dog/sow imagery.

The common theological pattern is:

> **external/shared privilege and real change can intensify accountability without themselves proving a changed regenerate nature.**

---

# 20. The “empty house = no Holy Spirit” inference

There is a legitimate theological inference:

> if the house is spiritually vacant, it is not being described as positively inhabited by God.

But word the conclusion carefully.

The text itself says:

- `σχολάζοντα` (Matthew) — unoccupied;

not:

- “the Holy Spirit once lived there and left.”

Thus publication wording should be:

> **Matthew stresses vacancy after external cleansing, which fits absence of saving occupation/transformation; it does not narrate loss of the Spirit.**

---

# 21. “This evil generation” and Israel — how far to go

Matthew explicitly applies the image to the contemporaneous evil generation.

Some interpreters extend this into a long historical schema of Israel:

- prior idolatry;
- post-exilic cleansing from idolatry;
- Pharisaic morality;
- rejection of Messiah;
- later national judgment.

Parts of this are canonically plausible.

But **do not make every detail of the demon’s travel a one-to-one national chronology** unless a technical commentary demonstrates it.

Safe conclusion:

> Jesus’ contemporaries had received enormous covenant/revelatory privilege and kingdom visitation, yet rejection of Messiah would leave them worse, not safer.

# 25. P1-2 closure verdict

**P1-2 is architecturally CLOSED.**

Reader-facing synthesis:

> **Иисус предупреждает не о “Святом Духе, который ушёл из спасённого человека”, а о страшной недостаточности внешнего очищения без принадлежности Христу.
