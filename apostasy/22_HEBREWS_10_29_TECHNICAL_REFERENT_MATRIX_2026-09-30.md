## 1. Контролирующая синтаксическая проблема

Евр. 10:29 строит единое описание виновного:

- он попрал Сына Божия;
- счёл кровь завета `κοινόν`;
- `ἐν ᾧ ἡγιάσθη` — «которою он был освящён» / иной proposed referent;
- оскорбил Духа благодати.

Ближайшая грамматическая линия делает **отступника естественным референтом**: тот же человек является субъектом окружающих описаний. Это теперь подтверждается не только оппонентами Reformed reading, но и прямыми technical/translator sources.

Однако «естественный референт» ≠ «единственная грамматически мыслимая конструкция». Историческая Christ-referent линия (Оуэн и др.) должна быть представлена, но burden of proof лежит на ней именно потому, что она меняет обычное чтение цепочки.

## 3. Craig Koester — strongest technical loss-reading acquired in substance

Получен substantial full-text access to Craig R. Koester, *Hebrews: A New Translation with Introduction and Commentary* (Anchor Bible / AYB 36/37 tradition, 2001), including the 10:26–31 section.

### Koester’s reading is explicit

His translation itself renders the blood as that **“by which he was sanctified.”** In the commentary he treats the apostates as people who:

- received knowledge of the truth / conversion;
- were sanctified by Christ’s blood;
- can profane that sanctifying blood;
- can thereby restore the defilement that separates them from God;
- are warned precisely so disaster may be averted.

His broader theology section likewise says Christ’s blood/death sanctifies and purifies people for covenant relationship and that people can lose holiness by rejecting Christ and persisting in sin.

### Why this matters

This is no longer merely an Arminian blog saying “the apostate was sanctified.” It is a major technical commentary offering a coherent whole-Hebrews argument.

Therefore the future Reformed Product article must answer the **strong Koester version**:

> apostate referent + real covenant/saving sanctification + subsequent repudiation/judgment.

Anything weaker would be a straw man.

## 5. George H. Guthrie — direct modern impersonal reading

Получен direct public lecture/transcript by **George H. Guthrie** on Heb 10:26–39.

Guthrie explicitly says he translates the phrase not as:

> “by which **he** was sanctified”

but impersonally:

> “by which **one** is sanctified.”

He argues this is grammatically possible and fits his reading that the warning is too severe to describe regenerate believers.

### Why this matters

This gives the Reformed/non-loss side a **modern direct grammatical proposal**, not merely an appeal to a seventeenth-century Christ-referent.

### Pressure on Guthrie’s proposal

The impersonal translation must explain why the surrounding description is so consistently singular and personal and why the author would shift into generic “one” precisely inside the same clause. It is possible as a proposed reading; it is not self-evident.

### Product handling

Do not conflate Guthrie’s **impersonal apostate-excluded rendering** with Owen’s **Christ-referent**. They are different grammatical strategies and deserve separate rows.

## 6. Christ-referent remains a historical alternative, but not the default technical result

Owen/Gill historically argue that Christ may be the one sanctified/consecrated by His own blood to priestly ministry.

This reading has genuine theological architecture:

- priestly consecration;
- John 17:19;
- Christ as High Priest;
- avoidance of making the apostate object of blood-sanctification.

But after the present technical pass, it would be inaccurate to present it as the **most natural grammar** without additional argument.

Current direct/technical balance:

- Ellingworth/Nida → person/apostate referent;
- Koester → person/apostate referent;
- Lane locator lead → person/apostate referent;
- Guthrie → impersonal “one”; 
- Owen/Gill → Christ-referent.

Thus the question has shifted. The live Reformed problem is no longer primarily “can we make Christ the referent?” but:

> **If the apostate is the natural referent, what kind of sanctification does Hebrews 10:29 attribute to him, and how does that relate to Hebrews 10:10, 10:14, 13:12 and the epistle’s preservation/warning theology?**

That is a stronger and more honest formulation.

## 7. Theological meaning of `ἁγιάζω`: referent does not settle saving status

Even if apostate-referent is accepted, at least three semantic-theological models remain.

### Model A — saving sanctification later lost

Koester / Lane-type reading:

- Christ’s blood truly sanctifies the person;
- this belongs to conversion/new-covenant participation;
- apostasy profanes/reverses that holiness and ends in judgment.

This is the strongest loss-of-salvation pressure.

### Model B — real covenantal/consecratory sanctification without proven regeneration

Classical Reformed covenant/community reading:

- the apostate is genuinely set apart by covenant blood / visible church identification / consecrated relationship;
- the experience is not fictive;
- but `sanctified` is not automatically identical to effectual calling + justification + regeneration.

This model must be defended from Hebrews’ cultic/covenantal semantics, **not merely asserted because perseverance requires it**.

### Model C — generic/impersonal source-of-sanctification statement

Guthrie-type reading:

- “the blood by which one is sanctified” identifies the sanctifying efficacy of Christ’s blood in general;
- it does not say the apostate himself actually received that sanctification.

This is grammatically argued but has discourse pressure from the singular personal chain.

### Model D — Christ-referent priestly consecration

Owen/Gill historical reading.

It remains possible enough to record but currently requires the most special pleading at the discourse level and must prove that Hebrews elsewhere supports Christ being sanctified **by his own blood** in this sense.

## 8. Koester adds an important rhetorical correction

Koester’s contextual treatment is valuable even if one rejects his final perseverance conclusion.

He observes that 10:24–25 precedes the warning and that the community’s apparently ordinary weaknesses — neglect of gathering, need for mutual encouragement — receive severe warning language to **awaken them to danger and avert disaster**, not merely to describe outsiders already lost.

This substantially supports our canonical “warnings as means” architecture:

- warning is addressed pastorally to the hearers;
- warning describes a genuine catastrophic path;
- warning seeks to prevent that path;
- 10:32–39 then deliberately rebuilds confidence and perseverance.

Therefore a Reformed response does not need to deny the pastoral address to Christians. It can argue about the **counterfactual/means relation and the identity disclosed by final perseverance**, while leaving the warning fully operational.

## 9. 10:29 must be interpreted with Hebrews’ full `ἁγιάζω` system

Before publication, create exact table for every occurrence and nearby cognate logic:

- 2:11 — sanctifier and sanctified;
- 9:13 — cultic sanctification toward cleansing of flesh;
- 10:10 — “we have been sanctified” through offering of Jesus’ body once for all;
- 10:14 — by one offering he has perfected forever those being sanctified;
- 10:29 — blood by which `ἡγιάσθη`;
- 13:12 — Jesus suffered outside gate to sanctify people through his own blood.

### Key methodological point

Heb 9:13 proves `ἁγιάζω` can be used in a cultic/consecratory register that is not identical to final eschatological salvation. But 10:10/14/13:12 prove that the word also lives inside the strongest soteriological claims of the letter.

Therefore neither side gets a lexical shortcut.

## 11. What changed from earlier Reformed apologetic instincts

The corpus should now explicitly retire three weak moves:

### Weak move 1

> “`ἡγιάσθη` obviously refers to Christ.”

No. Technical evidence presently leans away from obviousness.

### Weak move 2

> “If it refers to the apostate, perseverance is disproved.”

No. Referent and theological type of sanctification are separate questions.

### Weak move 3

> “Sanctification can be external, therefore the verse is solved.”

No. Hebrews uses `ἁγιάζω` strongly elsewhere. A covenantal/consecratory reading must be demonstrated from Hebrews’ own cultic logic and context.

This makes the eventual Reformed argument **harder but materially stronger**.
