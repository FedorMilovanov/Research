## 1. Why these chapters must be read together

John 15 cannot responsibly be used as a stand-alone proof-text for either:

- “a regenerate believer can certainly lose salvation,” or
- “the warning has no real force because true believers are eternally secure.”

The Fourth Gospel itself supplies a network of controls:

- **John 6:37–44** — the Father gives, people come, the Son receives/keeps, loses none, raises at the last day;
- **John 6:64, 70–71** — Judas is already placed in the category of unbelief while genuinely belonging to the Twelve by office;
- **John 10:27–30** — Jesus’ sheep hear/follow, receive eternal life, never perish, and cannot be snatched from Father/Son;
- **John 13:10–11, 18, 27, 30** — the Twelve are internally differentiated; not all are clean; Judas departs into betrayal;
- **John 15:1–8** — branches “in Me,” fruit, removal/pruning, abiding, fire;
- **John 17:6–24** — people given by Father, Christ’s keeping/intercession, Judas as son of destruction, protection from evil one;
- **John 18:8–9** — John explicitly sees Jesus’ protection of the disciples at arrest as fulfillment of the saying that He lost none of those given Him;
- **1 John 2:19** — secession retrospectively manifests non-belonging: if they had been “of us,” they would have remained.

### Controlling synthesis

The Johannine corpus simultaneously teaches:

1. **strong divine preservation** of those truly given to the Son;
2. **real commands/warnings to abide**;
3. **real outward discipleship/office/proximity without saving faith**, with Judas as the clearest narrative control;
4. **perseverance/remaining as a mark of genuine discipleship**, not an optional higher-Christian tier.

The exegetical task is to allow all four to stand.

# 2. John 6:37 — Father’s giving, coming, and Christ’s refusal to cast out

Greek:

`Πᾶν ὃ δίδωσίν μοι ὁ πατὴρ πρὸς ἐμὲ ἥξει, καὶ τὸν ἐρχόμενον πρός με οὐ μὴ ἐκβάλω ἔξω.`

Core sequence:

`Father gives → the given come → Christ will certainly not cast the comer out`.

The double negative `οὐ μὴ` is emphatic.

### Carson locator

D. A. Carson, *The Gospel According to John*, PNTC, p. 290, argues that in context the litotes does more than state Christ’s willingness to welcome a penitent; it emphasizes that the one who comes as the Father’s gift will be **kept/preserved**, not expelled.

Accessible locator corroboration:
https://new.bible.org/seriespage/lesson-34-certain-success-jesus-mission-john-635-40

Ardel Caneday also preserves Carson’s p.290 wording in an SBJT article on resurrection/security.

Locator witness:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377111564_Caneday_-_God%27s_Incarnate_Son_as_the_Embodiment_of_Last_Day_Resurrection_ST-036-2015-SBJT-18-4-Winter-2015-A4

### Conservative control

MacArthur repeatedly traces the same chain:

`given → drawn → come → received → kept → raised`.

Primary GTY:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/90-180/the-purpose-of-salvation

### Guardrail

Verse 37 does not make human coming/believing unreal. It joins divine giving with actual coming.

# 3. John 6:39–40 — “lose nothing” and resurrection at the last day

John 6:39:

> of all that the Father has given the Son, He should `μὴ ἀπολέσω ἐξ αὐτοῦ` — lose nothing from it — but raise it at the last day.

Public text/translation control:
https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/John%206%3A39

John 6:40 immediately phrases the same final horizon in personal believing terms:

- everyone seeing the Son and believing in Him has eternal life;
- Christ will raise that person on the last day.

### Theological force

The passage does not merely promise a good start.

It explicitly spans:

`Father’s gift → present coming/believing → Son’s keeping → last-day resurrection`.

This is one of the Fourth Gospel’s strongest preservation texts.

### Anti-abuse

It must not be used to erase John 15’s warning.

A Reformed perseverance model should say:

> the same Christ who guarantees the final resurrection of the Father’s gift also commands disciples to abide; the command is part of the means and marks of persevering faith, not evidence that preservation is irrelevant.

---

# 4. John 6:44 — drawing and resurrection

`οὐδεὶς δύναται ἐλθεῖν πρός με ἐὰν μὴ ὁ πατὴρ ... ἑλκύσῃ αὐτόν, κἀγὼ ἀναστήσω αὐτὸν ἐν τῇ ἐσχάτῃ ἡμέρᾳ.`

The same individual grammatical object is:

- drawn by Father;
- raised by Son at the last day.

### Disputed systematic question

Arminian/Provisionist interpreters rightly stress v.45 (“taught by God,” hearing/learning) and resist treating `ἑλκύω` as a magic lexical synonym of irresistible regeneration.

The Reformed case is strongest when it does **not** rest on the dictionary word alone but on the discourse’s result-chain:

- inability to come apart from Father;
- Father’s drawing;
- coming;
- Son’s reception;
- final resurrection.

### Claim boundary

`ἑλκύω` alone does not lexicalize an entire doctrine of effectual calling.

The **syntax/discourse** supplies the stronger argument.

# 5. John 6:64 — Judas and unbelief “from the beginning”

John inserts a decisive narrator-like explanation:

> Jesus knew from the beginning `τίνες εἰσὶν οἱ μὴ πιστεύοντες` — who the unbelieving ones were — and who would betray Him.

Within the near context, John 6:71 identifies Judas as the betrayer.

### Importance

The Gospel does **not** narrate Judas as:

`saving believer → later unbeliever → traitor`.

Instead, betrayal is framed against a prior/unmasked category of unbelief.

This remains one of the strongest controls against using Judas as an uncomplicated example of lost regeneration.

`02_JUDAS_ISCARIOT_EXEGETICAL_DOSSIER_2026-09-29.md`.

# 6. John 6:70–71 — “I chose you Twelve” is not automatically election to salvation

Jesus says:

> “Did I not choose you, the Twelve? Yet one of you is a devil.”

John immediately identifies Judas.

The object/domain of the choosing in context is **the Twelve** — apostolic office/group.

### Calvin’s necessary distinction

Calvin explicitly distinguishes Judas’ election to apostolic office from election to eternal salvation.

Primary:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.v.xxiii.html

Calvin’s formulation is especially useful for the project’s wider lexical rule:

> the presence of election/choice language does not by itself identify its saving referent; context determines **election to what**.

### Canonical parallel

Acts 1:17 calls Judas one who was numbered among the apostles and received a share in the ministry.

Real office/privilege ≠ proven saving union.

---

# 7. John 13 — the discourse distinguishes Judas before John 15

John 13 provides immediate narrative controls:

- 13:10–11 — “you are clean, but not all”; the narrator ties the exception to the betrayer;
- 13:18 — Jesus distinguishes the group again: “I know whom I have chosen”; Scripture of betrayal;
- 13:27 — Satan enters Judas at the betrayal climax;
- 13:30 — Judas goes out; “and it was night.”

### Why this matters for John 15

By the time the farewell discourse reaches the vine imagery, the reader already knows:

> close discipleship, apostolic office and table fellowship can coexist with non-cleanness/unbelief in Judas.

That does not automatically prove every fruitless branch = Judas.

But it makes Judas a **highly relevant Johannine control** against assuming that every relational metaphor means saving union.

# 8. John 15:2 — `αἴρει` means remove/take away, not therapeutic “lift up”

Greek:

`πᾶν κλῆμα ἐν ἐμοὶ μὴ φέρον καρπὸν αἴρει αὐτό`.

The popular claim that `αἴρει` here means the Father lovingly “lifts up” fruitless branches to restore them is lexically/contextually weak.

Bill Mounce directly addresses the issue:

- `αἴρω` has a broad range including lift/take/remove;
- in this context v.6 controls the image;
- the semantic suggestion “raise/propping up so it can become fruitful” is not the natural contextual meaning;
- major translations rightly use “takes away/removes/cuts off.”

Primary Mounce:
https://www.billmounce.com/blog/mwm/airo-john-15-2

Later stronger restatement:
https://www.billmounce.com/monday-with-mounce/one-example-the-passion-mistranslation-john-15-2

Zondervan Academic version:
https://zondervanacademic.com/blog/airo

NET independently makes the same contextual judgment, citing Johannine uses and v.6:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John.15.2&version=NET

### Closure

- `remove/take away`: `GREEN`;
- therapeutic “lift up and restore”: `REJECTED as controlling translation`.

This does **not** yet identify the soteriological status of the removed branch. It only removes a bad lexical escape route.

# 9. “Every branch in Me” — the hardest phrase

`πᾶν κλῆμα ἐν ἐμοί`

Conditional-security interpreters press the obvious surface force:

> the branch is said to be **in Christ**, then removed and later burned.

That pressure must not be evaded.

### Reformed response must be contextual, not lexical denial

The phrase `ἐν ἐμοί` is strong, but a metaphor is not identical to Pauline technical formulas simply because it contains `ἐν` + Christ.

The question is what **vine-membership** signifies in this specific Johannine discourse.

Relevant controls:

- Judas’ real but non-saving apostolic connection;
- John 6:64 unbelief within disciple-circle;
- John 13:10–11 not all clean;
- John 15:8 fruit “proves/shows” discipleship;
- John 8:31ff. people can be called believers/disciple-like and then expose rejection;
- 1 John 2:19 says remaining manifests true belonging.

### Calvin’s formulation

Calvin explicitly asks whether one truly engrafted into Christ can be fruitless and answers that many are **supposed to be in the vine according to human appearance/profession while lacking actual root**.

Primary:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/comment3/comm_vol35/htm/v.htm

### Important concession

This is a theological reading of the metaphor, not a dictionary definition of `ἐν ἐμοί`.

The final article must state the difficulty rather than pretending the phrase is weak.

# 10. John 15:3 — “you are already clean” and Judas contrast

Jesus tells the remaining disciples:

`ἤδη ὑμεῖς καθαροί ἐστε διὰ τὸν λόγον...`

“You are already clean because of the word...”

John 13:10 had used the same clean-language but added:

> “not all,”

with the narrator explaining the betrayer.

### Narrative effect

John 15:3, after Judas’ departure, lacks the “not all” qualification.

This strengthens — though does not alone prove — the reading that John contrasts:

- clean/fruitful/abiding disciples;
- Judas-like outward attachment without cleanness/fruit.

Bill Mounce explicitly notices the `καθαίρω / καθαρός` wordplay around 15:2–3 and the Judas background.

Primary:
https://www.billmounce.com/blog/mwm/play-words-john-15-2-3

---

# 11. John 15:4–5 — abiding is not an optional elite-Christian add-on

`μείνατε ἐν ἐμοί` — abide/remain in Me.

The branch cannot bear fruit apart from remaining in the vine.

Jesus concludes:

> `χωρὶς ἐμοῦ οὐ δύνασθε ποιεῖν οὐδέν` — apart from Me you can do nothing.

### TMS practical layer

TMS uses the passage to emphasize radical dependence on Christ for sanctification rather than autonomous moral effort.

Source:
https://blog.tms.edu/abiding-in-christ

This is useful practical theology, but the source does not itself adjudicate every warning-status question in vv.2,6.

### Key theological correction

Free Grace readings sometimes sharply separate:

- possessing eternal life;
- abiding/fellowship/fruitfulness.

There is a real distinction between justification and varying fruitfulness, but John 15’s warning is too severe to make abiding merely an optional advanced fellowship tier without further argument.

# 12. John 15:6 — fire is a judgment image

Greek:

`ἐὰν μή τις μένῃ ἐν ἐμοί, ἐβλήθη ἔξω ὡς τὸ κλῆμα καὶ ἐξηράνθη, καὶ συνάγουσιν αὐτὰ καὶ εἰς τὸ πῦρ βάλλουσιν καὶ καίεται.`

The sequence:

`not abide → cast outside → wither → gathered → fire → burned`.

NET concludes that the image most naturally points to eschatological judgment and evokes OT vine judgment, especially Ezekiel 15.

Source:
https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Joh&chapter=15&verse=6

### Therefore

The interpretation:

> “the fire is merely harmless loss of reward / ordinary pruning”

has substantial contextual pressure against it.

### But

Judgment imagery still does not by itself prove the branch was previously regenerate.

That remains the central disputed question.

# 13. Three serious models of the fruitless branch

## A. Reformed / false-or-outward disciple model

Thesis:

- branch has real outward/covenantal/discipleship attachment;
- is called “in Me” within the metaphor;
- but fruitlessness, failure to remain and fire expose absence of saving life;
- Judas is the immediate narrative paradigm;
- John 6 / 10 / 17 and 1 John 2:19 control the theology of true belonging.

Strengths:

- harmonizes strong preservation texts inside the same Johannine corpus;
- takes v.6 judgment seriously;
- explains Judas and John 13 “not all clean” naturally;
- treats perseverance/fruit as evidence of genuine discipleship.

Pressure:

- must explain the strong surface phrase “branch **in Me**” without making it meaningless.

### Carson / Köstenberger locators

D. A. Carson’s main John 15 discussion is pp.513–530; secondary summaries preserve:

- true vine / Israel background around pp.513–514;
- pruning/cleansing around pp.514–515;
- fruit as the outcome/evidence of persevering dependence around p.517.

Locator corroboration:
https://www.pastorjasonelder.com/the-gospel-of-john/the-vine-and-the-branches-john-151-17
https://www.wittenbergcomo.com/blog/category/carson-1991

Andreas J. Köstenberger, *John*, BECNT, John 15 discussion pp.447–465; independent scholarly article cites Köstenberger 2004 pp.448,455–457 in this unit.

Locator corroboration:
https://verbumetecclesia.org.za/index.php/ve/article/view/2802/6885

Exact original pages remain `AMBER` before direct quotation.

## B. Conditional-security / genuine believer can apostatize

Thesis:

- `ἐν ἐμοί` is genuine union;
- commands to remain imply real possibility not to remain;
- branch is then cast out and burned;
- therefore a genuine believer may finally abandon Christ and perish.

Strengths:

- takes “in Me” and warning conditionality at maximum surface force;
- takes v.6 fire as final judgment;
- does not weaken `μένω` into irrelevant advice.

Representative public Arminian argument:
https://evangelicalarminians.org/arminius-vs-calvin-on-assurance-of-salvation-and-perseverance/

Classical older conditional reading is also visible in Cambridge/Alford-style commentary traditions.

Pressure:

- must integrate John 6:37–44; 10:27–30; 17; 1 John 2:19;
- must explain Judas/unbelief controls;
- cannot simply assume every metaphorical `ἐν ἐμοί` is a technical declaration of regeneration/justification.

## C. Free Grace / genuine believer disciplined or loses reward/fellowship

This family contains more than one version.

### C1. Laney — “abiding is believing”

J. Carl Laney, Western Conservative Baptist Seminary, *Bibliotheca Sacra* 146:581 (1989):55–66, argues against a sharp faith/fruit divorce and examines whether genuinely saved persons can remain fruitless.

Primary article landing page:
https://www.galaxie.com/article/bsac146-581-05

### C2. Dillow / fellowship model

Joseph Dillow responded that abiding should be distinguished as ongoing fellowship; other dispensational/Free Grace interpreters read fruitless branches as true believers under discipline/loss.

Bibliographic trail:
https://deanbible.org/component/rsfiles/download-file/files?Itemid=211&path=Doctrines%252FAbiding_in_Christ-part1.pdf

Bible.org lays out both non-Arminian alternatives:

- disobedient believer under severe discipline;
- never-genuine believer (Judas / John 6:66), with v.6 eternal judgment.

Source:
https://bible.org/seriespage/exegetical-commentary-john-15

Pressure on reward/discipline-only reading:

- v.6’s withering/fire sequence naturally sounds like rejection/judgment;
- `αἴρει` should not be softened to “lift up”;
- John 15:8 makes fruit evidence of discipleship.

# 14. Mounce: a necessary lexical closure on `αἴρω`

Bill Mounce’s 2023 treatment is particularly useful because he explicitly complains that theology often drives translation here.

He concludes:

- every major translation renders removal/cutting;
- “raise/propping up the branch” does not fit v.6;
- the context contains two claimed-disciple branches: fruit-bearing and non-fruit-bearing;
- destruction follows the latter.

Primary:
https://www.billmounce.com/monday-with-mounce/one-example-the-passion-mistranslation-john-15-2

### Editorial consequence

Do not let any final article make the soteriological problem disappear by mistranslating `αἴρει`.

The problem must be solved by identifying **what kind of relationship the fruitless branch represents**, not by changing “remove” to “support.”

# 15. John 17:6,9,24 — people given by Father

John 17 repeatedly uses the language of the Father giving people to the Son:

- 17:6 — “the people whom You gave Me out of the world”;
- 17:9 — “those whom You have given Me, for they are Yours”;
- 17:24 — “those whom You have given Me” to be with Christ and see His glory.

This clearly connects the farewell prayer to John 6’s Father-to-Son gift motif.

### MacArthur / GTY control

MacArthur explicitly links John 17 with John 6:

> Father chooses/gives → Son receives/keeps → brings to glory.

Primary:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/43-95/the-lords-greatest-prayer-part-3

The doctrinal synthesis is Reformed, but the textual connection is genuine and should be kept.

# 16. Critical textual issue in John 17:12 — `ᾧ` vs `οὓς`

This is a P0-level subtlety for any public article.

## Modern critical text

SBLGNT / THGNT read substantially:

`ἐτήρουν αὐτοὺς ἐν τῷ ὀνόματί σου ᾧ δέδωκάς μοι, καὶ ἐφύλαξα...`

Meaning:

> “I was keeping them in Your name, **which You have given Me**, and I guarded [them]...”

Source:
https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/grc/John%2017%3A12

## TR / later plural reading

A later reading has `οὓς δέδωκάς μοι`:

> “those whom You have given Me...”

Manuscript/edition comparison:
https://greeknewtestament.net/jn17-12

### Consequence

Do **not** argue:

> “John 17:12 itself explicitly says ‘those whom You gave Me I kept’ in all critical editions.”

That is textually inaccurate.

The **people-given** motif is nevertheless explicit in 17:6, 9, 24 and John 6, while 17:12 unquestionably says Christ kept/guarded the disciples and none perished except Judas.

This distinction materially improves the project’s precision.

# 17. John 17:12 — what does Judas’ exception establish?

Critical text:

`καὶ οὐδεὶς ἐξ αὐτῶν ἀπώλετο εἰ μὴ ὁ υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας, ἵνα ἡ γραφὴ πληρωθῇ.`

> none of them perished except the son of destruction/perdition, so that Scripture might be fulfilled.

Greek witness:
https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/grc/John%2017%3A12

### Maximum surface pressure

`εἰ μή` is an actual exception construction.

Older Meyer and Alford traditions take Judas as a tragic exception within the immediate disciple group rather than pretending `εἰ μή` means “but someone totally unrelated.”

Technical historical commentary:
https://lemma.gebible.app/v/john/17/12

### But same-category inference must be controlled

An exception in a **relational/discourse group** does not prove identity of every deeper soteriological property.

The Gospel already says of Judas:

- Jesus knew his unbelief from the beginning (6:64);
- he was a devil while one of the chosen Twelve (6:70–71);
- not all were clean because of the betrayer (13:10–11);
- Satanic betrayal reaches culmination (13:27);
- he is son of destruction (17:12).

Thus the strongest Reformed reading does **not** need to deny that Judas is an exception inside the historically guarded disciple-circle.

It says:

> **he was not an exception to Christ’s promise that a savingly given/sheep believer can finally be lost; he was the already-unbelieving apostolic exception within the historical Twelve.**

### Calvin

Calvin says Judas appeared among the elect/true flock by dignity of office, but was not one of the saving elect.

Primary Institutes:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.v.xxiii.html

Primary commentary trail:
https://www.biblia.work/bible-commentary/exegetical-and-hermeneutical-commentary-of-john-1712/

# 18. John 17:12 and divine sovereignty/human guilt

The phrase:

`ἵνα ἡ γραφὴ πληρωθῇ`

does not turn Judas into a morally neutral robot.

John’s narrative holds together:

- Scripture fulfillment;
- Jesus’ foreknowledge;
- Satanic activity;
- Judas’ own greed/betrayal/responsibility.

This is consistent with Acts 2/4 patterns where divinely purposed events remain culpable human acts.

### Editorial restraint

Do not make this dossier solve every metaphysical problem of predestination/free will.

The narrower point is enough:

> Judas’ destruction is not narrated as a surprise failure of Christ’s preservation mission.

# 19. John 18:8–9 — John himself quotes “I lost none” in the arrest scene

At the arrest Jesus tells the soldiers to let His disciples go.

John adds that this happened:

> to fulfill the word Jesus had spoken: “Of those whom You gave Me I lost not one.”

### Importance

John himself applies the preservation saying to **concrete physical protection at arrest**.

This does not reduce John 6/17 to merely physical safety.

Rather it demonstrates Johannine multi-level fulfillment:

- Jesus guards His own even in the crisis of arrest;
- this concrete protection participates in the larger keeping mission.

### Judas control

Judas is no longer among the protected disciples in the arrest group; he is with the arresting party.

Therefore 18:9 should not be used to force Judas into the same savingly-given category and then make him an exception to it.

# 20. John 10:27–30 — sheep, eternal life, and non-perishing

The strongest contextual control on John 15 is not a later confession but John’s own Gospel.

Jesus’ sheep:

- hear His voice;
- He knows them;
- they follow Him;
- He gives them eternal life;
- they shall `οὐ μὴ ἀπόλωνται εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα` — emphatically never perish;
- no one snatches them from His hand;
- no one snatches from Father’s hand.

### Relation to John 15

Any interpretation of the burning branch as a once-saving sheep who finally perishes must explain this passage, not merely repeat “in Me.”

Conversely, Reformed interpretation must not use John 10 to mute the **real warning command** to abide in John 15.

The Johannine synthesis is preservation **through persevering attachment/faith**, not preservation irrespective of final unbelief.

# 21. 1 John 2:19 — Johannine retrospective diagnostic

`εἰ γὰρ ἐξ ἡμῶν ἦσαν, μεμενήκεισαν ἂν μεθ’ ἡμῶν`

> if they had been of us, they would have remained with us.

This is exceptionally relevant because the same Johannine family uses `μένω` as the diagnostic category.

Separation manifests a prior deeper non-belonging.

Existing dossier:
`19_1_JOHN_2_19_REMAINING_AND_DEPARTURE_2026-09-30.md`.

### Guardrail

1 John 2:19 addresses antichristological secessionists and should not be mechanically imposed on every temporary lapse.

James 5 / Peter show recoverable wandering and grievous fall.

But for **final secession**, John’s own theological pattern weighs strongly toward manifestation rather than loss of saving union.

# 22. Schreiner — warnings as means, not hypothetical decorative language

Thomas Schreiner’s perseverance model is valuable for preventing a bad Reformed use of John 6/10.

Schreiner emphasizes that New Testament warnings are addressed to believers/communities as real means God uses to lead His people to final salvation.

Primary publisher/author context:
https://www.crossway.org/books/run-to-win-the-prize-ebook/

Google Books:
https://books.google.com/books/about/Run_to_Win_the_Prize.html?id=TtKe-0uff3UC

Accessible teaching summary from Schreiner himself:
https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/academy/bt201-a-guide-to-biblical-theology/bt201-17-election-and-the-christian-life

### Application to John 15

A Reformed reading should **not** say:

> “Because John 6 guarantees preservation, John 15:6 cannot be a real warning.”

Instead:

> **the threat of non-abiding is one of the means by which disciples are summoned to remain in the saving reality God preserves.**

This preserves both assurance and urgency.

# 23. TMS / Shepherds-compatible practical layer

TMS material on abiding rightly stresses that the vine metaphor teaches radical dependence:

- believers have no independent life-source;
- sanctification comes by dependence on Christ through His Word;
- John 15:7 interprets abiding in connection with Christ’s words abiding in disciples.

Source:
https://blog.tms.edu/abiding-in-christ

This is useful for the **positive** half of the final article:

> perseverance is not self-generated grit; it is continuing dependence upon Christ and His Word.

It should not substitute for the technical branch-status debate.

# 24. Puritan/Reformed practical enrichment — Owen and Calvin

## Calvin

Calvin on John 15 explicitly links the warning with perseverance while denying final drying-up of the elect.

Primary:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/john/15.htm

His language is valuable:

- warning/exhortation to perseverance is not unnecessary because perseverance is God’s gift;
- outwardly green hypocrites may later expose fruitlessness;
- true branches depend continuously on Christ.

## Owen

Owen’s *Perseverance* uses vine/branch union as one biblical image of believers’ abiding union with Christ.

Accessible primary/transcription:
https://www.joelkell.com/resources/owen/owen-perseverance/chapter-8/

### Practical-theology synthesis

The strong Reformed tradition does **not** teach:

> “If preservation is certain, warnings, abiding and dependence are optional.”

It teaches:

> God’s preserving grace produces and sustains continuing union, faith, repentance and fruit through appointed means.

# 25. Competing-view matrix

| Question | Reformed / perseverance | Conditional security | Free Grace / discipline-fellowship |
|---|---|---|---|
| John 6:37–44 | effectual gift/call + certain keeping/resurrection | divine initiative, but continued faith can be abandoned | eternal-life security usually affirmed strongly |
| Judas | office/outward disciple, unbelieving from beginning | can be read as genuine disciple who apostatized, though John 6:64 is difficult | often non-saving/outward or exceptional office |
| John 15 `in Me` | metaphorical/visible disciple relation need not equal saving union | genuine saving union | often genuine believer |
| `αἴρει` | removes | removes | should still be translated removes; some older FG variants try discipline/lift |
| John 15:6 fire | final judgment of false/apostate professor | final judgment of apostate believer | discipline/loss/reward in some models; disputed |
| abiding | mark/means of saving perseverance | condition of continued saving union | ongoing fellowship/discipleship in many FG models |
| John 17 keeping | confirms preservation of true gift | prayer/keeping can be resisted in final apostasy | typically strong security text |
| 1 John 2:19 | final departure manifests non-belonging | applies to those secessionists, not universal rule | varies |

### Editorial principle

Represent each model at its **strongest**, then distinguish:

- what the Greek directly says;
- what the narrative context says;
- what the wider Johannine theology says;
- what each systematic model infers.

# 26. Current weighting of John 15

After integrating John 6, 10, 13, 17, 18 and 1 John 2:

### Preferred reading

The fruitless/burned branches represent **real outward discipleship/attachment to Jesus within the visible covenantal-disciple sphere without saving, persevering life**.

Reasons:

1. Judas is already a narrative paradigm of maximal external proximity without saving belief;
2. John 13 distinguishes clean/not-clean before vine discourse;
3. John 6:64 locates Judas in unbelief before betrayal;
4. John 6:37–44 and 10:27–30 give unusually strong final-preservation promises;
5. 1 John 2:19 explicitly uses remaining as retrospective proof of deeper belonging;
6. John 15 itself makes fruit/abiding diagnostic of discipleship;
7. fire/judgment is stronger than a mere low-reward image;
8. `αἴρει` should be allowed its removal force.

### Confidence

`PREFERRED / STRONG`, not “lexically compelled.”

### Why not claim certainty from one phrase

`πᾶν κλῆμα ἐν ἐμοί` remains genuine pressure for the conditional-security reading.

The Reformed solution wins by **whole-Johannine coherence**, not by pretending “in Me” says nothing.

# 27. John 17:12 adjudication — precise formulation

Avoid both mistakes:

### Bad Reformed shortcut

> “Judas was not in the group at all, so `except` is meaningless.”

False. Judas was really one of the Twelve and `εἰ μή` is a real exception in the immediate disciple set.

### Bad conditional-security shortcut

> “Because Judas is the exception, he must have been savingly given/elected/regenerated exactly like the others and then lost salvation.”

Also not established.

### Better formulation

> **Judas is the tragic exception within the historically chosen/guarded apostolic circle, but John has already identified him as unbelieving from the beginning and unclean. His exception therefore does not overturn the Gospel’s claim that those truly given to the Son as saving believers/sheep are kept to resurrection; rather, it exposes the distinction between office/proximity and saving belonging.**

This formulation permits the full force of `εἰ μή` without importing prior regeneration.

# 31. P1-4 closure verdict

**P1-4 John 6 / 15 / 17 is architecturally CLOSED.**

Reader-facing synthesis:

> **Иоанн не позволяет ни ослабить предупреждение “пребудьте во Мне”, ни превратить ветвь “во Мне” в автоматическое доказательство прежнего оправдания. В том же Евангелии Отец даёт людей Сыну, они приходят, Сын не теряет их и воскресит в последний день; овцы Христа не погибнут; Иуда же, несмотря на реальное апостольское избрание и близость, назван неверующим ещё до предательства и “нечистым” внутри Двенадцати. Поэтому наиболее цельное иоанново чтение видит в бесплодной ветви реальную внешнюю ученическую связь без спасающей, пребывающей жизни. Но это заключение достигается не словарным трюком: `αἴρει` действительно значит удалить, огонь — реальный суд, а “ветвь во Мне” остаётся серьёзным текстовым давлением. Уверенность и предупреждение соединяются так: Христос сохраняет Своих, и сохранённые Им пребывают через Слово, веру, послушание и плод; предупреждение о непребывании является реальным средством этой стойкости.**
