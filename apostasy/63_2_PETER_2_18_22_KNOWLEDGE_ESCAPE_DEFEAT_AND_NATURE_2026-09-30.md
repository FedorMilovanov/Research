## 1. Почему 2:20–22 нельзя свести к одному образу собаки и свиньи

Пётр последовательно утверждает о рассматриваемых людях:

1. они **избежали** `ἀποφυγόντες` скверн мира;
2. сделали это `ἐν ἐπιγνώσει` Господа и Спасителя Иисуса Христа;
3. затем `πάλιν` — снова — запутались в них;
4. были `ἡττῶνται` — побеждены/порабощены;
5. их последнее состояние стало хуже первого;
6. они **познали путь праведности**;
7. затем **отвратились/повернули назад** от переданной святой заповеди;
8. собака возвращается к рвоте;
9. **вымытая** свинья возвращается валяться в грязи.

Поэтому Reformed exegesis не может честно сказать:

> «они вообще ничего реально не знали, ни от чего реально не ушли и никак реально не очистились».

Но conditional-security exegesis также не может просто соединить все положительные слова знаком равенства с `regenerated + justified + elect unto final salvation`.

Главный вопрос:

> **какой тип реальной перемены описывает Пётр и что именно финальная animal imagery раскрывает о её глубине?**

# 2. DIRECT EXEGESIS — `ἀποφυγόντες` означает реальный escape

2:20:

`Εἰ γὰρ ἀποφυγόντες τὰ μιάσματα τοῦ κόσμου ...`

`ἀποφυγόντες` — aorist active participle nominative plural masculine от `ἀποφεύγω`: escape/flee away.

Morphology:
- https://biblehub.com/lexicon/2_peter/2-20.htm

### Same-letter control

2 Pet 1:4:

> believers become partakers of divine nature, `ἀποφυγόντες` corruption in the world through lust.

2 Pet 2:20:

> the relevant subjects `ἀποφυγόντες` defilements of the world through knowledge of Christ.

English parallel:
- https://www.esv.org/2P2.20%3B2P1.4/

### DIRECT EXEGESIS conclusion

The verb in 2:20 should not be emptied to:

> «они никогда реально не выходили из прежнего поведения».

Peter attributes a real escape.

### Necessary restriction

The objects differ:

- 1:4 — `φθορά`, corruption;
- 2:20 — `μιάσματα`, pollutions/defilements.

Therefore the same verb does not prove the two experiences are soteriologically identical.

**Claim level:** `DIRECT EXEGESIS — REAL ESCAPE; SOTERIOLOGICAL IDENTITY NOT ESTABLISHED`.

# 3. DIRECT EXEGESIS — `ἐπίγνωσις` is strong knowledge-language

2:20:

`ἐν ἐπιγνώσει τοῦ κυρίου καὶ σωτῆρος Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ`

Peter’s own uses of `ἐπίγνωσις`:

- 1:2 — grace and peace multiplied in knowledge of God/Jesus;
- 1:3 — life and godliness granted through knowledge of the One who called;
- 1:8 — Christian virtues make believers fruitful in knowledge of Jesus Christ;
- 2:20 — escape from world-defilements through knowledge of Lord/Savior Jesus Christ.

Concordance:
- https://biblehub.com/greek/strongs_1922.htm

### Lexical pressure

This makes the shortcut untenable:

> «`ἐπίγνωσις` в 2:20 просто означает слышал какие-то факты».

The noun is strong and, within 2 Peter, overwhelmingly positive.

### MacArthur’s useful concession

MacArthur explicitly says:

- `ἐπίγνωσις` tends to convey full/complete/thorough knowledge;
- most NT uses concern saving knowledge;
- but Heb 10:26 and 2 Pet 2:20 show that the noun itself cannot mechanically prove salvation.

Direct GTY:
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/61-3/our-precious-faith-part-2

This is methodologically correct even if one later disagrees with his precise classification of 2:20.

### DIRECT EXEGESIS conclusion

> **The knowledge is substantial, Christ-centered and morally effective; the noun itself is not a technical sacramental stamp “regenerate.”**

# 4. Technical literature is genuinely divided on the soteriological force of `ἐπίγνωσις`

The SIL Exegetical Summary acquired in `21_2_PETER_2_TECHNICAL_COMMENTARY_ACQUISITION_2026-09-30.md` records two real camps.

## Strong/saving knowledge reading

The summary attributes strong/full/saving force to a significant technical group, including WBC and multiple commentary traditions.

This is important evidence against minimizing the noun.

## Non-saving/full-information reading

Other technical sources argue that subsequent destruction shows the knowledge was not savingly transformative, though it caused genuine moral/religious reform.

### Project judgment

Do not decide the question by dictionary gloss.

The correct order:

1. concede strong knowledge;
2. observe its real moral effect;
3. examine subject chain and final nature imagery;
4. distinguish epistemic/religious privilege from new birth;
5. integrate the entire letter.

# 5. Who is the subject of 2:20–22?

This matters materially.

The immediate prior context distinguishes:

- false teachers;
- those whom they entice.

Technical literature has three readings.

## A. False teachers

This is the strongest continuity reading in the existing technical acquisition.

Reasons:

- v.19: false teachers are themselves slaves of corruption;
- Peter states that whatever overcomes someone enslaves him;
- v.20 then naturally explains how they became re-enslaved;
- vv.20–22 fit the teachers’ own apostasy trajectory.

Schreiner’s published treatment also integrates v.1 and vv.20–22 as descriptions of the same false teachers.

## B. Their victims/recent converts

Some commentaries see v.20 as applying especially to those just described as being enticed out of error.

This reading has contextual plausibility because vv.18–19 focus on targets being lured.

## C. General maxim applying to both

Others read conditional `if` generally: anyone who escapes and is again overcome.

### Current project weighting

> **False-teacher continuity is preferred, with vv.20–22 simultaneously serving as warning to their converts/victims.**

Confidence: `HIGH-MEDIUM`, not absolute.

Do not publish: «Greek grammar makes every other referent impossible.»

# 6. `πάλιν ἐμπλακέντες ἡττῶνται` — real reversal and defeat

The structure is severe:

- `πάλιν` — again;
- `ἐμπλακέντες` — having become entangled;
- `ἡττῶνται` — they are overcome/defeated.

The last verb connects directly with v.19:

> “by whatever a man is overcome, by this he is enslaved.”

### Therefore

The subjects are not merely tempted.

They are re-captured and defeated.

This makes 2:20 much stronger than:

> «после хорошего периода они совершили один тяжёлый грех».

The described movement is:

`escape → renewed entanglement → defeat/slavery`.

### Category control

This is why David/Peter/restorable believer cases must not be equated with this passage.

A believer who sins grievously but fights, confesses and returns is not thereby described as conquered by a renewed enslaving apostasy.

# 7. “The last state worse than the first” and Luke 11 / Matt 12

2 Pet 2:20 has a striking conceptual parallel with Jesus’ unclean-spirit saying:

- initial departure/cleaning;
- return;
- worse final condition.

Cross references commonly note Matt 12:43–45 / Luke 11:24–26.

MacArthur explicitly links the passages as examples of **reformation without regeneration**.

GTY:
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/42-161/the-danger-of-moral-reformation

### CANONICAL INFERENCE

The analogy is useful:

> externally real cleansing/reform can make later apostasy worse because greater light and privilege increase responsibility.

### Restriction

Peter does not explicitly cite Jesus’ saying here.

Do not claim a direct quotation unless literary dependence is established.

# 8. 2:21 — knowing “the way of righteousness” and turning back

Peter says:

> it would have been better not to have known `τὴν ὁδὸν τῆς δικαιοσύνης` than, having known it, `ἐπιστρέψαι` from the holy commandment delivered to them.

### Direct force

- the way was known;
- the holy command was received/delivered;
- direction was reversed.

This is **high-light apostasy**.

### What it establishes

It establishes real Christian teaching, moral understanding and accountability.

### What it does not lexically establish

It does not contain:

- δικαιόω;
- παλιγγενεσία;
- υἱοθεσία;
- σφραγίζω;
- language explicitly naming election unto glory.

That absence cannot by itself prove non-regeneration, but it blocks the claim that v.21 grammatically says “a justified man became unjustified.”

# 9. 2:22 — dog and washed sow: nature argument

The first proverb cites Prov 26:11:

> dog returns to its vomit.

The second:

> a sow, after washing, returns to wallowing in mire.

### Why Reformed interpreters emphasize nature

Peter does not say:

- sheep became dog;
- clean animal became intrinsically unclean species.

He says:

- dog behaves as dog;
- sow, although washed, returns to mud.

This strongly supports an inference of underlying continuity.

### But the washed sow matters

The sow **was washed**.

The proverb therefore itself acknowledges real cleansing at one level.

This is exactly why the strongest Reformed formulation is not:

> «никакой перемены не было».

but:

> **real cleansing/reformation occurred without the deepest nature-change that produces perseverance.**

### Calvin

Calvin explicitly treats the gospel as a laver/purgative that can cleanse people who later return like swine/dogs. He distinguishes such persons from faithful believers who may sin but are not conquered and do not renounce sound doctrine.

Primary:
- https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentaries_on_the_catholic_epistles/2_peter_2_20-22.htm
- CCEL: https://www.ccel.org/c/calvin/comment2/2peter.htm

This is much stronger than the caricature “Calvin says nothing real happened.”

# 10. MacArthur’s “reformation without regeneration” model

MacArthur reads 2:20–22 as people who:

- move into serious relationship with Christianity;
- understand the gospel;
- reduce/escape previous moral contamination;
- are externally and morally reformed;
- but lack inward regeneration;
- ultimately return and are overcome.

Direct sources:
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/81-144/christian-deconstruction-part-2
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/42-161/the-danger-of-moral-reformation
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/61-22/creatures-born-to-be-killed-part-3

### Strength

It explains:

- real escape;
- real knowledge;
- real washing;
- worse latter state;
- unchanged dog/sow identity.

### Weakness/pressure

When MacArthur paraphrases `ἐπίγνωσις` as merely “some idea about Christ” or “understand something,” that rhetoric is **weaker than Peter’s intra-letter vocabulary**.

The project should retain his **reformation/regeneration distinction** but not minimize `ἐπίγνωσις`.

### Better formulation

> They possessed deep Christian knowledge and a real morally transforming encounter with gospel truth, but final return raises the question whether that transformation ever reached regeneration.

# 11. Schreiner’s phenomenological model applied to vv.20–22

Schreiner integrates v.1 and vv.20–22.

He argues:

- the people truly looked like Christians;
- their knowledge/escape/profession was sufficiently deep to justify Christian description phenomenologically;
- final apostasy reveals spurious faith;
- dog/sow imagery is retrospective interpretive key.

Direct accessible chapter:
- https://www.monergism.com/%E2%80%9Cproblematic-texts%E2%80%9D-definite-atonement-pastoral-and-general-epistles?page=1

### Strength

This model reads the whole paragraph and preserves perseverance.

### Pressure

It must still explain why narrator-level statements:

- escaped;
- knew;
- turned back

should be treated as appearance-language rather than straightforward factual spiritual history.

The final animal proverbs supply evidence, but they do not explicitly attach an “appeared to” qualifier to every earlier predicate.

`SERIOUS REFORMED MODEL / CANONICAL-SYSTEMATIC INFERENCE / NOT DIRECT LEXICAL MEANING`.

# 12. Strong conditional-security reading

A strong Wesleyan/Arminian reading says:

1. `ἐπίγνωσις` is the same strong Christ-knowledge used positively in 2 Peter;
2. `ἀποφεύγω` is the same escape verb used for believers in 1:4;
3. they really escaped world pollution;
4. they really knew the way of righteousness;
5. they actually turned back;
6. they were again overcome;
7. last state is worse;
8. therefore formerly saved believers lost final salvation.

### Strengths

- takes each positive descriptor at full surface force;
- integrates vv.20–22 rather than isolating one word;
- takes the reversal language seriously;
- fits the “better never to have known” severity.

### Main disputed inference

The reading still equates:

`strong Christ-knowledge + real escape + washing + moral transformation`

with:

`regeneration + justification + irrevocable saving union`.

Peter does not make that equation explicitly.

The dog/sow imagery supplies a substantial reason to distinguish the two.

### Therefore

The conditional-security interpretation is **serious and textually motivated**, but loss of regeneration remains a systematic conclusion, not a single-word deduction.

# 13. Nature vs condition — one of the most useful distinctions in the dossier

Peter’s imagery allows a precise distinction:

## Condition can change profoundly

- escape defilements;
- knowledge of Christ;
- washed condition;
- membership/participation in Christian environment;
- moral reform.

## Underlying identity may remain unchanged

- dog remains dog;
- sow remains sow;
- eventual return manifests affinity.

### SYSTEMATIC SYNTHESIS

This supplies a strong biblical model for categories historically called:

- temporary faith;
- common operations of the Spirit;
- external/covenantal cleansing;
- moral reformation;
- non-final participation.

Peter does not use those later labels, but his imagery gives them conceptual room.

# 14. “Washed sow” blocks two opposite errors

## Error 1 — deny any real cleansing

False.

The sow is explicitly washed.

## Error 2 — infer regeneration from cleansing

Not necessary.

A washed sow is still a sow in the image.

### Therefore

The proverb itself teaches:

> **cleansing and nature are distinguishable categories.**

That is one of the strongest local supports for the Reformed non-regeneration reading — stronger than simply redefining `ἐπίγνωσις` as weak.

# 15. Relationship to 1 John 2:19

1 John 2:19 says final antichristological secession manifests that the seceders were not truly “of us.”

2 Pet 2:22 supplies a similar retrospective nature-revelation image.

### CANONICAL INFERENCE

The two passages can mutually support a category in which:

- participation is deep and visible;
- departure is real;
- final departure reveals a deeper non-belonging.

### Guardrail

Do not use 1 John 2:19 to rewrite Peter’s positive historical claims as unreal.

John says they genuinely “went out from us.” Peter says they genuinely escaped/learned/washed at the level he describes.

# 16. Relationship to James 5 and restoration

2 Peter 2 cannot be universalized to every backslider.

James 5:19–20 commands believers to turn back one who wanders from truth.

Gal 6 commands restoration of the overtaken brother.

Peter himself was restored after denial.

### Therefore

The distinctive elements in 2 Pet 2 are:

- false-teacher identity;
- destructive heresy;
- sensual exploitation;
- return to corruption;
- being overcome/enslaved;
- persistent final trajectory.

This is not merely “a Christian sinned badly.”

# 18. Preferred project synthesis

> **Second Peter 2:20–22 must not be neutralized. Peter attributes to the apostates a real escape from the world’s defilements, a substantial knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, knowledge of the way of righteousness, and an actual prior washing. They are then again entangled, defeated and enslaved; their latter state becomes worse than the first. Yet the final proverbs interpret this history through the continuing identities of dog and sow: the animal’s condition changed, but the image does not depict a sheep becoming a pig. This gives strong contextual support to the distinction between profound gospel-produced reformation and regeneration. Calvin and MacArthur both recognize a real cleansing/reformation while denying that it must equal saving new birth; Schreiner frames the same data phenomenologically, arguing that their final apostasy exposes spurious Christian identity. Conditional-security interpreters rightly resist any attempt to minimize `ἐπίγνωσις` or `ἀποφεύγω`, but the further equation of strong knowledge, escape and washing with prior justification/regeneration is a systematic inference rather than a lexical necessity. The text therefore teaches a terrifying category of deep Christian privilege, knowledge and transformation that can end in enslaving apostasy, while the dog/sow imagery provides substantial—but still interpretive—grounds for saying the deepest nature was never transformed.**
