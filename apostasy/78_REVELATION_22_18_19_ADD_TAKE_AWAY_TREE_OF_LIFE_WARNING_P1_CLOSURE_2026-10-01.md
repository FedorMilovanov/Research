# 1. Closure questions

Rev. 22:18–19 raises four distinct questions that must not be collapsed:

1. What does the warning against **adding to / taking away from** the words of this prophecy target?
2. Is the original reading in v.19 **tree of life** or **book of life**?
3. Does “God will take away his share” prove that a previously regenerate/elect person loses salvation?
4. Does the passage itself close the entire biblical canon, or immediately protect Revelation while also participating in a wider canonical no-tampering pattern?

Working verdict:

> **Rev. 22:18–19 is a real salvation-level covenantal/canonical sanction against deliberate falsification, rejection, addition to, or subtraction from the prophetic revelation entrusted in Revelation. The original text almost certainly reads “tree of life,” not the Textus Receptus/KJV “book of life.” Thus this verse must not be used as another proof text for a name being erased from the book of life. “God will take away his share from the tree of life and from the holy city” threatens exclusion from the New Creation and therefore eternal condemnation. Yet the wording does not narrate the prior regeneration of the offender; “share/portion” language can state the eschatological inheritance forfeited/denied without giving a complete prior ordo-salutis biography. The immediate scope is Revelation itself, while Deut. 4:2; 12:32; Prov. 30:5–6; Jer. 26:2 establish the broader canonical principle that revealed words are not subject to human editing.**

# 2. Greek text

Critical / THGNT / SBL-type text:

`Μαρτυρῶ ἐγὼ παντὶ τῷ ἀκούοντι τοὺς λόγους τῆς προφητείας τοῦ βιβλίου τούτου· ἐάν τις ἐπιθῇ ἐπ᾽ αὐτά, ἐπιθήσει ὁ θεὸς ἐπ᾽ αὐτὸν τὰς πληγὰς τὰς γεγραμμένας ἐν τῷ βιβλίῳ τούτῳ·`

`καὶ ἐάν τις ἀφέλῃ ἀπὸ τῶν λόγων τοῦ βιβλίου τῆς προφητείας ταύτης, ἀφελεῖ ὁ θεὸς τὸ μέρος αὐτοῦ ἀπὸ τοῦ ξύλου τῆς ζωῆς καὶ ἐκ τῆς πόλεως τῆς ἁγίας, τῶν γεγραμμένων ἐν τῷ βιβλίῳ τούτῳ.`

Core structure:

`human addition → God adds plagues`

`human subtraction → God subtracts his share from tree/city`

The rhetoric is deliberately talionic / measure-for-measure.

---

# 3. Morphology and force

## 3.1. `ἐάν τις ἐπιθῇ`

- `ἐάν` + aorist subjunctive;
- `ἐπιτίθημι` — add/put upon.

If anyone adds to these prophetic words.

## 3.2. `ἐπιθήσει ὁ θεὸς`

Future active indicative:

> God **will add** to that person the plagues written in the book.

The same verbal root creates judicial correspondence.

## 3.3. `ἐάν τις ἀφέλῃ`

- `ἀφαιρέω`;
- aorist subjunctive after `ἐάν`;
- remove/take away/subtract.

## 3.4. `ἀφελεῖ ὁ θεὸς`

Future active indicative:

> God **will take away**.

Again, action corresponds to offense.

## 3.5. `τὸ μέρος αὐτοῦ`

`μέρος` = part, share, portion, allotted lot.

Revelation itself uses `μέρος` with eschatological destiny:

- 20:6 — having a share in first resurrection;
- 21:8 — the wicked have their `μέρος` in the lake of fire;
- 22:19 — share/portion with respect to tree of life and holy city.

Therefore this is not a trivial ministry-reward noun.

It is **inheritance/destiny language**.

---

# 4. Textual variant: `ξύλου` (“tree”) vs `βίβλου` (“book”)

This must be settled before any soteriological use.

## Critical reading

`ἀπὸ τοῦ ξύλου τῆς ζωῆς`

> from the **tree of life**.

## Textus Receptus reading

`ἀπὸ βίβλου τῆς ζωῆς`

> from the **book of life**.

KJV and translations based directly on the TR therefore read “book of life.”

Modern critical translations and also the Byzantine/Majority Greek tradition read “tree of life.”

---

# 5. External evidence strongly favors “tree of life”

Modern manuscript collation overwhelmingly supports `ξύλου`.

The current digital ECM-based evidence summarized in manuscript databases reports dozens of Greek tree-reading witnesses across the tradition, including major early witnesses and the Byzantine stream.

The exact TR form `βίβλου` has no known pre-Erasmian Greek manuscript support.

A few late Greek witnesses/corrections preserve a **related** `βιβλίου` (“book”) reading, but these are late and at least some show dependence on or exposure to printed transmission.

### Important precision

Do not say:

> “No Greek manuscript has ever had any form of ‘book’.”

That is too broad.

Better:

> **The exact TR reading lacks early Greek manuscript support; the overwhelming Greek tradition reads ‘tree,’ while related ‘book’ forms occur in very late/corrected witnesses and the Latin tradition.**

---

# 6. Erasmus and the final six verses — primary historical fact

The manuscript Erasmus used for Revelation, now GA 2814 / Codex Reuchlini, lacked Rev. 22:16–21 from the point near the end of v.16 onward.

Jan Krans’ detailed study of Erasmus’s own statements establishes this directly.

Erasmus wrote that he **supplied the Greek from his Latin copies** rather than leave a gap.

Krans cites Erasmus’s own formulations such as:

- `ex Latinis adiecimus`;
- `ex nostris Latinis supplevimus Graeca`.

Thus the broad historical claim is not a Protestant/critical-text legend invented centuries later.

Erasmus himself acknowledged Latin-to-Greek supplementation.

### Source-policy note

Krans is used here as a specialist historian/textual critic for a narrow historical claim.

His theology/confessional position does not control this dossier.

---

# 7. Why “book” entered Erasmus’s Greek text

The Latin tradition included both:

- `ligno vitae` — tree of life;
- `libro vitae` — book of life.

A plausible Latin transmission error is `ligno` → `libro`, especially because “book” language occurs repeatedly in the same verse and “book of life” is already a biblical phrase.

Erasmus’s Latin Vorlage represented the “book” reading, and his retroversion produced Greek `βίβλου`.

NET’s textual note correctly explains this history in accessible form.

Krans’ academic study confirms the broader Latin-retroversion history and demonstrates that the supposed early Greek defenses of the TR reading do not overturn it.

---

# 8. Internal evidence also favors “tree”

Revelation 22 itself has just described:

- tree of life — 22:2;
- right/access to tree of life — 22:14;
- holy city — 21:2 onward / 22:14.

Then 22:19 pairs:

- tree of life;
- holy city.

These are precisely the two New Creation blessings immediately in view.

Krans also notes that “take away his **share** from the tree of life” fits Revelation’s inheritance imagery naturally, whereas “take away his share from the **book** of life” is atypical: elsewhere Revelation speaks of **names written in** or **not written in** the book, and Rev. 3:5 speaks of **erasing a name**, not removing a “share” from the book.

### High-confidence text-critical conclusion

`TREE OF LIFE = ORIGINAL / GREEN`.

`BOOK OF LIFE = TR/LATIN-DERIVED SECONDARY READING / RED for original wording`.

---

# 9. This corrects a major apostasy proof-text error

Because the KJV/TR reads “book of life,” the verse is sometimes used to argue:

> “Here Scripture explicitly says God removes a believer’s part from the book of life; therefore names are erased and salvation is lost.”

But the original reading is **tree of life**.

Therefore:

> **Rev. 22:19 must not be used as a second ‘book of life erasure’ text alongside Rev. 3:5.**

The actual warning is no less severe.

It is simply a different image:

> exclusion from the tree of life and the holy city.

---

# 10. Severity remains salvation-level under either textual reading

Correcting `book` to `tree` does not weaken the judgment.

The tree of life and holy city in Rev. 21–22 are the blessings of final New Creation.

The opposite destiny is outside the city / second death / lake of fire.

Bryan Murphy (TMS) correctly states that tree of life and holy city are descriptions of eternal life and New Creation blessing and concludes that the tamperer faces eternal condemnation.

Robert Thomas similarly treats the two inheritances as the comprehensive blessings promised to the saints in Rev. 21–22.

### Therefore

The text is **not** merely about:

- loss of a sermon opportunity;
- lesser millennial rank;
- temporal chastisement only.

It is an ultimate salvation/judgment warning.

---

# 11. But does `his share` prove previous regenerate possession?

No — not by itself.

The phrase says God will take away:

`τὸ μέρος αὐτοῦ`

with respect to the tree/city.

It does not narrate:

- when this share was acquired;
- whether it means realized possession or claimed/anticipated inheritance;
- whether the offender was regenerate;
- whether the offender was elect;
- whether forensic justification had already occurred and was then reversed.

Compare ordinary inheritance/judgment language:

- a person may be said to lose a promised/claimed portion without the text asserting that final possession had already been irrevocably conferred;
- Rev. 21:8 says the wicked have their `μέρος` in the lake of fire — `μέρος` can denote eschatological lot/destiny.

### Safe conclusion

> **The person who finally falsifies/subtracts from the prophecy will have no share in the eschatological life it promises.**

That is direct.

The person’s prior regenerative biography is not.

---

# 12. Who is targeted? “Everyone who hears” — not merely professional scribes

22:18 addresses:

`παντὶ τῷ ἀκούοντι`

> everyone hearing the words of the prophecy.

This matters.

The warning cannot be restricted to:

> “a copyist who accidentally skips a line.”

Bryan Murphy notes that the immediate audience category is hearers, not simply copyists.

Revelation begins similarly:

- blessed is the one who reads;
- blessed are those who hear;
- and keep what is written (1:3).

It ends with the matching negative sanction against falsifying the message.

### Literary frame

`1:3 blessing for hear/keep`

↕

`22:18–19 curse for add/subtract`

This strongly supports a **reception/obedience/teaching** dimension, not merely manuscript mechanics.

---

# 13. What does “add/take away” include?

There are three major proposals.

## A. Physical corruption of the written text

Certainly included at principle level.

Deliberately rewriting the revelation is a direct form of adding/subtracting.

## B. Prohibition of further prophecy / canonical closure

TMS/Thomas strongly develop this implication.

There is real canonical significance because the book self-identifies as prophecy and the warning occurs at the end of the final canonical book.

But immediate syntax says first:

> do not add to / subtract from **this prophecy/book**.

The entire canon-closure doctrine should not rest on an anachronistic assumption that first hearers possessed a 66-book codex with Revelation physically last.

## C. Falsifying the prophecy by teaching/interpretation/obedience

Strongly supported by:

- audience = hearers;
- Revelation’s concern with false prophets/teachers (2:14–15, 20);
- Deuteronomic no-tampering background adjacent to false-prophet warnings;
- blessing on hearing and keeping, not merely copying.

### Best synthesis

> **The direct prohibition protects the integrity and authoritative message of Revelation: no one may alter its wording or falsify/revise its prophetic content. As canonical Scripture, the same divine no-tampering principle extends to all inspired revelation. Canon closure is a theological/canonical implication, not the bare lexical meaning of `this book`.**

---

# 14. Textual criticism is NOT forbidden by this passage

A superficial objection says:

> “If Rev. 22:18–19 forbids changing words, textual critics sin whenever they reject the KJV/TR wording.”

This reverses the passage’s purpose.

If God forbids altering His revelation, then interpreters must seek to know **what He originally caused to be written**.

Bryan Murphy (TMS) explicitly draws this implication: the unalterability of the inspired text justifies careful textual criticism aimed at recovering/confirming the autographic wording.

The Rev. 22:19 variant itself is the perfect demonstration.

Retaining a demonstrably secondary Latin-derived reading merely because it is familiar is not fidelity to the original wording.

### Important distinction

- **textual criticism** asks what was originally written;
- **tampering** knowingly adds/removes/falsifies what God gave.

They are opposite aims.

---

# 15. Accidental scribal errors and translation differences are not automatically damnable apostasy

The warning must not be weaponized against:

- an accidental copyist omission;
- a typographical error;
- a good-faith translation decision;
- an honest disputed textual variant;
- an interpreter who reaches a non-heretical but mistaken conclusion.

The severity of the sanction and broader context point toward **culpable falsification/rejection** rather than human fallibility as such.

### Why

If every accidental omission fulfilled v.19 literally as final apostasy, the history of manuscript transmission would make salvation depend on flawless copying technique.

That is neither stated nor coherent with the broader NT.

### Publication formulation

> **The passage targets willful or culpable handling that changes the prophetic message against its divine authority, not the mere existence of textual transmission problems.**

---

# 16. Deuteronomy 4:2 — covenant-document background

Deut. 4:2:

> do not add to the word I command you and do not take from it, so that you may keep YHWH’s commandments.

Important features:

- covenant/legal setting;
- divine speech is fixed;
- alteration would change the obligations of the covenant;
- hearing/keeping is the goal.

Bryan Murphy’s TMS study treats this as an ANE-style no-tampering clause embedded in covenant-document authority.

### Connection to Revelation

Both texts combine:

- authoritative divine words;
- hear/obey setting;
- no adding/subtracting;
- covenant blessings/judgments.

This is not accidental vocabulary overlap.

---

# 17. Deuteronomy 12:32 and false-prophet context

Deut. 12:32 repeats:

> whatever I command, be careful to do; do not add or take away.

Immediately following, Deut. 13 warns about sign-working false prophets who entice Israel toward other gods.

This is strikingly relevant to Revelation:

- false signs;
- false prophets;
- idolatrous pressure;
- beast worship;
- Jezebel/Balaam-type teaching;
- need to preserve divine revelation.

### Canonical insight

Adding/subtracting is not mere editorial pedantry.

It can become the doctrinal mechanism by which idolatry is legitimized.

---

# 18. Proverbs 30:5–6

Every word of God is tested/pure.

Therefore:

> do not add to His words lest He rebuke you and you be found a liar.

Here the no-addition principle extends beyond one Mosaic legal clause into wisdom theology of divine speech.

TMS’s Bryan Murphy emphasizes the movement from `every word` to the prohibition on human addition.

### Apostasy relevance

A false teacher may preserve biblical vocabulary while inserting claims God did not make.

This is **addition by theological authority**, not only addition by ink.

---

# 19. Jeremiah 26:2 — do not diminish a word

YHWH commands Jeremiah to speak all He commands and:

> do not diminish/omit a word.

The prophet himself has no authority to edit revelation to make it safer or more acceptable.

### Practical implication

The problem is especially acute for teachers/preachers:

- softening judgment texts because an audience dislikes them;
- adding divine authority to human opinions;
- omitting doctrines for cultural approval;
- claiming “God told me” beyond revelation.

These actions differ in degree and culpability, but Revelation’s closing warning establishes the direction of danger.

# 20. Does Rev. 22:18–19 by itself close the 66-book canon?

Immediate answer:

> **Its direct grammatical scope is the prophecy/book of Revelation.**

It does not say:

> “the collection of exactly 66 books is now complete.”

Yet canonical placement and biblical pattern permit a broader theological inference.

## TMS / Thomas argument

Bryan Murphy, following Robert Thomas, argues:

- Deuteronomy establishes no-tampering for Mosaic revelation;
- Proverbs generalizes purity of divine words;
- Jeremiah forbids prophetic omission;
- Revelation closes its prophecy with final no-tampering sanction;
- Revelation is the final prophetic witness of the apostolic era;
- therefore the principle coheres with cessation of new canonical revelation.

Thomas’s JETS article “The Spiritual Gift of Prophecy in Rev 22:18” explicitly develops the closure implication.

### Needed nuance

The doctrine of a closed canon rests on more than one local phrase:

- apostolic/foundational revelation;
- completion of the apostolic witness;
- once-for-all faith entrusted to saints;
- historical recognition of the apostolic canon;
- Revelation’s own closure formula.

Thus:

> **Rev. 22:18–19 is a major canonical-closure text, but its first exegetical referent remains Revelation itself.**

---

# 21. The speaker: Jesus or John?

The immediate context alternates voices, so precise speaker assignment in vv.18–19 has been discussed.

Bryan Murphy argues for Jesus because:

- Jesus speaks explicitly in 22:12–16;
- 22:20’s “He who testifies” says “Surely I am coming quickly,” clearly Christ;
- the `I testify` in v.18 can naturally belong to the same speaker.

Others take John as prophetic witness speaking with divine authority.

### Theological impact

Minimal for the warning’s authority.

Whether Jesus directly voices it or John speaks as inspired prophet, the sanction is presented as divine and God executes it.

### Publication wording

Prefer:

> **The closing divine/prohetic witness solemnly testifies...**

unless speaker identification is specifically being argued.

---

# 22. Reformed perseverance reading

The strongest Reformed interpretation should not say:

> “The threat cannot happen to elect people, so it is not serious.”

Instead:

1. the threat is objectively real;
2. eternal exclusion is genuinely threatened;
3. deliberate mutilation/rejection of revelation is incompatible with persevering faith;
4. God uses this very warning to prevent His people from joining that apostasy;
5. a person who finally demonstrates contempt for Christ’s revelation manifests a non-persevering relation to the faith.

Robert Thomas’s conservative line, as reported in his Revelation commentary tradition, treats deliberate tampering as conduct inconsistent with genuine faith.

### Important refinement

Do not reduce this to:

> “a true believer is metaphysically incapable of ever making a textual mistake.”

The issue is final culpable falsification/rejection, not fallible scholarship.

---

# 23. Conditional-security reading represented fairly

A strong conditional reading argues:

- warning is addressed to hearers/churches;
- “his share” in tree/city sounds like a real inheritance relation;
- God can take that share away if one finally corrupts/rejects the prophecy;
- therefore a real believer may forfeit final salvation by apostasy.

Grant Osborne is frequently cited in this direction: distortion of the message can become apostasy and exclusion from eternal life.

### Strength

Takes the salvation-level sanction at full seriousness.

### Main burden

The phrase does not specify prior regeneration.

Conditional security must establish that from broader canonical data, not from `μέρος` alone.

### Important correction

Conditional-security argument must use **tree of life**, not build its case on the secondary TR “book of life.”

---

# 24. Free Grace / reward-only reading

A Free Grace reading may say:

- an eternally secure believer could mishandle Revelation;
- God therefore threatens severe discipline/reward loss;
- `share` could be an inheritance/reward share rather than eternal life itself.

### Exegetical pressure

In Revelation 21–22:

- tree of life;
- holy city;
- outside/inside contrast;
- second death/lake of fire

are final salvation/New Creation categories.

Bryan Murphy/TMS correctly reads v.19’s pair as eternal-life blessing.

Therefore a purely optional-reward reading is difficult to sustain unless it can demonstrate that someone excluded from tree and holy city nevertheless remains saved elsewhere — a category Revelation itself does not supply.

### Verdict

`REWARD-ONLY = LOW PLAUSIBILITY` for v.19’s ultimate sanction.

---

# 25. Why the offender’s prior state remains underdetermined

The verse tells us the offender’s **final loss/exclusion**.

It does not tell us:

- whether he was regenerate;
- whether he possessed temporary faith;
- whether he was merely a professing hearer;
- whether his “share” is covenant claim/expected inheritance;
- whether the act reveals a prior false profession.

### General project rule

> **A final-state sanction does not automatically give a complete biography of prior saving status.**

This rule already controls:

- Matt. 7 (“I never knew you” explicitly does give a prior-state clue);
- Judas;
- Heb. 6/10;
- 2 Pet. 2;
- Rev. 3:5;
- Rev. 22:19.

Each must be adjudicated on its own wording.

---

# 26. Revelation’s own truth/falsification theme

The closing warning is not isolated.

Revelation contrasts:

- true and faithful words;
- lying deception;
- false prophet;
- beast propaganda;
- those who love/practice falsehood;
- prophetic testimony.

Immediately before the warning:

22:15 excludes `πᾶς φιλῶν καὶ ποιῶν ψεῦδος`

— everyone loving and practicing falsehood.

### Therefore

Tampering with prophecy is part of the book’s larger moral conflict:

> truth of Lamb vs lie of dragon/beast/false prophet.

It is not merely a publishing-house rule.

---

# 27. “Tree of life + holy city” recapitulates the New Creation

Rev. 22:19 is positioned after:

- New Jerusalem description;
- river of life;
- tree of life;
- no curse;
- God’s face;
- eternal reign;
- invitation to drink water of life freely.

The judgment matches exactly what the book has just offered.

### Measure-for-measure logic

If one subtracts from God’s prophetic promise/command:

> God subtracts the person from the promised inheritance.

This is literary, covenantal and eschatological justice.

# 28. Relation to Rev. 22:14 textual variant

An adjacent major textual issue exists in 22:14:

- critical text: “those who wash their robes”;
- TR/Byzantine-related tradition: “those who do His commandments.”

This dossier does not adjudicate that variant fully.

But it reinforces one methodological lesson:

> **The closing chapter of Revelation has significant textual history; therefore theological arguments must state which Greek reading they are using.**

Do not quote KJV/TR in 22:19 and silently treat “book of life” as uncontested Greek.

# 30. What Rev. 22:18–19 establishes with high confidence

1. Revelation’s prophetic words are divinely authoritative and may not be deliberately altered.
2. Adding and subtracting receive measure-for-measure divine sanctions.
3. The warning is directed to hearers, not merely professional scribes.
4. The authentic reading in v.19 is overwhelmingly `tree of life`, not TR `book of life`.
5. Erasmus retroverted the missing Rev. 22:16–21 material from Latin into Greek.
6. The `book` reading therefore must not control apostasy theology.
7. Tree of life + holy city are final New Creation / eternal-life blessings.
8. The sanction in v.19 is salvation-level / eternal-judgment level.
9. `μέρος` means share/portion/lot and fits eschatological inheritance language.
10. The verse does not state the offender’s prior regenerate/elect status.
11. Honest textual criticism is not the sin prohibited; it seeks the original wording the warning tells us to preserve.
12. Accidental textual/translation error is not automatically final apostasy.
13. The immediate scope is Revelation itself.
14. Deut. 4 / 12, Prov. 30 and Jer. 26 establish a broader canonical no-tampering principle.
15. The passage strongly supports the finality/inviolability of canonical revelation when integrated with broader canon doctrine, though `this book` lexically means Revelation.

# 31. What it does NOT establish by itself

It does not establish:

- that the original text says “book of life”;
- that an elect name is erased from the Lamb’s book;
- that every copyist typo damns;
- that textual criticism violates Scripture;
- that every translation disagreement is apostasy;
- that every mistaken interpretation is equivalent to deliberate subtraction;
- that all offenders were previously regenerate;
- that the warning is merely reward loss;
- that Rev. 22:18–19 alone proves all details of the 66-book canon’s historical recognition;
- that a new prophecy claimant can evade the principle merely by not writing words into Revelation’s physical manuscript;
- that every later theological formulation is an illicit “addition” simply because its exact sentence is not verbatim in Scripture.

# 34. Final closure sentence

> **Последнее предупреждение Библии нельзя строить на вторичном чтении. В Откр. 22:19 исходный греческий текст говорит не о “книге жизни”, а о “дереве жизни”: человек, который отнимает от слов пророчества, сам лишается своей доли в дереве жизни и святом городе — то есть в благословениях окончательной новой твари. Это угроза вечного суда, а не мелкой потери награды. Но она не рассказывает нам автоматически, что нарушитель прежде был возрождён и затем раз-возродился: текст сообщает его окончательный удел, а не полную историю его ordo salutis. Особенно важно, что восстановление исходного `tree of life` через текстуальную критику не нарушает запрет “не отнимай”; напротив, запрет требует как можно точнее хранить именно те слова, которые Бог дал, а не позднее знакомое чтение.**
