# 2. Acts 7:25–39 — rejection of God’s appointed deliverer

Stephen deliberately presents Moses positively and Israel’s response negatively.

A modern study of Luke’s Jewish roots cites:

- Eckhard J. Schnabel, *Acts*, p. 375;
- Darrell L. Bock, *Acts*, p. 293.

The Schnabel observation quoted there is that Moses is characterized positively as a savior sent by God while Israel is described as failing to understand divine salvation.

This is important because Stephen later repeats the same rejection pattern after Moses’ commissioning:

- God appoints Moses ruler/redeemer;
- Moses performs signs;
- Moses receives living oracles;
- the fathers refuse obedience;
- push Moses away;
- turn in their hearts to Egypt.

### Source discovery / locator confirmation

- Schnabel, *Acts*, p. 375.
- Bock, *Acts*, p. 293.

Secondary locator witness:
Christopher J. H. Wright-style Luke/Jewish-roots study accessible online, citing both exact pages.

Publication rule: quote Schnabel/Bock only after exact edition text is checked if verbatim wording is used. Page locator itself is now **GREEN**; exact quotation remains `VERIFY BEFORE VERBATIM`.

# 3. Acts 7:39 — `ἐστράφησαν ... ταῖς καρδίαις ... εἰς Αἴγυπτον`

The decisive feature is not only physical memory of Egypt but **heart-direction**.

Stephen says Israel:

- refused obedience (`οὐκ ἠθέλησαν ὑπήκοοι γενέσθαι`);
- rejected/pushed Moses away;
- `ἐστράφησαν ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις αὐτῶν εἰς Αἴγυπτον`.

Thus:

> **their bodies had left Egypt while their hearts turned back to Egypt.**

This is one of the strongest canonical examples for the project’s “heart apostasy precedes/grounds outward apostasy” category.

The next verses interpret the inward movement externally:

`heart-return → demand for gods → calf → sacrifice → rejoicing in works of hands`.

### Important lexical control

The `στρέφω` movement language does not encode soteriology by itself.

Its force here comes from:

- object: Egypt;
- context: refusal of Moses;
- result: idolatry.

Status: **GREEN**.

# 4. Acts 7:41 — idolatry as enacted inward return

Stephen’s sequence should not be broken apart.

The calf is not a random later sin unrelated to v.39.

The narration makes it the public expression of the prior heart-turn:

`Egypt in heart → alternative gods → idol → sacrifice → joy in human handiwork`.

This helps the wider apostasy taxonomy:

> inward allegiance can reverse before geographical/social status visibly changes.

It also guards against reducing apostasy to a merely formal change of church membership or explicit verbal renunciation.

---

# 5. Acts 7:42 — people turn; God turns

After Israel turns in heart, Luke writes:

`ἔστρεψεν δὲ ὁ θεὸς καὶ παρέδωκεν αὐτοὺς λατρεύειν τῇ στρατιᾷ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ`

> God turned and gave them over to serve the host of heaven.

The rhetorical reversal is striking:

`Israel turns away/back → God turns away and gives over`.

## Judicial handing-over

`παρέδωκεν` belongs naturally with biblical judicial handing-over patterns:

- Psalm 81:11–12;
- Romans 1:24, 26, 28;
- 2 Thessalonians 2:10–12.

This is more than passive divine non-intervention.

Luke presents God as judicially handing them over to the idolatry they chose.

### Guardrail

Acts 7 narrates a corporate-historical judgment trajectory. Do not mechanically infer that every individual act of idolatry equals final irreversible reprobation.

Status: **GREEN**.

# 6. Acts 7:42–43 — Amos 5:25–27

Stephen quotes the Twelve/Amos to extend the calf incident into a larger history of idolatry and exile.

The logic is:

`wilderness rebellion → continuing idolatrous worship → prophetic indictment → exile`.

This prevents an interpretation in which the golden calf was a one-off aberration with no later trajectory.

# 7. “Beyond Babylon” vs Amos “beyond Damascus”

This remains a real quotation/textual problem and must be shown openly.

## Data

Amos 5:27 MT/LXX horizon is Damascus.

Acts 7:43 in the standard Greek text says Babylon.

## Exact modern locator confirmation

A Southern Baptist Theological Seminary dissertation discussing the Lukan textual/quotation issue cites:

- Darrell L. Bock, *Acts*, p. 452;
- David G. Peterson, *The Acts of the Apostles*, p. 387 n. 61;
- Metzger, *Textual Commentary*, p. 357;

in the discussion of the textual relationship and why the divergent Acts reading should not simply be harmonized away.

This is useful because it confirms exact page targets in conservative major Acts commentaries.

## Serious explanatory options

1. Luke/Stephen intentionally updates/amplifies Amos’s exile horizon to the historically climactic Babylonian exile.
2. “Beyond Babylon” summarizes the actual destination of Israel/Judah’s exile history and therefore extends rather than contradicts Amos’s threat.
3. Luke uses a textual/traditional form different from surviving MT/LXX witnesses.
4. scribal explanation/harmonization theories.

### Current adjudication

The project should say only what is secure:

- Stephen invokes Amos’s idolatry-to-exile logic;
- Acts deliberately reads “Babylon” in the received critical text;
- the precise mechanism behind the alteration is disputed.

Do **not** write:

> “Luke definitely changed Damascus to Babylon for reason X”

until Bock/Peterson/Schnabel/Metzger are checked directly at the exact pages.

Status: **AMBER reason; GREEN existence of textual difference and locator targets**.

# 8. Acts 7:51 — “always resisting the Holy Spirit”

Stephen’s climax combines major OT covenant-rebellion images:

- stiff-necked;
- uncircumcised hearts;
- uncircumcised ears;
- always resisting Holy Spirit.

Background:

- Exod. 32:9; 33:3,5 — stiff neck;
- Lev. 26:41; Deut. 10:16; Jer. 4:4; 9:25–26 — uncircumcised heart;
- prophetic refusal-to-hear language;
- Isa. 63:10 — rebellion/grieving Holy Spirit.

## Meaning in Stephen’s speech

“Resist the Holy Spirit” is concretely tied to resistance to the Spirit’s word through God’s messengers:

- fathers persecuted prophets;
- present hearers betrayed/murdered the Righteous One;
- they received law and did not keep it.

Calvin’s precision is useful here: the verse particularly concerns resistance to the Spirit speaking in prophetic ministry/Word, not a complete standalone systematic treatment of every mode of resisting the Spirit.

### Pastoral force

Long possession of Scripture, temple tradition, covenant heritage and theological vocabulary can coexist with resistance to the God who speaks through Scripture.

Status: **GREEN**.

# 11. What this contributes to whole-canon apostasy theology

Acts 7 yields a high-value sequence:

1. privilege/revelation can be real;
2. obedience can still be refused;
3. God’s appointed messenger can be rejected while religious identity is maintained;
4. heart direction can reverse before outward identity changes;
5. inner return becomes idolatrous practice;
6. persistent idolatry may receive judicial handing-over;
7. prophetic resistance can become a transgenerational corporate pattern;
8. the climax is not “lack of information” but resistance to Spirit-given revelation.

### Not proven by Acts 7 alone

- former individual regeneration of every wilderness Israelite;
- loss of justification by a regenerate person;
- a numerical threshold after which repentance is impossible;
- ethnic condemnation of Jewish people as such.

Stephen says “our fathers”; the indictment is covenantal and moral, not racial.
