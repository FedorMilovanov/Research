# 1. Главная идея серии

Название серии:

# **ОТСТУПНИКИ**

Подзаголовок/hub-line:

> **От падения Петра и предательства Иуды до отпавших времён гонений: что Библия называет отступничеством, кого можно вернуть и как Бог сохраняет Своих.**

Это должна быть не серия изолированных prooftexts о «можно ли потерять спасение», а цельная драматургия:

1. определить категорию;
2. показать реальные библейские типы;
3. провести границу между тяжёлым падением и окончательным отступничеством;
4. проверить категории на реальной истории Церкви;
5. закончить церковной/личной практикой.

Главная pastoral tension:

> **не назвать Иуду Петром и не назвать Петра Иудой.**

То есть:

- не утешать человека, который сознательно и окончательно отвергает Христа, будто предупреждения ничего не значат;
- не объявлять погибшим человека, который тяжело пал, отрёкся под страхом, блуждает или оказался под влиянием заблуждения, пока Scripture оставляет путь призыва, покаяния и восстановления.

# 3. Часть I — **Что такое отступничество?**

## Рабочий H1

# **Отступничество: что Библия на самом деле называет отпадением от веры**

## Главный вопрос

Что такое apostasy в Библии и почему нельзя сваливать в одно слово:

- любой грех;
- тяжёлое падение;
- временную веру;
- корпоративную неверность;
- лжеучение;
- practical denial;
- окончательное отвержение Христа?

## Opening hook

Один человек трижды говорит, что не знает Христа, и становится апостолом-проповедником Пятидесятницы.

Другой три года ходит со Христом, проповедует, изгоняет бесов, участвует в апостольском служении — и уходит «в своё место».

Если обоих просто назвать «отступниками», мы потеряем половину библейского учения.

## Обязательные блоки

### 1. Слова и образы

Hebrew:

- `שוב`;
- `סור`;
- `עזב`;
- `מעל`;
- `משובה`;
- `זנה`.

Greek:

- `ἀφίστημι`;
- `ἀποστασία`;
- `παραπίπτω`;
- `πλανάω`;
- `ὑποστρέφω / ἐπιστρέφω`;
- `σκληρύνω`;
- related directional/status vocabulary.

Central rule:

> **никакой “one word → full ordo salutis”.**

### 2. Минимум 8 различных категорий

1. обычный грех/уклонение;
2. тяжёлое падение верующего;
3. обратимое блуждание;
4. временная вера;
5. внешние/заветные привилегии без доказанного saving union;
6. doctrinal departure;
7. practical denial при profession;
8. corporate/national apostasy;
9. progressive hardening;
10. terminal/final apostasy там, где текст действительно позволяет такой вывод.

### 3. Whole-Bible line

Коротко, но канонически:

- Eden;
- wilderness generation;
- Judges;
- Saul / Solomon / Jeroboam;
- prophets;
- Jesus’ warning parables;
- Judas / Peter;
- Acts/Pastorals;
- Hebrews;
- 2 Peter/Jude;
- Revelation.

### 4. Предупреждения и сохранение

Показать сразу, что Bible не заставляет выбирать:

- либо warnings real;
- либо preservation real.

Jude is compact control:

`kept → keep yourselves → rescue others → God able to keep`.

### 5. False-positive warning

Joshua 22 как обязательный box:

> Израиль употребил тяжёлый `מעל / שוב / מרד` язык против восточных колен, но после расследования Финеес снял обвинение.

То есть ревность о верности должна включать ревность о правде обвинения.

## Центральный тезис части

> **Отступничество — не один момент и не одна лексема, а семейство реально различающихся библейских явлений. Главная задача — определить, что именно происходит в каждом тексте и каждом человеке.**

# 4. Часть II — **Отступники Библии**

## Рабочий H1

# **Иуда, Саул, Димас и “грех Иеровоама”: отступники Библии и разные дороги к погибели**

Альтернативный более сильный H1:

# **Отступники Библии: Иуда, Саул, Димас, лжеучители и отступивший Израиль**

## Job

Не делать gallery of villains.

Показать **разные механизмы и уровни**, чтобы слово «отступник» перестало быть плоским.

## Core cases

### A. Иуда Искариот — proximity without saving faith

- chosen to apostolic office;
- ministry/share in ministry;
- John 6:64 — Jesus knew from beginning who did not believe;
- John 12 hidden greed;
- John 13 not all clean;
- Matt 27 remorse;
- Acts 1 ministry-share + final replacement.

Central distinction:

> office / gifts / proximity / ministry ≠ saving election automatically.

### B. Саул — privilege, Spirit-empowerment, rejection and hardening

Use `81_...SAUL...`:

- another heart;
- Spirit really upon him;
- `שב מאחרי`;
- rejected from kingship;
- Spirit departs in royal-context transfer;
- repeated resistance;
- massacre of priests;
- medium;
- `מעל` in 1 Chr 10;
- final-state restraint.

Important:

> severe trajectory, but do not claim narrator gives a clean eternal-state prooftext.

### C. Иеровоам — when apostasy becomes a system

Use `84_...JEROBOAM...`:

- political fear;
- calves / exodus vocabulary;
- alternative sanctuary/priesthood/calendar;
- religion invented “from his heart”;
- refused to turn after prophetic sign;
- “made Israel sin”;
- system outlives founder.

### D. Израиль — corporate apostasy

From Judges → Kings → Prophets:

- forgetfulness;
- idolatry;
- syncretism;
- repeated call to return;
- exile.

This must be distinguished from hidden regeneration status of each Israelite.

### E. Димас

- once co-worker;
- later abandoned Paul, loving present age;
- final-state not explicitly supplied.

Use as restraint case, not Judas clone.

### F. Hymenaeus / Alexander / Philetus

- shipwreck concerning faith;
- blasphemy;
- doctrinal gangrene;
- faith of some upset;
- corrective/disciplinary language remains in some passages.

### G. 2 Peter/Jude false teachers

- strong privilege/knowledge language;
- turning back;
- no Spirit in Jude 19;
- destructive teaching + sensuality/covetousness.

## Mechanism boxes

1. fear of people — Saul;
2. fear of losing power — Jeroboam;
3. money — Judas/Balaam/1 Tim 6;
4. love of present age — Demas;
5. doctrinal novelty — Pastorals;
6. religious self-assurance — Micah/Jeroboam;
7. repeated warning → hardening — Israel/Saul.

## Part-II conclusion

> **Библия не даёт одного психологического портрета “отступника”. Кто-то никогда не имел спасительной веры, кто-то имел огромные covenant/ministry privileges, кто-то становится проводником ложного учения, а корпоративная община может десятилетиями сохранять религиозный язык и одновременно уходить от Бога.**

# 5. Часть III — **Пётр и Иуда: падение, отречение, покаяние и возвращение**

## Рабочий H1

# **Пётр и Иуда: почему одно отречение было восстановлено, а другое предательство стало погибелью?**

Альтернативный H1:

# **Падение или отступничество? Пётр, Иуда и те, кого ещё можно вернуть**

## This is the theological center of the entire series

Part I defines.

Part II displays severe/terminal patterns.

Part III answers the pastoral question everybody actually asks:

> «Если человек реально отрёкся, ушёл, тяжело согрешил — всё уже кончено?»

## A. Peter case in detail

### Before denial

Luke 22:31–32:

- Satan demands to sift;
- Christ prays **for Peter**;
- “that your faith may not fail”;
- “when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers.”

Important:

> Christ predicts both real fall and real return before it occurs.

### Denial

Not trivial:

- repeated;
- public;
- oath/cursing elements in Synoptics;
- explicit denial of knowing Christ.

No sentimental minimization.

### Return

- bitter weeping;
- resurrection appearance;
- John 21 restoration/commission;
- Acts public confession and ministry.

### Theological result

> Peter’s denial proves that **real verbal denial under fear is not automatically identical with final apostasy**.

## B. Judas comparison

Do not reduce contrast to:

> Peter cried, Judas did not.

Deeper canonical differences:

1. Jesus says Peter’s faith will not finally fail;
2. Jesus says Judas is unbelieving from earlier internal controls (John 6:64);
3. Peter’s act occurs under acute fear/pressure and is followed by return;
4. Judas’s long hidden greed, betrayal trajectory and final action are part of different biography;
5. Peter is restored to brothers/ministry;
6. Judas’s remorse `μεταμεληθείς` does not culminate in return to Christ/community but self-destruction.

### Important anti-formula

Do not teach:

> “real Christian can deny Christ as much as he wants because Peter did.”

Peter’s case is not licence; it is a testimony to preserving intercession and restoring grace.

## C. Other biblical return/recovery cases

### James 5:19–20

A person **among you** wanders from truth and can be turned back.

Church task is rescue, not instant terminal diagnosis.

### Gal 6:1

Restore a person caught in transgression with gentleness, watching yourself.

### Jude 22–23

Mercy/rescue/snatching from fire; textual variants do not remove stable rescue-kernel.

### 2 Tim 2:24–26

Correction in hope God grants repentance so opponents escape devil’s snare.

### Manasseh

Exceptionally severe OT rebellion followed by humbling/return/reform in 2 Chron 33.

Useful against despair after grave sin.

### David

Grievous moral collapse, concealment, prophetic confrontation, confession, discipline, restoration.

Not apostasy proper, but essential contrast.

## D. Israel as macro-case of apostasy + divine restoration

This belongs **here**, not only Part II.

Old Testament rhythm:

- apostasy;
- prophetic “return”;
- exile/judgment;
- remnant;
- promise of heart renewal/new covenant.

Major texts:

- Deut 30;
- Jer 3 / 31;
- Ezek 36–37;
- Hos 14;
- Zech 12–13;
- Rom 11.

### Romans 11 editorial rule

The reader-facing article should state interpretation fairly:

> Paul says a partial hardening has happened to Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles comes in, and “thus all Israel will be saved.” A major historic Reformed and contemporary conservative reading understands this as a future large-scale conversion of ethnic Israel; other Reformed interpreters understand “all Israel” differently. The series should defend the preferred interpretation from Romans 11 rather than present it as though no exegetical debate exists.

### TMS/MacArthur line

TMS explicitly teaches future national Israel’s repentance/salvation and ties Rom 11:25–29 to new-covenant fulfillment; MacArthur does likewise.

### Historical Reformed line

Future Jewish conversion is not merely modern dispensational invention; the view appears among Calvin, Bucer, Beza, many Puritans, Gill, Edwards and others, though precise models differ.

## Part-III thesis

> **The decisive contrast is not “one never stumbled, the other did.” Both Peter and Israel show that severe departure can be followed by divine restoration. Final apostasy is distinguished not merely by the outward severity of one moment but by the whole canonical trajectory: unbelief/hardening, repudiation, absence of return, and God’s own diagnostic word.**

# 6. Часть IV — **Отступники и вернувшиеся в истории Церкви**

## Рабочий H1

# **Отреклись от Христа: реальные истории падения, возвращения и окончательного ухода из Церкви**

Alternative:

# **Отступники в истории Церкви: страх, пытки, отречение, покаяние и возвращение**

## Editorial rule

Исторические кейсы не используются для доказательства doctrine.

Они проверяют, насколько библейская taxonomy работает на реальной человеческой сложности.

Every case needs:

- primary source where possible;
- historical distance/custody;
- no invented motive;
- no claim to know eternal state beyond evidence.

## Case 1 — Pliny the Younger, c. AD 112: unnamed former Christians

Pliny reports people who said:

- they had been Christians;
- ceased to be Christians years earlier, some reportedly as much as about twenty years;
- worshiped imperial image/gods;
- cursed Christ.

This is extraordinary because the evidence is **hostile Roman administrative testimony**, not Christian apologetic literature.

Use:

> Christianity had very early real cases of people who publicly ceased identifying as Christians and demonstrated it through cultic renunciation.

Do not infer their previous regeneration.

Primary:
Pliny, *Letters* 10.96–97.

## Case 2 — Quintus the Phrygian, *Martyrdom of Polycarp* ch.4

Quintus voluntarily presented himself for martyrdom, then became afraid when confronted with beasts and was persuaded to swear/sacrifice.

The early church text itself uses him as a warning against self-confident voluntarism in seeking persecution.

Pastoral value:

> zeal and confidence before trial are not the same as grace to stand inside trial.

Guardrail:

The source calls him apostate, but gives no later biography sufficient for a confident eternal-state verdict.

## Case 3 — Decian persecution (AD 250): the `lapsi`

This should be a major section, not a footnote.

Under pressure Christians fell in different ways, later producing a church-wide restoration crisis.

Useful categories traditionally distinguished:

- sacrificati — sacrificed;
- thurificati — offered incense;
- libellatici — obtained certificates;
- other pressured/compromised cases.

Cyprian’s *On the Lapsed* shows both severity of denial/idolatry and insistence on real repentance rather than cheap readmission.

## Case 4 — Ninus, Clementianus, Florus: documented fall **and return-seeking**

Cyprian Epistle 52:

- they had first confessed Christ and withstood magistrates/populace;
- under severe prolonged torture they were overcome and fell;
- then for **three years** did not cease repenting;
- bishops consulted Cyprian about receiving them to communion;
- Cyprian judged restoration appropriate, subject to conciliar consideration.

This is one of the strongest historical parallels to the Peter question.

Central lesson:

> the early church could treat grave public lapse as truly grave **without treating every lapsed person as irrecoverable**.

## Case 5 — Cyprian / Cornelius versus Novatian rigorism

The controversy must be handled carefully.

Question:

> Can people who denied Christ/sacrificed under Decian persecution be restored to church communion after repentance?

Cyprian/Cornelius line:

- repentance required;
- sin severe;
- restoration possible;
- in some cases long discipline;
- church should not be permanently closed to penitents.

Novatianist rigorism became the contrasting ecclesial model.

This section directly informs Part V practice.

## Case 6 — Julian “the Apostate” (4th c.)

Raised/formed in Christianity and later explicitly rejected Christianity while emperor, seeking pagan religious revival.

Useful as clear historical case of public religious reversal with no restoration narrative.

Guardrail:

Do not turn imperial biography into a proof of former regeneration.

## Case 7 — Thomas Cranmer (1556): recantation and recantation of recantations

Cranmer signed/issued recantations under Marian pressure, returned formally toward Catholicism, then before execution publicly renounced his recantations and died affirming Protestant convictions.

Oxford/Ashmolean institutional history explicitly summarizes:

> Cranmer recanted his Protestant faith and returned to Catholicism, then renounced his recantation before being burned.

This is probably the strongest Reformation-era case for Part IV.

Central question:

> Was Cranmer’s recantation final apostasy — or grievous fear-driven denial followed by recovery?

Biblical comparison with Peter is natural but must remain analogy, not identity.

## Case 8 — Francesco Spira (1548): recantation and despair

Italian lawyer/Protestant convert who publicly recanted under pressure tied to Inquisition, property and family concerns, then became famous for profound despair that he had committed the unpardonable sin.

Use with extreme caution:

- historically important;
- became a major Protestant cautionary narrative;
- later tellings are theologically shaped;
- **do not pronounce his eternal state**.

Pastoral value:

> fear-driven recantation can be followed not only by repentance but by catastrophic despair; despair itself must not be confused with God’s infallible verdict.

## Recommended exclusions unless separately verified

- legendary apostasy/restoration stories without strong early sources;
- living public figures used merely for sensational effect;
- internet “deconstruction” stories without stable documentation;
- cases where “apostasy” merely means changing denominations inside Christianity.

## Part-IV thesis

> **Church history confirms why Scripture’s distinctions matter. Some denied Christ under fear and later sought restoration; some institutionalized renunciation; some returned dramatically; some died in unresolved despair. History supplies cases, but only Scripture supplies the categories by which cases should be judged.**

# 7. Часть V — **Что делать, когда человек отступает?**

## Рабочий H1

# **Когда человек отходит от веры: как спасать, обличать, дисциплинировать и не отчаиваться раньше времени**

Alternative:

# **Как реагировать на отступничество: церковь, семья, покаяние, дисциплина и надежда**

## This must be highly practical, not generic encouragement

### Section 1 — First diagnose category

Ask:

- ordinary sin?
- grievous fall?
- doctrinal confusion?
- temporary wandering?
- public denial?
- false teaching?
- persistent unrepentant immorality?
- actual repudiation of Christ?
- hardened teacher pulling others away?

Do not apply terminal texts before category is established.

### Section 2 — Do not abandon at first sign

James 5:19–20.

Jude 22–23.

2 Tim 2:24–26.

Gal 6:1.

Heb 3:12–13.

The church is itself one of God’s means of keeping people.

### Section 3 — Confront clearly

No therapeutic euphemisms where Scripture says:

- unbelief;
- rebellion;
- sexual immorality;
- greed;
- false teaching;
- denial of Christ.

Mercy and precision are not opposites.

### Section 4 — Church discipline

Matt 18;
1 Cor 5;
2 Thess 3;
Titus 3;
1 Tim 1:19–20.

Explain goals:

- truth;
- holiness;
- protection of flock;
- exposure of danger;
- where possible restoration.

Discipline ≠ declaration that God has revealed eternal reprobation.

### Section 5 — Restoration after repentance

2 Cor 2 likely useful as discipline/restoration control (careful on exact identity of offender).

Gal 6:1.
James 5.
Peter/John 21.
Cyprian/lapsi historical analogy.

### Section 6 — When someone denies Christ under fear

Do not trivialize Matt 10 warning.

But do not erase Peter.

Pastoral questions:

- Is there repentance?
- Is there return to Christ/confession?
- Is faith being rebuilt?
- Is person willing to accept consequences/accountability?

### Section 7 — When someone becomes false teacher

Acts 20;
Pastorals;
2 Peter/Jude;
Titus 3.

Protect others while still recognizing 2 Tim 2:25–26 possibility of God-granted repentance where text leaves it open.

### Section 8 — When a person fears “I committed the unforgivable sin”

Use blasphemy-against-Spirit dossier.

Distinguish:

- intrusive thought;
- grievous sin;
- fear/tender conscience;
- settled malicious Spirit-repudiation/hardening.

A person seeking Christ’s mercy should not be treated as though his very desire for mercy proves terminal apostasy.

### Section 9 — Family response

- do not badger every hour;
- do not normalize unbelief;
- keep relationship where morally/safely possible;
- speak truth without pretending agreement;
- pray;
- involve mature church shepherds;
- distinguish adult autonomy from parental responsibility.

Need dedicated pastoral sourcing before publication.

### Section 10 — Do not perform final judgment God did not give you

Use:

- Joshua 22 false-positive accusation;
- Demas uncertainty;
- Saul uncertainty;
- James 5 recoverability;
- 1 John 2:19 only where final antichristological secession actually fits.

### Section 11 — But do not offer false assurance

If a person:

- repudiates Christ;
- rejects gospel;
- persists in unbelief;
- refuses repentance;
- teaches destructive error;

church should not say:

> «ты когда-то помолился, значит всё безопасно».

Warnings remain real.

### Section 12 — Means of perseverance

Positive close:

- remember;
- abide/hold fast;
- prayer in Spirit;
- Word;
- church assembly;
- mutual exhortation;
- Lord’s Supper rightly received;
- confession;
- discipline;
- fleeing temptation;
- contentment;
- watchfulness;
- hope in Christ’s intercession;
- confidence in God’s keeping power.

## Part-V thesis

> **The biblical response to apostasy danger is neither panic nor fatalism. The church warns, pursues, reasons, disciplines, rescues and restores — while finally trusting the God who alone can keep His people from falling and present them blameless.**

# 9. Israel must be a recurring canonical thread, not a detached appendix

## Part I

Israel demonstrates **corporate apostasy** and why corporate language is not a direct map of hidden individual regeneration.

## Part II

Judges → Jeroboam → 2 Kings 17 shows how apostasy becomes cultural/institutional and brings exile.

## Part III

Prophetic return promises + new covenant + Rom 11 show that judgment does not exhaust God’s covenant purpose.

### Preferred theological line for this project

Subject to final Romans 11 source hardening:

> **Israel is the great corporate Bible-story showing both the severity of covenant apostasy and the invincibility of God’s redemptive purpose: hardening, exile and unbelief do not make God unfaithful; Paul looks beyond present hardening to a future saving work of God in Israel.**

### Fairness note

Do not pretend all Reformed interpreters define `all Israel` identically.

Reader-facing note should name the three main interpretations and then argue for the project’s preferred future-ethnic-Israel reading.

TMS/MacArthur sources strongly support that preferred line; historic Reformed testimony shows it predates modern dispensationalism.
