## 2. Exact `ἁγιάζω` concordance in Hebrews

### Heb 2:11

`ὅ τε γὰρ ἁγιάζων καὶ οἱ ἁγιαζόμενοι ἐξ ἑνὸς πάντες`

- `ὁ ἁγιάζων` — «освящающий»;
- `οἱ ἁγιαζόμενοι` — «освящаемые».

**Agent/object:** Христос как освящающий ↔ люди как освящаемые.  
**Context:** многие сыновья к славе, Христос как `ἀρχηγὸς τῆς σωτηρίας`, братская солидарность, далее первосвященство (2:17).

### Research pressure

Это уже задаёт базовую ролевую схему Послания: **Христос освящает; люди освящаются**. Само по себе это не делает Christ-referent в 10:29 невозможным, но означает, что попытка представить Христа как обычный объект `ἁγιάζω` идёт против главной ролевой конфигурации книги и требует отдельного доказательства.

---

### Heb 9:13

`... τοὺς κεκοινωμένους ἁγιάζει πρὸς τὴν τῆς σαρκὸς καθαρότητα`

Старозаветные кровь животных/пепел телицы **освящают** осквернённых `πρὸς τὴν τῆς σαρκὸς καθαρότητα` — «к чистоте плоти».

### Что этот стих действительно доказывает

Это самый сильный внутренний текст против тезиса:

> `ἁγιάζω` всегда и по самому слову означает внутреннее возрождение / окончательное спасение.

Нет. Автор Евреям прямо применяет `ἁγιάζω` к **реальному ритуально-культовому освящению**, эффект которого он сам ограничивает сферой `σάρξ`.

Это не фиктивное освящение: осквернённый действительно возвращался к культовой чистоте/допуску. Но это **не очищение совести**, потому что v.14 немедленно противопоставляет ему кровь Христа, очищающую `συνείδησις` от мёртвых дел для служения живому Богу.

### Critical limit

9:13 доказывает **семантическую возможность ограниченного культового освящения**, но не доказывает, что 10:29 употребляет слово именно в этом ограниченном смысле.

Причины:

1. в 9:13 средство — кровь животных / пепел телицы;
2. эффект ограничен явным qualifier: `πρὸς τὴν τῆς σαρκὸς καθαρότητα`;
3. 9:14 строит `πόσῳ μᾶλλον` — новозаветная кровь Христа превосходит эту внешнюю сферу и очищает совесть;
4. 10:29 говорит именно о **крови Нового Завета**, а не о левитическом ритуале;
5. в 10:29 нет эквивалента «только к чистоте плоти».

Следовательно, реформатскому толкованию нельзя писать:

> «Раз 9:13 имеет внешнее освящение, 10:29 очевидно означает только внешнее освящение».

Это non sequitur. 9:13 открывает категорию; контекст 10:29 должен отдельно показать, принадлежит ли случай к ней.

---

### Heb 10:10

`ἐν ᾧ θελήματι ἡγιασμένοι ἐσμὲν διὰ τῆς προσφορᾶς τοῦ σώματος Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐφάπαξ`

- `ἡγιασμένοι ἐσμέν` — perfect passive periphrastic: «мы освящены / были освящены с продолжающимся результатом»;
- средство: offering of body of Jesus Christ;
- temporal qualifier: `ἐφάπαξ` — once for all.

### Research judgment

Здесь `ἁγιάζω` находится внутри **максимально сильного христологически-сотериологического контекста**. Автор не описывает простой внешний church status. Освящение связано с исполнением Божьей воли через единственное жертвоприношение Христа и противопоставлено бессилию повторяющихся жертв.

Любая модель 10:29, утверждающая «освящение там явно слабое/внешнее», должна объяснить, почему всего за 19 стихов до warning тот же глагол несёт настолько сильную once-for-all sacrificial force.

---

### Heb 10:14

`μιᾷ γὰρ προσφορᾷ τετελείωκεν εἰς τὸ διηνεκὲς τοὺς ἁγιαζομένους`

- одна жертва;
- `τετελείωκεν` — «усовершил/довёл к цели»;
- `εἰς τὸ διηνεκές` — непрерывно / навсегда;
- объект: `τοὺς ἁγιαζομένους` — освящаемых / освящённых как определённую группу.

### Why this is the hardest control for a weak external-sanctification solution

10:14 прямо связывает sanctified-group с **permanent efficacy** единственной жертвы. Поэтому если 10:29 описывает **тот же тип blood-sanctification того же сотериологического класса**, loss-reading получает очень сильное внутреннее напряжение: как человек, которого одна жертва «усовершила навсегда», оказывается окончательно погибающим?

Но обратный вывод тоже требует осторожности. 10:14 не даёт права просто объявить:

> «Следовательно, 10:29 не может говорить об апостате».

Автор сам написал оба стиха. Экзегеза должна объяснить **отношение классов**, а не аннулировать один из текстов другим.

Основные возможности:

1. 10:29 = genuine saving sanctification later forfeited; тогда 10:14 interprets efficacy conditionally/for persevering sanctified persons;
2. 10:29 = real covenantal/cultic consecration, distinguishable from 10:10/14 definitive/effective sanctification;
3. 10:29 = impersonal/generic “blood by which one is sanctified”;
4. 10:29 = Christ-referent (historic minority route).

The lexeme alone does not choose among them.

---

### Heb 10:29

`τὸ αἷμα τῆς διαθήκης ... ἐν ᾧ ἡγιάσθη`

Form: `ἡγιάσθη`, aorist passive indicative 3sg.

### Internal lexical pressures

**Toward apostate-person + strong sanctification:**

- surrounding singular actor tramples Son, evaluates blood, insults Spirit;
- 10:10 has `ἡγιασμένοι` by Christ’s sacrifice;
- 13:12 explicitly says Jesus sanctifies **the people** by his own blood;
- blood/covenant language belongs to the new-covenant sacrifice, not merely old ritual.

**Toward a differentiated covenantal/consecratory sense:**

- 9:13 proves `ἁγιάζω` is capable of real cultic set-apartness not equal to conscience-cleansing/final salvation;
- Hebrews thinks in covenant/cultic categories, not only later systematic categories;
- warning audience is a visible covenant community in which very high privileges can be real without the author always naming justification/regeneration.

### Critical balance

The data do **not** presently support saying “external sanctification is the obvious lexical meaning.” The Reformed covenantal model remains **possible and worth defending**, but it bears an evidential burden:

> show from the new-covenant context why 10:29 should be classified with 9:13-type consecratory sanctification rather than 10:10/14/13:12-type saving blood-sanctification.

That burden is now explicit in the corpus.

### Heb 13:12

`διὸ καὶ Ἰησοῦς, ἵνα ἁγιάσῃ διὰ τοῦ ἰδίου αἵματος τὸν λαόν, ἔξω τῆς πύλης ἔπαθεν`

Syntax:

- agent: Jesus;
- purpose: `ἵνα ἁγιάσῃ` — so that He might sanctify;
- means: `διὰ τοῦ ἰδίου αἵματος` — through His own blood;
- object: `τὸν λαόν` — the people.

### Major impact on 10:29 referent debate

13:12 is the **closest explicit blood-sanctification construction elsewhere in Hebrews**. It says not that Jesus is sanctified by his blood, but that **Jesus sanctifies the people by his blood**.

Therefore the historical Christ-referent reading of 10:29 faces a serious intra-book burden. It must posit:

- in 13:12: Christ = sanctifier, blood = means, people = sanctified;
- in 10:29: Christ = sanctified one by his own blood.

This reversal is not impossible in principle, but it is not the book’s ordinary pattern and requires positive evidence beyond doctrinal convenience.

**Research weighting:** 13:12 materially strengthens apostate/person referent over Christ-referent.

## 3. Related holiness language that prevents a purely static model

The `ἁγιάζω` concordance is incomplete if detached from the rest of holiness language.

### Heb 10:22

Believers are invited to approach with:

- hearts `ῥεραντισμένοι` from evil conscience;
- body `λελουσμένοι` with pure water.

This keeps inner conscience + cultic cleansing together. Hebrews does not simply oppose “external cult” and “inner salvation”; it **transposes cultic categories into new-covenant access**.

### Heb 12:10

God disciplines sons `εἰς τὸ μεταλαβεῖν τῆς ἁγιότητος αὐτοῦ` — so that they may share His holiness.

### Heb 12:14

`τὸν ἁγιασμόν, οὗ χωρὶς οὐδεὶς ὄψεται τὸν κύριον` — pursue holiness, without which no one will see the Lord.

### Consequence

Hebrews has both:

- a **definitive sacrificial sanctification** (10:10,14; 13:12);
- an **experienced/pursued holiness** under paternal discipline and perseverance (12:10,14).

This makes a simplistic either/or inaccurate:

- not merely “sanctified once = nothing more matters”;
- not merely “sanctification is always progressive moral improvement”.

The book holds gift, access, cultic cleansing, discipline, pursuit and endurance together.

## 4. Semantic taxonomy emerging from Hebrews itself

| Text | Subject/object | Means | Explicit scope/result | Best semantic label | Soteriological pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2:11 | Christ sanctifies people | Christ’s saving mission / priestly context | solidarity, sons/brothers | Christ-mediated sanctification | STRONG |
| 9:13 | old-covenant rite sanctifies defiled | animal blood/ashes | **purity of flesh** | cultic/ceremonial consecration | LIMITED by explicit qualifier |
| 10:10 | “we” are sanctified | body-offering of Jesus | once for all | definitive new-covenant sanctification | VERY STRONG |
| 10:14 | sanctified group | one offering | perfected forever | effective/permanent sacrificial sanctification | VERY STRONG |
| 10:29 | disputed person/referent sanctified | blood of covenant | warning/judgment context | **DISPUTED TYPE** | VERY HIGH-STAKES |
| 13:12 | Jesus sanctifies people | His own blood | people made holy/access-compatible | blood-mediated new-covenant sanctification | VERY STRONG |
| 12:10/14 | sons share/pursue holiness | divine discipline / perseverance | seeing Lord | lived/teleological holiness | STRONG perseverance dimension |

### Main finding

There is **semantic breadth**, but not semantic chaos.

- `ἁγιάζω` can denote restricted cultic purification (9:13).
- When the means is **Christ’s own once-for-all sacrifice/blood**, Hebrews normally uses the language with profound new-covenant/soteriological force (10:10,14; 13:12).

Therefore 10:29 cannot be weakened simply because 9:13 exists.

## 5. What 9:13 does — and does not — buy the Reformed covenantal reading

### It does buy this

A person can be **truly sanctified in a covenant/cultic sense** without that single use of `ἁγιάζω` proving inward regeneration or final salvation. Hebrews itself provides the example.

This is enough to refute a pure lexical syllogism:

1. `ἁγιάζω` appears;
2. therefore regeneration;
3. therefore saved person loses salvation.

Step 2 is not lexically necessary.

### It does not buy this

It does **not** show that 10:29’s sanctification is merely external.

The specific new-covenant markers in 10:29 — `blood of covenant`, Son, Spirit of grace — and the nearest blood-sanctification parallels put much more weight on the phrase than 9:13’s explicitly flesh-limited rite.

### Stronger Reformed route

If the corpus ultimately defends covenantal/non-regenerating sanctification at 10:29, the argument must be cumulative:

- natural apostate referent conceded;
- real sanctification conceded;
- visible new-covenant community/cultic dedication demonstrated;
- distinction from 10:10/14 saving efficacy demonstrated by discourse/category, not dictionary;
- final perseverance texts and 10:39 integrated as theological controls;
- warning retained as God-appointed means rather than hypothetical theater.

That is harder than the older “Christ is the referent” escape, but much more robust.

## 6. Why 13:12 sharply weakens the Christ-referent shortcut

This point should be elevated to P0.

A Christ-referent advocate often appeals to priestly consecration and John 17:19. But within **Hebrews itself**, 13:12 supplies a much closer lexical construction than John 17:

> Jesus sanctifies the people **through his own blood**.

Thus an argument that 10:29 naturally means “Christ was sanctified by his own blood” must explain why the same author, only a few chapters later, expresses the blood relation in the opposite direction.

This does not make Owen’s proposal grammatically impossible. It makes it **intra-textually less probable** unless stronger priestly-consecration evidence is produced.

### Updated research probability

- apostate/person referent: **HIGH**;
- impersonal generic sanctification: **MEDIUM**;
- Christ-referent: **LOW-MEDIUM**.

This agrees with, but now no longer depends only on, external commentators: it comes from the epistle’s own role pattern.

## 7. The deepest tension: 10:14 vs 10:29

The article’s hardest theological question is not actually the antecedent alone.

It is this:

> How can Hebrews say that by one offering Christ has perfected forever `τοὺς ἁγιαζομένους` (10:14), and soon speak of one who has been sanctified by covenant blood and nevertheless faces destruction (10:29)?

Any serious model must answer **both clauses without semantic cheating**.

### Loss-of-salvation model

Answer:

- same strong sanctification class;
- 10:14 describes the efficacy available/experienced in Christ;
- continued apostasy can forfeit that state.

Pressure:

- what does `τετελείωκεν εἰς τὸ διηνεκές` mean if its perfected object may cease to belong to that class?
- how does this cohere with 10:39 and broader preservation promises?

### Covenant/consecratory differentiation model

Answer:

- 10:10/14 = effective saving sanctification;
- 10:29 = real covenantal consecration/identification through covenant blood in the visible community, not identical with the perfected class.

Pressure:

- where does the text mark that semantic shift?
- why does the new-covenant blood in 10:29 receive a weaker effect than in 10:10/14/13:12?

### Impersonal Guthrie-type model

Answer:

- 10:29 identifies the sacred blood “by which one is sanctified” generically; apostate need not have personally received saving sanctification.

Pressure:

- why shift to generic “one” inside a tightly personal singular description?

### Christ-referent model

Answer:

- Christ is consecrated to priestly office through his own blood.

Pressure:

- 2:11 and 13:12 make Christ the sanctifier, people the sanctified;
- explicit “Christ sanctified by his own blood” formulation is absent elsewhere in Hebrews;
- discourse antecedent remains awkward.

**Research judgment:** the Reformed corpus should treat the second model as its primary candidate, Guthrie-type impersonal as secondary, and Christ-referent as a historical minority alternative rather than default solution.

## 8. Recent open scholarship: useful but not neutral consensus

Adriani Milli Rodrigues, “Priestly Christology and Holiness: Christ’s Priesthood and the Sanctification of Believers,” *Religions* 17/1 (2026), article 71, provides a recent open-access whole-Hebrews holiness survey.

Useful findings for our corpus:

- explicitly catalogs `ἁγιάζω` at 2:11; 9:13; 10:10,14,29; 13:12;
- stresses covenantal + cultic framework;
- sees Christ’s blood as purifying/sanctifying believers;
- distinguishes 9:13 flesh-purification from 9:14 conscience-purification;
- treats 10:29 as believers sanctified by covenant blood who can apostatize;
- connects definitive sanctification with ongoing discipline/pursuit in ch.12.

### Evidence classification

- open peer-reviewed/source-identifiable modern scholarship;
- useful B1 scholarly counter-reading and semantic synthesis;
- **not** to be used as sole support for disputed final-loss claims.

It is valuable precisely because it develops the strongest whole-Hebrews version of the reading the Reformed article must answer.

## 10. No-go formulations after the concordance pass

Retire these from future Product prose:

- **“`ἁγιάζω` always means regenerate.”** False: 9:13.
- **“`ἁγιάζω` in Hebrews is normally merely external.”** False: 10:10,14; 13:12.
- **“9:13 solves 10:29 as external sanctification.”** Unsupported leap.
- **“13:12 supports Christ being sanctified by his own blood.”** It actually has Jesus sanctifying **the people** by his blood.
- **“10:14 makes 10:29 impossible.”** The author wrote both; the relation requires explanation.
- **“Since 10:29 is a warning, its sanctification language is only hypothetical.”** The verb itself does not say this.
- **“The only choices are loss of salvation or Christ-referent.”** False: covenantal/consecratory and impersonal models remain live.

## 11. Product-grade provisional synthesis

The future article can now be built around a more precise central paragraph:

> В Послании к Евреям слово «освящать» не имеет одного механического значения. Евр. 9:13 употребляет его о реальном ритуальном очищении Ветхого Завета, ограниченном «чистотой плоти»; следовательно, само слово ещё не доказывает возрождение. Но когда Евреям говорит о жертве и крови Христа, язык становится значительно сильнее: верующие «освящены… однажды» Его жертвой (10:10), одной жертвой Он «усовершил навсегда освящаемых» (10:14), а в 13:12 Иисус страдает, чтобы «освятить народ Своей кровью». Поэтому Евр. 10:29 нельзя обезвредить простой ссылкой на внешнее освящение. Наиболее естественно видеть освящённым самого отступника; главный спор затем переносится на вопрос, является ли это тем же эффективным спасительным освящением, что в 10:10–14, или реальным заветным посвящением в сфере народа Нового Завета, которое автор отличает от окончательно спасающего действия жертвы. Это должно решаться всей аргументацией Послания, а не одним словарным значением.

## 13. Research verdict

The concordance changes the burden of proof in a useful way.

- **Yes:** Hebrews itself proves that `ἁγιάζω` may denote genuine cultic consecration short of inward conscience-cleansing (9:13).
- **No:** that does not by itself make 10:29 an external-sanctification verse.
- In Christ-blood contexts, Hebrews normally gives sanctification profound and durable force (10:10,14; 13:12).
- 13:12 makes the Christ-referent workaround notably less natural because the book’s explicit pattern is **Christ sanctifies the people through his own blood**.
- Thus the strongest Reformed answer is no longer “change the referent to Christ” and not “weaken `sanctified`”. It must argue a **carefully demonstrated distinction between effective saving sanctification and real covenantal/consecratory sanctification**, while integrating 10:14, 10:39 and the warning-as-means architecture.
- The loss-of-salvation reading now has to answer the opposite pressure: `τετελείωκεν εἰς τὸ διηνεκές` in 10:14 and the preservation framework.

This is a harder equilibrium, but it preserves more of Hebrews’ actual language on both sides.
