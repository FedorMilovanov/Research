## 1. Главный вывод

2 Тим. 2 не позволяет ни одной из двух упрощающих схем:

> «Если мы неверны, Христос всё равно спасёт нас независимо от окончательного отречения».

или

> «Христова верность в ст. 13 означает только то, что Он верно осудит неверующих».

Текст удерживает вместе:

1. реальное обещание тем, кто умер со Христом / терпит с Ним;
2. реальную угрозу: `если отречёмся — и Он отречётся от нас`;
3. неизменную верность Христа Своей собственной сущности и слову;
4. разрушительное действие ложного учения, которое `опрокидывает веру некоторых`;
5. немедленный контрапункт: `твёрдое Божие основание стоит`;
6. двойную печать: **Господь знает Своих** и **исповедующий Господа должен отступить от неправды**;
7. надежду исправления заблуждающихся: Бог может дать покаяние, ведущее к познанию истины и освобождению из сети дьявола.

Иными словами:

> **preservation, warning, ecclesial damage, holiness and restorative correction находятся в одном параграфе и не должны быть разорваны богословской системой.**

# 2. Контекст 2:8–10 — воскресший Христос, страдающий Павел и избранные

Перед `πιστὸς ὁ λόγος` Павел говорит:

- помни Иисуса Христа, воскресшего из мёртвых, из семени Давидова;
- за это Евангелие он страдает до уз;
- слово Божие не связано;
- он всё переносит ради избранных, чтобы **и они получили спасение во Христе Иисусе с вечной славой**.

Это важно по двум причинам.

## 2.1. Perseverance context уже присутствует

Павел не обсуждает абстрактную метафизику спасения.

Он призывает Тимофея продолжать служение под давлением, используя собственное страдание как образец endurance.

William Mounce в своём обзоре Pastoral Epistles справедливо описывает 2:1–13 как personal appeal to Timothy to persevere: soldier / athlete / farmer / Paul himself / faithful saying.

Accessible Mounce resource:
https://www.biblicaltraining.org/library/2-timothy-2/pastoral-epistles

## 2.2. Election не делает средства ненужными

Paul терпит `διὰ τοὺς ἐκλεκτούς` именно **чтобы они получили** salvation/glory.

Это важный Reformed canonical control:

> divine election does not make preaching, suffering ministry and endurance superfluous; God ordains ends together with means.

# 3. 2:11–13 — exact Greek structure

Mounce reverse interlinear preserves the lexical structure:

- `εἰ γὰρ συναπεθάνομεν, καὶ συζήσομεν`
- `εἰ ὑπομένομεν, καὶ συμβασιλεύσομεν`
- `εἰ ἀρνησόμεθα / ἀρνούμεθα, κἀκεῖνος ἀρνήσεται ἡμᾶς`
- `εἰ ἀπιστοῦμεν, ἐκεῖνος πιστὸς μένει`
- `ἀρνήσασθαι γὰρ ἑαυτὸν οὐ δύναται`

Source:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+2%3A11-13&version=MOUNCE

Greek analytical witness:
https://greekdoc.com/DOCUMENTS/nt-poly/2tim02.html

NET notes also correctly flag the unit as commonly treated as poetic/hymnic without requiring us to prove that Paul is quoting a pre-existing hymn.

Source:
https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php/o/w/passage.php?mode=print&passage=2Ti+2%3A11-13

### Guardrail

`πιστὸς ὁ λόγος` guarantees the trustworthiness/weight of the saying. It does not by itself settle whether every line was pre-Pauline liturgy, a Pauline composition, or adapted traditional material.

---

# 4. First pair: death with Christ → life with Christ

`εἰ ... συναπεθάνομεν, καὶ συζήσομεν`

Interpretive options include:

1. union-with-Christ / conversion-baptismal death, resonating with Rom 6 / Col 2–3;
2. willingness to suffer/martyrdom with Christ in the immediate persecution context;
3. a formulation broad enough to include identity with Christ in death expressed through suffering discipleship.

The text itself does not require choosing an excessively narrow version for the apostasy project.

Secure point:

> association with the crucified Christ leads toward life with Him, not away from suffering.

---

# 5. Second pair: endurance → reigning

`εἰ ὑπομένομεν, καὶ συμβασιλεύσομεν`

The present `ὑπομένομεν` fits the letter’s repeated call to ongoing endurance.

Canonical controls:

- Matt 10:22 — enduring to the end;
- Rom 8:17 — suffering with Christ / glorified with Him;
- Rev 2:10,26; 3:21 — faithfulness/endurance and eschatological reward/reign.

### Important

Paul does not say endurance merits salvation as autonomous human achievement.

The same letter grounds ministry in grace (2:1), divine calling/election (1:9; 2:10,19), gift, Spirit and divine preservation.

Endurance is the path/mark of faithful participation, not an alternative to grace.

---

# 6. Third pair: denying Christ → Christ denying us

`εἰ ἀρνησόμεθα / ἀρνούμεθα, κἀκεῖνος ἀρνήσεται ἡμᾶς`

Mounce gives `ἀρνέομαι`; GTY’s technical exposition correctly notes the semantic force “deny / disown / renounce.”

Source:
https://www.gty.org/resources/study-guides/chapters/55-8/motives-for-sacrificial-ministry-part-2

## 6.1. Strong Jesus-tradition resonance

Matt 10:32–33 and Luke 12:8–9 provide the obvious conceptual parallel:

> confess Christ before people / Christ confesses; deny Christ / Christ denies.

Therefore the warning cannot honestly be transformed into:

> «no serious consequence follows final repudiation of Christ».

## 6.2. Peter is a necessary control

Peter denied Jesus verbally three times and was restored.

Therefore `deny` as an isolated act cannot automatically equal terminal apostasy.

The distinction is not:

> one occurrence of a Greek verb = eternal condemnation.

Rather:

- Peter’s denial occurs in fear/weakness and is followed by bitter grief, Christ’s intercession, restoration and continued confession;
- terminal denial is settled repudiation without return.

This prevents both cheapening the warning and condemning every grievous lapse as final.

## 6.3. Calvin’s warning emphasis

Calvin treats v.12 as a genuine threat aimed at shaking off sloth and fear-driven abandonment of Christ.

Primary:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43.iv.iii.ii.html

His pastoral insistence is useful: Christ’s warning is not theatrical intimidation.

---

# 7. Fourth line: `εἰ ἀπιστοῦμεν` — what kind of faithlessness?

`ἀπιστέω` has a semantic range including:

- disbelieve;
- be unbelieving;
- be unfaithful / prove faithless.

Therefore **lexicon alone cannot establish**:

> “v.13 necessarily means only a regenerate believer temporarily doubting.”

Nor can it establish the opposite:

> “v.13 necessarily means only an apostate unbeliever.”

The clause must be interpreted within the four-line structure and Paul’s wider usage.

Mounce reverse interlinear:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+timothy+2%3A13&version=MOUNCE

---

# 8. Two major readings of v.13

## Reading A — warning continues

Meaning:

> even if humans prove faithless/unbelieving, Christ remains true to Himself and therefore will carry out His warning.

Calvin strongly emphasizes this:

- human desertion removes nothing from Christ’s glory;
- Christ does not alter His truth because people alter theirs;
- apostates cannot imagine that their instability makes Christ unstable.

Primary:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/2_timothy/2.htm

MacArthur’s 1987 exposition also strongly accents this warning-side:

https://www.gty.org/resources/study-guides/chapters/55-8/motives-for-sacrificial-ministry-part-2

### Strength

It respects the immediate warning in v.12 and the repeated `ἀρνέομαι`:

- we deny Him;
- He denies us;
- He cannot deny Himself.

### Risk

If stated as **the only possible content of divine faithfulness**, it may become narrower than Paul’s normal positive use of God’s faithfulness.

---

## Reading B — reassurance amid believers’ weakness

Meaning:

> when believers prove weak/unfaithful, Christ remains faithful to them because His character and promise are immutable.

NET explicitly lists warning vs hope and judges the hopeful reading more likely based on Paul’s repeated use of God’s faithfulness as reassurance (Rom 3:3; 1 Cor 1:9; 10:13; 1 Thess 5:24; 2 Thess 3:3).

Source:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+2%3A11-13&version=NET%3BNIV

MacArthur’s later perseverance sermon also uses v.13 in this reassuring sense for temporary weakness of true believers while maintaining v.12 as the mark of false final denial.

Source:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/90-272/the-perseverance-of-the-saints-part-3

### Strength

It takes seriously:

- Pauline faithfulness language;
- the reality of temporary weakness in true saints;
- Peter as canonical example.

### Risk

If made unconditional toward final apostasy, it **cancels v.12**, which the text does not permit.

---

# 9. Best working synthesis — Christ is faithful to Himself, therefore to all that He has said

The strongest theological synthesis should not force divine faithfulness into only one narrow function.

Christ cannot deny Himself means:

> **He remains perfectly self-consistent with His own character, covenant purpose, promise and warning.**

Therefore:

- He will not abandon His own because of their temporary weakness;
- He will not declare settled repudiation harmless after warning that He will deny those who deny Him;
- His preservation promises are reliable;
- His judgment warnings are reliable.

This does not evade exegesis by saying “both” cheaply.

It follows from the grounding clause:

`for He cannot deny Himself`.

The ultimate referent of faithfulness is first **Christ Himself**, not human deserving.

### Publication rule

Do not quote v.13 alone as:

> «whatever a Christian finally does, salvation is guaranteed».

And do not quote it alone as:

> «God’s faithfulness here is merely punitive».

State the tension created by vv.12–13 and show the interpretive options.

---

# 10. 2:14–18 — false teaching has real destructive effects

Paul shifts from the faithful saying to truth-handling.

False discourse:

- ruins hearers (v.14);
- increases ungodliness (v.16);
- spreads like gangrene (v.17).

Then Paul names:

- Hymenaeus;
- Philetus.

Mounce reverse interlinear:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+2%3A17-19&version=MOUNCE

## 10.1. `ἠστόχησαν περὶ τὴν ἀλήθειαν`

`ἀστοχέω` = miss the mark / deviate / wander away.

They have **swerved concerning the truth**.

The same family appears in the Pastoral Epistles for serious doctrinal deviation.

## 10.2. Their error

They say:

> `the resurrection has already happened`.

This is not harmless eschatological speculation.

It damages Christian hope and resurrection doctrine.

MacArthur reasonably reconstructs it as likely spiritualizing the resurrection so that future bodily resurrection is effectively denied, but because Paul does not explain their full system, publication wording should remain:

> they falsely relocated/completed the resurrection in the past and thereby corrupted future Christian hope.

GTY source:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/print/47-32/facing-death-confidently-part-2

---

# 11. `ἀνατρέπουσιν τήν τινων πίστιν` — “upsetting the faith of some”

`ἀνατρέπω` can mean overturn / upset / subvert.

Paul does not pretend false teaching has no real effect because God preserves His people.

It genuinely **upsets the faith of some**.

This must be allowed its full pastoral weight.

But the next word is equally important:

`μέντοι` — nevertheless/however.

> **Nevertheless, God’s firm foundation stands.**

Thus Paul himself places visible doctrinal wreckage beside divine stability.

---

# 12. 2:19 — `ὁ μέντοι στερεὸς θεμέλιος τοῦ θεοῦ ἕστηκεν`

Greek:

`ὁ μέντοι στερεὸς θεμέλιος τοῦ θεοῦ ἕστηκεν`

- `στερεός` — firm/solid;
- `θεμέλιος` — foundation;
- `ἕστηκεν` — perfect of `ἵστημι`: has stood / stands.

Mounce:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+2%3A17-19&version=MOUNCE

NET morphology:
https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=2Ti&chapter=2&verse=19

### What precisely is the “foundation”?

Major proposals include:

- God’s church/people;
- God’s saving purpose/election;
- apostolic gospel/truth;
- God’s own sure work underlying the church.

The project should not pretend the noun alone resolves this.

What Paul himself immediately interprets is the **seal with two inscriptions**.

Those inscriptions are more decisive for the apostasy question than speculative overprecision about the architectural referent.

---

# 13. First seal inscription — “The Lord knows those who are His”

`Ἔγνω κύριος τοὺς ὄντας αὐτοῦ`

This clearly draws from Numbers 16:5 in the Korah rebellion.

Cambridge Greek Testament notes the direct allusion:
https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/236/cambridge-greek-testament-for-schools-and-colleges-commentary/2-timothy/2/19

The Numbers setting matters enormously.

Korah’s rebellion created a crisis:

> Who truly belongs to / is accepted by YHWH?

Moses answers that God Himself knows and will make known His own.

Paul invokes that language immediately after false teachers have overturned some people’s faith.

### Theological force

> human doctrinal chaos does not erase God’s discriminating knowledge of His own.

Calvin strongly reads this as consolation amid scandalous apostasy: the treachery of some professors cannot make God’s saving foundation collapse.

Primary Calvin context:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43.iv.iii.ii.html

---

# 14. Second seal inscription — profession requires departure from unrighteousness

`Ἀποστήτω ἀπὸ ἀδικίας πᾶς ὁ ὀνομάζων τὸ ὄνομα κυρίου`

> let everyone naming the Lord’s name depart from unrighteousness.

Important irony for the whole project:

`ἀφίστημι` — often discussed as “depart/apostatize” — here is commanded positively:

> **depart from unrighteousness.**

Again:

> lexeme itself is not apostasy doctrine; object/direction determines moral meaning.

## 14.1. Is this a quotation from Numbers 16:26?

Unlike the first inscription, the second is **not an exact quotation** of one OT verse.

It likely echoes the same Korah narrative:

- Num 16:26 — separate/depart from the tents of the wicked rebels;
- possibly Isa 52:11 — depart, depart, touch no unclean thing;
- compare Isa 26:13 and later Jesus-tradition about departing from workers of lawlessness.

Cambridge:
https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/236/cambridge-greek-testament-for-schools-and-colleges-commentary/2-timothy/2/19

Expositor’s Greek Testament:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/egt/2_timothy/2.htm

### Publication wording

Say:

> first inscription is a clear Num 16:5 citation/allusion; second likely continues Korah/separation imagery and may also resonate with Isaiah.

Do **not** falsely call both exact quotations from Numbers.

---

# 15. Divine preservation and human holiness are on the same seal

Paul does not write:

> “The Lord knows His own, therefore ethical separation is unnecessary.”

Nor:

> “Depart from unrighteousness, therefore preservation depends on autonomous human strength.”

The seal has both sides:

1. **divine knowledge / ownership**;
2. **human departure from evil**.

This is one of the clearest compact formulas for the project:

> **God preserves a people who are summoned to holiness; the imperative is not the rival of preservation but belongs to its biblical shape.**

---

# 16. 2:20–22 — separation from falsehood is not merely intellectual

The large-house/vessel imagery moves directly into:

- cleansing;
- sanctification;
- usefulness to the Master;
- preparation for every good work;
- fleeing youthful passions;
- pursuing righteousness, faith, love, peace;
- doing so with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart.

So the answer to doctrinal gangrene is not simply:

> “win the argument.”

It includes holy life and holy fellowship.

---

# 17. 2:24–26 — even opponents are not automatically classified terminal

The Lord’s servant must:

- not be quarrelsome;
- be gentle;
- able to teach;
- patiently endure evil;
- correct opponents with gentleness.

Why?

`μήποτε δώῃ αὐτοῖς ὁ θεὸς μετάνοιαν εἰς ἐπίγνωσιν ἀληθείας`

> perhaps God may give them repentance unto knowledge of truth.

Then:

`ἀνανήψωσιν ἐκ τῆς τοῦ διαβόλου παγίδος`

> they may come to their senses / sober up out of the devil’s snare.

NET technical notes:
https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=2Ti+2%3A24-26

Greek v.26:
https://www.greekbible.com/2-timothy/2/26

## 17.1. Divine initiative

Repentance is something God **may grant**.

This fits Acts 11:18 and a Reformed doctrine of grace without removing the command to teach/correct.

Again means and divine action are joined.

## 17.2. Serious bondage

Opponents are described as:

- ensnared by devil;
- captured;
- needing restored sobriety.

This is not a trivial disagreement.

## 17.3. But restorative hope remains

Paul does not say:

> “everyone currently opposing truth is already a terminal apostate.”

He commands patient correction precisely because repentance and recovery remain possible.

This is a major control on how Hymenaeus-like doctrinal trajectories are handled pastorally before Scripture itself closes the case.

---

# 18. MacArthur — useful evidence, but do not flatten his own emphases

MacArthur’s corpus contains two complementary emphases.

## 18.1. Strong warning

In the 2 Tim 2 study guide, he reads v.12 as settled renunciation and v.13 strongly as Christ’s faithfulness to His own truth, including judgment.

https://www.gty.org/resources/study-guides/chapters/55-8/motives-for-sacrificial-ministry-part-2

## 18.2. Preservation amid temporary weakness

In his perseverance series, he distinguishes final denial from temporary struggles of real believers and uses v.13 as assurance that Christ remains faithful.

https://www.gty.org/sermons/90-272/the-perseverance-of-the-saints-part-3

### Research conclusion

Do not quote one sermon as though MacArthur’s entire treatment had only one edge.

His broader perseverance theology itself wants both:

- final defection manifests false profession;
- genuine believers can experience severe temporary weakness;
- God preserves His own.

That combination is closer to the whole paragraph than either simplistic extreme.

---

# 19. Calvin — stronger warning-side control

Calvin interprets v.13 mainly as Christ remaining unchanged even when apostates desert Him.

This contributes a needed warning against presumption:

> human apostasy cannot force Christ to contradict His warning or character.

At 2:19 Calvin then strongly emphasizes divine preservation amid the scandal of visible apostasy.

Thus Calvin’s own sequence is also two-sided:

- apostates cannot make Christ unfaithful;
- apostasy of visible professors cannot overthrow God’s knowledge/preservation of His own.

Primary:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/2_timothy/2.htm

# 20. Puritan practical layer

John Owen’s apostasy/perseverance corpus is especially apt here because 2 Tim 2 itself combines:

- false teaching;
- doctrinal decay;
- profession;
- divine preservation;
- holy separation;
- means of recovery.

Owen should be brought into the final pastoral section under these categories:

1. apostasy can begin gradually, not only as dramatic public renunciation;
2. doctrinal error and corrupt affections can reinforce each other;
3. visible profession must not be confused automatically with saving union;
4. preserving grace works through warnings, watchfulness, truth and holiness;
5. scandalous falls of professors must not destabilize confidence in God’s purpose.

Primary-access control:
https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/owen_natureapostasy.html

Thomas Watson adds the complementary perseverance distinction:

- saints may suffer serious eclipses/relapses;
- perseverance is not sinless continuity;
- holy fear and watchfulness are means of perseverance.

Primary:
https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity/divinity.ix.x.html

These witnesses belong **after** grammar/exegesis, not in place of it.

# 21. Competing interpretive positions to represent before publication

## A. Reformed perseverance reading

Typical synthesis:

- enduring faith marks the elect;
- final denial manifests non-saving profession;
- temporary lapses can occur in saints;
- God remains faithful to preserve His own;
- v.19 explicitly stabilizes believers after v.18.

## B. Conditional-security / Arminian-Wesleyan reading

Typically stresses:

- warning in v.12 as a real possibility for believers;
- denial may result in loss of final salvation;
- v.13 means Christ remains faithful to His own stated covenant conditions, not unconditional preservation.

## C. Free Grace reward-loss reading

Some distinguish:

- eternal life secured;
- endurance/reigning related to reward;
- denial may mean loss of reward/fellowship rather than final salvation.

This view must confront the strong Jesus-tradition resonance of “He will deny us,” and should not be represented by a caricature.

### Project adjudication

The current canonical project favors the Reformed perseverance synthesis because:

- the Pastoral Epistles repeatedly distinguish profession from reality;
- 2:19 immediately anchors stability in God’s knowledge of His own;
- final perseverance is repeatedly commanded and promised elsewhere;
- recoverable temporary denial exists canonically (Peter);
- final defection is manifestation language elsewhere (1 John 2:19).

But publication must state the competing readings and their strongest textual evidence.

# 22. Publication-safe synthesis

> «Во 2 Тимофею 2 Павел не ослабляет ни предупреждение, ни сохранение. “Если терпим — с Ним и царствовать будем; если отречёмся — и Он отречётся от нас.” Следующая строка — “если мы неверны, Он пребывает верен” — не может означать, что окончательное отречение вдруг стало безопасным: Христос верен именно потому, что не может противоречить Самому Себе. Но Его верность нельзя сводить и к одному наказанию: Тот же неизменный Христос верен Своим обещаниям и сохраняет Своих даже среди их реальной слабости. Поэтому после сообщения, что Именей и Филит “ниспровергают веру некоторых”, Павел не говорит, будто вред ложного учения нереален; он отвечает: “однако твёрдое основание Божие стоит”, и печать имеет две надписи — “познал Господь Своих” и “да отступит от неправды всякий, исповедующий имя Господа”. Божье знание Своих и обязанность удаляться от зла стоят не друг против друга, а на одной печати. А в конце параграфа даже противников истины велено исправлять кротко — в надежде, что Бог даст им покаяние и освободит их из сети дьявола. Так Павел одновременно оставляет реальными угрозу отречения, разрушительность ереси, Божье сохранение и возможность восстановления заблуждающегося.»
