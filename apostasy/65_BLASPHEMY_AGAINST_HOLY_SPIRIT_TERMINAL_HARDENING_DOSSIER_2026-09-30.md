# 2. DIRECT EXEGESIS — Matthew 12: historical trigger

Контекст не абстрактный.

Jesus heals a demon-oppressed blind and mute man.

The crowds ask whether Jesus may be the Son of David.

The Pharisees answer:

> He casts out demons by Beelzebul / prince of demons.

Jesus replies:

- Satan divided against himself cannot stand;
- exorcisms show Satan’s house is being plundered;
- if Jesus casts out demons **by the Spirit of God**, then the kingdom of God has come upon them;
- therefore (`διὰ τοῦτο`) He warns about blasphemy against the Spirit.

Matthew 12:28 is decisive:

> the disputed agency is **the Spirit of God**.

Matthew 12:31–32 therefore cannot be detached from the prior accusation.

Direct context:
- https://www.biblehub.com/context/matthew/12-22.htm

### DIRECT EXEGESIS conclusion

The sin is not generic profanity.

Its historical form is:

> **consciously attributing the Spirit-attested messianic work of Jesus to Satanic power.**

# 3. Mark 3:30 is the sharpest inspired explanatory control

Mark states:

> `ὅτι ἔλεγον· Πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον ἔχει.`

“because they were saying, ‘He has an unclean spirit.’”

Greek:
- https://biblehub.com/text/mark/3-30.htm

The imperfect `ἔλεγον` naturally portrays an ongoing/repeated accusation: **they were saying**.

Mark 3:29:

- no forgiveness `εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα`;
- guilty of `αἰωνίου ἁμαρτήματος` — eternal sin.

Interlinear/context:
- https://biblehub.com/bib/mark/3.htm
- https://biblehub.com/passage/mark/3/22-35

### DIRECT EXEGESIS conclusion

Mark himself explains why Jesus gave the warning.

Therefore no interpretation may sever “blasphemy against the Spirit” from the conscious accusation that the Holy Spirit’s work in Jesus is unclean/Satanic.

This is the strongest historical anchor of the dossier.

---

# 4. It is not a magical phrase or accidental slip

The context shows a pattern:

- abundant evidence;
- conscious evaluation;
- deliberate contrary verdict;
- repeated accusatory speech;
- escalating opposition to Jesus.

William W. Combs emphasizes this strongly:

> it is not a flippant act or slip of the tongue; it proceeds from the heart and is committed in full knowledge of the truth.

Primary technical source:

William W. Combs, “The Blasphemy Against the Holy Spirit,” *Detroit Baptist Seminary Journal* 9 (2004): 57–96.

Direct PDF:
- https://dbts.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/The-Blasphemy-against-the-Holy-Spirit-Combs.pdf

Key pages:
- historical-context analysis pp.72–93;
- conclusion pp.95–96.

### Closure

A person does not commit this sin merely by:

- having an intrusive blasphemous thought;
- emotionally saying something terrible in panic/anger;
- momentarily doubting Christ;
- committing an ordinary grievous sin;
- even verbally denying Christ under fear, as Peter did.

---

# 5. Peter’s denial is an inspired counterexample against over-broadening

Peter:

- knowingly denied Jesus;
- did so repeatedly;
- swore/cursed;
- had already received explicit warning;
- yet was restored.

Calvin uses Peter precisely as a control:

> conscious denial through weakness/fear is not the same as blasphemy against the Spirit arising from incurable malice.

Primary Calvin commentary:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom33.ii.xxxv.html

### CANONICAL INFERENCE

Knowledge + deliberate words do not, by themselves, equal unpardonable sin.

The decisive difference is the **settled, malicious, truth-reversing posture toward the Spirit’s testimony to Christ**.

This protects the project from the formula:

> “Any conscious denial after knowledge = unforgivable.”

False.

---

# 6. Calvin — known truth maliciously resisted

Calvin’s Matthew commentary gives one of the strongest Reformed formulations.

He argues that the Pharisees:

- could not deny the reality of Jesus’ works;
- nevertheless knowingly and willingly poured contempt on the Spirit;
- turned revealed divine glory into dishonor;
- acted not from ignorance but from deliberate hostility toward truth already made evident.

Primary:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom32.ii.xiii.html
- parallel study text: https://www.ccel.org/study/Matthew_12%3A22-32

Calvin distinguishes:

- ignorance/error that can be forgiven;
- malicious resistance to truth recognized as divine.

He further treats this sin as a sign of judicial hardening/reprobation: the sinner does not rise again because God gives him over to hardness.

### Important classification

Calvin’s equation of this sin with apostasy in Hebrews 6 is **SYSTEMATIC/CANONICAL SYNTHESIS**, not direct lexical identity.

Combs explicitly documents that Reformed history commonly followed this broader Calvinian apostasy model.

---

# 7. MacArthur — determined unbelief after full light

MacArthur’s Matthew commentary maintains the historical Beelzebul setting but broadens the essence.

Direct GTY excerpt:
- https://shop.gty.org/library/bibleqnas-library/QA0243/what-does-it-mean-to-blaspheme-the-holy-spirit

He argues:

- ordinary blasphemy can be forgiven;
- Paul was a blasphemer in ignorance and received mercy;
- Peter’s cursing denial was forgiven;
- the exceptional sin is determined unbelief after sufficient/full evidence;
- the opponents saw the Spirit’s divine power through Jesus and knowingly assigned it to Satan;
- their finality lay in absolute refusal to believe/repent.

### Strength

This preserves:

- concrete gospel context;
- difference between ignorance and full-light rejection;
- pastoral distinction between Peter/Paul and hardened opponents.

### Pressure

“Full evidence” must be defined from the narrative, not turned into a precise psychological threshold we can measure in another person.

### Weight

`HIGH CONSERVATIVE PASTORAL-EXEGETICAL VALUE`.

---

# 8. Combs — the strongest narrow sign-miracle model

William Combs performs an extensive history-of-interpretation and Synoptic study.

His final position:

> blasphemy against the Holy Spirit is blaspheming the Spirit’s **miracle-working power**, specifically attributing undeniable divine sign-miracles to Satan.

He argues:

1. Pharisees knew the miracle was real;
2. Satanic causation had been refuted;
3. Spirit causation was undeniable;
4. they persisted in attributing it to Satan;
5. the sin therefore was knowingly denying the undeniable.

Mark 3:30 is central to his case.

Primary:
- https://dbts.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/The-Blasphemy-against-the-Holy-Spirit-Combs.pdf

Especially pp.92–96.

### Combs on who can commit it

He concludes:

- only unbelievers;
- not true believers.

### Combs on whether it can be committed today

This is his most distinctive conclusion:

- because the sin specifically concerns supernatural sign-miracles;
- because he holds apostolic sign-miracles ceased;
- therefore the exact sin cannot be committed in the present church age.

He allows it during:

- Jesus’ ministry;
- apostolic sign-miracle ministry;
- potentially future Revelation 11 witness ministry.

### Evaluation

Strength:
- exceptionally tight historical/contextual control;
- Mark 3:30 given full force;
- no vague psychologizing.

Pressure:
- Luke 12:8–12 relocates the saying into a confession/persecution/Spirit-witness setting;
- Calvin/MacArthur/Schreiner identify a broader theological essence beyond one class of miracle;
- Scripture never explicitly states, “this sin exists only when apostolic sign miracles are occurring.”

### Current weight

`SERIOUS CONSERVATIVE TECHNICAL COMPETING MODEL / HIGH HISTORICAL PRECISION / MEDIUM ON PRESENT-DAY LIMITATION`.

Do not hide this view, but do not let cessationism settle the text by itself.

# 9. Luke 12 prevents an overly narrow reconstruction

Luke 12:8–12 sequence:

1. confess Christ before people;
2. deny Christ before people;
3. word against Son of Man can be forgiven;
4. blasphemy against Holy Spirit will not be forgiven;
5. disciples will be brought before synagogues/rulers;
6. **the Holy Spirit will teach them what to say**.

Direct context:
- https://mail.biblehub.com/context/luke/12-8.htm

Greek/interlinear:
- https://biblehub.com/bib/luke/12.htm

### Why Luke matters

Matthew/Mark preserve the Beelzebul incident as the historical trigger.

Luke places the saying in a **future witness/persecution setting** where the Spirit testifies through Christ’s disciples.

This suggests the category is not merely:

> “someone standing physically before Jesus watches this one exorcism and says Beelzebul.”

The Spirit’s testimony to Christ continues to confront people through the mission.

### Combs’s response

Combs uses Luke to extend the possibility into the apostles’ sign-miracle ministry, while retaining miracle-working power as the essential object.

### Broader reading

Calvin/Schreiner/MacArthur can plausibly understand Luke as extending the principle to hardened rejection of the Spirit’s witness to Christ more generally.

### Current project conclusion

Luke makes a **strict once-only historical limitation less persuasive**, while Mark keeps the category from becoming vague “persistent sin in general.”

# 10. Thomas Schreiner — terminal-apostasy family

Official Southern Seminary source:
- https://equip.sbts.edu/video/what-is-the-unforgivable-sin/

Schreiner explicitly says:

- historical Gospel case = Pharisees attribute Jesus’ work to the devil;
- he regards the unforgivable sin as the same ultimate category as:
  - 1 John 5 sin unto death;
  - Hebrews warnings;
  - recrucifying Christ;
  - trampling the Son;
  - insulting the Spirit of grace;
- no true Christian will finally commit it;
- anyone who genuinely desires to turn to Christ in repentance and faith will be forgiven;
- those who commit the terminal sin never want to return; they have utterly repudiated Christ.

### Classification

The historical equation:

> Pharisaic Spirit-blasphemy = Beelzebul attribution

is **DIRECT/DISCOURSE EXEGESIS**.

The equation:

> Matt/Mark Spirit-blasphemy = Heb 6/10 apostasy = 1 John 5 deathward sin

is **SYSTEMATIC/CANONICAL SYNTHESIS**.

It is a strong conservative Reformed synthesis, but the project must label it rather than pretend all passages use the same lexeme/category explicitly.

# 11. Historical core vs broader theological essence

The project should preserve both levels.

## Historical core — HIGH confidence

The opponents:

- witness Spirit-empowered messianic liberation;
- cannot plausibly deny supernatural action;
- call the Holy Spirit’s work unclean/Satanic;
- do so from hardened opposition.

## Broader theological essence — HIGH-MEDIUM confidence

The sin represents:

> **malicious, settled reversal of known divine testimony to Christ, in which the Spirit’s witness is treated as satanic/evil and the sinner hardens into final repudiation rather than repentance.**

This broader wording explains why:

- Peter’s denial is forgivable;
- Paul’s ignorant blasphemy is forgivable;
- Pharisaic malicious reversal is not;
- Hebrews’ Christ-repudiation has family resemblance;
- 1 John’s deathward anti-Christ rejection has family resemblance.

---

# 12. Why “word against the Son of Man” can be forgiven

Matthew/Luke contrast:

- a word against the Son of Man — forgivable;
- blasphemy against the Spirit — unforgivable.

This is **not** because the Spirit is ontologically “more important” than the Son.

MacArthur/Calvin both emphasize degrees of light:

- Christ in humiliation could be misunderstood/attacked through ignorance;
- the Spirit’s confirming witness removes that excuse;
- calling that light darkness becomes deliberate resistance to recognized divine testimony.

### Biblical control: Paul

1 Tim 1:13:

Paul was a blasphemer/persecutor, yet received mercy because he acted `ἀγνοῶν ἐν ἀπιστίᾳ` — ignorantly in unbelief.

### CANONICAL INFERENCE

The contrast turns substantially on **light, knowledge and malicious opposition**, not a hierarchy within the Trinity.

---

# 13. Why it is unforgivable

### DIRECT EXEGESIS

Jesus says it will not be forgiven.

Mark calls it eternal sin.

Matthew says neither in this age nor the age to come.

Do not weaken that to:

> “forgiveness is merely difficult.”

Calvin explicitly rejects that evasion.

### SYSTEMATIC explanations

Conservative interpreters explain the finality in related ways:

- Calvin: judicial hardening/reprobate mind; they never desire repentance;
- MacArthur: hardened final refusal of the only path to pardon;
- Combs: full-knowledge denial of undeniable Spirit power; the sinner never seeks forgiveness;
- Schreiner: terminal repudiation of Christ, belonging to the apostasy/deathward-sin family.

### Project synthesis

The safest formulation:

> **The sin is unforgivable not because Christ’s atonement lacks value for some unusually large vocabulary offense, but because Jesus describes a terminal form of hardened Spirit-rejection that does not return to the Son in repentance and faith.**

Tag: `SYSTEMATIC SYNTHESIS`, not raw lexical definition.

The preferred reading is:

`ἔνοχός ἐστιν αἰωνίου ἁμαρτήματος`

“guilty of an eternal sin.”

Older/secondary manuscripts/readings include variants such as judgment/condemnation language.

ICC/older critical discussion:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/icc/mark/3.htm

### Publication rule

Before verbatim technical publication, check current NA28/ECM/UBS apparatus directly.

But no known variant materially removes the finality/unforgivability of the saying because Mark 3:29 already says no forgiveness forever.

# 15. Can a true believer commit it?

## Historical actors

Matthew/Mark address hostile scribes/Pharisees, not regenerate disciples.

No Synoptic text narrates a regenerate believer committing this sin and losing salvation.

## Peter control

A genuine disciple can deny Christ grievously through fear and be restored.

## Schreiner / Calvin / MacArthur / Combs convergence

Despite differences in precise definition, all four conservative models represented here deny that a true believer finally commits this terminal Spirit-blasphemy.

### SYSTEMATIC SYNTHESIS

The category fits final hardening/reprobate manifestation better than “loss of regeneration.”

This coheres with:

- John 10:27–30;
- Rom 8:29–39;
- 1 Pet 1:5;
- 1 John 2:19;
- 1 John 5:18;
- Jude 24;
- Heb warnings-as-means model.

### Restriction

That conclusion is not proven merely by the word `βλασφημέω`; it comes from actor identity, whole context and canonical preservation theology.

# 16. Relation to Hebrews 6 / 10 and 1 John 5

## Similarities

All involve:

- great light;
- conscious repudiation;
- severe/final judgment;
- hardening rather than ordinary weakness;
- Spirit/Christ rejection;
- no ordinary restoration promise.

## Differences

### Gospels

Concrete historical act:
- Spirit’s work through Jesus called satanic/unclean.

### Hebrews 6

- extraordinary covenant/Spirit-age experience;
- fall away;
- recrucify and disgrace Son.

### Hebrews 10

- trample Son;
- profane covenant blood;
- insult Spirit of grace.

### 1 John 5

- sin toward death within anti-Christ/secessionist context.

### Therefore

Best category:

> **terminal-hardening family with different textual manifestations**, not one mechanically identical formula.

This improves on saying “all are exactly the same sin” while preserving Schreiner’s theological insight.

# 17. Could it happen today? Competing conservative models

## Model A — Combs narrow sign-miracle limitation

- exact sin concerns supernatural sign-miracle power;
- apostolic sign miracles ceased;
- exact sin therefore not commit-able today.

Weight: `SERIOUS / MEDIUM`.

## Model B — Calvin/MacArthur broader full-light malicious rejection

- Beelzebul accusation is historical paradigm;
- essential sin is malicious reversal of clearly recognized Spirit testimony;
- analogous terminal hardening remains possible wherever Spirit-borne gospel witness is knowingly rejected.

Weight: `HIGH-MEDIUM`.

## Model C — Schreiner terminal apostasy synthesis

- historical manifestation is Beelzebul attribution;
- wider category culminates in utter rejection of Christ;
- same deathward family as Hebrews/1 John.

Weight: `HIGH-MEDIUM SYSTEMATIC`.

### Current project preference

Model B/C combined is preferable:

> **The exact first-century manifestation cannot be abstracted from Jesus’ Spirit-authenticated works, but the theological category of malicious final rejection of the Spirit’s testimony to Christ is not obviously confined to that one historical moment.**

Luke 12 especially prevents overconfidence in an absolute “then only” restriction.

# 18. Pastoral theology — the worried person

This is one of the most pastorally abused texts in Scripture.

## Strong evidence against the feared diagnosis

The Gospel opponents:

- do not grieve that they have rejected Christ;
- do not seek pardon;
- do not ask how to return;
- they confidently identify Christ’s Spirit-wrought work as satanic;
- they move toward killing Him.

Schreiner’s official SBTS summary is pastorally precise:

> anyone who wants to turn to Christ in repentance and faith is forgiven.

Source:
- https://equip.sbts.edu/video/what-is-the-unforgivable-sin/

### But avoid a mechanical syllogism

Do not say:

> “If you ever worry, mathematically you cannot have committed it.”

Better:

> **A tender conscience that hates unbelief and genuinely desires Christ is the opposite posture from the hardened malicious repudiation described in the Synoptics.**

That is strong pastoral evidence, not an inspired psychological diagnostic formula.

# 21. Preferred project synthesis

> **The Synoptic blasphemy against the Holy Spirit is not an accidental word, intrusive thought, ordinary blasphemy, grievous lapse, or Peter-like denial under fear. Its historical core is explicit: religious leaders saw Jesus liberating people by the Spirit of God and persistently declared that this divine work was satanic; Mark says Jesus issued the warning “because they were saying, ‘He has an unclean spirit.’” The sin therefore involves extraordinary light joined to malicious reversal of the Spirit’s testimony to Christ. Jesus calls it absolutely unforgivable and Mark calls it an eternal sin. Conservative interpreters differ over how narrowly the category should be carried forward: William Combs restricts the exact sin to periods of Spirit-wrought sign miracles, whereas Calvin, MacArthur and Schreiner identify a broader theological essence of hardened, knowing repudiation of the Spirit’s witness to Christ. Luke 12, where the warning stands amid confession, persecution and the Spirit’s future witness through disciples, makes an exclusively one-time historical limitation less certain. The safest synthesis is to preserve the concrete Beelzebul context while recognizing a wider terminal-hardening category. This category has strong family resemblance to Hebrews 6/10 and 1 John 5, but the texts should not be declared lexically identical. Nothing in the Synoptic narratives shows a regenerate believer losing salvation through this sin; Peter’s restored denial supplies the opposite control.**
