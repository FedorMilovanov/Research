# Иеремия 2–3 — источник и разбитые водоёмы, заветная измена, ложное покаяние, возвращение и исцеление отступления

> Иер. 2–3 — один из центральных корпусов всей темы. Он показывает apostasy одновременно как **оставление Личности**, **обмен источника на заменитель**, **брачную измену**, **нежелание принимать исправление**, **самооправдание**, **ложное покаяние** и всё же как состояние, к которому Бог адресует реальный призыв: «возвратись». Кульминация 3:22 использует плотную игру одного семейства слов: «обратитесь, отвратившиеся дети; Я исцелю ваши отступления».

---

# I. Иер. 2:1–3 — отступничество оценивается на фоне прежней заветной любви

## 1. Бог начинает не с абстрактного закона, а с отношений

Яхве вспоминает раннюю верность/любовь Израиля, язык брачного начала и следования за Ним в пустыне.

Исторически поколение исхода было далеко от идеала; функция образа сравнительная и заветная: нынешняя измена выглядит тем страшнее на фоне первоначального посвящения.

### Для taxonomy

Отступничество — не только нарушение нормы. Это **измена полученной благодати и известным отношениям**.

### Guardrail

Corporate marital language ≠ утверждение, что каждый член народа был индивидуально возрождён.

---

# II. Иер. 2:5 — первый вопрос: «какую неправду нашли во Мне?»

## 2. Apostasy begins with false moral judgment of God

Бог спрашивает, что отцы нашли в Нём неправого, что удалились.

Это перекликается с Быт. 3: отступление требует хотя бы практического verdict:

> «Бог/Его путь недостаточно добр; иной путь даст мне больше».

### Deception pattern

Before the idol is embraced, God’s sufficiency/trustworthiness is functionally downgraded.

---

# III. Иер. 2:11–13 — apostasy as exchange

## 3. Народы обычно не меняют своих богов — Израиль меняет славу на бесполезное

Иеремия усиливает вину covenant people: даже народы демонстрируют своеобразную религиозную постоянность, тогда как Израиль меняет славу на то, что не приносит пользы.

Это важный precursor к Рим. 1 language of exchange.

## 4. «Два зла» 2:13

Israel has committed two evils:

1. **оставили Меня** — Источник живой воды;
2. **высекли себе** водоёмы — разбитые, неспособные удерживать воду.

### Extremely important structure

Apostasy is not merely subtraction:

> Бог → пустота.

It is usually exchange:

> **leave living source → construct substitute**.

The substitute can be:

- idol;
- political alliance;
- wealth;
- self-righteous religion;
- human wisdom;
- autonomous desire.

Immediate Jeremiah context primarily concerns idolatry and false trusts; later canonical application must not erase historical referent.

### Water metaphor

Ancient commentators and modern summaries note contrast between fresh/running/life-giving water and human cisterns that are limited and here cracked.

Sources/discovery:
- NET Jeremiah 2:13
- Cambridge/Ellicott collected commentary

### Canonical bridges

- Ps 36:9 — fountain of life;
- Isa 55 — why spend for what does not satisfy;
- John 4; 7 — living water Christ/Spirit context;
- Rev 21–22 — water of life.

Do not claim direct quotation where not established; use as canonical motif.

# IV. Иер. 2:17,19 — sin becomes its own teacher

## 5. «Не сам ли ты причинил себе это, оставив Господа?»

Judgment is not arbitrary attachment to otherwise harmless choice.

## 6. `Твоё нечестие накажет тебя, и отступления твои обличат тебя`

One of the deepest apostasy principles:

> evil carries revelatory consequences; the broken cistern eventually demonstrates that it is broken.

### But consequences do not automatically produce repentance

Jeremiah later shows people persisting even after correction/judgment. Experience alone does not convert without grace.

---

# V. Иер. 2:20–25 — addiction imagery before modern addiction vocabulary

## 7. Broken yoke / sexual-animal metaphors

Israel’s pursuit of idols is portrayed as:

- refusal of covenant yoke;
- prostitution;
- camel running about;
- wild donkey in heat, difficult to restrain.

### Meaning

Apostasy is not always calm intellectual reassessment. Desire can become **compulsive allegiance**.

### Taxonomy

This belongs between:

- divided heart;
- entrenched habit;
- deeds that resist return (cf. Hos 5:4);
- judicial hardening.

---

# VI. Иер. 2:23,35 — self-deception: «я не осквернился» / «я невиновен»

## 8. One of the most dangerous stages

The people are not only sinful; they deny accurate diagnosis.

### Progression

sin → rationalization → innocence claim → resistance to correction.

### Canonical parallels

- Saul in 1 Sam 15: «я исполнил слово Господа» before excuses;
- Prov 30:20 adulteress wipes mouth and says no wrong;
- 1 John 1:8–10 denial of sin;
- Rev 3:17 Laodicea says rich/needing nothing.

### Pastoral insight

The point at which a person loses ability/willingness to name sin as sin is often more dangerous than the first fall itself.

---

# VII. Иер. 2:27–28 — crisis religion without covenant return

## 9. «В бедствии скажут: встань и спаси нас»

People turn to God as emergency resource while maintaining rival gods.

### Thin distinction

**Calling for rescue ≠ returning to God.**

This is OT counterpart to Hosea’s shallow return and Judas’s remorse: distress can generate religious words without changed allegiance.

---

# VIII. Иер. 2:29–30 — rejecting correction escalates guilt

God says discipline has not produced response; people do not accept correction and even destroy prophetic messengers.

### Project-wide mechanism

A decisive escalation marker is **response to rebuke**:

- Cain warned before murder;
- Saul argues with Samuel;
- Judah rejects Jeremiah;
- Heb 3 says exhort daily before hardened;
- Rev 2–3 repeatedly: hear what Spirit says.

### Working rule

> Fall is serious; **war against correction** is often the gateway to hardened apostasy.

---

# IX. Иер. 3:1–5 — legal impossibility sharpens grace

## 10. Deuteronomy 24 divorce background

Jeremiah uses the shocking marriage/divorce analogy: wife leaves husband, joins another, and return appears legally impossible under Deut 24 analogy.

TGC Jeremiah commentary emphasizes the dilemma: covenant adultery creates legal/judicial seriousness, not sentimental estrangement.

### Why this matters

When God later says «return», grace is not cheap overlooking of law. Restoration will require an act of God that ultimately fits Jeremiah’s wider new-covenant/redemptive horizon.

### Caution

The precise canonical resolution via Christ/Rom 7 is theological synthesis; do not claim Jer 3 itself explicitly explains cross mechanics.

---

# X. Иер. 3:6–11 — Israel apostate, Judah worse because she saw the warning

## 11. `Backsliding Israel` vs `treacherous Judah`

Northern Israel’s covenant apostasy and exile become a **warning example** to Judah.

Judah sees the judgment and still follows.

### Increased light → increased accountability

This is a major canonical principle:

> witnessing another’s judgment and refusing the lesson aggravates guilt.

Parallels:

- Daniel 5: Belshazzar knew Nebuchadnezzar’s humbling but did not humble heart;
- Jude 5 and wilderness examples;
- Heb 3–4 reads Israel as warning to church;
- 1 Cor 10: examples written for us.

---

# XI. Иер. 3:10 — Judah returns only falsely

## 12. `not with her whole heart, but in pretense/falsehood`

This is essential for evaluating Josiah-era reform: external reform can coexist with unconverted corporate heart.

### Project distinction

There is:

- no return;
- shallow/feigned return;
- real return;
- God-given/healed return.

Do not collapse them.

### Canonical parallel

Hosea 6:1–4 similarly shows beautiful return-language followed by exposure of evaporating covenant love.

---

# XII. Иер. 3:12–13 — astonishing call to already judged/exiled apostate Israel

## 13. «Возвратись, отступница Израиль»

God calls the northern kingdom, already under covenant judgment/exile.

Required response includes **acknowledge guilt** — restoration does not bypass confession.

### Massive taxonomy control

The label “apostate/backsliding Israel” is not treated as semantically equivalent to:

> «a category to which God can no longer meaningfully say return».

Therefore English/Russian apostasy terminology must remain stratified.

---

# XIII. Иер. 3:14 — «возвратитесь, отступившие дети»

## 14. Covenant/family language persists in the call

God addresses them as wayward/backsliding sons/children and speaks of bringing a remnant to Zion.

### Corporate-remnant caution

Do not infer universal restoration of every individual exile. The promise has remnant/corporate/eschatological dimensions.

But do not erase the relational force either: judgment has not annihilated covenant purpose.

---

# XIV. Иер. 3:15–18 — restoration includes shepherds and nations

## 15. God returns people not merely to private spirituality

He promises shepherds after His heart who feed with knowledge/understanding.

### Perseverance-through-means implication

Restoration creates **pastoral structures of truth**.

This connects strongly with:

- Ezek 34;
- John 10;
- 1 Pet 2:25; 5:1–4;
- Heb 13 shepherd accountability.

### 3:17 nations

Future restoration expands beyond Israel; nations gather to Yahweh.

This helps explain how Israel-restoration vocabulary can later shape Gentile Christian identity without requiring identical biography.

---

# XV. Иер. 3:21–22 — linguistic center: turn the turning ones; heal their turnings

## 16. Hebrew family

Jer 3:22 famously clusters the turn/backslide family:

- imperative from `שׁוּב` — return/turn;
- address `שׁוֹבָבִים` — turning-away / faithless / backsliding children;
- noun `מְשׁוּבֹתֵיכֶם` — your turnings/backslidings/faithlessness.

Ellicott summarizes the force as something like:

> turn, children who have turned; I will heal your turnings.

TGC commentary similarly emphasizes the wordplay and interprets the healing as divine action overcoming their inability/waywardness toward repentance.

### Crucial doctrine-method lesson

One root-family can describe **both apostasy and repentance**, depending on direction.

> The moral question is not merely whether movement exists, but **which way the heart turns**.

This validates the directional-language rule established in 1 Pet 2:25 / Gal 4.

---

# XVI. «Я исцелю ваши отступления» — not only forgive consequences

## 17. Healing metaphor

`רָפָא` + `מְשׁוּבָה` presents waywardness as moral-spiritual sickness requiring God’s cure.

### Parallel Hosea 14:4

Hosea ends with nearly the same theology: God heals Israel’s `מְשׁוּבָה`.

### Jeremiah deepens it toward new covenant

Jer 24:7; 31:31–34; 32:39–40 will later promise:

- heart to know God;
- law written on heart;
- one heart/way;
- fear of God so that people will not turn away from Him.

### Project-level synthesis

> Final answer to chronic covenant apostasy is not a stronger external threat alone but **God’s heart-transforming covenant action**.

Warnings remain means; new-heart grace addresses the disease warnings expose.

---

# XVII. 3:22–25 — genuine repentance reinterprets former saviors

The response confesses:

- Yahweh is our God;
- hills/mountains/idolatrous worship are delusion/false salvation;
- shame/idolatry consumed inherited labor;
- sin is ours and fathers’;
- we did not obey God’s voice.

### Contrast with Jer 2:35

Earlier: «I am innocent.»

Now: «we have sinned.»

### Diagnostic marker

Real repentance includes **new interpretation of the past**:

- idol once called savior → now confessed as shame;
- self once called innocent → now guilt acknowledged;
- God once abandoned → now confessed as only salvation.

---

# XVIII. Jer 2–3 and the anatomy of deceptive apostasy

The sequence can be mapped:

1. known covenant love;
2. questioning/forsaking God;
3. exchange for substitutes;
4. desire becomes compulsive;
5. crisis without true return;
6. correction rejected;
7. innocence claimed;
8. apostasy becomes corporate inheritance;
9. another generation watches judgment and repeats it;
10. external reform can be feigned;
11. God still summons return;
12. genuine repentance requires confession;
13. God promises healing, shepherding and heart transformation.

This is one of the richest canonical trajectories in Scripture.

---

# XIX. Jer 2:13 ↔ John 6:68 — broken cisterns vs “to whom shall we go?”

Not a claimed direct literary quotation, but a powerful canonical contrast:

Jeremiah:

> leave living fountain → construct broken alternatives.

Peter:

> «Lord, to whom shall we go? You have words of eternal life.»

### Perseverance as sane recognition of reality

Perseverance is not bare heroic self-control. It is recognition:

> every alternative cistern leaks; life is in God/Christ alone.

This can become a strong flagship motif if clearly labeled canonical synthesis rather than direct intertext.

---

# XX. Jeremiah and terminal/judicial hardening later in the book

Jer 2–3 offers real return/healing. Later Jeremiah also reaches phases where historical judgment becomes irreversible for that generation/city despite continued prophetic ministry (e.g. 19:15 and surrounding judgment trajectory).

### Therefore

Jeremiah itself prevents both simplifications:

- «God always gives endless historical opportunities with no judicial turning point»;
- «once Scripture calls a people apostate/backsliding, no return can be offered».

The book knows **invitation, feigned return, inability, healing grace, prolonged refusal and judicial closure**.

---

# XXI. Relationship to Hebrews 3

Jeremiah’s people repeatedly refuse to hear and stiffen themselves; Hebrews warns Christian hearers:

> today, if you hear His voice, do not harden hearts.

### Canonical warning

Covenant history becomes means of preservation for later church.

### New-covenant difference

Hebrews also draws heavily on Jeremiah 31: new covenant is superior and heart-level. Therefore the same prophetic book supplies both:

- anatomy of old-covenant apostasy;
- promise of God’s deeper covenant remedy.

This relationship deserves a later Jeremiah31↔Hebrews8/10 technical dossier.

---

# XXII. What Jer 2–3 clearly proves

High confidence:

1. Apostasy is leaving God Himself, not merely breaking abstract rules.
2. Apostasy normally includes replacement/exchange, not empty neutrality.
3. Idols/false saviors are structurally unable to provide what God provides.
4. Desire, self-deception and rejection of correction deepen apostasy.
5. Corporate covenant apostasy can persist across generations.
6. Witnessing prior judgment increases accountability.
7. Religious return can be false/half-hearted.
8. God can meaningfully command an apostate/backsliding people to return.
9. God promises to heal their backsliding/faithlessness.
10. Genuine return includes confession and reclassification of former idols as false salvation.
11. Restoration includes shepherding and eventually heart-level covenant renewal.

---

# XXIII. What Jer 2–3 does NOT prove alone

It does not by itself prove:

- every Israelite described as spouse/child was individually regenerate;
- every terminal NT apostate can be renewed;
- historical exile/judgment was cancelled for all who heard return-language;
- feigned return and genuine repentance are indistinguishable;
- human repentance is meritorious cause of restoration;
- `backsliding` is a harmless temporary wobble;
- Jeremiah’s marriage metaphor mechanically maps to individual NT justification loss;
- new covenant can be understood without later Jeremiah/Hebrews fulfillment.

# XXV. Working conclusion

> **Иеремия изображает отступление как безумный обмен: оставить Самого Бога — живой источник — и трудиться над резервуарами, которые не удержат воду. Измена углубляется через желание, самообман, ложную невиновность и сопротивление исправлению; даже религиозное “возвращение” может оказаться притворным. Но именно такому народу Бог говорит: “возвратитесь, отвратившиеся дети; Я исцелю ваши отступления”. Значит, библейское backsliding/apostasy-language нельзя автоматически отождествлять с терминальной невозможностью Евр. 6. Иеремия знает и реальный призыв к возврату, и Божье исцеление сердца, и позднейший судебный предел для упорно отвергающего слово поколения.**
