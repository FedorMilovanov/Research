## 1. P0 textual correction: привычная KJV/Синодальная синтаксическая форма не является современной critical-text reading

### SBLGNT research witness

Exact SBLGNT John 17:12:

`ὅτε ἤμην μετʼ αὐτῶν ἐγὼ ἐτήρουν αὐτοὺς ἐν τῷ ὀνόματί σου ᾧ δέδωκάς μοι, καὶ ἐφύλαξα, καὶ οὐδεὶς ἐξ αὐτῶν ἀπώλετο εἰ μὴ ὁ υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας, ἵνα ἡ γραφὴ πληρωθῇ.`

The crucial form is singular/neuter-dative **`ᾧ`**:

> “I was keeping them in your name **which you have given me**, and I guarded [them], and none of them perished except the son of destruction...”

The traditional/Byzantine/TR form has plural **`οὓς`** (“whom you gave me”), generating the familiar KJV/NKJV-style clause “those whom You gave Me I have kept.”

### Manuscript / edition status

A textual-variant survey records:

- critical reading `ᾧ` (“which [name]”): אᶜ B C* L W 33 etc.; represented by modern critical editions/translations;
- traditional `οὓς` (“whom [people]”): A C³ D K X Δ Θ Π Ψ f¹ f¹³ etc., Byzantine tradition;
- shorter form without the relative clause also exists (P66* / א* etc.).

The survey explicitly notes substantial textual doubt but judges the plural may have been influenced by 17:6 (“the men whom you gave me”). SBLGNT, NA-family, THGNT, WH and Tregelles print the singular referring to the **name**.

### Research consequence

Do **not** build the Judas argument on the sentence:

> “Ин. 17:12 прямо говорит: ‘тех, которых Ты дал Мне, Я сохранил; никто из них не погиб, кроме Иуды’.”

That is a legitimate traditional-text reading, but it is not the wording of the current SBLGNT research witness.

Under the critical reading, the disputed theological question moves one step back:

> Who are the `αὐτούς / ἐξ αὐτῶν` whom Jesus kept and guarded, and does `εἰ μή` place Judas inside exactly the same salvific class described in 17:6–11?

This distinction must appear in Product if the article discusses the grammar.

## 2. The saving-gift vocabulary in John is genuinely strong

The fact that 17:12 has a textual variant does **not** erase the Johannine “given ones” theology.

### John 6:37

`πᾶν ὃ δίδωσίν μοι ὁ πατὴρ πρὸς ἐμὲ ἥξει`

All that the Father gives the Son **will come** to Him; the one coming will not be cast out.

### John 6:39

`πᾶν ὃ δέδωκέν μοι μὴ ἀπολέσω ἐξ αὐτοῦ ἀλλὰ ἀναστήσω αὐτὸ τῇ ἐσχάτῃ ἡμέρᾳ`

The Father’s will is that Christ lose **nothing of all He has given**, but raise it at the last day.

### John 17:2

`πᾶν ὃ δέδωκας αὐτῷ ... ζωὴν αἰώνιον`

The “given” class is directly connected with eternal life.

### John 17:6–9

The men given out of the world are characterized as those who:

- kept the Father’s word (17:6);
- received Jesus’ words (17:8);
- truly knew His divine mission (17:8);
- believed that the Father sent Him (17:8);
- belong to the Father and are the objects of Jesus’ prayer (17:9).

### John 18:9

At the arrest John explicitly says Jesus’ action fulfilled His saying:

`ὅτι Οὓς δέδωκάς μοι οὐκ ἀπώλεσα ἐξ αὐτῶν οὐδένα`

> “Of those whom you have given me I lost not one.”

This is an explicit **given-ones + no-loss** statement with no Judas exception in the arrest scene.

### Research conclusion

John undeniably has a strong saving/preserving use of “given by the Father.” Therefore the solution cannot simply be:

> “In John, being given to Christ is just an office term.”

That is false as a general Johannine statement.

The narrower question is whether **Judas shares that saving-gift category**, or belongs to a different relation to Jesus (chosen to the Twelve / apostolic office / visible discipleship) while the prayer’s saving-gift class is the believing remainder.

## 3. John 6 gives the most important prehistory of Judas

### 6:64 — unbelief before betrayal

John writes:

`ᾔδει γὰρ ἐξ ἀρχῆς ὁ Ἰησοῦς τίνες εἰσὶν οἱ μὴ πιστεύοντες καὶ τίς ἐστιν ὁ παραδώσων αὐτόν.`

Jesus knew “from the beginning” who the **unbelieving ones** were and who would betray Him.

This is still the strongest narrative control against treating Judas as a straightforward example of saving faith later lost.

### 6:65 — coming depends on the Father’s grant

Immediately after the unbelief statement:

`οὐδεὶς δύναται ἐλθεῖν πρός με ἐὰν μὴ ᾖ δεδομένον αὐτῷ ἐκ τοῦ πατρός.`

No one can come unless it is granted from the Father.

### 6:70–71 — a different category of “choice”

Jesus says:

`Οὐκ ἐγὼ ὑμᾶς τοὺς δώδεκα ἐξελεξάμην; καὶ ἐξ ὑμῶν εἷς διάβολός ἐστιν.`

John then identifies Judas.

This proves at minimum:

- Judas was genuinely **chosen as one of the Twelve**;
- that office/mission choice does not itself prove saving faith;
- John can hold “chosen among Twelve” and “devil / betrayer / unbelief” together.

### Methodological payoff

Do not flatten distinct Johannine relations:

- Father **gives** people to Son in a saving/preserving frame;
- Jesus **chooses** the Twelve in an apostolic/mission frame;
- Judas unquestionably belongs to the second;
- whether he belongs to the first must be proved, not assumed from his apostolic inclusion.

## 4. John 13:18 creates a second internal distinction within the Twelve

John 13:18:

`οὐ περὶ πάντων ὑμῶν λέγω· ἐγὼ οἶδα τίνας ἐξελεξάμην`

> “I am not speaking of all of you; I know whom I have chosen...”

Then Psalm 41 is invoked in connection with betrayal.

This statement is difficult for any simplistic equation:

> every “choice” of the Twelve = identical saving election.

John 6:70 can say all Twelve were chosen for their role, while 13:18 uses election language with an exclusion inside the group.

The future article should therefore distinguish:

1. corporate/apostolic selection of the Twelve;
2. the narrower saving/relational category manifested in faith, keeping the word, abiding and final preservation.

Do not claim that `ἐκλέγομαι` itself has two lexically fixed technical senses; the distinction is contextual/narrative.

## 5. Does `εἰ μή` force Judas inside the same class?

This is the central grammatical dispute after adopting the critical text.

John 17:12 says:

`καὶ οὐδεὶς ἐξ αὐτῶν ἀπώλετο εἰ μὴ ὁ υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας`

Natural surface reading:

> none of them perished **except** the son of destruction.

### Inclusion reading

Older technical interpreters such as Alford take the exception strongly: Judas is one of the relevant `αὐτοί`, and therefore one of those included in the guarded group in some sense. Alford explicitly used this against irresistible-preservation readings.

Ellicott likewise reads Judas as included in the group under Jesus’ guardianship and then lost by his own act.

This is a genuine historic Protestant counter-reading and should not be hidden.

### Non-class-membership / limited-exception reading

Other interpreters point to constructions where `εἰ μή` introduces an exception without requiring the excepted individual to belong to **every defining feature** of the preceding class. Standard examples invoked include Luke 4:26–27: the widow of Zarephath is not thereby a widow **in Israel**, nor Naaman a leper **in Israel**.

Godet, Pulpit, older Reformed interpreters and others therefore argue that Judas may be the exception only to the proposition **“none perished”**, not an assertion that he shared every salvific predicate of 17:6–11.

### Best research formulation

`εἰ μή` alone does **not** settle the dogmatic question.

It certainly marks Judas as the tragic contrast within the disciple scene. But moving from:

> “Judas is the one who perished”

to:

> “Judas therefore possessed the same Father-given saving faith and eternal-life status as the men described in 17:6–9”

requires an additional discourse argument.

That argument is under pressure from 6:64 and 13:18.

## 6. John 18:9 is a crucial canonical control

At the arrest, Jesus causes His disciples to be released, and John comments that this fulfilled the saying:

> “Of those whom You gave Me I lost none.”

### Immediate narrative level

The fulfillment is physical: Jesus ensures the Eleven are not arrested with Him.

### Johannine theological level

John deliberately uses the **same no-loss vocabulary** that already appeared in 6:39 and 17:12. Physical preservation in the garden functions as an enacted sign/witness of Jesus’ shepherding preservation.

### Judas and the omission of the exception

By John 18 Judas is not among the protected disciples; he stands with the arresting party. The evangelist can therefore apply the “given ones / none lost” formula to the disciples without repeating the Judas exception.

This does not mathematically prove Judas was never in any “given” category. But it creates strong narrative pressure against using 17:12 as proof that Christ **failed to preserve one savingly given sheep**.

The Gospel’s own later fulfillment statement presents Christ’s no-loss word as successfully fulfilled.

## 7. The “Scripture fulfilled” clause is more complex than one proof text

A dedicated 2019 study by Adam Kubiś (*The Biblical Annals* 9/1, 131–176) surveys the history of interpretation and shows that `ἡ γραφή` in 17:12 has been linked to:

- Judas’ tragic fate;
- the preservation of the other disciples;
- specific OT passages (especially Psalm 41 through John 13:18, Psalm 109 through Acts 1, other proposals);
- or the **general tenor of Scripture** rather than one verse;
- a minority proposal also hears Jesus’ own prior prophetic words (6:39, 6:70–71), especially in light of 18:9.

Kubiś finally prefers, with J. Ramsey Michaels, a general Scriptural pattern rather than insisting on one exact OT proof text, while allowing Johannine double entendre / correspondence with Jesus’ own prophetic word.

### Why this matters for Judas

The clause should not be used to make God/Scripture the efficient cause of Judas’ unbelief in a way that removes Judas’ agency.

The Fourth Gospel simultaneously portrays:

- Scripture/divine foreknowledge being fulfilled;
- Judas as thief (12:6), betrayer and morally responsible actor;
- satanic influence (13:2,27);
- Jesus as unsurprised by the betrayal (6:64,70–71; 13:18–19).

Foreordination/fulfillment and moral responsibility are narrated together, not as alternatives.

## 8. “Son of perdition” is a verdict, not a standalone proof of the route to that verdict

`ὁ υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας` is a Semitic-style characterization: a person marked by/deserving/bound up with destruction.

The phrase powerfully identifies Judas’ end. It does **not by itself** tell us:

- whether he once possessed regeneration;
- exactly how divine foreknowledge and human freedom relate;
- whether `ἀπώλεια` here is merely temporal death (the Johannine context strongly pressures beyond that);
- which theory of predestination must be adopted.

The soteriological judgment must come from the whole Judas dossier, not the idiom alone.

## 9. Strongest competing models

### Model A — Reformed office/saving-gift distinction

- Judas truly chosen to the Twelve and given apostolic office/privileges;
- John 6:64 locates betrayal against prior unbelief;
- 17:6–9’s given men are believers who keep/receive the word;
- 17:12’s exception marks Judas’ ruin without requiring him to share every saving predicate;
- 18:9 / 6:39 confirm successful preservation of the Father-given saving class.

**Strength:** best integration of 6:37–40, 6:64–71, 13:18, 17:6–12, 18:9.

**Pressure:** must not evade the ordinary exceptive force of `εἰ μή` or pretend Judas was a mere outsider; his inclusion in Jesus’ closest disciple body was completely real.

### Model B — Judas included among the given/guarded disciples and truly falls

Alford/Ellicott-type historic reading:

- `εἰ μή` naturally makes Judas the exception inside the group;
- he received real guardianship and disciple status;
- his destruction proves preservation is not irresistible/automatic.

**Strength:** respects the surface exception and intensity of Judas’ real inclusion.

**Pressure:** must explain 6:64’s prior unbelief, 13:18’s internal distinction and the very strong no-loss/last-day promises in 6:39 and 18:9.

### Model C — Moloney-type maximal openness about Judas’ final judgment

In scholarship surveyed by Kubiś, Francis Moloney pushes further and resists treating the Gospel as making a simple final judgment on Judas, emphasizing the Father’s care and the complex narrative relation of 17:11–12.

**Strength:** resists importing later dogmatic finality into every narrative detail.

**Pressure:** difficult to reconcile with `υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας`, 6:64/70, 13:18, 13:27 and the Gospel’s sustained negative characterization.

For our conservative Product this should be presented as a scholarly alternative, not adopted.

## 10. Updated research verdict on Judas

After the textual-critical and discourse pass, the previous dossier’s main conclusion is **strengthened but must be worded more carefully**.

### Strongly supported

- Judas was genuinely one of the Twelve, chosen to apostolic office/mission.
- He is not portrayed as a mere external stranger or fabricated disciple.
- John 6:64 places the coming betrayal in the category of unbelief known to Jesus “from the beginning.”
- 6:70–71 shows office-choice can coexist with radical spiritual evil.
- 13:18 introduces an explicit distinction within the circle.
- John’s saving “given ones” language in 6:37–40 and 17:2,6–9 is extremely strong and terminates in eternal life/preservation.
- 18:9 presents Jesus’ “none of those given to me was lost” saying as fulfilled, not failed.

### Therefore

The best current reading is **not**:

> “Judas was a saved sheep whom Christ lost.”

It is:

> Judas was truly given a place in the apostolic circle and genuinely guarded within Jesus’ historical disciple body, but the Fourth Gospel never gives us a clean basis for placing him inside the same saving gift/election class whose defining marks are coming to Christ, believing, keeping the word, receiving eternal life and being raised at the last day. John 6:64 supplies the strongest internal reason: his final betrayal manifests an unbelief Jesus already knew.

### Calibration

Do not write:

> “John 17:12 proves Judas was **not** one of the given ones.”

That is too strong because the exception grammar and textual history remain genuinely debated.

Write instead:

> “Ин. 17:12 сам по себе не доказывает, что Иуда принадлежал к тому же спасительному классу ‘данных Отцом’, о котором Ин. 6:37–40 обещает, что Христос не потеряет никого. В критическом тексте фраза ‘которое Ты дал Мне’ относится к имени, а не к людям; принадлежность Иуды к спасительному классу должна решаться всей Иоанновой картиной. А она уже в Ин. 6:64 помещает будущего предателя на фоне неверия.”

This is the current strongest Product-oriented research judgment.
