# 6. Cross-tradition fairness gate

Before **any** deep-dive article becomes candidate, its strongest opposing view must be represented from a source that would recognize itself.

Minimum:

## Reformed

Primary/confessional + modern critical exegesis. Do not substitute Calvin/Owen for modern textual work.

## Arminian/Wesleyan / conditional security

At least one serious scholarly/primary treatment of the exact passage. Do not use only popular apologetic summaries.

## Lutheran

Use where historically/doctrinally relevant; not necessary in every verse article if it adds no distinct exegetical model.

## Roman Catholic

Use for confessional spectrum, especially doctrine of post-justification loss; do not pretend Trent is an exegetical commentary on every passage.

## Free Grace

Represent where it supplies genuinely distinct reading (Hebrews, John15, 1Cor9, Rev2–3). Clearly label advocacy/confessional sources and test locally.

### Fairness test

Before rejecting a view, record:

1. its strongest textual argument;
2. what it explains better than competitors;
3. the exact pressure point causing rejection/limitation.

No straw-man paragraphs such as “Arminians simply ignore election” or “Calvinists simply redefine every word.”

# 7. Evidence-quality gate

A claim cannot become reader-facing merely because there are many links.

For every nontrivial claim require:

1. **Biblical locus** — exact verse/context.
2. **Textual state** — stable / variant / disputed.
3. **Lexical/grammatical claim** — backed by critical text/lexicon/commentary, not sermon etymology.
4. **Historical attribution** — primary object + exact section/page/version.
5. **Modern scholarly claim** — exact page/section where practical.
6. **Counter-reading** — strongest available.
7. **Confidence label** — explicit text / strong inference / disputed inference.
8. **Translation/rights state** for quotations.

### Automatic HOLD triggers

- quote without exact locator;
- text-critical variant ignored when conclusion depends on it;
- aggregator used as final authority for contested lexical claim;
- search snippet used as proof;
- confessional conclusion substituted for exegesis;
- reader-facing statement stronger than source claim;
- one tradition represented only by hostile secondary description.
