# 2. DIRECT EXEGESIS — ближайший референт `ἡγιάσθη`

Евр. 10:29 описывает одного виновного через последовательность singular forms:

- `ἀξιωθήσεται` — будет признан достойным наказания;
- `καταπατήσας` — попрал Сына Божия;
- `ἡγησάμενος` — счёл кровь завета κοινόν;
- `ἡγιάσθη` — был освящён;
- `ἐνυβρίσας` — оскорбил Духа благодати.

На уровне обычного discourse flow естественный implicit subject `ἡγιάσθη` — тот же предупреждаемый/апостат.

### DIRECT EXEGESIS verdict

**Apostate/person referent = most natural reading.**

Это не значит, что иные конструкции абсолютно неграмматичны. Но:

- Christ-referent требует внезапной смены implicit subject внутри единой цепи;
- impersonal “one is sanctified” требует generic shift внутри той же персональной цепи.

Поэтому burden of proof лежит на этих специальных чтениях.

# 3. Christ-referent: исторически серьёзный, но технически слабый

## John Owen

Оуэн толкует Христа как Того, Кто был посвящён/освящён Своей собственной кровью к вечному первосвященству.

Это не бессмысленная догматическая уловка: у позиции есть priestly-consecration architecture и связь с Ин. 17:19.

Но внутрипосланный material теперь весит против неё.

## Hebrews 2:11

Основная ролевая схема:

> Христос — `ὁ ἁγιάζων`; люди — `οἱ ἁγιαζόμενοι`.

## Hebrews 13:12

Ближайшая явная конструкция “blood + sanctify”:

> Иисус пострадал, **чтобы освятить народ Своей собственной кровью**.

То есть:

- Christ = sanctifier;
- His blood = means;
- people = sanctified object.

Чтобы в 10:29 Христос внезапно стал **sanctified object by His own blood**, нужно позитивное доказательство, а не просто теологическая возможность.

## Thomas Schreiner

Secondary web witnesses reproduce his commentary discussion with exact locator:

- Thomas R. Schreiner, *Commentary on Hebrews* (B&H, 2015), pp. 326–327;
- he calls Christ-referent “awkward and unnatural” and stresses that Jesus is the sanctifier in Hebrews, not the one sanctified by His blood.

Research witnesses:
- https://answeringislam.blog/limited-atonement-debate-pt-2/
- https://scripturalmormonism.blogspot.com/2017/10/an-examination-and-critique-of.html

**Custody warning:** these are hostile/secondary quotation hosts. The page locator is useful; verbatim publication quote requires original book verification.

### Weight

`CHRIST-REFERENT = HISTORICAL MINORITY / POSSIBLE BUT LOW CURRENT TECHNICAL WEIGHT`.

Do not present it as “the Greek naturally means Christ.”

# 4. George Guthrie’s impersonal reading

George H. Guthrie explicitly translates:

> “the blood of the covenant by which **one** is sanctified”

rather than “by which **he** was sanctified.”

Direct public lecture:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/nt538-hebrews/nt528-21-hebrews-10-26-39

His rationale:

- the impersonal/generic construction is grammatically possible;
- he regards the warning description as too severe to fit regenerate believers;
- the phrase then identifies what covenant blood does generally, not what had actually happened savingly to the apostate.

### Strength

Unlike Christ-referent, this leaves Christ in the normal role as source/agent of sanctification.

### Weakness

The sentence does not naturally advertise a generic subject shift. The surrounding forms all describe the same singular offender.

The question is therefore not:

> “Can Greek ever leave a generic subject implicit?”

but:

> “Is that the most likely discourse move here?”

Current answer: **possible, but less natural than apostate-person referent**.

### Weight

`IMPERSONAL READING = MEDIUM / SERIOUS REFORMED GRAMMATICAL OPTION / NOT DEFAULT`.

# 5. Thomas Schreiner materially changes the Reformed decision tree

This is the most important result of the new pass.

Schreiner does **not** primarily escape 10:29 by:

- making Christ the sanctified one;
- making `ἡγιάσθη` merely generic;
- reducing Christ’s blood-sanctification to an Old-Covenant-like external ritual status.

The secondary witnesses to his commentary reproduce the following substantive claims from pp.326–327:

1. the warned people are described as true believers;
2. Christ’s blood sanctified them;
3. the sanctification is definitive/positional, not merely progressive;
4. 10:10, 10:14 and 13:12 make Christ’s sacrificial blood genuinely sanctifying;
5. 9:13 cannot simply be imported to say Christ’s blood only gave an external sanctification;
6. the new-covenant sacrifice really cleanses the conscience, unlike the limited efficacy of old-covenant rites.

Research witnesses carrying the same page locator:
- https://answeringislam.blog/limited-atonement-debate-pt-2/
- https://scripturalmormonism.blogspot.com/2017/01/refuting-mike-thomas-on-soteriology.html

Again: `VERIFY ORIGINAL BEFORE QUOTING`.

### Why this matters

Schreiner is a major conservative Reformed Baptist NT scholar. Therefore the final project must not suggest:

> “A Reformed interpreter has to prove that `ἡγιάσθη` is only external/covenantal.”

No. Schreiner shows a third route:

> **accept very strong believer/saving-language at the level of the warning’s pastoral description, while treating the warning as one of God’s ordained means by which the elect persevere.**

That model was already established in the Heb 6 closure and applies naturally here.

# 6. Schreiner’s 10:29 reading and his perseverance model must be held together

If Schreiner’s commentary statement is detached from his warning-passages theology, he can be made to sound like a conditional-security theologian.

That would be inaccurate.

His public Hebrews teaching explicitly argues:

- warning passages address believers as believers;
- apostasy would end in condemnation;
- warnings are not fake hypotheticals;
- yet God preserves His elect through the warnings;
- the elect heed them by grace and therefore do not finally apostatize.

Direct public lecture on warning passages:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/survey-acts-revelation-nt504/nt504-21-hebrews-part-3

Official SBTS summary:
- https://www.sbts.edu/news/schreiner-joslin-discuss-hebrews-warning-passages-at-alumni-academy/

### SYSTEMATIC SYNTHESIS

Schreiner’s model allows us to say simultaneously:

1. **the warning genuinely says what final apostasy would mean**;
2. **the people are addressed according to real Christian/covenant identity and privileges**;
3. **the warning does not narrate an actual loss of election in an elect person**;
4. **the warning is causally used by God to preserve the elect in persevering faith**.

This is stronger than weakening `ἡγιάσθη` merely because perseverance is true.

# 7. Can 9:13 still support covenantal/consecratory sanctification?

Yes — but with a narrower claim than some Reformed apologetics make.

Heb 9:13 states that old-covenant rites:

> `ἁγιάζει πρὸς τὴν τῆς σαρκὸς καθαρότητα`

That is real sanctification to fleshly/cultic purity.

Therefore:

> `ἁγιάζω` as a Greek lemma does not lexically mean “regenerate and eternally saved.”

This remains an important anti-word-study point.

### But

Hebrews itself sharply contrasts old-covenant efficacy with Christ’s blood:

- 9:14 — Christ’s blood cleanses conscience;
- 10:10 — “we have been sanctified” through Christ’s body once for all;
- 10:14 — one offering has perfected forever the sanctified;
- 13:12 — Jesus sanctifies the people through His own blood.

So the argument:

> “9:13 has external sanctification, therefore 10:29 is external sanctification”

is invalid.

### Current weighting

A real covenantal/consecratory reading remains **possible**, especially given Hebrews’ cultic categories and visible covenant-community warnings. But it is no longer the preferred project wording unless stronger book-specific evidence is supplied.

`COVENANTAL/EXTERNAL SANCTIFICATION = PLAUSIBLE REFORMED OPTION / BURDEN NOT YET FULLY MET`.

# 8. The strongest conditional-security / loss reading

The strongest opposing case should not be a popular Wesleyan article.

It runs roughly:

1. the apostate is the natural referent;
2. `ἡγιάσθη` is genuine new-covenant sanctification by Christ’s blood;
3. 10:10, 10:14, 13:12 define that sanctification in saving terms;
4. 10:32–34 describes the hearers’ prior costly Christian endurance;
5. 10:26–31 warns those same believers against a future deliberate repudiation;
6. if they apostatize, no sacrifice remains and final judgment follows;
7. therefore a genuinely sanctified believer may cease to be finally saved.

Major representatives/technical witnesses already in corpus include:

- Craig Koester;
- William Lane (locator lead p.294);
- B. J. Oropeza;
- Scot McKnight’s broader Hebrews warning model;
- recent holiness scholarship emphasizing strong new-covenant sanctification.

### This reading’s strongest point

It integrates 10:29 with 10:10/14 and 13:12 rather than weakening the verb.

### Its disputed move

The move from:

> “the warning describes a genuinely sanctified believer and threatens final destruction”

to:

> “therefore an elect/regenerate believer actually will finally apostatize and lose salvation”

is **not a lexical deduction**.

It is a systematic conclusion about the relationship between warning, election, perseverance and counterfactual/conditional discourse.

Schreiner demonstrates that one can accept nearly all the exegetical premises and reject that final systematic conclusion.

# 9. Deuteronomy 32 makes the warning covenantal, not merely individualistic

Hebrews 10:30 cites:

- “Vengeance is mine; I will repay”;
- “The Lord will judge His people.”

The source is the Song of Moses / Deut 32.

This matters because the background already combines:

- covenant people;
- severe covenant infidelity;
- divine vengeance;
- judgment within/against the people;
- preservation/vindication of God’s faithful servants.

### CANONICAL INFERENCE

Heb 10:26–31 deliberately speaks in covenant-judgment categories.

But this background does not automatically resolve individual regeneration-status.

It prevents two reductions:

- ❌ “only outsiders can be addressed”; 
- ❌ “corporate covenant judgment automatically proves loss of individual eternal election.”

---

# 10. Hebrews 10:32–39 is not an appendix; it controls the warning pastorally

Immediately after severe warning the author recalls:

- their former enlightenment;
- public affliction/reproach;
- solidarity with prisoners;
- joyful acceptance of property loss;
- knowledge of a better and lasting possession.

Then:

- do not throw away confidence;
- you need endurance;
- the righteous one lives by faith;
- “we are not of shrinking back to destruction, but of faith to preservation/acquisition of the soul/life.”

### Direct rhetorical effect

The writer does not end at “you might be destroyed.”

He does what he also does in Heb 6:

1. severe real warning;
2. reminder of grace/fruit;
3. confidence concerning the hearers;
4. summons to endurance.

This is strong internal support for a **warnings-as-means** reading.

### Important restriction

10:39 must not be used as a magic eraser for 10:26–31.

It establishes the author’s pastoral confidence; it does not make the threatened apostasy unreal.

# 11. Three questions must remain separate in the final article

## Question A — Who is `ἡγιάσθη`?

Best current answer:

> **the apostate/warned person is the most natural referent.**

## Question B — What kind of sanctification?

Current evidence:

- strong new-covenant blood-sanctification is the most contextually pressured reading;
- external/covenantal/consecratory nuance is semantically possible but needs more contextual proof;
- impersonal Guthrie reading remains a real but less natural alternative;
- Christ-referent is historically important but technically weakest of the four.

## Question C — Does this prove loss of salvation/election?

No lexical or syntactic form directly answers that systematic question.

The conclusion depends on broader theology of Hebrews:

- warning function;
- perseverance language;
- divine promise;
- authorial confidence;
- 3:6/14;
- 6:9–20;
- 7:25;
- 10:14;
- 10:35–39;
- 12:1–17;
- whole-canon preservation theology.

This separation is mandatory.

# 15. Revised Reformed publication wording

Do **not** publish:

> “The ‘sanctified’ one is obviously Christ.”

Do **not** publish:

> “Sanctified here clearly means only outward church membership.”

Do **not** publish:

> “Because the apostate was sanctified, Hebrews proves a regenerate elect person can lose salvation.”

A stronger formulation is:

> **The most natural grammatical referent in Hebrews 10:29 is the person being warned, and the surrounding uses of sanctification in Hebrews make it unwise to trivialize the phrase as merely nominal. Jesus is ordinarily the Sanctifier, and Hebrews 13:12 explicitly says that He sanctifies the people by His own blood. Yet even this strong reading does not by itself settle the doctrine of perseverance. Thomas Schreiner, for example, accepts the language as genuinely Christian and still argues that Hebrews’ warnings are among the means God uses to preserve the elect. Thus the decisive theological question is not whether the warning is real, but how real warnings, real Christian privileges, divine preservation and final perseverance function together in the argument of Hebrews.**

This wording is currently the strongest project-level synthesis.

# 16. Pastoral implications

## 16.1. The sin is not ordinary post-conversion struggle

10:29 defines deliberate sin by three acts:

- trampling the Son;
- profaning covenant blood;
- insulting the Spirit of grace.

A believer fighting sin, mourning failure and clinging to Christ must not be told that every deliberate act of sin equals this apostasy.

## 16.2. Severe warning belongs inside pastoral care

The author addresses a community with real history of costly faithfulness and tells them not to throw away confidence.

Warning is therefore not anti-assurance.

It is one of the means by which presumption is destroyed and perseverance strengthened.

## 16.3. Do not give false assurance to explicit Christ-repudiation

The opposite pastoral error is to say:

> “If you once had a Christian experience, final repudiation of Christ cannot matter.”

Hebrews 10 gives no such comfort.

The warning is intended to be feared and obeyed.
