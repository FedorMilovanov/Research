## 1. Executive conclusion

Three Revelation nodes need different treatment:

1. **Rev 3:5** — the reader-facing clause “I will never erase his name from the book of life” is textually stable enough that the apostasy debate is primarily exegetical, not textual-critical.
2. **Rev 22:14** — genuinely important two-reading variation: “those who wash their robes” versus “those who do his commandments.” Modern critical editions prefer the robe-reading, but the commandment-reading is ancient and seriously defended. Publication must disclose the variant if the wording itself carries argumentative weight.
3. **Rev 22:19** — “tree of life” is overwhelmingly preferable to the Textus Receptus “book of life.” The history of Erasmus’ back-translation is directly documented, and the exact TR form does not represent an independent early Greek tradition.

### Project-level result

None of these variants overturns the larger apostasy/perseverance synthesis.

> **Revelation still joins severe warnings, commands to repent/hold fast/overcome, cleansing through the Lamb, obedient fidelity, and eschatological exclusion or inheritance. The flagship should use claims stable across the textual evidence rather than deriving a whole soteriology from a disputed phrase.**

# 2. Rev 3:5 — book-of-life promise is not materially text-unstable

Critical text:

`Ὁ νικῶν ... καὶ οὐ μὴ ἐξαλείψω τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ ἐκ τῆς βίβλου τῆς ζωῆς`

Working translation:

> “The one who conquers ... I will certainly not erase his name from the book of life.”

The NA28/Westcott-Hort comparison at Rev 3 shows no material variant in v.5 that changes:

- `οὐ μὴ ἐξαλείψω`;
- `τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ`;
- `ἐκ τῆς βίβλου τῆς ζωῆς`.

Public comparison:
https://biblehub.com/whna28/revelation/3.htm

### Consequence

The real dispute is interpretive:

- promise by litotes / emphatic assurance to the conqueror;
- warning implication from the image;
- whether one may infer actual erasure of a previously regenerate person;
- relation to other book-of-life texts.

Those questions belong in `77_...`, not in a textual-variant argument.

### Publication rule

Do not imply that Rev 3:5 is textually uncertain merely because its theological interpretation is debated.

Status: `GREEN / TEXT-STABLE FOR CURRENT CLAIMS`.

# 3. Rev 22:14 — two materially different readings

## Reading A — critical text

`οἱ πλύνοντες τὰς στολὰς αὐτῶν`

> “those who wash their robes”

This is the reading printed in modern Nestle-Aland / UBS editions and followed by many modern translations.

It directly resonates with Rev 7:14:

- robes are washed;
- made white in the blood of the Lamb.

It also coheres with Revelation’s repeated white-garment imagery.

## Reading B — received / Byzantine-associated reading

`οἱ ποιοῦντες τὰς ἐντολὰς αὐτοῦ`

> “those who do his commandments”

This is represented in the Textus Receptus/KJV tradition and has substantial later Greek support plus ancient patristic/versional support.

It resonates with:

- Rev 12:17 — those keeping God’s commandments;
- Rev 14:12 — perseverance of saints who keep God’s commandments and faith in Jesus;
- Rev 22:7,9 — keeping the words of the prophecy.

---

# 4. Rev 22:14 — why this should not be oversimplified

It is inaccurate to present the issue as:

> “one obviously authentic early reading versus a worthless late corruption.”

Stephen Goranson, “The Text of Revelation 22.14,” *New Testament Studies* 54.4 (2008; published online 2009), explicitly calls both readings **well attested** and argues that patristic, literary and theological considerations favor the commandment-reading.

Cambridge abstract:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/text-of-revelation-2214/7409E6E65146420ECAFFFC338F17FF25

By contrast, the UBS/NA editorial tradition and Metzger favor the robe-reading, commonly explaining the commandment-reading as a scribal alteration facilitated by the visual/aural similarity of the Greek phrases.

Metzger locator, cited in standard discussions:

- Bruce M. Metzger, *A Textual Commentary on the Greek New Testament*, 2nd ed., discussion of Rev 22:14.

Accessible reproduction of the textual-commentary judgment:
https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/66/ellicotts-commentary-on-the-whole-bible/revelation/22/14

### Methodological conclusion

Rev 22:14 is a **real textual crux**.

Modern critical preference for `πλύνοντες ... στολάς` is strong and should normally govern the main text, but a publication claiming technical depth should disclose the alternative when making a theological argument from the exact phrase.

Status: `GREEN / VARIANT-DISCLOSED`, not `TEXTUALLY UNDISPUTED`.

# 5. Rev 22:14 — apostasy/perseverance synthesis survives both readings

## If “wash their robes” is original

The beatitude emphasizes cleansing and granted access to the tree/city.

Canonical controls inside Revelation:

- 7:14 — robes washed in Lamb’s blood;
- 12:11 — conquering by Lamb’s blood and testimony;
- 19:7–8 — bridal linen imagery.

It must not be turned into autonomous human self-cleansing meriting salvation.

## If “do his commandments” is original

The beatitude emphasizes obedient covenant fidelity.

Canonical controls:

- 12:17;
- 14:12;
- 22:7,9.

It must not be turned into works-righteousness, because Revelation itself grounds victory and cleansing in the Lamb.

## Stable claim across both readings

> **Those admitted to eschatological life are the Lamb’s purified and faithful people; Revelation does not separate saving participation in the Lamb from persevering allegiance to God.**

This is the publication-safe claim.

# 6. Rev 22:19 — “tree of life” versus “book of life”

## Critical reading

`ἀπὸ τοῦ ξύλου τῆς ζωῆς`

> “from the tree of life”

## Textus Receptus reading

`ἀπὸ βίβλου / βίβλου τῆς ζωῆς`

> “from the book of life”

This difference matters because “book of life” can be pulled directly into debates over names being written/erased, whereas “tree of life” connects the warning to eschatological participation/inheritance in the New Jerusalem.

---

# 7. Erasmus and the last verses of Revelation

The historical point is unusually well documented.

Jan Krans, “Erasmus and the Text of Revelation 22:19,” *Journal of Biblical Textual Criticism* 16 (2011), demonstrates from Erasmus’ own statements and the produced Greek text that:

- Erasmus’ manuscript of Revelation (2814 / old 1r) lacked the ending beginning late in Rev 22:16;
- Erasmus supplied the missing Greek from Latin;
- elements of this retranslation entered the printed Textus Receptus tradition.

Primary scholarly PDF:
https://jbtc.org/v16/Krans2011.pdf

Krans reproduces Erasmus’ own explanation that words absent from his Greek copies were added from the Latin and shows that the Greek Erasmus produced contains characteristic signs of retranslation.

### Important precision

Do **not** say simply:

> “No Greek manuscript has any form of ‘book of life’.”

That is too absolute.

Later Greek witnesses/readings related to “book” exist, but Krans shows that the exact `ἀπὸ βίβλου` witnesses are dependent on Erasmus’ printed text and therefore have no independent value for reconstructing the pre-Erasmian Greek reading.

Krans’ conclusion on the external evidence is that `ἀπὸ τοῦ ξύλου` is original.

Relevant discussion: article pp.12–13 (PDF pages around the section “The Meaning of Revelation 22:19”).

Status: `HIGH-CONFIDENCE TEXTUAL CLOSURE`.

# 8. Why “tree of life” also fits Revelation internally

The critical reading is not merely externally stronger.

Rev 22 already repeatedly joins:

- tree of life — 22:2;
- right/access to tree of life — 22:14;
- holy city — 22:14,19.

Krans notes that the wording “share/part” fits participation in the tree/city context, whereas Revelation’s “book of life” idiom normally uses:

- names written;
- names not written;
- opening the book;
- erasing a name (3:5).

Thus the immediate literary context strongly supports `tree of life`.

NET textual note also records the same general conclusion and the Erasmus/Vulgate history:
https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Rev&chapter=22&tab=commentaries&verse=19

### Source-policy note

NET is used here as a technical cross-check, not as doctrinal authority. The historical/text-critical backbone is Krans + critical-text tradition.

# 9. Rev 22:19 — what changes for the apostasy project

## Do not publish

> “Rev 22:19 says God will erase the apostate from the book of life.”

That wording depends on the inferior TR reading.

## Publish instead

> **Rev 22:19 threatens the person who takes away from the prophecy with loss of his share in the tree of life and the holy city — exclusion from the eschatological inheritance described by Revelation.**

This is already an extremely severe warning.

It does not need the “book of life” wording to be terminal.

---

# 10. Does Rev 22:19 prove loss of prior regeneration?

No — not by itself.

The wording speaks of:

- threatened loss of a `μέρος` / share;
- tree of life;
- holy city;
- deliberate tampering with the prophetic words.

The exact soteriological identity of the hypothetical offender must be argued from broader context rather than assumed from the noun `share`.

### Reformed/perseverance reading

The warning can function as a real divine means preventing the elect from such terminal repudiation.

### Conditional-security reading

The warning can be read as a genuine possibility addressed to Christians who may forfeit final salvation.

### Project rule

The text establishes the **severity and eschatological finality of the threatened outcome** more directly than it settles the prior regenerate status of the warned person.

This is consistent with the project’s general warning-text method.

# 11. Relationship to Rev 3:5

The temptation created by the TR reading is to collapse:

- 3:5 — “I will never erase his name from the book of life”;
- 22:19 TR — “take away his part from the book of life.”

The critical text prevents that artificial verbal linkage.

The actual relationship is conceptual, not lexically identical:

- 3:5 promises the conqueror secure acknowledgment and non-erasure;
- 22:19 threatens a textual rebel with exclusion from tree/city inheritance.

Both belong to Revelation’s larger binary of:

- conquer / inherit / enter;
- persist in rebellion / remain outside / receive judgment.

# 13. Competing textual position — received-text advocates

Because the user-facing audience may encounter KJV/TR defenses, publication should not pretend the alternative case does not exist.

## Rev 22:14

The received reading “do his commandments” has a much stronger case than the TR reading at 22:19.

It has:

- actual Greek manuscript support;
- versional/patristic support;
- a coherent internal argument;
- a modern scholarly defense (Goranson).

Therefore it deserves explicit disclosure.

## Rev 22:19

Received-text defenses sometimes appeal to Latin witnesses, Fathers, later Greek manuscripts, or preservation theology.

But the decisive historical problem remains:

- Erasmus’ Greek ending was reconstructed from Latin;
- the exact printed “book” reading lacks an independent early Greek line;
- related late Greek support does not overturn that genealogy.

Fairness does not require pretending the evidential situations in vv.14 and 19 are equal.

# 15. Stable theological conclusions after apparatus cleanup

1. Revelation’s warning system remains severe under the best critical text.
2. The project does not need disputed TR wording to establish terminal eschatological exclusion.
3. Cleansing by the Lamb and persevering obedience are complementary Revelation themes even though Rev 22:14’s exact phrase is disputed.
4. Rev 3:5 remains an exegetical hard text, not a textual-critical hard text.
5. Rev 22:19 should be argued from `tree of life / holy city`, not `book of life`.
6. No single Revelation variant should be allowed to dictate the entire doctrine of perseverance or apostasy.
