# 2. DIRECT EXEGESIS — синтаксический каркас vv.4–6

Текст начинается:

`Ἀδύνατον γὰρ τοὺς ... πάλιν ἀνακαινίζειν εἰς μετάνοιαν`

Главный предикативный центр:

- `ἀδύνατον` — невозможно;
- объект выражен артиклем `τοὺς` и цепью accusative participles;
- инфинитив `ἀνακαινίζειν` — обновлять / восстанавливать;
- `πάλιν` — снова;
- `εἰς μετάνοιαν` — к покаянию.

Описание объекта:

- `ἅπαξ φωτισθέντας` — однажды просвещённых;
- `γευσαμένους ... δωρεᾶς ... ἐπουρανίου` — вкусивших/испытавших небесный дар;
- `μετόχους γενηθέντας πνεύματος ἁγίου` — ставших участниками/причастниками Святого Духа;
- `καλὸν γευσαμένους θεοῦ ῥῆμα` — вкусивших доброе Божье слово;
- `δυνάμεις ... μέλλοντος αἰῶνος` — силы будущего века;
- `καὶ παραπεσόντας` — и отпавших.

Затем:

- `ἀνασταυροῦντας` — crucifying again;
- `παραδειγματίζοντας` — exposing to public disgrace.

Оба последних participles — present active participles, тогда как `παραπεσόντας` — aorist active participle.

Direct morphology:
- Heb 6:4: https://biblehub.com/text/hebrews/6-4.htm
- Heb 6:6: https://biblehub.com/text/hebrews/6-6.htm

### DIRECT EXEGESIS conclusion

`ἀδύνατον` грамматически управляет всей конструкцией. Текст не говорит «это редко» или «крайне трудно».

Но **сам синтаксис ещё не отвечает**, является ли невозможность:

- абсолютным judicial impossibility;
- невозможностью при продолжающемся apostate posture;
- невозможностью восстановить к прежнему состоянию христианского confession после definitive repudiation.

Эту часть решает discourse/context, не словарь одного `ἀδύνατον`.

# 3. DIRECT EXEGESIS — `γεύομαι` нельзя использовать как “barely tasted”

В 6:4 и 6:5 стоит `γευσαμένους`.

Самый сильный same-book control — Евр. 2:9:

> Христос «вкусил смерть».

Он не «слегка прикоснулся» к смерти.

Джордж Гатри в открытой лекции прямо отвергает объяснение, будто “taste” означает partial experience; он использует 2:9 как контроль и понимает глагол как реальное experiencing.

Source:
- George H. Guthrie, *Hebrews*, lecture on 5:11–6:12, Part 2: https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/nt538-hebrews/nt528-12-hebrews-5-11-6-12-part-2

### DIRECT EXEGESIS conclusion

> `γεύομαι` доказывает **реальность опыта**, но не кодирует автоматически его soteriological category.

Поэтому обе грубые формулы запрещены:

- ❌ «они только лизнули, а не вкусили по-настоящему»;
- ❌ «раз вкусили реально, значит обязательно были оправданы и возрождены».

Первое противоречит usage; второе делает из experiential verb полный ordo salutis.

# 4. DIRECT EXEGESIS — Hebrews-wide `μέτοχος` concordance

`μέτοχος` встречается в НЗ шесть раз, пять из них — в Hebrews.

Hebrews:

1. **1:9** — у Помазанника есть `μετόχους`, companions/associates;
2. **3:1** — `κλήσεως ἐπουρανίου μέτοχοι`, участники небесного призвания;
3. **3:14** — `μέτοχοι τοῦ Χριστοῦ`, участники/причастники Христа;
4. **6:4** — `μετόχους ... πνεύματος ἁγίου`, участники Святого Духа;
5. **12:8** — все истинные сыновья являются `μέτοχοι` discipline.

NT occurrence control:
- https://biblehub.com/greek/3353.htm
- https://www.thekoinebible.com/text/lexicon/rahlfs/%CE%BC%CE%B5%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%87%CE%BF%CF%82.html

### Что слово устанавливает

`μέτοχος` не означает spectator.

Нормальное поле:

- sharer;
- participant;
- companion/associate;
- partner.

Поэтому описание в 6:4 **сильное**.

### Что слово НЕ устанавливает

Same lemma принимает semantic force от complement/context:

- participant in calling;
- participant in Christ;
- participant in discipline;
- companion of king;
- participant in Spirit.

Следовательно, нельзя создать lexical equation:

> `μέτοχος = regenerate believer by definition`.

И нельзя создать обратную:

> `μέτοχος = mere external observer`.

### George Guthrie

Гатри предлагает перевод/смысл companion/associate/sharer и подчёркивает: люди в 6:4 действительно каким-то образом associated with Holy Spirit, но **точная природа этой связи самим словом не определяется**. Он связывает описание с wilderness generation, среди которой действовал Дух, хотя поколение ответило неверием.

Source:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/nt538-hebrews/nt528-12-hebrews-5-11-6-12-part-2

### John Owen

Оуэн проводит различие между:

- personal gracious inhabitation;
- powerful spiritual operations/gifts.

Он утверждает, что человек может быть реальным участником operations/gifts Святого Духа, не имея saving indwelling.

Primary:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.v.html

### Classification

- `μέτοχος = real participation` — **DIRECT EXEGESIS**.
- `this participation = non-saving operations/gifts` — **REFORMED EXEGETICAL/SYSTEMATIC INTERPRETATION**, defended especially by Owen.
- `this participation = genuine regenerate participation` — **COMPETING EXEGETICAL/SOTERIOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION**, defended by McKnight/conditional-security readings and, descriptively, given unusually strong weight by Schreiner.

# 5. CANONICAL INFERENCE — wilderness generation background

The strongest advance beyond isolated word study is the Exodus/wilderness background.

Hebrews itself already makes the wilderness generation central in chs. 3–4:

- they heard God’s voice;
- witnessed His works;
- were covenant people;
- yet hardened themselves;
- were excluded from rest because of unbelief.

Dave Mathewson’s WTJ study argues that 6:4–6 intentionally re-presents wilderness experience:

- enlightenment ↔ pillar/light;
- heavenly gift ↔ manna / heavenly provision;
- Holy Spirit participation ↔ Spirit among covenant people;
- good word / powers ↔ revelation and mighty acts;
- falling away ↔ wilderness rebellion.

Bibliographic object:
- Dave Mathewson, “Reading Heb 6:4–6 in Light of the Old Testament,” *Westminster Theological Journal* 61 (1999): 209–225.

Accessible article witness:
- https://docsbay.net/doc/687009/westminster-theological-journal-61-1999-209-25

Guthrie explicitly uses Mathewson’s article in his Hebrews course and finds the wilderness frame significant:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/nt538-hebrews/nt528-12-hebrews-5-11-6-12-part-2

### CANONICAL INFERENCE

The analogy helps explain how people may receive **objectively enormous covenant blessings and divine manifestations** while still ending in unbelief.

But wilderness typology does not, by itself, answer every individual’s regeneration status.

The safe inference is:

> **shared covenant/redemptive-historical privilege is not identical with final participation in promised salvation.**

This coheres strongly with 1 Cor. 10:1–12.

# 6. DIRECT EXEGESIS — `παραπεσόντας` is not a normal lapse

`παραπεσόντας` is aorist active participle from `παραπίπτω`.

Its rhetorical interpretation is governed by what follows:

- recrucifying the Son of God;
- exposing Him to public disgrace.

Thus the fall is not:

- Peter weeping after a fearful denial;
- David after adultery/murder and Nathan’s rebuke;
- James 5 wanderer whom church members are commanded to turn back;
- Gal 6 brother overtaken in a trespass.

The act is Christ-repudiating apostasy.

### Thomas Schreiner

In his public Hebrews lecture, Schreiner explicitly rejects a mere loss-of-rewards reading because the warning culminates in destruction, and reads the sin as apostasy with damnation as the stated consequence.

Source:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/survey-acts-revelation-nt504/nt504-21-hebrews-part-3

### Scot McKnight

McKnight likewise reads the five warning passages together and defines the warned-against sin as deliberate/public apostasy rather than ordinary Christian failure.

Bibliographic source:
- Scot McKnight, “The Warning Passages of Hebrews: A Formal Analysis and Theological Conclusions,” *Trinity Journal* 13.1 (1992): 21–59.
- https://www.galaxie.com/article/trinj13-1-03

### Closure

`παραπεσόντας` in this discourse is **terminal-category language**, but the word alone should not be exported as a universal lexeme meaning “irrevocably damned” in every Greek context.

# 7. DIRECT EXEGESIS — present participles in v.6

After aorist `παραπεσόντας`, the author uses present participles:

- `ἀνασταυροῦντας`;
- `παραδειγματίζοντας`.

George Guthrie notes the tense/aspect shift and recognizes two major syntactical possibilities:

1. **causal** — impossible to renew because they are recrucifying/shaming the Son;
2. **temporal/circumstantial** — impossible while/as they continue in this repudiating posture.

Guthrie cautions against reading too much raw clock-time from tense, but regards the shift as exegetically meaningful enough to discuss.

Source:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/nt538-hebrews/nt528-12-hebrews-5-11-6-12-part-2

### Important restriction

Grammar alone does **not** permit us confidently to publish:

> «Если они просто прекратят распинать Христа, снова смогут покаяться».

Nor does grammar alone prove:

> «God metaphysically removes every possibility of repentance the instant apostasy occurs».

The rhetoric is intentionally absolute/severe. Any temporal nuance must not be used to neutralize `ἀδύνατον`.

### Pastoral corollary

A person grieving his sin, desiring Christ and pleading for mercy is not presenting the posture described by `ἀνασταυροῦντας ... παραδειγματίζοντας`.

This is **CANONICAL/PASTORAL INFERENCE**, not a lexical exception to `ἀδύνατον`.

# 8. DIRECT EXEGESIS — land/rain analogy, vv.7–8

One rain falls on land.

Two outcomes:

### Fruitful land

- drinks the rain;
- bears useful crop;
- receives blessing from God.

### Thorn-bearing land

- also receives rain;
- bears thorns/briers;
- is `ἀδόκιμος` / rejected, worthless, failing the test;
- `κατάρας ἐγγύς` / near a curse;
- its end is burning.

### The key analogy

Shared rain does not guarantee shared fruit.

Therefore vv.7–8 provide a strong internal analogy for vv.4–6:

> **real shared blessing + different productive outcome**.

George Guthrie reads the rain as divine blessings poured on the people and the absence of fruit as placing them near curse/judgment.

### Against Free Grace reward-only reading

Schreiner argues publicly that the threatened outcome is more than loss of rewards; he sees eternal destruction in view.

Grudem’s detailed Reformed article also rejects the reward-only solution.

Sources:
- Schreiner lecture: https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/survey-acts-revelation-nt504/nt504-21-hebrews-part-3
- Wayne Grudem, “Perseverance of the Saints: A Case Study from Hebrews 6:4–6 and the Other Warning Passages in Hebrews,” pp. 133–182: https://www.waynegrudem.com/perseverance-of-the-saints-a-case-study-from-hebrews-64-6-and-the-other-warning-passages-in-hebrews

### Closure

The warning concerns **destruction/judgment**, not merely reduced heavenly reward.

# 9. DIRECT EXEGESIS — Hebrews 6:9 must carry full weight

Greek:

`Πεπείσμεθα δὲ περὶ ὑμῶν, ἀγαπητοί, τὰ κρείσσονα καὶ ἐχόμενα σωτηρίας, εἰ καὶ οὕτως λαλοῦμεν.`

Direct text:
- https://www.greekbible.com/hebrews/6/9
- morphology: https://biblehub.com/text/hebrews/6-9.htm

Key expressions:

- `Πεπείσμεθα` — we are convinced/persuaded;
- `περὶ ὑμῶν` — concerning you;
- `ἀγαπητοί` — beloved;
- `τὰ κρείσσονα` — better things;
- `καὶ ἐχόμενα σωτηρίας` — and things connected with / accompanying / pertaining to salvation;
- `εἰ καὶ οὕτως λαλοῦμεν` — even though we speak this way.

### What v.9 unquestionably does

It creates a rhetorical differentiation between:

- the terrifying example just described;
- the author’s confidence concerning his hearers.

It also explicitly introduces **salvation** as the category of the “better things.”

### What v.9 does NOT mechanically prove

It does not lexically say:

> “every blessing in vv.4–5 is non-saving.”

Nor does it explicitly say:

> “the people in vv.4–6 were unregenerate false professors.”

The inferential relationship must be argued.

### George Guthrie

Guthrie calls vv.9–12 a mitigation/encouragement after the harsh warning. He sees the hearers’ work and love as evidence/fruit of their real relationship with God and calls them to continue diligently to the end.

### Thomas Schreiner

Schreiner goes further in an important direction for this project:

- he believes the warning is addressed to believers;
- he reads the described people as Christians rather than merely “almost Christians”;
- he takes “partakers of the Holy Spirit” as strong Christian description;
- yet he maintains perseverance of the elect because the warnings are **means God uses to preserve them**;
- vv.9–20 then give strong assurance immediately beside the warning.

Public lecture transcript:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/survey-acts-revelation-nt504/nt504-21-hebrews-part-3

SBTS report on Schreiner/Joslin warning-passages lectures:
- https://www.sbts.edu/news/schreiner-joslin-discuss-hebrews-warning-passages-at-alumni-academy/

### Major methodological consequence

A Reformed reading does **not have to depend** on proving that every person hypothetically described in vv.4–6 is lexically identifiable as unregenerate.

Schreiner provides a different Reformed model:

> **the warning addresses real Christians conditionally; apostasy would bring damnation; God preserves His elect by causing them to heed the warning.**

This must now be represented as a major Reformed option alongside Owen/Grudem.

# 10. THREE DIFFERENT REFORMED MODELS — do not collapse them

## R1 — Owen / classic common-operations distinction

Core:

- privileges are real;
- participation in Spirit can be participation in operations/gifts;
- description does not require saving indwelling/regeneration;
- final apostasy manifests lack of saving grace.

Strength:

- preserves strong experiential language without teaching loss of regeneration;
- historically deep practical theology of common operations.

Risk:

- can sound artificial if every descriptor is individually weakened merely to protect perseverance.

Owen avoids much of that risk by **not** calling the experiences unreal.

Primary:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.v.html

---

## R2 — Grudem / “not explicitly saving descriptors” + 6:9 distinction

Core:

- vv.4–5 contain very strong experiences but omit the language the author could use for definitive salvation;
- v.9 distinguishes “better things” and “things accompanying salvation”;
- warning displays what occurs with people deeply embedded in covenant/community privilege who nevertheless prove barren.

Major source:
- Wayne Grudem, pp.133–182 in the published essay; direct author-hosted document entry:
  https://www.waynegrudem.com/perseverance-of-the-saints-a-case-study-from-hebrews-64-6-and-the-other-warning-passages-in-hebrews

Locator lead:
- pp.158–159 are repeatedly cited for v.9 argument in secondary literature; direct PDF page verification remains required before verbatim quotation.

Strength:

- gives 6:9 substantial internal controlling weight.

Risk:

- must not imply that strong terms such as `γεύομαι` or `μέτοχος` themselves mean superficial/non-real experience.

---

## R3 — Schreiner / warnings as efficacious means of perseverance

Core:

- recipients are treated pastorally as believers;
- warning is genuinely conditional and truly threatens damnation;
- author does not speak from God’s exhaustive perspective of each individual’s final election;
- elect believers heed the warning because God uses it as an ordained means of preservation;
- assurance and warning therefore stand together without contradiction.

Direct public lecture:
- https://www.biblicaltraining.org/learn/institute/survey-acts-revelation-nt504/nt504-21-hebrews-part-3

SBTS summary:
- https://www.sbts.edu/news/schreiner-joslin-discuss-hebrews-warning-passages-at-alumni-academy/

Commentary locator lead:
- Thomas R. Schreiner, *Hebrews: Evangelical Biblical Theology Commentary*;
- exposition of Heb 6:4–8 begins around p.178; 6:9–12 around p.192;
- book table-of-contents witness: https://www.kriso.lv/hebrews-evangelical-biblical-theology-commentary-db-9781683594307.html

### Why R3 matters

It prevents the project from presenting this false dichotomy:

> either “vv.4–5 describe unbelievers” or “perseverance of saints is false.”

Schreiner accepts unusually strong believer-address language while preserving Reformed perseverance through **means theology**.

# 11. Strongest conditional-security reading

## Scot McKnight — synthetic warning-passage approach

Article:

Scot McKnight, “The Warning Passages of Hebrews: A Formal Analysis and Theological Conclusions,” *Trinity Journal* 13.1 (1992): 21–59.

Bibliographic/full-index access:
- https://www.galaxie.com/article/trinj13-1-03

McKnight’s method is stronger than a proof-text argument because he treats the five warning passages as one system:

1. audience/subjects;
2. sin;
3. exhortation;
4. consequences.

His conclusion:

- audience is phenomenologically described as genuine believers;
- sin is deliberate/public apostasy;
- exhortation is persevering faithfulness;
- consequence is final condemnation if faith is abandoned.

### Strengths of this reading

1. Takes cumulative positive language seriously.
2. Does not weaken `μέτοχος` or `γεύομαι`.
3. Reads all warnings together.
4. Takes destructive outcome at full strength.
5. Correctly insists that apostasy is much more than ordinary sin or temporary weakness.

### Reformed answer must not caricature

The answer cannot simply be:

> “They never believed anything at all.”

The text plainly attributes very deep covenant/church/Spirit-age experience.

Nor can the answer be:

> “taste means only touching with the tongue.”

That is refuted by Heb 2:9.

### Main Reformed response points

1. Hebrews routinely joins genuine warning to strong divine assurance.
2. Conditional statements do not automatically narrate an event that actually occurs in the elect.
3. 6:9 intentionally distinguishes the author’s confident assessment of hearers and “things accompanying salvation.”
4. wilderness typology shows that enormous covenant privilege does not itself equal entry into promised rest.
5. 3:6 / 3:14 and 10:39 make perseverance evidentiary/necessary in ways compatible with preservation-through-means.
6. whole-canon promises of preservation must be integrated rather than overridden by one warning text — without reducing the warning.

# 12. MacArthur / GTY — valuable pastoral control, but lexical overstatement must be filtered

GTY clearly takes vv.4–6 as describing people exposed to full revelation but not truly saved.

Useful sources:
- https://www.gty.org/library/questions/QA198
- https://www.gty.org/resources/study-guides/chapters/1613/the-tragedy-of-rejecting-full-revelation-part-2

Strong contribution:

- rejects loss-of-salvation reading;
- sees hardening after maximal gospel light;
- correctly distinguishes the passage from ordinary Christian failure;
- stresses irreversible gravity of conscious rejection.

### Required correction/filter

Some GTY formulations contrast “tasted” with “consumed/ate” as though the verb itself implied partiality.

This project **must not repeat that lexical argument**.

Why:

- Heb 2:9 uses `γεύομαι` for Christ’s real death;
- George Guthrie explicitly identifies the “partial taste” argument as incorrect.

Therefore:

> MacArthur is retained as strong conservative pastoral/systematic witness, but not followed where a particular lexical simplification is weaker than same-book Greek usage.

This is exactly how source-policy should work: theological agreement does not override textual control.

---

# 13. John Calvin — severity without pastoral despair

Calvin refuses to weaken `ἀδύνατον` to “rare/difficult.”

He also distinguishes this decisive apostasy from the ordinary grievous falls of those who return seeking mercy.

Primary:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom44/calcom44.xii.ii.html

### Practical value

Calvin helps maintain both poles:

- do not blunt the warning;
- do not terrorize the penitent as though desire for Christ itself proved unpardonable apostasy.

---

# 14. John Owen — stronger than the caricature “mere professor felt nothing”

Primary apostasy exposition:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.v.html

Perseverance treatment:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/perseverance.xxiv.html

Owen explicitly grants substantial operations of the Spirit:

- illumination;
- convictions;
- gifts;
- real experience of gospel excellency;
- powerful movements tending toward repentance/faith;
- usefulness in the church.

His distinction is not:

> real experience vs imaginary experience.

It is:

> **common/powerful Spirit operations vs regenerating/saving inhabitation and union.**

This is far more sophisticated and should be the historical Reformed presentation.

# 16. Working synthesis for the project

The project should no longer say too simply:

> «Евр. 6 описывает только невозрождённых почти-христиан».

That formulation is **too narrow** because major conservative Reformed scholar Thomas Schreiner himself reads the warning as addressed to and describing Christians pastorally while holding that God uses the warning to preserve the elect.

Nor should the project say:

> «раз они названы причастниками Духа, текст доказал потерю возрождения».

That conclusion moves beyond lexical evidence.

### Preferred synthesis

> **Hebrews 6:4–8 deliberately uses very strong language for people who have received extraordinary new-covenant light, Spirit-age participation and powers, and it warns of a decisive Christ-repudiating apostasy whose outcome is curse and destruction. The text must not be weakened by saying that they “barely tasted” anything or merely observed the Spirit from afar. Yet the passage itself does not use the full technical vocabulary of justification/new birth, and v.9 deliberately distinguishes the author’s beloved hearers by “better things, things connected with salvation.” Within Reformed interpretation, this can be handled either through Owen’s distinction between powerful common operations and saving indwelling, through Grudem’s contrast with the salvation-language of v.9, or through Schreiner’s especially strong warnings-as-means model, in which real believers are genuinely warned that apostasy ends in damnation, while God preserves the elect by causing them to heed the warning. Conditional-security readings such as McKnight’s rightly expose any attempt to trivialize vv.4–6, but their further conclusion—that regenerate believers actually cease to be finally saved—is not forced by these lexical descriptions alone and must be adjudicated against Hebrews’ assurance texts and the whole canon.**

# 17. Pastoral theology

## 17.1. This text is designed to destroy presumption, not assurance in Christ

The hearer must not reason:

> “I have seen God work, felt conviction, served in church, possessed gifts, therefore final apostasy cannot concern me.”

The warning says precisely the opposite: privilege increases accountability.

## 17.2. The tender penitent is not the portrait of vv.4–6

The apostate is portrayed as recrucifying and publicly shaming the Son.

A person who:

- hates his unbelief;
- longs to return;
- confesses Christ;
- fears having abandoned Him;
- asks for mercy

must not be told mechanically, “Hebrews 6 says repentance is impossible for you.”

The church is elsewhere commanded to restore wanderers (Gal 6; James 5; Jude 22–23).

## 17.3. Warnings are means, not enemies of assurance

Schreiner’s model is especially useful pastorally:

- promise does not make warning fake;
- warning does not make promise fake;
- God preserves through Word, exhortation, church, discipline and perseverance-producing grace.

This aligns with Heb 3:12–14:

- beware evil unbelieving heart;
- exhort one another every day;
- hold fast to the end.
