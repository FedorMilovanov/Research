## 1. Почему эти два варианта — реальные publication blockers

В Иуде два textual nodes способны materially менять читательскую формулировку:

1. **Иуд. 5** — кто именно спас Израиль из Египта и затем погубил неверующих: `Ἰησοῦς` («Иисус»), `κύριος` («Господь»), `θεός` («Бог») или одна из расширенных форм?
2. **Иуд. 22–23** — сколько групп людей имеет в виду Иуда и как именно распределяются imperatives «милуйте / спасайте / исторгайте из огня / бойтесь»?

Оба места имеют древнюю и сложную вариативность.

Поэтому запрещено:

- строить центральный догматический тезис так, будто один вариант не существует;
- выдавать NA28 за безошибочную реконструкцию;
- использовать старый перевод как доказательство original text;
- делать pastoral taxonomy «трёх типов людей» обязательной, если текст 22–23 действительно может быть двухчленным.

Правильная задача:

> **Определить наиболее вероятное чтение, но сформулировать итоговую доктрину так, чтобы она не зависела от спорного слова там, где сам textual evidence не позволяет абсолютной уверенности.**

# 2. Иуд. 5 — variation unit значительно шире, чем просто “Jesus vs Lord”

Современные текстологи подчёркивают, что в v.5 варьируется не только subject.

В традиции встречаются:

- `Ἰησοῦς`;
- `κύριος / ὁ κύριος`;
- `θεός / ὁ θεός`;
- редкие расширенные формы, включая необычное чтение `θεὸς Χριστός` в 𝔓72;
- разные позиции `ἅπαξ` и окружающих слов.

Полезный современный survey, подробно опирающийся на Wasserman / ECM / Flink:
https://www.adfontes.mitchellbk.com/2026/09/13/jude-5-the-textual-variant-%E1%BC%B0%CE%B7%CF%83%CE%BF%E1%BF%A6%CF%82-%CE%BA%CF%8D%CF%81%CE%B9%CE%BF%CF%82-and-the-agent-of-the-exodus/

### Methodological consequence

Нельзя оценивать чтение только так:

> «какое имя кажется более богословски привычным?»

Нужно взвешивать:

- external evidence;
- transcriptional probability;
- intrinsic probability;
- Jude’s own style;
- coherence of the larger variation unit.

# 3. Editorial history — важное предостережение против “критический текст всегда говорил одно”

История печатных critical editions сама показывает трудность.

- UBS2 печатал `Ἰησοῦς`.
- UBS3/UBS4 и NA27 предпочитали `[ὁ] κύριος`.
- NA28 / UBS5 печатают `Ἰησοῦς`.
- ECM Catholic Letters также даёт `Ἰησοῦς` в основном тексте, сохраняя серьёзность альтернатив.
- THGNT также печатает `Ἰησοῦς`.

Elijah Hixson, Evangelical Textual Criticism:
https://evangelicaltextualcriticism.blogspot.com/2021/03/a-mythmistake-about-esv.html

### Project conclusion

> **Современное преобладание “Jesus” в critical editions существенно, но editorial history запрещает говорить, будто вопрос никогда серьёзно не обсуждался.**

Status: `DIRECT TEXT-CRITICAL OBSERVATION`.

# 4. External evidence: “Jesus” имеет очень сильную раннюю поддержку

Tommy Wasserman после полного исследования текстовой традиции Иуды признаёт, что `(ὁ) Ἰησοῦς` имеет особенно сильную manuscript support, даже хотя сам в окончательном решении предпочитает `κύριος` по internal considerations.

Основной монографический источник:

Tommy Wasserman, *The Epistle of Jude: Its Text and Transmission*, ConBNT 43 (2006), esp. pp.255–266.

Secondary/current survey with exact locator:
https://www.adfontes.mitchellbk.com/2026/09/13/jude-5-the-textual-variant-%E1%BC%B0%CE%B7%CF%83%CE%BF%E1%BF%A6%CF%82-%CE%BA%CF%8D%CF%81%CE%B9%CE%BF%CF%82-and-the-agent-of-the-exodus/

### Guardrail

Manuscript **count** is not enough.

Later numerical dominance of one reading does not automatically outweigh:

- date;
- genealogical relationships;
- geographical spread;
- quality/independence;
- transcriptional explanation.

---

# 5. Internal case for `κύριος`

Wasserman’s preference for `κύριος` is important precisely because it shows the debate is not “traditionalists ignoring evidence.”

His internal reasoning includes:

1. Jude elsewhere regularly uses the personal name together with `Χριστός` rather than bare `Ἰησοῦς`;
2. bare pre-incarnate `Ἰησοῦς` in Jude would therefore be stylistically striking;
3. ambiguous `κύριος` could plausibly generate explanatory substitutions toward either `θεός` or `Ἰησοῦς`;
4. nomina-sacra transmission may have contributed to variation.

### Strong point

`κύριος` has intrinsic stylistic plausibility and can explain why scribes might clarify the referent.

### Limitation

Jude is extremely short, so frequency arguments from a tiny sample must not be overstated.

Also, the very difficulty of bare `Ἰησοῦς` can count transcriptionally **for** its originality.

---

# 6. Internal/transcriptional case for `Ἰησοῦς`

The modern preference for `Ἰησοῦς` rests not only on theology.

Arguments include:

1. strong early external support;
2. lectio difficilior plausibility — scribes could find explicit pre-incarnate “Jesus” in the Exodus startling;
3. a difficult `Ἰησοῦς` can plausibly produce several smoothing alternatives (`κύριος`, `θεός`, expanded readings);
4. NT precedent exists for associating Christ with wilderness Israel, especially 1 Cor. 10:4 and the textual question in 1 Cor. 10:9.

Timo Flink explicitly argues for `Ἰησοῦς` in Jude 5:
https://www.bsw.org/scripture/filologia-neotestamentaria/reconsidering-the-text-of-jude-5-13-15-and-18/224/article-p110.html

### Christological significance if original

If `Ἰησοῦς` is original, Jude explicitly identifies Jesus as the divine agent who:

- delivered Israel from Egypt;
- subsequently judged unbelief.

That is a striking high-Christology statement.

### Guardrail

Even then, do not make Jude 5 the **sole** proof of Christ’s pre-existence/deity; those doctrines are multiply attested elsewhere.

# 7. Schreiner as conservative commentary control — and why his earlier judgment must be dated

Thomas R. Schreiner, *1, 2 Peter, Jude*, NAC 37 (2003), pp.444–445, discusses the variant in detail.

Secondary exact-locator witness:
https://scripturalmormonism.blogspot.com/2024/07/thomas-r-schreiner-on-jude-5-and.html

Schreiner’s 2003 discussion:

- recognizes that external evidence strongly favors `Jesus`;
- argues the reading is not theologically impossible;
- notes 1 Cor. 10 and early Christian pre-incarnate-Christ traditions;
- nevertheless then leans toward a `Lord`/Father referent because of internal considerations and possible nomina-sacra confusion.

### Current author-specific online position

Schreiner’s later public Jude commentary simply reads the critical text as:

> Jesus saved Israel from Egypt and judged unbelief.

Author-specific page:
https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/jude/

### Editorial significance

Do not write:

> “Schreiner has always confidently supported Jesus.”

His older NAC discussion was cautious and leaned the other way, while his later accessible commentary follows `Jesus`.

This is a useful example of why textual decisions can change with editions/evidence.

# 8. Safe theological synthesis for Jude 5

## If `Ἰησοῦς` is original

Direct conclusion:

> Jude explicitly identifies Jesus with the divine Lord active in Exodus salvation and wilderness judgment.

## If `κύριος` is original

Direct conclusion:

> Jude identifies “the Lord” as the Exodus savior and judge; the immediate referent must then be resolved contextually and cannot simply be replaced with “the Father” without argument.

## Stable claim across both readings

> **The same divine Lord who genuinely delivered the covenant people also judged the unbelieving generation afterward. Prior redemptive privilege did not make unbelief harmless.**

This stable claim is the one most important for the apostasy project.

It does **not** depend on deciding Jesus/Lord.

---

# 9. Jude 5 and soteriological over-reading

The Exodus generation received real corporate redemption from Egypt.

Then unbelievers were judged in the wilderness.

### What this proves

- prior deliverance/privilege;
- later unbelief;
- severe judgment;
- warning to the visible covenant community.

### What it does not prove by itself

- that every person delivered from Egypt was individually regenerate;
- that each later unbeliever lost justification;
- that corporate historical salvation from Egypt equals individual eternal salvation.

This aligns with:

- 1 Cor. 10;
- Heb. 3–4;
- Isa. 63;
- the project’s corporate/individual distinction.

# 10. Jude 22–23 — why the text is notoriously unstable

The manuscript tradition preserves multiple arrangements involving:

- two versus three clauses/groups;
- `ἐλεεῖτε / ἐλεᾶτε` (“have mercy”);
- `ἐλέγχετε` (“reprove/convince”);
- the sense/form of `διακρινομένους`;
- placement of `ἐν φόβῳ`;
- presence/absence and order of relative pronouns.

Older critical discussion:
https://bterry.com/tc2/lay29jud.htm

Modern scholarly reassessment:

James B. Prothro, “Triads, Groups, and the Text of Jude 22–23: Internal Evidence and the Shortest Reading,” *Novum Testamentum* 66.3 (2024): 364–381.

DOI / abstract:
https://brill.com/view/journals/nt/66/3/article-p364_7.xml

# 11. The familiar three-clause reading

The reading printed in NA/UBS and reflected by ESV/NIV-type translations is approximately:

1. show mercy to some who doubt;
2. save others, snatching them from fire;
3. on others show mercy with fear, hating even the garment stained by flesh.

ESV public text:
https://www.esv.org/verses/Jude%2B22%E2%80%9323/

### Why it is attractive

- Jude often uses triadic patterns;
- progression can be read pastorally from doubt → urgent rescue → dangerous mercy;
- the clauses are rhetorically balanced.

### Major danger

Do not build a rigid pastoral classification:

> “Jude reveals exactly three ontological classes of apostates.”

Even if the three-clause text is original, the rhetoric need not establish a permanent taxonomy of three fixed spiritual states.

---

# 12. The shorter two-clause reading

The ancient textual tradition also has two-clause forms, including a particularly short reading in 𝔓72.

Gene L. Green’s BECNT discussion surveys:

- three-clause witnesses;
- longer two-clause readings;
- shortest 𝔓72 reading;
- long history of scholarly division.

Book metadata/source custody:
Gene L. Green, *Jude and 2 Peter*, BECNT.

### Fresh 2024 argument

James B. Prothro argues that the shortest two-clause reading has not only ancient external support but can also fit Jude’s triadic literary habits better than critics have often allowed.

Abstract:
https://brill.com/view/journals/nt/66/3/article-p364_7.xml

### Significance

The argument “Jude likes triads, therefore three clauses must be original” is no longer sufficient by itself.

# 14. `διακρινομένους` — “doubting” or “disputing”?

The participle can carry senses connected with:

- doubt/wavering;
- dispute/contend;
- differentiation/discrimination depending on construction/context.

Jude 9 uses related `διακρίνω` language in a dispute context.

Therefore v.22 cannot be interpreted by English gloss alone.

### Current editorial-safe wording

Where the longer reading is followed:

> “those who are wavering/doubting” is a plausible pastoral rendering, but the lexical alternative involving dispute must be acknowledged in technical notes.

### Do not infer

- every doubter is nearly apostate;
- every disputant is false teacher;
- lexical ambiguity by itself determines final spiritual state.

# 15. Stable imperatives across the textual instability

Despite the variants, several pastoral/theological features are remarkably stable.

## 15.1. Mercy

At least one mercy imperative is deeply embedded in the tradition.

## 15.2. Rescue / salvation

`σῴζω` and/or rescue imagery remains central in the major forms.

## 15.3. Fire-snatching imagery

`ἐκ πυρὸς ἁρπάζοντες` is stable and evokes urgent rescue from danger.

Likely canonical resonances include:

- Amos 4:11 — brand plucked from burning;
- Zech. 3:2 — brand plucked from fire.

## 15.4. Fear / contamination caution

Major forms retain the need for caution around contaminating sin, especially the garment/flesh image.

### Stable project conclusion

> **Jude does not instruct the church merely to label endangered people “apostates” and withdraw. He commands active mercy/rescue while requiring serious fear of the defiling power of sin.**

This conclusion survives both serious two- and three-clause reconstructions.

---

# 16. Jude 22–23 as anti-terminal-overdiagnosis control

This passage is crucial for the project’s pastoral taxonomy.

Whatever exact clause arrangement is original, Jude’s readers are told to act **toward people in danger**.

Therefore:

- some dangerous spiritual conditions remain proper objects of rescue;
- visible wavering/entanglement must not automatically be declared irreversible apostasy;
- church members must combine compassion and urgency;
- rescuers themselves must take contamination seriously.

### Contrast with terminal warnings

This is categorically different from simply announcing:

> “there is no repentance possible for this person.”

Hence:

> **Jude itself contains both fierce final-judgment warnings and rescue commands. A faithful theology needs both categories.**

---

# 17. Relation of vv.22–23 to Jude 20–21 and 24–25

The rescue commands are framed by two preservation poles.

## vv.20–21

Believers:

- build themselves in holy faith;
- pray in Holy Spirit;
- keep themselves in God’s love;
- wait for Christ’s mercy.

## vv.24–25

God is able:

- to keep from stumbling;
- to present His people blameless with joy.

### Canonical/theological synthesis

Human action and divine keeping are not rivals.

In one short paragraph Jude joins:

- keep yourselves;
- rescue others;
- God is able to keep you.

This is one of the strongest compact biblical controls for preservation-through-means.

The textual uncertainty in vv.22–23 **does not alter that macro-structure**.

# 18. Current textual decision hierarchy

## Jude 5

### Working text

`Ἰησοῦς` deserves primary-text status because:

- NA28/UBS5/ECM/THGNT print it;
- external evidence is exceptionally strong;
- difficult-reading/transcriptional logic is substantial.

### Confidence

`MODERATE`, not “certain.”

`κύριος` remains a serious scholarly alternative, especially in Wasserman’s full-transmission study.

### Publication practice

Main prose may say:

> “Most current critical editions read ‘Jesus’; an important textual variant reads ‘the Lord’.”

Then use the stable warning point before developing the optional high-Christology implication.

---

## Jude 22–23

### Working text

For ordinary reader-facing translation, retaining the familiar NA/UBS three-clause text is defensible.

### Confidence

`LOW–MODERATE` regarding exact clause structure.

Fresh scholarship gives significant weight to the shortest two-clause reading.

### Publication practice

Do not derive a rigid three-tier pastoral taxonomy.

State the broad imperative structure in variant-safe form.

# 20. Publication-safe claims

These may now be used confidently in synthesis.

### Jude 5

1. The text is genuinely disputed at the subject-name point.
2. Current major critical editions print `Jesus`.
3. `Lord` remains a serious scholarly alternative.
4. Under either reading, Jude’s main warning remains: the divine deliverer later judged unbelief within the delivered covenant people.
5. Corporate deliverance must not be automatically converted into individual regeneration of every Israelite.

### Jude 22–23

1. Exact clause/group structure is genuinely uncertain.
2. Two- and three-clause readings are ancient and serious.
3. Current NA/UBS-style three-group reading is not the only defensible reconstruction.
4. Mercy, urgent rescue and fear of contamination remain stable pastoral motifs.
5. The text forbids premature terminal labeling of every endangered person.
6. Jude integrates human rescue/keeping commands with divine keeping in vv.24–25.

---

# 21. Claims still prohibited without qualification

Do not publish:

- “Jude certainly wrote ‘Jesus’ in v.5”;
- “Jude 5 proves without textual qualification that Jesus personally led the Exodus”;
- “Jude 22–23 definitively teaches three classes of backsliders”;
- “the oldest manuscript settles Jude 22–23 by itself”;
- “doubting in Jude 22 means saving faith has already been lost”;
- “snatching from fire necessarily means rescuing a regenerate person from eternal damnation”;
- “mercy proves the endangered are all regenerate”; or
- “the variants make the passage unusable.”

# 22. Closure judgment

### Jude 5

`GREEN / VARIANT-DISCLOSED`.

The textual problem is sufficiently mapped for publication if the variant is disclosed and the main apostasy argument is formulated in reading-stable form.

### Jude 22–23

`GREEN / VARIANT-SAFE-SYNTHESIS`.

The exact clause structure should remain footnoted/technical. The pastoral-theological synthesis is stable across the serious variants.

### Global effect

The Jude textual-apparatus residue named in earlier blocker ledgers is no longer a structural publication blocker.

Remaining text-critical work should target **only variants that materially alter reader-facing claims**, not indiscriminately catalogue every apparatus entry.
