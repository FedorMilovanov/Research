## 1. Главный вывод

Послание к Титу даёт один из самых ясных текстов о **практическом отрицании Бога при сохранении религиозного исповедания**:

> `θεὸν ὁμολογοῦσιν εἰδέναι, τοῖς δὲ ἔργοις ἀρνοῦνται`
>
> «они исповедуют, что знают Бога, но делами отрекаются от Него» (1:16).

Это не означает:

> «всякий отдельный грех христианина = отречение от Христа».

Контекст описывает устойчивый профиль лжеучителей:

- непокорность;
- пустословие и обман;
- разрушение домов;
- корыстный мотив;
- человеческие заповеди;
- отворачивание от истины;
- осквернённые ум и совесть;
- исповедание Бога, опровергаемое жизнью.

Но письмо также показывает **порядок пастырской реакции**:

- обличать с целью оздоровления веры (1:13);
- увещевать;
- дать первое и второе предупреждение (3:10);
- затем прекратить бесплодное участие в разделяющей деятельности человека.

То есть Тит удерживает одновременно:

> **истину, коррекцию, возможность исправления и реальную границу церковного общения.**

---

# 2. 1:9 — задача старейшины задаёт контекст

Старейшина должен:

- держаться верного слова;
- быть способен увещевать здравым учением;
- обличать противящихся.

Поэтому 1:10–16 — не отвлечённая полемика Павла.

Это объяснение, **зачем церкви нужны доктринально зрелые пастыри**.

False teaching рассматривается как pastoral threat to households and godliness.

---

# 3. 1:10–11 — rebellious, empty talkers, deceivers, shameful gain

Paul describes many as:

- `ἀνυπότακτοι` — insubordinate/rebellious;
- `ματαιολόγοι` — empty talkers;
- `φρεναπάται` — deceivers of minds;
- especially connected with circumcision party;
- overturning whole households;
- teaching what they ought not;
- for `αἰσχροῦ κέρδους χάριν` — shameful gain.

This connects Titus to the money/covetousness matrix:

> corrupted doctrine and corrupted motive can reinforce one another.

MacArthur’s detailed Titus 1 exposition rightly emphasizes that these teachers operate **within/around Christian profession**, not merely as external pagans.

GTY:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/print/56-10/men-who-must-be-silenced-part-1

### Guardrail

Do not infer from “circumcision” that Jewish ethnicity itself is the problem. Paul targets a Judaizing false-teaching movement and its moral/doctrinal fruit.

---

# 4. 1:13 — severe rebuke has a restorative purpose

Paul says:

> rebuke them sharply **so that they may be sound in the faith**.

This phrase is critical.

Sharpness is not authorized as clerical aggression for its own sake.

Its stated telos is health:

`rebuke → soundness in faith`.

Therefore the same letter that commands silencing error also rejects a merely punitive pastoral model.

### Whole-project implication

Even a serious false-teaching context does not allow us to label every offender terminal **before the text’s own corrective means have been used**.

---

# 5. 1:14 — turning away from the truth

The false teachers attach themselves to:

- Jewish myths;
- human commands;
- people `ἀποστρεφομένων τὴν ἀλήθειαν` — turning away from the truth.

This is another return/turn lexeme control:

> the theological force comes from **object and context**, not from the bare movement verb.

Here the object is truth and the direction is away from it.

Thus the phrase contributes directly to the apostasy corpus.

---

# 6. 1:15 — defiled mind and conscience

Paul rejects a purity system that concentrates on external things while internal faculties are corrupted.

To the defiled/unbelieving:

- nothing is pure;
- mind is defiled;
- conscience is defiled.

The point is not antinomian:

> “external behavior never matters.”

The very next verse says behavior can **deny God**.

Rather, Paul attacks the attempt to manufacture purity by external regulations while heart/mind/conscience remain corrupted.

This strongly parallels:

- Mark 7 — defilement proceeds from within;
- 1 Tim 1:5,19 — good conscience / conscience rejected;
- 1 Tim 4:2 — seared conscience;
- 2 Tim 3:5 — form of godliness without power.

---

# 7. 1:16 — exact Greek: profession vs practical denial

Greek:

`θεὸν ὁμολογοῦσιν εἰδέναι, τοῖς δὲ ἔργοις ἀρνοῦνται, βδελυκτοὶ ὄντες καὶ ἀπειθεῖς καὶ πρὸς πᾶν ἔργον ἀγαθὸν ἀδόκιμοι.`

Greek witness:
https://www.greekbible.com/titus/1/16

Mounce translation/concordance witness:
https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Titus%201%3A16

---

## 7.1. `ὁμολογοῦσιν` — profession is real as profession

The verb means confess/profess/acknowledge.

Paul does not need to say they are knowingly lying every moment.

The core contradiction is objective:

> lips claim knowledge of God; deeds function as denial.

This is why “profession” must remain a distinct category from saving possession.

---

## 7.2. `ἀρνοῦνται` — denial by works

The same `ἀρνέομαι` family appears in 2 Tim 2:12:

> if we deny Him, He will deny us.

Titus 1:16 expands the category:

> denial need not occur only through the sentence “I reject Christ.”

A sustained life can contradict the Lord verbally confessed.

MacArthur makes this lexical connection explicitly in his 2 Tim exposition:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/55-8/motives-for-sacrificial-ministry-part-2

### Critical guardrail

Do not infer:

> every Christian sin is equivalent to Titus 1:16 denial.

Paul describes men whose **characteristic works** contradict profession and who are simultaneously false teachers, rebellious and corrupt in mind/conscience.

Peter’s restored denial and David’s restored fall remain canonical controls against overextension.

---

# 8. `ἀπειθεῖς` — disobedient / unpersuaded

`ἀπειθής` has the semantic field of disobedience/unpersuadability.

Mounce concordance confirms its use in Titus 1:16:
https://www.billmounce.com/concordance/579/272597

This matters because biblical unbelief is often not merely lack of information.

It involves refusal of obedient submission to truth.

But again, one adjective does not tell the entire history of each individual’s prior spiritual state.

---

# 9. `ἀδόκιμοι` — unfit / failing the test, not automatic decree-language

This is an important translation trap.

Older English “reprobate” can suggest later systematic doctrine of reprobation.

Here `ἀδόκιμος` means:

- not approved;
- failing the test;
- unfit / worthless with respect to good work.

Cambridge notes the sense “unable to stand the test / tried and found wanting.”

Source:
https://www.biblehub.com/commentaries/cambridge/titus/1.htm

### Therefore

Do not write:

> «Titus 1:16 explicitly calls these people eternally reprobate in the decretal sense».

The text gives a severe moral/ministry verdict:

> **unfit for every good work.**

Their final judgment can be argued from broader false-teacher/apostasy texts where appropriate, but not smuggled into the lexical value of `ἀδόκιμος`.

---

# 10. Whole-letter “good works” contrast

Titus repeatedly emphasizes good works.

The strongest contrast is:

## 1:16

False teachers are `ἀδόκιμοι` for every good work.

## 2:14

Christ gave Himself:

- to redeem from lawlessness;
- purify a people for His own possession;
- **zealous for good works**.

## 3:1

Believers are to be ready for every good work.

## 3:8

Those who believe God must devote themselves to good works.

## 3:14

Our people must learn to devote themselves to good works and not be unfruitful.

Thus 1:16 is not an isolated moralistic slogan.

It sits inside a Christological grace-and-fruit architecture.

---

# 11. Grace, not works-righteousness: Titus 2:11–14 and 3:3–7

This whole-letter control is essential.

## 11.1. Grace trains

Titus 2 does not say:

> grace saves, while holiness is unrelated afterward.

Grace **trains** believers to renounce ungodliness and worldly desires and live self-controlled, upright, godly lives.

## 11.2. Christ purifies a people for good works

Good works are the fruit/purpose of redemption, not the purchase price of redemption.

## 11.3. Titus 3 explicitly excludes works as the basis of salvation

God saved us:

> **not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to His mercy.**

Then only a few verses later Paul insists believers devote themselves to good works.

### Exact Pauline balance

> **works do not cause justification/regeneration; saving grace creates a people whose life is transformed toward good works.**

Therefore Titus 1:16 uses works evidentially: deeds can expose the falsity of religious profession.

---

# 12. Charnock — practical atheism as a powerful Puritan synthesis

Stephen Charnock explicitly uses Titus 1:16 in *A Discourse on Practical Atheism*.

Primary-access text:
https://www.monergism.com/discourse-practical-atheism

His core insight is especially useful here:

- words may acknowledge God;
- conduct may deny His practical authority;
- refusal to honor God in life reveals practical contempt for the God confessed by mouth.

Charnock’s formulation provides precisely the kind of “твёрдая пища” practical theology needed in the final series.

### Guardrail

Use Charnock to unfold **implications of Paul’s text**, not to flatten every believer’s inconsistent act into evidence of total practical atheism.

Paul’s target in Titus is a settled false-teacher pattern.

---

# 13. Calvin on Titus 1:16 — external scrupulosity can hide contempt of God

Calvin notices the irony:

people occupied with minute external observances can despise the weightier reality of a godly life.

Primary:
https://www.ccel.org/study/Titus_1%3A16-1%3A16

This strengthens the series’ false-assurance theme:

> religious strictness in selected external matters is not proof of true knowledge of God when the broader life openly contradicts Him.

---

# 14. Titus 3:9 — useless controversy vs profitable good works

Immediately before the factious-person instruction Paul contrasts:

- foolish controversies;
- genealogies;
- strife;
- law-quarrels;

with the profitable emphasis of 3:8 on good works.

The disputes are:

- `ἀνωφελεῖς` — unprofitable/harmful/useless;
- `μάταιοι` — vain/empty.

This resembles 2 Tim 2:

- word-fights;
- empty chatter;
- quarrels;
- false teaching;

vs accurate handling of truth and useful holy service.

Thus doctrinal seriousness must not be confused with endless contentiousness.

---

# 15. Titus 3:10 — `αἱρετικὸν ἄνθρωπον`

Mounce:

`αἱρετικὸν ἄνθρωπον μετὰ μίαν καὶ δευτέραν νουθεσίαν παραιτοῦ`

> avoid/refuse a factious person after a first and second warning.

Source:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Titus+3%3A9-11&version=NIV%3BMOUNCE

Greek analytical:
https://www.greekdoc.com/DOCUMENTS/nt-poly/tit03.html

---

## 15.1. Do not import the later technical meaning of “heretic” without qualification

`αἱρετικός` is related to `αἵρεσις`, whose NT range includes:

- party/sect;
- faction;
- division;
- in some contexts destructive false teaching.

In Titus 3 the surrounding concern is:

- controversies;
- strife;
- quarrels;
- division-making obstinacy.

Therefore “factious/divisive person” is safer as the immediate sense than assuming the later ecclesiastical technical definition:

> “anyone holding one formally defined heresy.”

Cambridge/Ellicott discussion:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/titus/3-10.htm

### But do not swing to the opposite error

The letter already contains doctrinal false teachers in ch.1.

A factious person can certainly be doctrinally erroneous.

The point is that **the lexeme itself** should not carry centuries of later technical development without context.

---

# 16. First and second admonition — correction precedes boundary

`μετὰ μίαν καὶ δευτέραν νουθεσίαν`

Paul explicitly builds in warning/admonition before refusal/avoidance.

This is highly important pastorally.

The order is not:

> dislike → label → immediately cast away.

It is:

`recognized destructive pattern → admonition → second admonition → persistence → boundary`.

Calvin rightly emphasizes that one may not rashly label someone a heretic without first attempting restoration to sound views.

Primary:
https://ccel.org/study/Titus_3%3A10-3%3A11

MacArthur likewise connects the text to correction followed by separation when persistence remains.

GTY:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/print/48-40/restoring-the-sinning-brother

---

# 17. `παραιτοῦ` — avoid/refuse; exact ecclesial mechanics require care

The verb can mean refuse, decline, avoid, have nothing to do with.

It does **not by itself** spell out a complete church-discipline procedure.

Some interpreters take it as effectively removal/exclusion from fellowship; others stress Titus personally ceasing debate/association.

John Gill, interestingly, argues that this verse alone should not be made a technical proof that an individual pastor possesses unilateral excommunication power; he distinguishes Titus’s command from congregational disciplinary texts.

Gill primary:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical.iii.vi.html

### Project conclusion

Safe wording:

> after repeated admonition, Titus is to stop treating the factious person as an endlessly open debate partner and impose a real ecclesial boundary.

For the exact polity mechanics, correlate:

- Matt 18:15–17;
- Rom 16:17;
- 1 Cor 5;
- 2 Thess 3:6,14–15;
- 2 John 10–11.

Do not derive an entire polity solely from `παραιτοῦ`.

---

# 18. 3:11 — `ἐξέστραπται`, sinning, self-condemned

Greek:

`εἰδὼς ὅτι ἐξέστραπται ὁ τοιοῦτος καὶ ἁμαρτάνει ὢν αὐτοκατάκριτος`

Mounce:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Titus+3%3A10-12&version=MOUNCE

## 18.1. `ἐξέστραπται`

Perfect passive/middle form of `ἐκστρέφω`.

Sense:

- turned aside;
- warped;
- perverted/deviated.

The perfect form presents an established condition/result, but **do not build a metaphysical irreversible-state doctrine from verbal aspect alone**.

## 18.2. `ἁμαρτάνει`

The person is actively sinning.

Division is not treated as a neutral personality difference.

## 18.3. `αὐτοκατάκριτος`

“self-condemned.”

The most natural force is that persistent behavior **after clear admonition** supplies the person’s own condemning evidence.

It does not mean:

> he necessarily verbally announces, “I know I am wrong.”

Nor does it require us to claim we can read his secret conscience infallibly.

His persistence after correction objectively exposes the contradiction.

---

# 19. Calvin’s “no hope” language must be handled more cautiously than the biblical text

Calvin comments strongly that the person is so ruined that there is no hope of repentance.

Primary:
https://ccel.org/study/Titus_3%3A10-3%3A11

His pastoral point is understandable:

- ministers should not spend endless time in fruitless controversy;
- persistent rejection of repeated admonition reveals hardening.

However the project should **not automatically promote Calvin’s phrase into an infallible eternal-state verdict**.

Titus commands an ecclesial boundary based on observable obstinacy.

It does not explicitly say:

> God can never subsequently grant this person repentance.

Compare:

- 2 Tim 2:25–26 — God may grant repentance to opponents caught in devil’s snare;
- 1 Tim 1:20 — disciplinary handing-over has pedagogical wording (“that they may learn”);
- James 5:19–20 — wandering person may be turned back.

### Better formulation

> **Pastors may reach the point where further argument is no longer their present duty; that is not identical to omniscient declaration that God can never save the person later.**

This distinction is crucial.

---

# 20. Owen — a church may need to reject after means of conviction

John Owen’s *Eshcol* directly cites Titus 3:9–11 in a practical ecclesial section.

He counsels:

- avoid fruitless novelties/disputes/jangling;
- recognize that these can become beginnings of fearful apostasies;
- after means of conviction and admonition, separate from the obstinate.

Primary:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/eshcol.i.vi.x.html

This is a strong Puritan practical control because it connects:

`contentious novelty → doctrinal/ecclesial disorder → admonition → boundary`.

It fits Paul’s own movement from 3:9 to 3:10–11.

# 21. Practical denial vs ordinary Christian inconsistency

Titus 1:16 must be applied with precision.

## Ordinary regenerate inconsistency

A believer may:

- sin seriously;
- act inconsistently with confession;
- need rebuke;
- repent and be restored.

## Titus 1 pattern

The described teachers:

- persist in false teaching;
- rebel against truth/authority;
- exploit households;
- seek shameful gain;
- turn from truth;
- have defiled mind/conscience;
- maintain religious profession while characteristic works deny God.

Therefore “practical denial” is best used for a **settled contradiction between profession and governing life**, not as a rhetorical weapon for every stumble.

---

# 22. Church discipline is neither relativism nor vengeance

Titus rejects two opposite errors.

## Error A — endless tolerance

> “As long as someone claims Christianity, persistent divisiveness and false teaching cannot justify separation.”

False. Titus commands a boundary.

## Error B — instant denunciation

> “Once I think someone wrong, I may label him heretic and discard him immediately.”

False. Titus explicitly requires admonition before rejection.

## Biblical pattern

> **clarify → admonish → admonish again → observe response → set boundary if obstinacy persists.**

MacArthur’s restoration sermon usefully emphasizes this movement from attempted restoration to separation when a person insists on sin.

https://www.gty.org/sermons/print/48-40/restoring-the-sinning-brother

---

# 23. Titus and apostasy taxonomy

Titus contributes several distinct categories.

### A. Doctrinal defection

`turning away from truth` (1:14).

### B. Practical denial

`profess God / deny by works` (1:16).

### C. Corrupted inner faculties

`mind and conscience defiled` (1:15).

### D. Ministry exploitation

`teaching for shameful gain` (1:11).

### E. Recoverability under rebuke

`rebuke sharply that they may be sound in faith` (1:13).

### F. Persistently factious hardening

first/second admonition ignored → avoid/refuse (3:10–11).

### G. Grace-produced good works

Christ redeems/purifies a people zealous for good works (2:14), while salvation is explicitly not by our righteous works (3:5).

This is why Titus is unusually valuable for preventing both antinomian and works-righteousness distortions.

# 25. Publication-safe synthesis

> «Послание к Титу показывает, что отречение от Бога бывает не только устным. Лжеучители “исповедуют, что знают Бога, но делами отрекаются от Него” (Тит. 1:16). Это не означает, что каждый грех верующего равен окончательному отступничеству: Павел описывает устойчивый комплекс — непокорность, обман, корыстное лжеучительство, отвращение от истины, осквернённые ум и совесть и жизнь, систематически опровергающую исповедание. При этом цель первого обличения ещё восстановительная: “обличай их строго, чтобы они были здравы в вере”. Только в конце письма появляется граница: человека, упорно создающего разделение, после первого и второго увещания велено избегать. Даже здесь важно не читать в текст больше написанного: греческое αἱρετικός прежде всего указывает на фракционность/разделение, а παραιτοῦ требует реальной границы, но сам по себе не описывает всю процедуру церковной дисциплины. Павел не выбирает между истиной и терпением: он сначала велит исправлять, а затем — когда увещания сознательно отвергаются — перестать превращать служение в бесконечный спор. И всё это стоит внутри евангелия благодати: Бог спасает “не по делам праведности, которые мы сотворили”, но эта же благодать очищает Себе народ, ревностный к добрым делам.»
