Verification:
https://irp.cdn-website.com/85eee812/files/uploaded/Proverbs%201.20-33%20%28Manuscript%29.pdf

The citation on p.212 is especially valuable because it explicitly links:

- the “turning away” vocabulary;
- Wisdom’s authority;
- the death consequence;
- Mosaic/prophetic apostasy language as a canonical resonance.

### Important hermeneutical restraint

Canonical resonance does **not** mean the simpletons are thereby proven to be previously regenerate covenant saints.

The immediate literary subject is the `פֶּתִי` / simple or gullible person refusing Wisdom’s correction.

The text proves lethal waywardness; it does not independently prove loss of regeneration.

# 3. Proverbs 1:32 — lexical control for `מְשׁוּבָה`

A lexical summary of TWOT/NIDOTTE material notes:

- `מְשׁוּבָה` denotes backsliding/disloyalty/faithlessness/turning away;
- it occurs heavily in Jeremiah and Hosea;
- Hosea 14:4 can speak of God healing such backsliding;
- **Prov 1:32 is the only occurrence where the noun is applied to an individual simpleton** rather than Israel/Judah corporately.

Accessible lexical witness:
https://community.logos.com/discussion/191452/new-international-dictionary-of-old-testament-theology-and-exegesis-nidotte-5-vols-by-willem-a

### Why this matters

The prophetic corpus gives the noun a strong covenant-apostasy atmosphere, but Proverbs recontextualizes it in wisdom pedagogy.

Therefore publication should say:

> “The noun belongs to the OT field of turning away/backsliding and is used elsewhere especially of Israel’s covenant infidelity; in Prov 1:32 it describes the lethal waywardness of the simple person who rejects Wisdom.”

It should **not** say:

> “The word itself proves that this individual was previously regenerate and then lost salvation.”

**Status:** `GREEN LEXICAL CONTROL`.

# 5. Proverbs 14:14 — Hebrew phrase first

> **“a turning away of heart”**

and explain `סוּג` as moving away/backward, departing, backsliding.

Technical source:
https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=Pro+10%3A1--22%3A16

### Key inference

The verse localizes the problem **in the heart**.

This supports the project’s heart-apostasy matrix:

- external rupture need not be the first stage;
- inward direction can precede visible conduct.

But the phrase still does not by itself specify a prior regenerative state.

# 10. Important lexical-theological correction for Proverbs 1:32

The exact word-study now sharpens the earlier dossier:

### Too weak

> “`מְשׁוּבָה` simply means generic bad behavior.”

The wider OT evidence is stronger than that; it is genuine turning-away/backsliding language.

### Too strong

> “Because `מְשׁוּבָה` is used for Israel’s apostasy, the individual in Prov 1:32 must be a formerly regenerate believer losing salvation.”

That does not follow.

### Better

> **Prov 1:32 intentionally uses strong OT turning-away language for the simple person’s refusal of Wisdom. The word belongs to the apostasy/backsliding semantic field, but the wisdom context—not the lexeme alone—must determine what prior spiritual status can be inferred. The text itself emphasizes rejected rebuke, chosen folly, and lethal consequence, not an explicit prior-regeneration narrative.**
