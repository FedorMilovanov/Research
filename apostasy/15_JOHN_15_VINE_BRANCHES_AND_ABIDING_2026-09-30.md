## 1. Почему Ин. 15 нельзя решать одной фразой «ветви были только внешне во Христе»

Ин. 15:1–6 — один из главных текстов, который сторонники conditional security читают как прямое описание человека **в Христе**, который перестаёт пребывать, отсекается и сгорает. Реформатская традиция часто отвечает: бесплодная ветвь принадлежит Христу лишь внешне/по исповеданию, как Иуда или внешний Израиль. Free Grace tradition предлагает третью линию: ветви — настоящие верующие, но огонь/отсечение описывают дисциплину, потерю fellowship/reward или temporal judgment, а не ад.

Ни одну из этих моделей нельзя объявить доказанной одной лексемой. Нужно прочитать:

- immediate narrative context John 13–17;
- `ἐν ἐμοί`;
- `αἴρει`;
- `μένω`;
- `καθαίρει / καθαροί`;
- fruit;
- fire imagery;
- OT vine background;
- broader Johannine security/abiding texts.

---

## 2. Immediate narrative control: Иуда уже вышел, оставшиеся названы «чистыми»

### 2.1. John 13:10–11

Jesus says disciples are clean, **but not all**, and narrator explicitly links exception to betrayer.

### 2.2. John 13:30

Judas receives morsel and goes out; `ἦν δὲ νύξ` — “it was night.”

### 2.3. John 15:3

To the present disciples Jesus says:

`ἤδη ὑμεῖς καθαροί ἐστε διὰ τὸν λόγον...` — “already you are clean because of the word...”

This creates an important relation to v.2 `καθαίρει` (prunes/cleanses) and to 13:10–11.

### Implication

The warning is **not** addressed in an abstract theological classroom. Jesus speaks to the clean eleven after the unclean betrayer has left, and still commands them to abide.

That fact cuts in two directions:

- it strengthens the reality of the warning to genuine disciples;
- it also makes Judas a narratively available negative example of a disciple-figure who was inside the visible apostolic circle but excluded from “clean” status.

Therefore neither “warning means the eleven can definitely lose regeneration” nor “Judas solves everything” can be assumed without further argument.

Bible.org’s exegetical commentary explicitly uses 13:30 and 13:10–11 as contextual controls and treats Judas as the primary Johannine model for a non-abiding branch:
- https://bible.org/seriespage/exegetical-commentary-john-15

## 3. OT vine background: Israel, fruitlessness, judgment

The vine is not a neutral horticultural illustration. Major OT backgrounds include:

- Psalm 80:8–16 — vine brought from Egypt, later ravaged/burned;
- Isaiah 5:1–7 — carefully tended vineyard yields bad fruit and receives judgment;
- Jeremiah 2:21 — noble vine becomes degenerate;
- Ezekiel 15 — fruitless vine-wood is useless except for burning;
- Ezekiel 19; Hosea 10.

ICC material usefully emphasizes how often Israel-as-vine imagery carries degeneracy/judgment:
- https://www.biblehub.com/commentaries/icc/john/15.htm

Keil/Delitzsch on Ezekiel 15 likewise connects fruitless vine-wood with Israel and burning:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/kad/ezekiel/15.htm

### Christological shift

Jesus does not merely say “you are the new vine.” He says **“I am the true vine.”** The center of covenant fruitfulness is relocated/fulfilled in Messiah; branches have life/productivity only in relation to Him.

This makes `ἐν ἐμοί` covenantally and christologically weighty. It should not be trivialized as if Jesus said merely “people who claim to like me.”

## 4. `αἴρει` in 15:2 — “takes away” vs “lifts up”

Greek:

`πᾶν κλῆμα ἐν ἐμοὶ μὴ φέρον καρπὸν αἴρει αὐτό`

A popular modern rescue-reading translates `αἴρει` as “lifts up” a trailing branch so it may become fruitful.

### 4.1. Semantic fact

`αἴρω` can mean lift/take up and also remove/take away. Lexical range alone does not decide.

### 4.2. Contextual pressure

Bill Mounce notes that BDAG includes lifting/removing senses but argues John 15 context favors removal, especially because v.6 describes non-abiding branch as thrown out, withered, gathered, cast into fire and burned.

Source:
- https://zondervanacademic.com/blog/airo

Bible.org reaches the same contextual judgment and notes Johannine uses where `αἴρω` means remove (e.g. 11:39; 11:48; 16:22; 17:15):
- https://bible.org/seriespage/exegetical-commentary-john-15

### Research verdict

“Lift up to restore” is **possible at raw dictionary-range level but weak as contextual solution**. It should be represented if readers encounter it, but not used as primary answer to apostasy problem.

### Important translation caution

“Cuts off” can over-specify mechanism; “takes away/removes” stays closer to verb while v.6 supplies rejection/burning trajectory.

## 5. `καθαίρει` / `καθαροί`: deliberate wordplay matters

v.2 fruitful branch `καθαίρει` — prunes/cleanses.  
v.3 disciples `καθαροί` — already clean.

This is not cosmetic. It links vineyard treatment to cleansing by Jesus’ word and echoes 13:10.

The fruitful branches are not self-maintaining autonomous believers. Father actively tends/cleanses them so they bear **more** fruit.

For perseverance theology this is important:

> the passage’s positive side is not merely “disciples choose to remain”; divine husbandry actively produces greater fruitfulness in already-clean disciples.

That does not erase command `μένειν`, but it prevents a purely synergistic reading where Father is passive observer waiting to see whether branches survive.

---

## 6. `ἐν ἐμοί` — strongest pressure point against a superficial-profession reading

v.2 says **“every branch in Me”** that does not bear fruit.

Conditional-security argument is straightforward:

1. branch is `ἐν Χριστῷ`;
2. later removed/burned;
3. therefore genuinely united believer can perish.

Reformed response often says `ἐν ἐμοί` here describes visible/covenantal/professional relation, not necessarily regenerating union.

Calvin makes that move explicitly: many are “supposed to be in the vine” by human opinion / outward profession as Israel was called God’s vine:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/john/15.htm

### What must be admitted

The phrase is **strong**. We cannot write:

> “`ἐν ἐμοί` obviously just means external church membership.”

That is an interpretive conclusion, not lexical fact.

### What must also be admitted

Johannine literature can describe discipleship/belonging claims that later prove false:

- John 2:23–25 — “believed” language with Jesus withholding Himself;
- John 6:60–66 — disciples depart;
- John 8:30ff — belief/discipleship tested by abiding in word;
- Judas inside Twelve yet not clean;
- 1 John 2:19 — departure reveals they were “not of us.”

Thus the Gospel has categories of **real historical/communal attachment to Jesus that do not automatically equal final saving union**.

The case must therefore be made narratively/canonically, not by redefining `ἐν`.

---

## 7. `μένω` — “abide/remain” is not decorative

John 15 repeatedly commands/conditions fruitfulness on remaining in Christ.

`μείνατε ἐν ἐμοί` — “abide/remain in Me.”

### Johannine importance

`μένω` is one of John’s central relational verbs:

- John 8:31 — true disciples abide in Jesus’ word;
- John 6:56 — mutual abiding language;
- John 14:10 — Father/Son relation;
- 1 John 2:19, 24, 27–28 — remaining becomes decisive perseverance language.

### Research consequence

A Reformed article must not say “true believers need not really abide because perseverance is guaranteed.” The command is one of the means/marks by which true disciples continue.

Conversely, a conditional-security reading must still explain John 10:27–30, 6:37–40, 17 and 1 John’s retrospective “if they had been of us, they would have remained.”

---

## 8. Fruit — evidence of life, not optional advanced Christianity

The passage escalates:

- no fruit → removal;
- fruit → pruning → more fruit;
- abiding → much fruit;
- fruit glorifies Father and marks discipleship.

Therefore a “carnal but eternally secure branch can remain permanently fruitless and only lose rewards” reading faces serious local pressure.

Bible.org explicitly argues that in Johannine logic conduct/fruit reveals paternity/life rather than merely higher discipleship status.

This does not mean mature fruit is instant or uniform. It does mean **permanent fruitlessness is not treated as normal saving discipleship**.

---

## 9. v.6 fire — judgment imagery is stronger than “lost fellowship”

Sequence:

1. does not abide;
2. cast outside as branch;
3. withers;
4. gathered;
5. thrown into fire;
6. burned.

OT vine background, especially Ezekiel 15 and Psalm 80, makes judgment imagery natural.

### Main competing readings

A. **Eschatological judgment of false professors** — Reformed/common evangelical.

B. **Eschatological judgment of formerly true believers** — conditional security.

C. **Judgment/discipline/reward loss of true believers** — Free Grace.

### Evaluation of C

Bible.org notes that importing 1 Cor. 3 reward-fire into John 15 is contextually difficult because Fourth Gospel commonly contrasts believer with judgment (John 3:18; 5:24) and the vine-burning imagery itself resembles eschatological judgment.

Thus Free Grace view deserves representation but currently has weaker local-context fit than A/B.

---

## 10. Augustine — no softening of v.6

Augustine, *Tractates on John* 80–81, reads Christ as vine and disciples as branches and takes cut-off/burning language seriously; cut branch cannot live apart from root.

Sources:
- Tractate 80: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1701080.htm
- Tractate 81: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1701081.htm

Historical caution: Augustine’s broader perseverance theology is not reducible to one Johannine tractate. Use these for reception of the image, not to conscript him into a modern denominational matrix without wider context.

## 11. Competing-view matrix

| View | What “branch in Me” means | Fire | Strength | Main pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Reformed/false-professor | visible/covenantal/disciple relation to Christ, not saving union; Judas paradigmatic | final judgment | integrates Judas, John 6/8, 1 John 2:19, John 10 security | must justify strong `ἐν ἐμοί`; cannot simply redefine it |
| B. Conditional security | genuine saving union; believer fails to remain and is finally lost | final judgment | most direct reading of “in Me” + removal/fire | must integrate John 6:37–40; 10:27–30; 17; 1 John 2:19 and divine keeping |
| C. Free Grace/rewards-discipline | genuine believer; abiding = fellowship/fruitfulness, not possession of eternal life | discipline/reward loss/possibly temporal death | protects eternal security while retaining genuine branch status | local fire/OT judgment imagery + permanent fruitlessness + Johannine judgment theology press strongly against reward-only reading |
| D. `αἴρω = lift up` restoration | genuine weak/unfruitful branch is raised/tended, not removed | v.6 treated separately | removes immediate v.2 threat | lexical/contextual support weak; v.6 trajectory remains unresolved |

No future article should present only A vs B. C and D are common enough to require explicit treatment.

## 12. Judas as narrative control — useful but not total solution

A strong observation:

- Judas is inside Twelve;
- not clean (13:10–11);
- exits in 13:30;
- discourse then speaks of remaining branches/clean disciples;
- John 15 negative branch can naturally evoke Judas.

But do not overstate:

> Jesus does not explicitly say in 15:2/6, “the branch is Judas.”

The connection is narrative inference, strong but still inference.

Future prose should say “Judas provides the Gospel’s nearest narrative model,” not “v.6 grammatically identifies Judas.”

---

## 13. Relation to 1 John 2:19 — retrospective test

1 John 2:19:

- they went out from us;
- they were not of us;
- if they had been of us, **they would have remained** (`μεμενήκεισαν`) with us;
- departure manifests what was true.

This is among the strongest Johannine controls on how “remaining” functions theologically.

It does not mechanically decode every vine detail, but it shows John can say:

> non-continuance reveals absence of deeper belonging rather than necessarily destroying a prior saving identity.

This substantially strengthens View A at canonical-author level.

## 14. Relation to John 10 and 17

### John 10:27–30

Sheep:

- hear;
- are known by Christ;
- follow;
- receive eternal life;
- shall never perish;
- no one can snatch them from Christ/Father.

### John 17

Jesus distinguishes those Father gave Him; Judas as “son of perdition” requires careful treatment but the prayer strongly emphasizes divine guarding.

### Synthesis requirement

John 15 cannot be isolated from these texts. A whole-John reading must preserve both:

- real imperative/conditional language of abiding;
- strong divine-security language.

Reformed synthesis: abiding warnings are means/marks of preservation; apostasy reveals false attachment.  
Conditional synthesis: security promises are conditioned by ongoing faith/abiding.  
The article must argue, not assume, which better explains all data.

## 15. Current research judgment

The strongest provisional synthesis is:

1. `αἴρω = lift up` is not a satisfactory escape hatch; removal best fits context.
2. v.6 most naturally depicts serious/final judgment, not merely loss of rewards.
3. `ἐν ἐμοί` must retain its full force; external-profession reading requires Johannine/narrative evidence, not lexical dilution.
4. That evidence **does exist**: Judas/not-clean; departing disciples; John 8 tests; 1 John 2:19; Johannine permanence motif.
5. Therefore John 15 is compatible with a Reformed perseverance reading **only if** it admits real covenant/disciple attachment and real warning, rather than pretending the branch was “never connected in any meaningful sense.”
6. The strongest alternative remains conditional security: genuinely united branches may cease abiding and perish; it must be answered through whole-Johannine synthesis.
7. Free Grace reward-only reading currently has more local-context pressure because the fire image and Johannine judgment framework are severe.

This is stronger than either slogan:

- “branch in Christ = saved, case closed”; or
- “Judas = false professor, case closed.”
