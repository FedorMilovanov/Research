## 1. Главный вывод

1 Кор. 9:27 нельзя безопасно изолировать от 10:1–13.

Павел говорит:

> `μή πως ἄλλοις κηρύξας αὐτὸς ἀδόκιμος γένωμαι`
>
> «чтобы, проповедав другим, самому не оказаться `ἀδόκιμος` — не выдержавшим испытания / неодобренным / дисквалифицированным».

А затем критический текст начинает 10:1 союзом **`γάρ` — “ибо”**, то есть пример Израиля объясняет/обосновывает предостережение Павла.

Структура:

`гонка / самодисциплина / опасность ἀδόκιμος → ИБО Израиль получил огромные общие привилегии → большинство всё же пало под Божьим судом → эти события стали типами для нас → кто думает, что стоит, пусть смотрит, чтобы не упасть → НО Бог верен и даёт возможность выдержать испытание → поэтому бегите от идолопоклонства`.

### Research judgment

Наиболее сильное чтение не сводит `ἀδόκιμος` в 9:27 лишь к:

- потере пасторской должности;
- уменьшению награды;
- временному снятию с соревнования.

Контекст 10:1–12 придаёт слову **эсхатологически серьёзный вес**: Павел ставит себя под ту же систему предупреждения, которую затем применяет к коринфянам через погибшее поколение пустыни.

Но отсюда также не следует автоматически:

> «Павел считал, что вечное избрание и возрождение можно получить, затем потерять».

Сильнейшая реформатская модель (Schreiner / Ciampa–Rosner / Fee at warning-means level) говорит иначе:

> **вечная жизнь достигается по пути продолжающейся веры и послушания; окончательное отступление ведёт к суду; предупреждения реальны и являются средствами, которыми Бог сохраняет Своих. Божья верность в 10:13 не превращает предупреждения в фикцию — она обеспечивает реальную возможность выстоять.**

# 2. 1 Кор. 9:24–27 — гонка не является христианским motivational slogan

Paul’s sequence:

- все бегут, но бегите так, чтобы получить награду;
- спортсмен проявляет `ἐγκράτεια` — самообладание;
- земные спортсмены делают это ради тленного венца;
- христиане — ради нетленного;
- Павел не бежит бесцельно;
- не бьёт воздух;
- дисциплинирует тело и порабощает его;
- цель — не стать `ἀδόκιμος` после проповеди другим.

Text witness:
https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/1%20Corinthians%209%3A27

### Central point

Paul does not present apostolic office as immunity.

He places **himself** under the exhortation.

That is already a direct blow against false assurance based on:

- ministry fruit;
- preaching;
- office;
- past usefulness;
- theological status.

# 3. `ὑπωπιάζω` and `δουλαγωγῶ` — disciplined body, not bodily asceticism as merit

Greek:

`ἀλλὰ ὑπωπιάζω μου τὸ σῶμα καὶ δουλαγωγῶ`

The athletic/boxing metaphor is forceful:

- `ὑπωπιάζω` can evoke striking/buffeting, bringing under hard discipline;
- `δουλαγωγῶ` = enslave / bring into subjection.

### Calvin’s useful guardrail

Calvin explicitly warns against monastic/body-punishment readings that miss Paul’s moral point. He reads the language as **restraining bodily appetites so the body does not master the believer or hinder piety/service**.

Primary commentary:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/1_corinthians/9.htm

### Practical theology

This fits John Owen’s wider mortification framework:

- bodily desires are not destroyed by self-torture;
- sin must be mortified by the Spirit;
- nevertheless believers actively refuse the flesh’s rule.

Owen should be used as **practical synthesis**, not as lexical authority for `ὑπωπιάζω`.

# 4. What does `ἀδόκιμος` mean?

`ἀδόκιμος` fundamentally carries the idea:

> **failing the test / not approved / rejected as not meeting the standard.**

It is the negative of the `δοκιμ-` approval/testing word family.

### Other Pauline usage

The same adjective occurs in contexts such as:

- Rom 1:28 — debased/disapproved mind;
- 2 Cor 13:5–7 — test yourselves whether in the faith; unless `ἀδόκιμοι`;
- 2 Tim 3:8 — disapproved regarding the faith;
- Titus 1:16 — unfit/disqualified for every good work.

Therefore it is too weak to define the word itself as:

> “merely loses an extra ministry prize.”

### But lexical guardrail

The word by itself also does **not** equal the full proposition:

> “eternally damned.”

The level of rejection is determined by **context**.

And here the decisive context is 10:1–12.

# 5. Is `ἀδόκιμος` a technical athletic term meaning only “disqualified from the games”? 

Ciampa and Rosner directly investigate this claim.

Their conclusion (PNTC, pp.421–422):

- some have called `ἀδόκιμος` a technical athletic term;
- such a specialized usage is not actually established by the major lexicons/dictionaries;
- whether or not the athletic metaphor remains active, the word in this context points toward **God’s potential eschatological judgment** on Paul and the Corinthians;
- the ultimate evaluator is God, not merely a human contest official.

Locator witness preserving the argument and pages:
https://hermeneutics.stackexchange.com/questions/539/disqualification-in-1-cor-927

# 8. MacArthur — serious conservative narrower reading: ministry disqualification

John MacArthur consistently reads 9:27 in a narrower vocational sense:

> Paul fears disqualifying himself from ministry / leadership usefulness through bodily sin.

Primary GTY examples:

https://www.gty.org/resources/study-guides/chapters/54-33/qualities-of-an-excellent-servant-part-4

https://www.gty.org/blogs/B150109/sexual-fidelity

### Why this view has real strengths

- immediate context includes Paul’s apostolic ministry and self-denial for gospel mission;
- `κηρύξας` can evoke the herald/announcer imagery of games;
- ministry scandal genuinely can permanently disqualify someone from leadership without implying loss of regeneration.

### Why it is probably too narrow as the exhaustive meaning

1. 10:1 starts with `γάρ`, linking Israel’s disaster to 9:27;
2. Israel’s example is not merely “lost ministry platform”;
3. 10:5–10 repeatedly narrates divine destruction/judgment;
4. 10:11 says the events are written for **our admonition**;
5. 10:12 warns the one standing to beware lest he fall;
6. 10:14 culminates in “flee idolatry.”

### Best adjudication

Ministry disqualification is a **legitimate practical application** and may be part of Paul’s concern.

But a strictly service-only reading does not adequately explain the move into chapter 10.

Therefore:

`ministry disqualification included/possible` — `YES`.

`ministry disqualification exhausts ἀδόκιμος` — `LESS LIKELY`.

# 9. 10:1 begins with `γάρ`: Israel explains Paul’s fear

Critical reading:

`Οὐ θέλω γὰρ ὑμᾶς ἀγνοεῖν...`

Cambridge Greek Testament notes:

- `γάρ` has strong manuscript support;
- a later/alternative `δέ` likely arose because readers felt difficulty in the transition;
- but `γάρ` reveals the intended logic: Paul has spoken of the need for caution in the Christian race and now **illustrates it with Israel**.

Source:
https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/236/cambridge-greek-testament-for-schools-and-colleges-commentary/1-corinthians/10/1

### Argument function

This connection materially controls 9:27.

Paul’s concern about becoming `ἀδόκιμος` is immediately explained through a generation that:

- possessed immense privileges;
- indulged evil desires/idolatry/immorality/testing/grumbling;
- came under severe divine judgment.

# 10. Fivefold `πάντες` — “all” received the privileges

Paul deliberately repeats **all**:

- all were under the cloud;
- all passed through the sea;
- all were baptized into Moses;
- all ate the same spiritual food;
- all drank the same spiritual drink.

Text:
https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=1Co+10%3A1-5

Cambridge explicitly notes that `πάντες` is repeated to make the moral contrast unavoidable:

> all received the privileges, but most did not use/respond to them rightly.

Source:
https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/236/cambridge-greek-testament-for-schools-and-colleges-commentary/1-corinthians/10/1

### The point

Great religious privilege is **not** evidence of immunity from judgment.

This is one of the strongest anti-presumption texts in Paul.

---

# 11. “Baptized into Moses” — real covenant identification, not proof every Israelite was regenerate

1 Cor 10:2:

> all were baptized `εἰς τὸν Μωϋσῆν` in the cloud and sea.

Paul intentionally draws typological/covenantal correspondence with Christian baptism.

### What it establishes

- real identification with Moses/covenant community;
- real historical redemption privilege;
- real participation in God’s delivered people.

### What it does not establish automatically

- every Israelite was inwardly regenerate;
- every Israelite had justifying faith;
- Mosaic “baptism” is identical in every theological respect with Spirit baptism into Christ.

### Apostasy significance

Paul can speak of a community receiving corporate redemptive privileges and still distinguish faithfulness from unbelief/disobedience.

This is directly relevant to Hebrews, Romans 11 and visible-church categories.

---

# 12. “Spiritual food / spiritual drink / the Rock was Christ” — do not weaken the privilege

Paul says:

- all ate the **same spiritual food**;
- all drank the **same spiritual drink**;
- they drank from the spiritual Rock;
- the Rock was Christ.

### Two opposite errors

#### Error A — reduction

> “They had no real Christ-related privilege at all.”

Paul explicitly says otherwise.

#### Error B — overreading

> “Because Christ is named, every participant must have possessed saving union/regeneration.”

The whole warning is built on the fact that common extraordinary privilege did **not** guarantee God’s final approval.

### Best category

`real Christological/covenantal privilege without automatic proof of saving faith in every participant`.

This is consistent with the project’s distinction between:

- common/shared covenant blessings;
- final saving union.

---

# 13. 10:5 — “with most of them God was not pleased”

Paul pivots sharply:

> `ἀλλ’ οὐκ ἐν τοῖς πλείοσιν αὐτῶν εὐδόκησεν ὁ θεός`.

Despite the fivefold “all,” God was not pleased with **most**.

Result:

> they were struck down/scattered in the wilderness.

### Theological force

The contrast is deliberately shocking:

`all privileged → most judged`.

This is why sacramental/covenantal privilege cannot become false assurance.

### Guardrail

The physical deaths in the wilderness do not by themselves establish the eternal state of every individual who died.

Paul’s use is typological warning toward Christian final faithfulness; do not reverse-engineer an eternal verdict for each Israelite beyond what Scripture says.

---

# 14. 10:6 and 10:11 — Paul says explicitly these are examples/types **for us**

10:6:

> these things became `τύποι ἡμῶν` — examples/types for us.

10:11:

> these things happened `τυπικῶς` and were written `πρὸς νουθεσίαν ἡμῶν` — for our admonition.

### This blocks two evasions

1. “That was only Old Covenant Israel; Christians are irrelevant to the warning.”
2. “Paul merely gives historical trivia about national Israel.”

Paul explicitly says the events were written **to admonish the Christian church**.

### But typology is analogy, not identity

The church is not simply Israel repeated without distinction.

Paul uses real correspondence to warn Christians, not to erase covenant-historical development.

# 15. What sins does Paul select?

He does not give a random list.

He targets the very dangers surrounding Corinth:

1. craving evil — v.6;
2. idolatry — v.7;
3. sexual immorality — v.8;
4. testing Christ/Lord — v.9;
5. grumbling — v.10.

### Canon-wide mechanism link

These match established project matrices:

- idolatry + sexual immorality;
- presumption under privilege;
- testing God;
- refusing gratitude/providence;
- using grace as license.

The wilderness is therefore a **mirror for Corinth**, not merely ancient biography.

# 16. 1 Cor 10:9 textual issue — Christ / Lord / God

This verse contains a material textual variant.

Readings include:

- `Χριστόν` — Christ;
- `κύριον` — Lord;
- `θεόν` — God (limited support).

NET’s apparatus argues strongly for `Χριστόν` as the harder/original reading and lists substantial manuscript/versional support.

Technical note:
https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?mode=print&passage=1Co+10%3A9

Edition comparison:
https://biblehub.com/text/1_corinthians/10-9.htm

### Editorial rule

If the final article makes the Christ-preexistence point from v.9, include a textual note rather than pretending no variant exists.

### Apostasy argument unaffected

All major readings preserve the warning:

> do not put the divine Lord to the test as the wilderness generation did and was destroyed.

Thus the core perseverance argument does not depend on resolving the variant.

# 17. 10:12 — “let the one who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall”

Greek:

`ὥστε ὁ δοκῶν ἑστάναι βλεπέτω μὴ πέσῃ.`

### `δοκῶν` guardrail

Do not overtranslate:

> “the one who falsely imagines he stands.”

The verb can mean think/consider/seem; the following warning can address someone who really does stand but is becoming presumptuous.

The best force is:

> **the one confident of standing must watch so that he does not fall.**

### “Fall” in context

It at least means falling into the sins/judgments just exemplified.

It should not be reduced to:

> “momentarily lose emotional peace.”

The Israel narrative gives the warning severe theological weight.

### Relation to Romans 11

Paul uses the same anti-presumption pattern:

- standing is grace/faith-dependent;
- privilege must produce humility;
- warning addresses those tempted to feel secure in status.

---

# 18. 10:13 begins with divine faithfulness, not human self-confidence

`πιστὸς δὲ ὁ θεός` — but/and God is faithful.

This is the balancing center of the whole section.

Paul does **not** conclude from Israel:

> “Everyone will probably fail, so despair.”

Nor:

> “The danger was only hypothetical, so relax.”

He says:

> God is faithful.

### God’s faithfulness acts concretely

He will not permit the test beyond what can be borne **with His provision**.

He will make:

> `τὴν ἔκβασιν` — the way out / outcome / exit,

so that:

> `δύνασθαι ὑπενεγκεῖν` — you are able to endure/bear up under it.

### Important correction to popular slogan

The verse does **not** simply say:

> “God will never give you more suffering than you can handle.”

Context concerns moral/spiritual testing and temptation, especially idolatrous compromise.

And the ability is not autonomous human toughness; it rests on **God’s faithfulness and provision**.

# 20. MacArthur on 10:13 — divine protection through provided means

MacArthur’s exegesis of 10:13 is important because it prevents his narrow 9:27 ministry reading from becoming a shallow “security regardless of vigilance” doctrine.

Primary GTY, quoting the MacArthur NT Commentary:
https://shop.gty.org/library/bibleqnas-library/QA0300/is-a-temptation-also-a-trial

He explains:

- `πειρασμός` can be testing or temptation depending on response;
- believers are never placed in a temptation from which God supplies no path of faithful response;
- God controls the test and provides the possibility of victory/endurance.

Even more explicitly, in another GTY sermon MacArthur joins divine sovereignty and means:

- God will provide escape;
- therefore believer must flee idolatry;
- divine promises are not fulfilled **apart from the obedience of His people**.

Primary:
https://www.gty.org/sermons/62-22/the-purifying-hope-part-1

### Important synthesis

This is very close to the project’s preservation-through-means principle:

> **God’s certainty of preservation does not make vigilance unnecessary; His preserving action is what makes faithful endurance genuinely possible.**

# 21. God’s faithfulness in 10:13 does not erase 9:27 / 10:12

Bad inference:

> “Since God is faithful in v.13, the falls warned in vv.5–12 cannot have been real dangers for Christians.”

Paul’s sequence says the opposite:

1. remember destroyed Israel;
2. these things are examples for us;
3. take heed lest you fall;
4. **God is faithful**;
5. therefore the test is not irresistible;
6. v.14: **flee idolatry**.

### Reformed formulation

God does not preserve the saints **from having to heed warnings**.

He preserves them **through the warning, the Spirit’s enablement, providential escape, self-control, flight from idolatry and enduring faith**.

This is exactly the same architecture already established in:

- Heb 3;
- Rom 11;
- John 15;
- Jude 20–24.

# 22. Sacramental privilege and false assurance

Because Paul deliberately uses baptism/food/drink language, the passage bears directly on sacramental presumption.

### What Paul is not saying

> “Baptism and sacred meal are meaningless externals.”

His analogy works because Israel’s privileges were **real**.

### What he is saying

> **sacramental/covenantal privilege cannot be converted into immunity from unbelief, idolatry and divine judgment.**

This is analogous to:

- Jeremiah 7 — temple-presumption;
- Micah 3 — “the LORD is among us”;
- Romans 2 — circumcision/law privilege;
- Romans 11 — grafting without grounds for boasting.

### Reformed Baptist use

Do not attack sacraments/ordinances themselves.

Attack the false conclusion:

> “because I was baptized / take Communion / belong to church, final unbelief cannot matter.”

# 23. Strong conditional-security reading

The strongest Wesleyan/Arminian argument is straightforward:

1. Paul includes himself in possible `ἀδόκιμος` status;
2. the prize is eternal life;
3. Israel received real covenant/sacramental privileges;
4. Christians are expressly warned by their destruction;
5. one who stands may fall;
6. therefore a genuine Christian can cease persevering and finally perish.

### Strengths

- fully respects warning force;
- takes 9:27 → 10:1 transition seriously;
- avoids trivializing Israel’s destruction;
- takes “stand / fall” personally.

### Reformed reply

The disputed inference is not whether the warning is real.

It is:

> **does a real warning to professing/believing church members require that an eternally elect/justified individual can finally fall out of election/justification?**

The answer need not be yes.

Paul elsewhere can simultaneously teach:

- God will confirm believers to the end because He is faithful (1 Cor 1:8–9);
- God gives the way to endure (10:13);
- believers must run, discipline themselves, flee idolatry and heed warnings.

The warning is one of the means in the faithful God’s preserving economy.

# 24. Free Grace / reward-only reading

A reward-focused reading observes:

- explicit athletic imagery;
- crown/prize language;
- Paul’s ministry context;
- 1 Cor 3:15 — a person may suffer loss yet be saved.

It concludes:

> `ἀδόκιμος` concerns loss of reward/usefulness, not eternal salvation.

### Strengths

- recognizes Paul’s reward theology;
- avoids conflict with eternal security;
- fits the ministry dimension of ch.9.

### Pressure against it

1. 10:1’s `γάρ` connects the issue to Israel’s catastrophic failure;
2. 10:5–10 goes far beyond reduced reward;
3. `ἀδόκιμος` is not established as a technical games-only term;
4. 10:11 says Israel is a warning **to us**;
5. 10:12 threatens falling;
6. Schreiner/Ciampa–Rosner read the stakes as eschatological rather than bonus-reward loss.

### Adjudication

Reward/ministry concerns may be included, but **reward-only** is `LESS LIKELY` as the exhaustive reading.

---

# 25. Relation to 1 Corinthians 3:15

Free Grace/reward readings rightly point to 3:15:

> one’s work may burn, the person suffer loss, yet be saved.

### But contexts differ

1 Cor 3:

- quality of ministry building;
- works tested by fire;
- builder saved despite loss.

1 Cor 9–10:

- self-discipline;
- possible `ἀδόκιμος`;
- Israel destroyed under idolatry/sexual sin/testing/grumbling;
- warning to flee idolatry.

Therefore 3:15 proves Paul can distinguish **loss of reward from loss of salvation**.

It does not prove every later disqualification/fall belongs to the reward-only category.

---

# 26. Relation to 1 Corinthians 1:8–9 — preservation promise inside the same letter

Paul began the letter saying Christ will:

- sustain/confirm the Corinthians to the end;
- so they will be blameless in the day of Christ;
- because **God is faithful**.

Then 10:13 again:

> **God is faithful**.

### This repetition matters

The same letter contains:

- strong final-preservation language;
- strong warnings;
- calls to self-examination and discipline;
- threats against idolatry.

The text itself therefore demands a theology capable of holding both.

### Preferred synthesis

> **The faithful God who promises to confirm His people to the end is the same God who warns them through Israel, disciplines their desires, provides escape from temptation and commands them to flee idolatry.**

No contradiction is required.

---

# 27. Relation to 1 Cor 11:27–32 — discipline can include physical death without final condemnation

Later in the same letter Paul says:

- some are weak/sick;
- some have died (“sleep”);
- divine judgment/discipline occurs;
- purpose: `ἵνα μὴ σὺν τῷ κόσμῳ κατακριθῶμεν` — so that we may not be condemned with the world.

### Critical distinction

This proves that:

> **physical death under divine discipline does not automatically equal eternal damnation.**

Therefore in 10:5–10 we should not assign the eternal state of every killed Israelite merely from physical judgment.

### But this does not weaken 9:27–10:12

Paul uses the wilderness deaths precisely as **warnings against the path leading toward divine rejection**.

The application is eschatologically serious even if the eternal fate of each wilderness individual is not narrated.

---

# 28. `πειρασμός` in 10:13 — test and temptation

MacArthur correctly notes that the word can denote:

- trial/test;
- temptation/enticement,

depending on response/context.

Primary:
https://shop.gty.org/library/bibleqnas-library/QA0300/is-a-temptation-also-a-trial

### Immediate context

The temptations include concrete moral-religious compromise:

- idolatry;
- sexual immorality;
- testing the Lord;
- grumbling.

Thus the verse is particularly about **tests that can become occasions for sin**.

### Pastoral misuse to avoid

Do not tell a traumatized/sick/grieving person:

> “God promises every life burden will always stay within your natural psychological strength.”

Paul himself elsewhere describes burdens beyond his own strength (2 Cor 1:8–9).

The promise is that God faithfully supplies what is needed for **faithful endurance rather than sinful capitulation**.

---

# 29. `ἔκβασις` and `ὑπενεγκεῖν` — escape may be endurance, not instant removal

The phrase is striking:

> God gives the way out **so that you may endure**.

Fee notes the apparent paradox:

- “way out” sounds like removal;
- “endure” sounds like remaining under the burden.

The resolution is that God can supply:

- an actual exit from the sinful occasion;
- or the divinely appointed end/outcome while He gives power to endure until that end.

Fee locator around p.461:
https://wearecentral.org/mt-content/uploads/2020/05/nov-3-2013.pdf

ICC likewise emphasizes that temptation and the corresponding `ἔκβασις` are paired under God’s faithful providence.

Source:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/icc/1_corinthians/10.htm

### Practical application

The “escape” may require exactly what v.14 says:

> **flee**.

Divine provision does not validate staying unnecessarily close to idolatrous/sexual temptation.

# 30. 10:14 — the intended response to God’s faithfulness is action

`Διόπερ, ἀγαπητοί μου, φεύγετε ἀπὸ τῆς εἰδωλολατρίας.`

Therefore, beloved, flee idolatry.

This is the practical conclusion of vv.1–13.

### No antinomian use of sovereignty

Bad conclusion:

> “God is faithful, therefore I may stay at the idol feast and trust Him to keep me.”

Paul’s conclusion:

> “God is faithful, therefore you are not trapped — **flee**.”

This is one of the clearest biblical examples of preservation through active means.

# 32. Strong pastoral synthesis

## 32.1. Past usefulness does not guarantee future faithfulness

Paul preached to others and still subjected himself to warning.

## 32.2. Extraordinary privilege can coexist with disastrous unbelief

Israel had cloud, sea, baptismal typology, spiritual food/drink and Christological privilege.

## 32.3. The strongest person should not presume

The warning is addressed to the one who thinks he stands.

## 32.4. Fear is not despair

Immediately after “take heed” comes:

> God is faithful.

## 32.5. Assurance is God-centered, not self-confidence

Not:

> “I am too mature to fall.”

But:

> “God is faithful; therefore I heed His warning, use His means, flee idolatry and trust His provided escape.”

## 32.6. Perseverance is active dependence

Paul runs.

Paul disciplines his body.

Christians flee.

God supplies endurance.

These are not rivals.

# 36. P1-6 closure verdict

**P1-6 1 Corinthians 9:27–10:13 is architecturally CLOSED.**

Reader-facing synthesis:

> **Павел не считает собственное апостольство страховкой от необходимости бежать до конца. Он дисциплинирует себя, чтобы, проповедав другим, самому не оказаться не выдержавшим Божьего испытания; и сразу объясняет это примером Израиля: все получили необычайные заветные привилегии, но большинство пало под судом. Поэтому “дисквалификацию” трудно свести лишь к потере дополнительной награды или служебной должности. Однако Павел не ведёт верующего к отчаянию: “кто думает, что стоит, берегись падения” немедленно уравновешивается “Бог верен”. Он ограничивает испытание и даёт путь, на котором можно выдержать его. Именно поэтому следующий вывод — не “расслабьтесь, Бог всё сделает независимо от вас”, а “бегите от идолопоклонства”. В реформатской модели это один из наиболее ясных текстов о сохранении через средства: реальное предупреждение, реальная необходимость стойкости, активное бегство от греха и реальная Божья верность не конкурируют, а действуют вместе.**
