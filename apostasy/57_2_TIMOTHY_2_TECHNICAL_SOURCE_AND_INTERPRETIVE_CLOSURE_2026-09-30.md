# 2. Greek text: what is explicit

2 Tim. 2:11–13 contains four conditional lines:

1. `εἰ γὰρ συναπεθάνομεν, καὶ συζήσομεν`
2. `εἰ ὑπομένομεν, καὶ συμβασιλεύσομεν`
3. `εἰ ἀρνησόμεθα, κἀκεῖνος ἀρνήσεται ἡμᾶς`
4. `εἰ ἀπιστοῦμεν, ἐκεῖνος πιστὸς μένει· ἀρνήσασθαι γὰρ ἑαυτὸν οὐ δύναται`

Mounce Reverse Interlinear:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+2%3A11-13&version=MOUNCE

Secure observations:

- the first two lines are positive;
- the third is unmistakably warning-shaped;
- the fourth contrasts human `ἀπιστοῦμεν` with Christ’s `πιστὸς μένει`;
- the grounding clause is Christ’s inability to deny Himself.

### What grammar alone does not settle

The grammar does not by itself decide whether `ἀπιστοῦμεν` in v.13 means:

- temporary faithlessness/weakness of believers;
- unbelief/apostasy;
- a deliberately broad category including both.

Nor does `πιστός` by itself mean:

- “faithful only to save”; or
- “faithful only to judge.”

Those conclusions require context and broader Pauline/canonical theology.

# 3. The unit is an exhortation to perseverance

William D. Mounce’s Pastoral Epistles teaching places 2 Tim. 2:1–13 inside Paul’s personal appeal to Timothy to persevere through hardship.

Accessible Mounce lecture:
https://www.biblicaltraining.org/library/2-timothy-2/pastoral-epistles

The sequence of soldier / athlete / farmer / Paul himself / trustworthy saying is therefore not detached dogmatics. It presses Timothy toward enduring gospel ministry.

A secondary scholarly source cites Mounce, *Pastoral Epistles*, WBC 46, p. 501, for the observation that the rhythmic material need not be proven pre-Pauline; it fits Paul’s context directly.

Locator witness:
- William D. Mounce, *Pastoral Epistles*, WBC 46, p. 501.

### Publication rule

Do not write as certain:

> “Paul quotes a known early Christian hymn.”

Safer:

> “The lines are rhythmic/poetic and are often treated as hymnic or confessional; whether Paul composed or adapted pre-existing material is disputed.”

Status: **GREEN**.

# 4. Verse 12 cannot be neutralized

`εἰ ἀρνησόμεθα, κἀκεῖνος ἀρνήσεται ἡμᾶς`

The Jesus-tradition parallel is strong:

- Matt. 10:32–33;
- Luke 12:8–9.

The warning is not naturally reduced to:

> “we merely lose a secondary reward while final rejection of Christ is spiritually harmless.”

MacArthur’s detailed exposition correctly keeps the warning severe and distinguishes settled/final denial from Peter’s temporary denial under fear.

GTY:
https://www.gty.org/resources/study-guides/chapters/55-8/motives-for-sacrificial-ministry-part-2

### Canonical guardrail: Peter

The verb family `ἀρνέομαι` cannot be turned into a one-word terminal-state test.

Peter:

- truly denied Jesus;
- did so repeatedly;
- was nevertheless prayed for, broken, restored and recommissioned.

Thus the theological distinction must be trajectory-based:

- grievous temporary denial with restoration;
- settled/final repudiation.

Status: **GREEN**.

# 5. Verse 13 — two serious exegetical readings

## A. Warning-continuation reading

On this reading:

> if humans prove unbelieving/faithless, Christ remains true to Himself and therefore does not retract His warning.

Strengths:

- follows immediately after v.12;
- `ἀρνέομαι` recurs in the grounding clause (“cannot deny Himself”);
- protects the warning from being canceled.

Calvin strongly stresses this side.

MacArthur’s detailed 2 Timothy exposition likewise emphasizes Christ’s self-consistency, including judgment of final repudiation.

### Risk

If stated as:

> “Christ’s faithfulness here means nothing except punitive faithfulness,”

it becomes narrower than Paul’s normal positive faithfulness language.

---

## B. Reassurance reading

On this reading:

> even amid believers’ weakness and the visible faithlessness of some, Christ remains faithful because faithfulness belongs to His character.

Gordon D. Fee’s treatment is commonly cited on this side. A commentary with exact Fee references cites:

- Fee, *1 and 2 Timothy, Titus* (1988), pp. 251–252;

and summarizes his interpretation as rooting salvation in God/Christ’s faithful character and functioning as assurance to Timothy and suffering believers.

Important: this source is used here to establish **Fee’s locator and interpretive position**, not as project doctrinal authority.

### Strengths

- Paul elsewhere regularly uses divine faithfulness positively:
  - Rom. 3:3–4;
  - 1 Cor. 1:9;
  - 1 Cor. 10:13;
  - 1 Thess. 5:24;
  - 2 Thess. 3:3;
- the letter itself contains abandonment/scandal but also strong divine keeping language;
- Peter provides canonical evidence that real saints can be temporarily faithless without final repudiation.

### Risk

If expanded into:

> “final apostasy has no bearing because Christ must save a person regardless,”

it contradicts v.12 rather than interpreting v.13.

# 6. Best project adjudication: distinguish exegesis from synthesis

## Direct exegetical claim

The safest direct claim is:

> **Christ remains faithful because He cannot contradict/deny His own identity.**

That is exactly what the grounding clause says.

## Canonical/systematic Reformed synthesis

From this plus the broader canon, the project can then say:

> **Christ’s immutable faithfulness guarantees both the reliability of His saving promises and the truth of His warnings. He preserves His true people through real means, while final settled repudiation manifests a profession that does not persevere.**

But this sentence must be labeled what it is:

`CANONICAL / SYSTEMATIC SYNTHESIS`

not:

`LEXICAL DEFINITION OF πιστός IN 2 TIM 2:13`.

This distinction materially improves precision.

---

# 7. Why “both promise and warning” is not a cheap compromise

The synthesis is not:

> “two readings disagree, so say both.”

Its logic is:

1. v.12 explicitly contains warning;
2. v.13 explicitly grounds Christ’s faithfulness in His self-consistency;
3. Paul elsewhere uses divine faithfulness positively;
4. the same chapter immediately places real doctrinal damage beside an unshaken divine foundation;
5. the same chapter commands holiness and hopeful correction.

Therefore Christ’s self-consistency cannot honestly be invoked either:

- to erase the warning;
- or to erase His saving fidelity.

The precise semantic emphasis of v.13 remains debated, but the canonical theology is not unstable.

# 8. 2:17–19 — real damage, then `μέντοι`

Hymenaeus and Philetus:

- deviate concerning truth;
- teach resurrection has already happened;
- overturn/upset the faith of some.

Then:

`ὁ μέντοι στερεὸς θεμέλιος τοῦ θεοῦ ἕστηκεν`

> nevertheless God’s firm foundation stands.

Mounce:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+2%3A19&version=MOUNCE

### Importance of `μέντοι`

Paul does not deny visible damage.

The logic is not:

> “no one’s faith was really harmed.”

It is:

> **real doctrinal devastation does not overthrow God’s foundation.**

This is precisely the form needed for a serious perseverance doctrine: preservation does not require pretending warnings, heresy and falls are unreal.

Status: **GREEN**.

# 9. Knight NIGTC and the Numbers 16 background

A scholarly source cites George W. Knight III, *The Pastoral Epistles*, NIGTC, pp. 233–234, on the OT background of 2 Tim. 2:19.

This strengthens the existing dossier’s conclusion:

- first inscription, “The Lord knows those who are His,” is tied directly to Num. 16:5 / Korah;
- the second inscription likely continues the separation-from-rebels setting (cf. Num. 16:26) and may also resonate with Isaianic departure-from-uncleanness language.

### Why Korah matters

Numbers 16 asks, amid visible covenant rebellion:

> who actually belongs to / is accepted by YHWH?

The answer begins with God’s discriminating knowledge:

> YHWH knows His own.

Paul invokes this after named false teachers have upset the faith of some.

This is not abstract election language dropped from nowhere. It is **election/preservation language inside an apostasy crisis**.

Locator:
- George W. Knight III, *The Pastoral Epistles*, NIGTC, pp. 233–234.

Status: **GREEN locator; direct-edition wording must be checked before verbatim quotation**.

# 10. The seal has divine and ethical inscriptions

First:

> The Lord knows those who are His.

Second:

> Let everyone naming the Lord’s name depart from unrighteousness.

This is a compact anti-false-dilemma text.

Paul does not choose between:

- divine preservation;
- human holiness.

He places both on one seal.

### Important lexical irony

`ἀφίστημι`, often encountered in apostasy discussions, is commanded positively here:

> depart **from unrighteousness**.

Again:

> movement verb + object/direction = meaning.

No doctrine may be built from “depart” alone.

Status: **GREEN**.

# 12. 2:24–26 — opponents can still be recoverable

Paul commands:

- gentleness;
- teaching;
- patient correction;
- hope that God may grant repentance;
- knowledge of truth;
- recovery of sobriety;
- escape from devil’s snare.

Text:
https://www.esv.org/verses/2%2BTimothy%2B2%3A24%E2%80%9326/

This passage materially limits how the project may label present doctrinal opponents.

They may be:

- deeply wrong;
- captured in devil’s snare;
- resisting truth;

and still be objects of correction precisely because **God may grant repentance**.

### Therefore

Do not infer:

> “serious false teaching = terminal apostasy in every present case.”

Status: **GREEN**.

# 15. Competing views — stronger formulation

## Reformed perseverance

Best form:

- warnings are real means;
- final denial is incompatible with persevering saving faith;
- true believers can grievously fail and be restored;
- divine foundation/knowledge of God’s own remains firm;
- holiness is necessary fruit/path, not meritorious cause.

## Arminian / conditional security

Strongest textual argument:

- the warning is addressed within a Christian paraenetic context;
- “if we deny Him, He will deny us” is a genuine conditional threat;
- it should not be weakened by importing a prior conclusion that regenerate apostasy is impossible.

The Reformed response must not be “the warning is hypothetical theater.”

It should be:

> the warning is one of God’s appointed means through which the elect persevere; final apostasy manifests non-persevering profession.

## Free Grace / reward-loss

Strongest argument attempts to associate endurance/reigning with reward and distinguish denial from loss of eternal life.

Challenge:

- Jesus-tradition behind “deny / be denied” is severe;
- immediate sequence does not naturally signal merely secondary reward at the denial line.

Represent fairly; do not caricature.

# 18. Publication-safe synthesis

> **Во 2 Тим. 2:11–13 Павел одновременно обещает и предупреждает: терпящие будут царствовать со Христом, а отрекающихся Христос отречётся. Поэтому ст. 13 — “если мы неверны, Он пребывает верен, ибо Себя отречься не может” — нельзя превращать в индульгенцию окончательному отречению. Но и сводить Божью верность только к наказанию грамматика не заставляет. Прямой смысл последней строки проще и глубже: Христос остаётся верен Самому Себе. Уже на уровне всей канонической картины из этого следует, что Он не нарушает ни Своих спасительных обещаний, ни Своих предупреждений. Это именно канонический реформатский синтез, а не словарное значение `πιστός`. Тот же баланс Павел показывает через несколько стихов: ложные учители реально “ниспровергают веру некоторых”, однако “твёрдое основание Божие стоит”; печать одновременно говорит “познал Господь Своих” и “да отступит от неправды всякий, исповедующий имя Господа”. Божье сохранение и человеческая святость не конкурируют. И даже тяжело заблуждающихся Павел не велит заранее объявлять безнадёжными: их нужно исправлять кротко — в надежде, что Бог даст им покаяние и освобождение из сети дьявола.**
