## 1. Зачем нужен этот файл

Пуританский материал в проекте должен выполнять **определённую функцию**, а не быть ornamentation.

Он особенно силён в вопросах:

- temporary / counterfeit religious experience;
- common operations of the Spirit;
- false peace and presumption;
- first spiritual decays;
- mechanisms of temptation;
- hardening through repeated sin;
- grievous fall without automatic final apostasy;
- despair after sin;
- restoration of weak/backsliding believers;
- perseverance through watchfulness, prayer, mortification and appointed means.

Но Puritan theology не должна подменять:

- Hebrew lexicon;
- Greek syntax;
- textual criticism;
- immediate literary context;
- modern historical questions.

### Rule

> **Scripture establishes the exegetical claim; Puritan practical theology traces the anatomy of the soul under that truth.**

# 3. John Owen — the central Puritan source on apostasy

## Work

**John Owen, *The Nature and Causes of Apostasy from the Gospel*.**

Primary TOC/text:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.toc.html

This should be treated as the **backbone Puritan source** for the project, because Owen does not merely mention apostasy incidentally; the entire treatise analyzes:

- nature of apostasy;
- doctrinal defection;
- ignorance;
- pride;
- negligence;
- love of world;
- Satan;
- judicial judgments;
- decay of holiness;
- profaneness;
- corrupt worship;
- means of preservation.

### Exact chapter map

1. Ch. I — Hebrews 6:4–6 and nature of apostasy.
2. Ch. III — apostasy from gospel doctrine.
3. Ch. IV — spiritual enmity as cause.
4. Ch. V — darkness/ignorance.
5. Ch. VI — pride, sloth, love of world, Satan, divine judgments.
6. Ch. VIII — gradual apostasy from gospel holiness.
7. Ch. IX — apostasy into profaneness/sensuality; defects in teachers.
8. Ch. X — other causes of decay of holiness.
9. Ch. XI — apostasy from evangelical worship.
10. Ch. XII — danger under prevailing apostasy.
11. Ch. XIII — directions for preservation.

Primary TOC:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.toc.html

---

## 3.1. Owen: common operations ≠ saving inhabitation

### Primary locator

Ch. I:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.v.html

Owen explicitly distinguishes:

- purely natural doctrinal knowledge;
- genuine illumination wrought by the Holy Spirit;
- saving/sanctifying illumination.

He says the Hebrews 6 illumination is **real**, genuinely connected with operations of the Spirit, capable of:

- impressing conscience;
- producing abstinence from sins;
- producing duties;
- giving joy/delight in gospel things;

while still falling short of transforming saving light.

He likewise distinguishes participation in the Spirit’s **operations/gifts** from His gracious **inhabitation**.

### Claim supported

> **A non-regenerate professor may experience real supernatural Spirit-wrought privileges and effects which must not be dismissed as “nothing happened,” yet these effects need not equal saving union/regeneration.**

### Project relevance

- Heb. 6;
- Luke 8:13 temporary faith;
- 2 Pet. 2;
- Saul functional Spirit language by analogy only, not identical category;
- common operations vs saving grace.

### Limitation

Owen’s reading of Heb. 6 is a major Reformed interpretation, not a lexical trump card. Modern technical commentaries and competing readings remain separately represented.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 3.2. Owen: temporary joy and reform can be real

Same Ch. I:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.v.html

Owen discusses “tasting” the word and explicitly connects it to:

- delight in hearing;
- real joy;
- stony-ground hearers;
- temporary/evanescent joy;
- change/reformation of life;
- conviction/humiliation.

### Claim supported

> **Temporary faith/religious response should not be rewritten as conscious fraud. There may be joy, conviction, reform and genuine religious experience without saving root.**

This is particularly useful for Luke 8:13.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 3.3. Owen: apostasy is often gradual

Ch. VIII–X:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.xii.html
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.xiv.html

Owen emphasizes:

- deceitfulness of sin;
- gradual decay of holiness;
- worldliness;
- neglect of watchfulness;
- respectable labels masking spiritual decline;
- institutional/church identity being used to shelter ungodliness.

Particularly valuable is Ch. X on false appropriation of names/titles, where he uses Jeremiah’s “temple of the LORD” as a paradigm of people hardening themselves through outward religious privilege.

### Claim supported

> **Apostasy commonly develops through ordinary decay, false security and normalized sin before it appears as explicit doctrinal repudiation.**

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 3.4. Owen: preservation uses appointed means

Ch. XII–XIII:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.xii.html
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.xvii.html

Owen insists that preservation requires:

- continual warfare;
- diligence;
- watchfulness;
- perseverance;
- keeping the heart;
- gospel duties;
- exercise of gospel graces;
- dependence on divine help.

### Claim supported

> **Perseverance is not passive inevitability. Reformed preservation theology expects God to preserve His people through real warnings, watchfulness, mortification, prayer and obedience.**

This supports the project’s Hebrews/Jude synthesis without making means meritorious causes of justification.

Status: `P1/P3`.

# 4. Thomas Watson — false peace, presumption, mercy abused

## 4.1. False peace

**Watson, *A Body of Divinity*, false peace section.**

Primary text:
https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity/divinity.ix.vii.html

Watson distinguishes genuine peace from a counterfeit peace characterized by:

- strong confidence without sound ground;
- presumptuous appeal to mercy;
- separation of peace from holiness;
- Deut. 29:19 logic: “I shall have peace” while walking after one’s own heart.

### Claim supported

> **Assurance and presumption are not distinguished by strength of confidence but by the object, warrant and moral relation of that confidence to God’s word.**

Project nodes:

- Deut. 29;
- Jer. 7;
- Mic. 3:11;
- Matt. 7;
- Rev. 3:17.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 4.2. Mercy abused into permission to sin

Watson warns against using divine mercy as license for continued sin.

Primary text:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity.vi.ix.html

### Claim supported

> **Presumption does not trust mercy too much; it falsifies mercy by separating pardon from God’s holiness and transforming grace into protection for rebellion.**

This is highly useful for the false-assurance matrix.

Status: `P1/P3`.

# 5. Thomas Brooks — Satanic mechanisms of moral and doctrinal drift

## Work

**Thomas Brooks, *Precious Remedies Against Satan’s Devices*.**

Full historical-edition metadata:
https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24994938M/Precious_remedies_against_satan%27s_devices

Readable public text / chapter map:
https://www.gracegems.org/Brooks/precious_remedies_against_satan.htm

---

## 5.1. “Present the bait and hide the hook”

Primary/public section:
https://www.gracegems.org/Brooks/precious_remedies_against_satan02.htm

Mechanism:

- foreground pleasure/profit;
- conceal shame, wrath and consequence.

### Claim supported

> **Temptation frequently works by selective disclosure: the immediate perceived good is magnified while the covenantal and moral cost is hidden.**

Canonical controls:

- Gen. 3;
- Achan;
- David;
- money/covetousness cases.

Status: `P2/P3`.

---

## 5.2. Paint vice with virtue’s colors

Same chapter:
https://www.gracegems.org/Brooks/precious_remedies_against_satan02.htm

### Claim supported

> **Sin becomes more dangerous when morally relabeled: cowardice becomes prudence, compromise becomes peace, greed becomes stewardship, self-preservation becomes wisdom.**

This directly supports the project’s “masks” taxonomy.

Status: `P2/P3`.

---

## 5.3. Minimize “small” sins → progressive encroachment

Same chapter, Device 3.

Brooks explicitly argues that yielding to lesser sin makes way for greater and calls sin “encroaching,” advancing step by step.

### Claim supported

> **Progressive hardening often begins not with explicit apostasy but with tolerated lesser disobedience whose significance is rationalized.**

Project controls:

- Saul;
- Solomon;
- repeated-warning matrix;
- James 1 desire → sin → death.

Status: `P2/P3`.

---

## 5.4. Do not use the falls of saints to normalize sin

Brooks’ Device 4 contrasts:

- saints’ recorded sins;
- their sorrow, repentance and recovery.

### Claim supported

> **David/Peter cannot be used as proof that grievous sin is harmless; their narratives include repentance, discipline and restoration.**

This helps hold together grievous fall and perseverance.

Status: `P2/P3`.

---

## 5.5. Satan also drives sinners away from Christ through despair

Brooks, later device-set on keeping souls from believing in Christ:
https://www.gracegems.org/Brooks/precious_remedies_against_satano9.htm

He discusses the strategy of magnifying the greatness/vileness of sin so that a sinner concludes mercy in Christ is unavailable.

### Claim supported

> **The opposite of presumption is not despair. Satan can exploit sin both by minimizing it before commission and magnifying it into hopelessness afterward.**

Canonical controls:

- Cain/Judas despair trajectories;
- Manasseh as restoration counterexample;
- 2 Cor. 7 godly grief vs worldly grief.

Status: `P2/P3`.

# 6. John Flavel — heart keeping, first declensions, recurrent sin, restoration

## Work

**John Flavel, *A Saint Indeed / Keeping the Heart*.**

Primary CCEL TOC:
https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed.iii.html

---

## 6.1. First declensions matter

Flavel says practical theology should ask:

- how to distinguish special from common operations of the Spirit;
- how to discern first declinings from God;
- how a backsliding Christian may recover first love;
- how bosom sin may be discovered and mortified.

Primary text:
https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed/saintindeed.v.iv.i.html

### Claim supported

> **Spiritual decay should be detected before it becomes public collapse.**

This is unusually well matched to the project’s trajectory model.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 6.2. Temptation gains strength if not resisted early

“Ninth Season — How a Christian may keep his heart from yielding to sin”:
https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed.v.iii.ix.html

Flavel emphasizes early resistance and the vulnerability of an unguarded heart.

### Claim supported

> **Watchfulness belongs upstream of catastrophic sin; it is easier to arrest a trajectory at its first motions than after habit strengthens it.**

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 6.3. Breach of peace ≠ breach of covenant

Flavel’s “dark and doubting seasons” section:
https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed/saintindeed.v.iii.x.html

He explicitly distinguishes:

- loss/breach of peace with God;
- breach of covenant/relation with God.

He then addresses recurrent falling into the same sin and warns against instantly concluding that all prior repentance was hypocrisy.

### Claim supported

> **A believer’s grievous fall, loss of comfort or repeated struggle must not automatically be classified as final apostasy.**

This belongs with:

- David;
- Peter;
- Gal. 6;
- James 5;
- Jude 22–23.

### Limitation

Flavel is giving pastoral-theological categories; each biblical case still requires its own exegesis.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 6.4. Repeated sin can harden into custom

Same section gives a progression attributed through Bernard:

- grievous sin initially feels intolerable;
- then heavy;
- then light;
- conscience strikes faintly;
- insensibility grows;
- bitterness becomes pleasure;
- custom begins to defend itself.

### Claim supported

> **Repeated sin can alter moral perception and conscience sensitivity — a practical anatomy of hardening.**

Project controls:

- Prov. 29:1;
- Amos 4;
- Zech. 7;
- Heb. 3;
- Revelation refusal-to-repent texts.

Status: `P1/P3`.

# 7. Richard Sibbes — weakness, bruising, recovery, anti-despair

## Work

**Richard Sibbes, *The Bruised Reed and Smoking Flax*.**

Primary/public complete text:
https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/bruisedreed.html

TOC / edition access:
https://www.monergism.com/bruised-reed-and-smoking-flax-ebook

---

## 7.1. Weak grace must not be mistaken for absent grace

Sibbes’ chapters repeatedly distinguish:

- bruised reed;
- smoking flax;
- little grace;
- grace mixed with corruption;
- weak beginnings that Christ does not quench.

Key chapters:

- Ch. V — Grace is little at first;
- Ch. VI — Grace mingled with corruption;
- Ch. VII — Christ will not quench small and weak beginnings;
- Ch. XV — infirmities, discouragement and recovery of peace;
- Ch. XXII–XXVIII — Christ’s government and final victory.

### Claim supported

> **Spiritual weakness, bruising and remaining corruption are not themselves evidence of final apostasy.**

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 7.2. The answer to wounded conscience is Christ, not diagnostic cruelty

Sibbes directs the bruised person toward Christ rather than encouraging premature self-condemnation.

### Claim supported

> **Pastoral handling must distinguish a trembling, wounded believer from an obstinate apostate.**

This strongly supports the project’s concern not to treat every wanderer as Hebrews-6 terminal.

Status: `P1/P3`.

# 8. Stephen Charnock — practical atheism, conviction, resistance, regeneration

## 8.1. Practical atheism

**Charnock, *A Discourse on Practical Atheism*.**

Primary/public text:
https://www.monergism.com/discourse-practical-atheism

Charnock’s central category is that a person may retain orthodox God-language while living as though God’s authority did not govern him.

### Claim supported

> **Practical denial can coexist with verbal profession. Actions may disclose a functional theology contrary to stated orthodoxy.**

Project controls:

- Titus 1:16 — profess God / deny by works;
- Micah 3:11;
- false assurance;
- Jeroboam’s self-interest restructuring worship.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 8.2. Mere conviction can harden rather than regenerate

**Charnock, *A Discourse on Conviction of Sin*.**

Primary/public text:
https://www.monergism.com/discourse-conviction-sin

Charnock distinguishes legal conviction from evangelical transformation and observes that terror/conviction by itself may leave hardness intact or even leave a person harder after the impression passes.

### Claim supported

> **Strong conviction, fear and temporary reform are not identical with regeneration. If resisted without gospel transformation, they may coexist with later hardening.**

Canonical controls:

- Pharaoh;
- wilderness generation;
- Saul’s moments of confession;
- temporary faith;
- repeated warning → hardening.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 8.3. Regeneration is a change of nature, not mere taming

Charnock, *Discourse of the Efficient of Regeneration*:
https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/efficient_regeneration/efficient_regeneration.iii.html

He distinguishes:

- external/tamed behavioral change;
- divine transformation of nature.

### Claim supported

> **External moral reform must not be equated with saving regeneration.**

This helps sharpen Luke 11:24–26 and temporary-faith discussions.

Status: `P1/P3`.

# 9. Thomas Manton — perseverance, causes of apostasy, warnings as real means

Manton is particularly valuable because he gives practical warnings with high seriousness **inside a Reformed perseverance framework**, showing that severe exhortation need not imply denial of preservation.

---

## 9.1. Psalm 119:31 sermon — “I have stuck unto thy testimonies”

Manton, Works vol. VI, Sermon XXXIII on Ps. 119:31:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/manton/manton06.xxxv.html

He argues:

- right choice must be followed by perseverance;
- former zeal does not guarantee final steadfastness;
- beginnings are not sure prognostications of harvest;
- falling after tasting/profession is especially grave;
- believers should stand far from apostasy;
- perseverance requires guarding against errors, persecution, scandals and internal causes.

### Status

`P1/P3`.

---

## 9.2. Manton’s practical causes of falling away

Same sermon identifies unusually precise mechanisms:

1. **ungrounded assent** — lightly chosen convictions are lightly altered;
2. **want of solid rooting in grace** — superficial taste does not establish;
3. **unmortified lusts** — doctrine may be bent to protect desire;
4. **faulty easiness / people-pleasing**;
5. **self-confidence**;
6. **itch of novelty**;
7. neglect of **first decays**.

### Claim supported

> **Apostasy is often preceded by identifiable vulnerabilities long before public repudiation.**

This map is unusually compatible with the project’s cross-canonical mechanisms matrix.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 9.3. Manton on Hebrews 6 — preserve as a historical Reformed nuance, not final technical arbiter

In the same sermon Manton uses Hebrews 6 very severely and explicitly says the warning is proposed to believers so that they keep far from the judgment described.

He also distinguishes:

- loose/general professors;
- persons with taste/illumination/strict profession;
- deliberate apostasy;
- temporary lapses under temptation.

### Project use

This is valuable for **warning-as-means** and the distinction between grievous lapse and deliberate apostasy.

### Limitation

Do not use Manton alone to settle the disputed exegesis of every Heb. 6 phrase. Existing technical dossiers remain authority for the text.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 9.4. Receive truth “in the love of it”

Manton, Works vol. VI, another sermon:
https://ccel.org/ccel/manton/manton06/manton06.xiv.html

He distinguishes:

- receiving truth in intellectual evidence/light;
- receiving it in love so that it enters the heart and governs affection.

### Claim supported

> **Doctrinal cognition without affectional allegiance can be unstable under pressure from lust and interest.**

Canonical controls:

- 2 Thess. 2:10;
- 2 Tim. 4;
- Judas/false teachers;
- doctrinal apostasy.

Status: `P1/P3`.

# 10. Jonathan Edwards — not strictly an English Puritan, but indispensable Reformed heir for counterfeit experience

Historical label control:

> Edwards belongs to the eighteenth-century New England Reformed revival tradition and is often read as a Puritan heir; do not flatten him chronologically into the seventeenth-century English Puritan movement.

## Work

**Jonathan Edwards, *Religious Affections*.**

Primary TOC:
https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/affections/affections.toc.html

---

## 10.1. Powerful experiences are not infallible signs of saving grace

Part II lists phenomena that are **not certain signs** either way:

- very high religious affections;
- bodily effects;
- fluent religious talk;
- experiences not self-produced;
- Scripture texts coming powerfully to mind;
- appearance of love;
- many religious affections together;
- comforts following conviction;
- much religious activity;
- abundant praise;
- strong confidence that one is in a good state.

Primary section:
https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/affections/affections.iv.html

### Claim supported

> **Intensity, spontaneity and subjectively supernatural quality of an experience do not by themselves prove regeneration.**

This is a critical guardrail for:

- temporary faith;
- common operations;
- false assurance;
- charismatic/experiential over-reading.

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 10.2. Counterfeits can imitate many gracious affections

Part II, sign VII:
https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/affections/affections.iv.vii.html

Edwards explicitly uses biblical cases such as:

- Pharaoh;
- Saul;
- Ahab;
- wilderness Israel;
- Samaritans who “feared the Lord and served their own gods”;
- stony-ground hearers;
- Jehu;
- Balaam.

### Claim supported

> **A cluster of apparently spiritual emotions and behaviors may still be counterfeit; diagnosis must consider the whole enduring moral-spiritual pattern.**

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 10.3. False affections can harden

Edwards, Part III, sign concerning Christian tenderness:
https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/affections/affections.vi.viii.html

He argues that false affections can end in:

- dullness toward sin;
- less responsiveness to warnings;
- diminished conscience;
- greater carelessness.

### Claim supported

> **Religious excitement is not necessarily the opposite of hardening; counterfeit experience can itself become part of a hardening process.**

Status: `P1/P3`.

---

## 10.4. Edwards warns against certainty in judging other professors

Part III introduction:
https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/affections/affections.v.html

Edwards says Scripture gives enough rules for necessary pastoral/church judgment but does not give us infallible access to the hidden state of every neighboring professor.

### Claim supported

> **The church must make real judgments for safety/discipline without pretending omniscient certainty about every individual’s eternal state.**

This strongly supports the project’s final-state restraint.

Status: `P1/P3`.

# 12. Major synthesis gained from Puritan layer

The strongest combined practical trajectory now looks like this:

1. **Common light / conviction / joy** can be real without saving root — Owen, Edwards.
2. **False peace** converts privileges/promises into immunity — Watson, Owen.
3. **Temptation reframes sin** as pleasure, virtue or smallness — Brooks.
4. **First declensions** often occur quietly in the heart — Flavel, Manton.
5. **Unmortified desire and self-interest** seek doctrinal protection — Manton, Charnock.
6. **Repeated compromise** alters conscience and increases hardness — Flavel, Edwards.
7. **Public fall** may represent either recoverable grievous sin or deeper apostasy; do not diagnose from one episode — Flavel, Sibbes, Edwards.
8. **Despair** is not repentance; Satan may use post-sin hopelessness as another device — Brooks, Sibbes.
9. **Preservation** works through watchfulness, prayer, mortification, truth, church means and continued faith — Owen, Manton, Flavel.

This practical-theology layer strongly corroborates the project’s cross-canonical taxonomy without replacing exegesis.

# 13. Important disagreements / cautions inside the historical material

## 13.1. Do not pretend all Reformed divines phrase warning texts identically

Owen and Manton can emphasize different aspects in their handling of Hebrews 6.

That is useful, not embarrassing.

Historical Reformed theology should be represented as a family with substantial agreement on perseverance but some exegetical variation in explaining warning texts.

## 13.2. Do not force every “Puritan” into one chronology

- Owen, Watson, Brooks, Flavel, Sibbes, Charnock, Manton belong naturally to classic Puritan/Reformed practical-theology discussion.
- Edwards is later New England Reformed and should be labeled accurately.

## 13.3. Do not use practical analogies as lexical evidence

Examples:

- Brooks does not define Greek `παραπίπτω`;
- Watson does not define Hebrew `שׁוב`;
- Sibbes does not determine the referent of Heb. 10:29 `ἡγιάσθη`;
- Edwards does not settle textual variants.

Their role is downstream practical/systematic illumination.
