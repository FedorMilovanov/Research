# 3. What is now frozen across the whole series

## 3.1. Category discipline

The series must continue distinguishing:

1. ordinary sin;
2. grievous fall;
3. recoverable wandering;
4. temporary faith;
5. real covenant/religious privilege without proven saving union;
6. doctrinal defection;
7. practical denial;
8. corporate/institutional apostasy;
9. progressive hardening;
10. judicial hardening;
11. terminal/final repudiation where text warrants it;
12. restoration and divine preservation through appointed means.

No editor may flatten these to a single “saved then lost / never saved” binary before reading the relevant text.

---

## 3.2. Lexical discipline

Frozen rule:

> **No one word — “return,” “again,” “fall,” “deny,” “sanctified,” “bought,” “believe” — supplies a complete previous ordo salutis by itself.**

Original-language evidence matters, but context, syntax, canonical reuse and narrative argument govern the theological inference.

---

## 3.3. Final-state discipline

Frozen rule:

> **Narrator strength sets editorial strength.**

### High-confidence terminal

- Judas Iscariot;
- texts with explicit terminal/finality language as adjudicated in dedicated dossiers.

### Severe but prior-saving-status disputed

- Heb 6;
- Heb 10:29;
- 2 Pet 2;
- related hard texts.

### Severe trajectory but final eternal state not narrated

- Saul;
- Solomon;
- Demas;
- multiple historical cases.

No trusted theologian is allowed to upgrade an unstated final verdict.

---

## 3.4. Warning/preservation discipline

Frozen Reformed synthesis:

> **Warnings are real, prospective and spiritually consequential; divine preservation is real; Scripture can use the warnings themselves among the means by which God preserves His people.**

Primary controls include Heb 3, 1 Cor 10, Jude, 1 Pet 1:5, John 10, Rom 8.

Modern controls include Thomas Schreiner and Abner Chou, claim-by-claim.

No formulation may turn warnings into stage props or preservation promises into carnal-security slogans.

---

## 3.5. Recovery discipline

Frozen rule:

Where Scripture commands:

- restore;
- turn back;
- correct in hope of repentance;
- show mercy;
- snatch from fire;

the church must not prematurely replace those commands with a self-appointed final verdict.

Primary controls:

- Gal 6:1;
- James 5:19–20;
- 2 Tim 2:24–26;
- Jude 22–23.

# 9. Competing-view fairness

The series may argue firmly for a Reformed/Baptist synthesis.

Fairness requires that when a hard text is used explicitly to deny former saving status, serious alternatives are represented accurately where material:

- confessional Lutheran;
- classical/conservative Arminian;
- Reformed warning-as-means;
- Free Grace where genuinely distinct and relevant.

Fairness does not require false neutrality. The article may conclude that the Reformed synthesis best accounts for the whole canonical evidence, but must show the argument rather than caricature alternatives.

# 10. Direct-quote policy

The five reader drafts are intentionally quotation-free from modern commentators.

This remains the safest default.

Direct quotation may be added only when:

1. it materially improves the article;
2. exact work/edition/page/section is frozen;
3. wording is checked against the primary object;
4. copyright/quotation length is appropriate;
5. paraphrase would lose something significant.

Do not delay publication-candidate editing merely to accumulate impressive quotations.
