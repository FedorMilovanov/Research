## 1. Главный тезис

Саул — один из сильнейших библейских примеров **долгой траектории распада под привилегией, помазанием, Божьим Духом для царского служения, ясным словом, повторным пророческим обличением и реальными возможностями послушания**.

Но этот case особенно опасен для over-reading.

Нельзя ослаблять positive language начала его царствования:

- Бог даёт ему «другое сердце»;
- Дух Божий могущественно действует на нём;
- он пророчествует;
- он реально поставлен и помазан царём.

И нельзя ослаблять negative language конца:

- он «повернул назад от следования» YHWH;
- отверг слово YHWH;
- сам отвергнут как царь;
- Дух YHWH отступает от него;
- он преследует Давида;
- убивает священников;
- обращается к медиуму;
- Хронист говорит, что он умер за `מַעַל` — covenant unfaithfulness/breach of faith.

Но Scripture **не даёт простой фразы**:

> «Саул был возрождён, затем потерял возрождение и умер в аду».

И также не даёт простой фразы:

> «Саул несомненно был истинно возрождён и умер спасённым».

Поэтому safe classification:

> **Saul is an explicit case of covenant/royal unfaithfulness, rejection from kingship and judicial death after long resistance; his individual eternal state is not directly adjudicated in a way sufficient to make him a clean prooftext for either loss-of-regeneration or guaranteed salvation.**

# 2. Начало: нельзя делать Саула «никогда ничего не имевшим»

## 2.1. 1 Samuel 10:6 — «превратишься в иного человека»

Samuel говорит, что Spirit of YHWH rushes upon Saul, он пророчествует и будет «turned into another man».

Это strong transformation language.

Но immediate context — **царское призвание и prophetic sign**, не Pauline exposition of regeneration.

## 2.2. 1 Samuel 10:9 — Бог даёт «другое сердце»

`וַיַּהֲפָךְ־לוֹ אֱלֹהִים לֵב אַחֵר`

Literal force: Бог обратил/изменил для него другое сердце, дал ему другое сердце.

Современные переводы варьируют:

- gave him another heart;
- changed his heart;
- changed his inmost person.

### Anti-error

Нельзя переписать текст:

> «с Саулом внутри вообще ничего реального не произошло».

Произошло реальное Богом произведённое изменение, связанное с его новой ролью.

Но нельзя автоматически сделать обратное:

> `לֵב אַחֵר` = technical OT synonym of irreversible regeneration in the later new-covenant sense.

Для такого уравнения нужен дополнительный canonical argument.

MacArthur в разных exposition подчёркивает именно royal/functional context: изменение и Spirit-empowerment подготавливают Саула к царской роли и не должны автоматически приравниваться к NT indwelling-regeneration category.

---

# 3. Дух Божий на Сауле: real empowerment, but category must be controlled by context

## 3.1. 1 Samuel 10:10; 11:6

Дух Божий действительно приходит/устремляется на Саула:

- prophecy;
- empowerment;
- decisive leadership in battle.

Это не fiction и не merely external label.

## 3.2. Canonical OT pattern

В Judges/Samuel `רוּחַ יהוה` часто связан с empowerment for office/task:

- judges;
- Saul;
- David.

Поэтому presence of the Spirit в этих narratives нельзя без аргумента приравнять к full new-covenant pneumatology of permanent indwelling as seal of individual justification.

### Conservative control

GTY/MacArthur прямо предостерегает от такого переноса: coming/departing language у Саула/Давида связано прежде всего с divinely appointed kingship/function, а не является автоматическим commentary on personal salvation.

Это полезный guardrail, но не licence to deny all inward reality.

---

# 4. 1 Samuel 13 — первый royal judgment: dynasty, not yet full deposition

Saul presumptuously sacrifices/will not wait for Samuel.

Samuel announces:

- kingdom would have been established;
- now Saul’s kingdom/dynasty will not continue;
- YHWH has sought a man after His own heart.

### Important distinction

1 Sam 13 does not yet narrate Saul being instantaneously removed from office.

He continues to rule.

Judgment first falls on the **continuance of his dynasty**, while the larger trajectory develops.

This matters because later texts intensify the verdict.

---

# 5. 1 Samuel 15:11 — explicit directional apostasy language

YHWH says:

`כִּי־שָׁב מֵאַחֲרַי`

— «ибо он повернул назад / отступил от следования за Мною».

`שָׁב` = Qal perfect of `שׁוּב`.

Это один из strongest OT controls for the project’s return/back concordance.

### DIRECT EXEGESIS

Text actually says Saul **turned back from following YHWH** and failed to carry out His words.

### But not enough for full ordo salutis

Directional verb establishes movement in covenant obedience/allegiance.

It does not by itself establish:

- former regeneration;
- loss of justification;
- exact eternal state.

This is exactly why the project’s no-one-word-theology rule exists.

---

# 6. 1 Samuel 15:22–23 — religious self-justification exposed

Saul tries to frame retained spoil through sacrifice.

Samuel answers:

> obedience is better than sacrifice.

Then:

- rebellion is likened to divination;
- stubbornness/insubordination to iniquity and teraphim/idolatry;
- Saul **rejected** YHWH’s word;
- YHWH **rejected** Saul `מִמֶּלֶךְ` — from being king.

Hebrew repetition of `מָאַס` is rhetorically decisive:

`you rejected the word → He rejected you as king`.

### Crucial boundary

The object/domain is explicitly royal:

> `מִמֶּלֶךְ` — **from being king**.

Therefore this verse must not be silently rewritten:

> «He rejected you from eternal salvation.»

The text gives severe divine rejection, but its stated office/judgment referent is kingship.

---

# 7. Saul’s confession is real speech but narratively compromised

1 Sam 15:24:

> «I have sinned».

He acknowledges:

- transgression;
- fear of people;
- listening to their voice.

But then v30 asks:

> «honor me now before the elders of my people and before Israel».

### Narrative significance

The narrator does not let the reader equate verbal confession mechanically with David-like repentance.

Saul remains intensely concerned with public honour.

### Anti-error

Still do not say:

> «because his confession was imperfect, it was metaphysically impossible for him ever to repent.»

The narrative point is more modest and stronger:

> **his words of confession do not reverse the announced royal judgment, and his continuing concern for status exposes the mixed/compromised character of his response.**

---

# 8. 1 Samuel 16:14 — «Дух YHWH отступил от Саула»

Hebrew:

`וְרוּחַ יְהוָה סָרָה מֵעִם שָׁאוּל`

`סָרָה` — Qal perfect 3fs of `סוּר`, depart/turn aside/remove.

This is real departure-language.

## 8.1. Literary placement is decisive

Immediately before:

- David is anointed;
- Spirit of YHWH rushes upon David «from that day forward».

Immediately after:

- Spirit of YHWH departs from Saul;
- distressing/evil spirit from YHWH terrorizes him.

The transfer is therefore tightly connected to **transfer of royal divine empowerment/favour from Saul to David**.

## 8.2. What the verse proves

- Saul loses a real divine privilege/presence/empowerment associated with kingship;
- this loss marks judicial abandonment in his royal trajectory.

## 8.3. What the verse does not prove by itself

- that OT royal empowerment = NT sealing indwelling in every respect;
- that regeneration is here explicitly lost;
- that the narrator has issued an eternal-state verdict.

MacArthur explicitly makes this distinction in GTY material: the Spirit’s coming on Saul and departure in this context concerns his office and God’s rule through the king; MacArthur even states the text is not sufficient to decide whether Saul was a true believer.

That conservative restraint is useful for this corpus.

---

# 9. After rejection: a genuine downward trajectory, not one isolated mistake

The remainder of 1 Samuel prevents reduction of Saul’s case to one ceremonial error.

Trajectory includes:

1. jealousy of David;
2. repeated murderous attempts;
3. manipulative use of military danger;
4. massacre of Nob’s priests (1 Sam 22);
5. pursuit of David despite moments of emotional admission;
6. increasing fear;
7. loss of prophetic/priestly guidance;
8. resort to a medium in 1 Sam 28.

### Whole-project mechanism map

Saul illustrates:

`fear of people → religious rationalization → rejection of clear word → loss of office/favour → jealousy → violence → repeated resistance to providential restraint → desperation → forbidden spiritual alternative`.

This is one of the clearest narrative examples of progressive hardening.

---

# 10. Saul’s moments of apparent relenting do not equal stable repentance

In 1 Sam 24 and 26 Saul can:

- acknowledge David’s righteousness;
- confess wrong;
- weep;
- temporarily cease pursuit.

Yet the pattern resumes.

This is extremely important practical theology.

### Distinction

`emotion / admission / temporary behavioural retreat` ≠ necessarily `deep durable turning to YHWH`.

But again:

- Scripture does not give us a mechanical checklist by which every imperfect repentant person is declared false;
- the judgment arises from Saul’s **repeated narrated trajectory**, not from one tear or one sentence being insufficiently eloquent.

---

# 11. 1 Samuel 28 — oracle silence and the medium of Endor

## 11.1. Saul really does ask YHWH

1 Sam 28:6 uses:

`וַיִּשְׁאַל שָׁאוּל בַּיהוָה`

— Saul inquired/asked of YHWH.

YHWH does not answer by:

- dreams;
- Urim;
- prophets.

The project must not falsify this verse merely because 1 Chr 10:14 later says he «did not inquire».

## 11.2. He then turns to what Torah prohibited

Saul seeks a `בַּעֲלַת־אוֹב`, a medium.

This is especially dark because 1 Sam 15:23 had already likened rebellion to divination.

What was previously **prophetic analogy** becomes actual occult consultation.

This is a powerful narrative closure:

`rebellion is like divination → persistent rebellion → literal consultation of forbidden medium`.

---

# 12. 1 Samuel 28:19 — «tomorrow you and your sons will be with me»

This verse must not be used lazily either way.

Samuel announces Saul’s death and the army’s defeat.

`you and your sons will be with me` can naturally refer to joining Samuel in **the condition/realm of the dead**.

Keil & Delitzsch read it as Sheol, the realm of the dead.

Jonathan’s inclusion is a major guardrail against equating the phrase automatically with damnation.

### Therefore reject both shortcuts

Not safe:

> «with Samuel = Saul certainly went to heaven».

Not safe:

> «Saul died under judgment = the phrase proves he went to hell».

The verse securely predicts **death**, not a finely differentiated final-soteriological geography.

---

# 13. 1 Chronicles 10:13–14 — inspired retrospective verdict

This is the most important final canonical interpretation of Saul’s death.

## 13.1. `מַעַל` / `מָעַל`

Text:

`בְּמַעֲלוֹ אֲשֶׁר מָעַל בַּיהוָה`

Saul died «for his unfaithfulness/breach of faith by which he acted unfaithfully against YHWH».

The noun + cognate verb intensifies the charge.

`מעל` is covenant-treachery/unfaithfulness language elsewhere in Chronicles and Torah.

Thus Saul’s death is not merely tactical defeat.

The Chronicler gives a theological verdict:

> **covenant unfaithfulness against YHWH**.

## 13.2. Three explicit charges

1. he did not keep YHWH’s word;
2. he consulted a medium to seek guidance;
3. he did not inquire/seek YHWH.

Then:

`וַיְמִיתֵהוּ`

— YHWH put him to death / caused him to die.

And transferred the kingdom to David.

### DIRECT EXEGESIS

Saul’s death is divine judgment for covenant unfaithfulness.

That is stronger than merely saying «Saul made mistakes».

---

# 14. 1 Sam 28:6 vs 1 Chr 10:14 — do not solve the problem by fake lexical absolutism

1 Samuel 28:6:

- `שׁאל` — Saul asked/inquired of YHWH.

1 Chronicles 10:14:

- `דרש` — he did not seek/inquire of YHWH.

Some popular explanations say:

> `שׁאל` always means casual asking; `דרש` always means sincere seeking.

That is too rigid lexically. Both verbs have overlapping inquiry/seeking uses.

Better explanation is contextual/theological:

- Saul did make a request in crisis;
- he had already rejected clear revelation;
- he did not persevere in humble covenant-seeking;
- when no new oracle came, he turned immediately to prohibited medium;
- the Chronicler evaluates his whole conduct as failure genuinely to seek YHWH.

Thus the two statements are not flat contradiction, but neither should be harmonized by inventing lexical rules stronger than Hebrew usage permits.

# 15. Final-state question: evidence table

## Strong evidence of severe covenant judgment

- another heart / Spirit privilege at beginning;
- turned back from following YHWH;
- rejected YHWH’s word;
- rejected from kingship;
- Spirit departed;
- violent resistance to David;
- priest massacre;
- occult consultation;
- Chronicler: `מעל` against YHWH;
- YHWH put him to death.

This makes Saul one of Scripture’s strongest **judgment trajectories**.

## Evidence often used for salvation

- «another heart»;
- Spirit of God;
- «tomorrow ... with me».

Each is real, but none is a clean technical statement:

> «Saul was regenerate and finally saved.»

## Evidence often used for damnation

- Spirit departed;
- God rejected him;
- medium;
- suicide/death under judgment.

Again severe, but none explicitly says:

> «Saul’s eternal state is hell».

### Final research judgment

**Final-state certainty: AMBER / narrator-restrained.**

The corpus should confidently teach Saul’s covenant/royal apostasy trajectory and divine temporal-historical judgment while refusing to make his eternal state carry more weight than the text gives it.

# 16. Why Saul is especially valuable for the apostasy project

Saul prevents several bad equations.

## Equation rejected A

`Spirit came upon someone` = automatically full NT regenerate status.

## Equation rejected B

`Spirit departed` = automatically loss of NT justification.

## Equation rejected C

`I have sinned` = automatically durable repentance.

## Equation rejected D

`divine rejection` = unspecified; no need to ask what is rejected.

Text explicitly says rejected **from kingship**.

## Equation rejected E

`died under divine judgment` = automatic explicit eternal damnation verdict.

## But equally rejected

Using these guardrails to weaken Saul into an ordinary backslider with no profound covenant betrayal.

His trajectory is terrifying precisely because privileges, calling, Spirit-empowerment, repeated warning and moments of confession coexist with increasingly hardened disobedience.

# 17. Puritan/Reformed practical layer

Saul is especially useful for themes already central to Owen/Watson/Brooks/Flavel:

- partial obedience masquerading as obedience;
- fear of man;
- religious self-justification;
- temporary conviction without durable reformation;
- repeated resistance to light;
- envy and inward corruption after public honour;
- seeking illicit relief when God’s ordinary means are resisted.

### Important source discipline

Do not let a Puritan’s use of Saul decide the narrator’s final-state verdict.

Use Puritan material for **anatomy of sin**, not as substitute for 1 Samuel / Chronicles exegesis.

MacArthur’s own cautious line is useful here: in an exposition of 1 Sam 16 he explicitly distinguishes royal Spirit-empowerment from personal salvation and says Saul’s true-believer status is not settled merely by that Spirit passage.
