# 3. Judas Iscariot — terminal case with pre-betrayal unbelief control

## Primary owners

- John 6:64, 70–71;
- John 12:4–6;
- John 13:2, 10–11, 18, 21–30;
- John 17:12;
- Matt 26–27;
- Acts 1:16–25.

## Technical owner

`02_JUDAS_ISCARIOT_EXEGETICAL_DOSSIER_2026-09-29.md` plus Part III `103_...`.

## Frozen claims

1. Judas’s apostolic office and mission privilege were real.
2. “Chosen among the Twelve” must not be equated mechanically with saving election.
3. John 6:64 supplies explicit pre-betrayal unbelief evidence.
4. John 12 supplies a hidden greed/theft trajectory beneath religious language.
5. His final trajectory is terminal with unusually high narrative certainty.

## Guardrails

- Do not deny the reality of his privileges to protect perseverance.
- Do not use him as a clean example of a regenerate person losing justification.
- Do not make `μεταμεληθείς` in Matt 27:3, by itself, the reason his remorse is non-saving; the canonical trajectory does the work.

## Puritan reinforcement

Brooks on sin wearing the colors of virtue and on concealed bait is apt downstream practical theology; Watson on sorrow versus repentance is useful downstream of Matthew, not as Greek lexicography.

# 4. Saul — severe covenant/royal trajectory, final-state restraint

## Primary owners

- 1 Sam 10:1, 6, 9–10;
- 1 Sam 13;
- 1 Sam 15;
- 1 Sam 16:13–14;
- 1 Sam 18–26;
- 1 Sam 28;
- 1 Sam 31;
- 1 Chr 10:13–14.

## Technical owner

`81_SAUL_TRAJECTORY_SPIRIT_REJECTION_UNFAITHFULNESS_AND_FINAL_STATE_RESTRAINT_2026-10-01.md`.

## Claim freeze

Strong wording allowed:

> Saul receives real divine royal empowerment and real covenant-historical privilege, then moves through disobedience, fear of people, religious self-justification, jealousy, violence and forbidden consultation.

### Critical category guardrail

The Spirit’s royal empowerment/departure in 1 Samuel must not be imported without argument into the full NT doctrine of permanent regenerating indwelling.

### 1 Chr 10

The inspired retrospective explicitly interprets Saul’s death in terms of `מעל` / unfaithfulness, failure to keep YHWH’s word, and consulting a medium rather than seeking YHWH.

### Forbidden upgrade

Do not write:

> “The Bible explicitly says Saul was eternally regenerated, lost salvation and was damned.”

It does not.

Likewise do not soften the trajectory merely because the final eternal verdict is unstated.

## Practical-theology angle

1 Sam 15 is especially important for apostasy mechanisms: partial obedience is renamed as worship, and fear of people remains operative even during confession.

# 5. Jeroboam — personal fear institutionalized

## Primary owners

- 1 Kings 11:26–40;
- 1 Kings 12:25–33;
- 1 Kings 13–14;
- repeated Kings formula “sin of Jeroboam.”

## Technical owner

`84_JEROBOAM_2_KINGS_17_INSTITUTIONAL_APOSTASY_AND_EXILE_DOSSIER_2026-10-01.md`.

## Frozen trajectory

`fear of losing kingdom → alternative worship architecture → normalized cult → transgenerational formula`.

## Claim freeze

Jeroboam is a canonical control for **institutional apostasy**: a leader can make his compromise easier for future generations by embedding it in structures, offices, sacred places and calendar.

## Guardrail

Do not reduce the calves to a simplistic claim that Jeroboam openly announced “YHWH is false.” The religious system claims continuity while violating commanded worship.

That is precisely why the case is powerful: apostasy can be syncretistic/revisionary rather than atheistic.

# 13. People versus systems — an editorial distinction to preserve

Part II should explicitly preserve three scales:

### Personal

Judas, Saul, Solomon, Demas.

### Teacher/influence

Hymenaeus, Philetus, Balaam-pattern false teachers.

### Institutional/corporate

Jeroboam, northern kingdom, 2 Kings 17.

### Freeze

Apostasy is not only an individual interior event. Scripture also treats leaders, doctrine, institutions and cultures of compromise as causal environments.

But corporate diagnosis does not erase individual responsibility or settle every individual’s eternal state.

# 17. Editorial freeze list

1. Judas = real privilege plus explicit pre-betrayal unbelief; not a clean regenerate-loss prooftext.
2. Saul = real royal/Spirit empowerment plus severe rejection trajectory; final eternal state not explicitly narrated.
3. Jeroboam = institutionalized compromise; personal fear can become transgenerational religious structure.
4. 2 Kings 17 = inspired corporate apostasy/exile theology; not a census of individual eternal states.
5. Solomon = severe late religious apostasy; no narrator-supplied final eternal verdict.
6. Demas = real ministry association + desertion for present-age love; no invented final chapter.
7. Hymenaeus/Alexander/Philetus = serious doctrinal/corrective cases; discipline and warning remain distinct from automatic final verdict.
8. 2 Peter/Jude = strong real-experience language must not be minimized, but regeneration inference remains argument-dependent.
9. No single psychological ladder explains every case.
10. Narrator strength sets claim strength.
11. Conservative secondary sources are controls, not permission to overread.
12. TGC is not an authority layer.
