# 3. Pliny the Younger — “former Christians” under Roman examination

## Primary owner

Pliny, *Letters* 10.96–97.

Primary/source custody already frozen in `94_...`:

- Perseus Latin;
- Fordham translation;
- Attalus translation/notes.

## Exact historical claim

Pliny reports people who said they had been Christians but had ceased, some years earlier and at least one roughly twenty years earlier, and who passed the Roman cultic test involving pagan invocation/worship and cursing Christ.

## Freeze

Allowed:

> By the early second century a Roman governor encountered people who described themselves as formerly Christian and now publicly repudiated Christian allegiance in conduct recognizable to Roman authorities.

Forbidden:

- “Pliny verified that they had been regenerate.”
- “Every one of them died an apostate.”
- “The document proves conditional security.”

## Why the case is high-value

It is external/non-Christian evidence for the visible historical category “formerly Christian by profession/identity.”

# 4. Quintus in *Martyrdom of Polycarp* — zeal before trial, collapse under fear

## Primary/near-primary owner

*Martyrdom of Polycarp* 4; public text custody in `94_...`.

## Freeze

The text supports:

- voluntary self-presentation;
- prior conspicuous zeal;
- fear when faced with beasts;
- oath/sacrifice under pressure;
- the author’s warning against rashly volunteering for persecution.

## Canonical analogy

Useful beside:

- Peter’s overconfident pre-denial promises;
- 1 Cor 10:12.

But analogy must remain analogy.

## Final-state guardrail

The text does not narrate Quintus’s later life. Therefore:

> **fall = documented; final eternal state = unknown.**

Do not transform silence into damnation.

# 5. Decian persecution and the `lapsi` — public lapse was not one flat category

## Primary owner

Cyprian, *De Lapsis / On the Lapsed*; exact public locators in `94_...`.

## Historical freeze

The church faced multiple forms of public compliance under persecution, including sacrifice, incense and documentary/certificate issues. Later labels such as `sacrificati`, `thurificati`, `libellatici` are useful, but the reader draft must not pretend one primary passage supplies a neat later textbook taxonomy.

## Cyprian’s two-front pastoral concern

Freeze the tension:

1. public idolatrous lapse must not be trivialized into cheap peace;
2. the path of repentance must not be closed so completely that the fallen are driven to despair.

## Safe synthesis

> **The early church could treat public denial/idolatrous compliance as gravely serious while still regarding repentance, discipline and restoration as live categories.**

## Guardrail

This historical controversy does not itself resolve Calvinism vs Arminianism and must not be imported wholesale into later sacramental systems.

# 6. Ninus, Clementianus and Florus — costly confession, later collapse, prolonged repentance

## Primary owner

Cyprian, ANF Epistle 52, with numbering convention explicitly stated in `94_...`.

## Documentary sequence

The letter records that they:

- were seized;
- confessed the Lord;
- initially endured pressure;
- later faced prolonged/severe torture;
- were overcome and fell;
- continued repentance for three years;
- were considered by Cyprian as candidates for mercy/restoration to communion, subject also to conciliar judgment.

## Freeze

This is one of Part IV’s strongest historical controls against the formula:

> “public denial proves that all prior profession was necessarily counterfeit and restoration is impossible.”

It also blocks the opposite error:

> “fear makes public denial morally trivial.”

## Guardrail

Do not universalize the three-year period as a biblical restoration timetable.

# 7. Novatian controversy — seriousness without a cult of hopelessness

## Owners

Cyprian/Cornelius primary material plus critical historical synthesis; exact map in `94_...`.

## Freeze

Safe historical statement:

- indiscriminate instant restoration was resisted;
- rigorist exclusion associated with Novatian’s schism was also resisted in the Cyprianic/Cornelian line;
- penitence and eventual restoration remained possible.

## Editorial limit

Do not make Part IV a defense of later Roman Catholic penitential theology. The point is narrower: early ecclesial practice distinguished grave lapse from irrecoverability.

# 8. Julian — public doctrinal repudiation after Christian formation

## Evidence stack

- Julian’s own surviving anti-Christian writings — `A` for his mature repudiation;
- Ammianus — near-primary non-Christian witness to public pagan restoration;
- Gregory Nazianzen — near-primary hostile Christian witness useful for reception;
- modern critical scholarship — `B` for formation chronology and contested biographical details.

## Freeze

Allowed:

> Julian is a historically strong case of movement from Christian formation/identity into explicit doctrinal rejection of Christianity and public/state-backed pagan restoration.

## Attribution discipline

Details such as baptism or service as a reader must be attributed to the witnesses who report them when the evidence is not Julian’s own autobiographical statement.

## Soteriological guardrail

Do not say history proves prior regeneration.

His mature public repudiation is historically strong; his prior saving state is not an archival fact.

# 9. Thomas Cranmer — recantation under mortal pressure followed by public repudiation of the recantation

## Primary/source owners

- 1556 Marian printed recantation/submission material;
- final speech tradition / Foxe-related witness stream;
- modern critical Cranmer scholarship as control.

Exact locators already mapped in `94_...`.

## Frozen historical claim

Cranmer signed substantive recantation/submission texts under Marian pressure and, immediately before execution, publicly repudiated the recantations and renewed the confession for which he died.

## Evidence-discipline guardrail

Foxe is not an evenhanded modern historian. Where the narrative depends on Foxe, corroborate through independent documentary/modern-critical custody and disclose the source character.

## Reader-facing significance

Cranmer is a high-value historical analogue for:

> **serious public recantation under fear followed by equally public reversal/confession.**

He is not proof that every recanter was regenerate or will return.

# 10. Francesco Spiera/Spira — recantation and despair: maximum caution

## Source rule

This case is historically influential in Protestant practical theology, but the article must distinguish:

- documented recantation/controversy;
- later confessional retellings;
- theological use of his despair;
- unverifiable claims about his final inward state.

## Freeze

Use Spira only for the history of **public recantation and the pastoral/theological interpretation of despair**, not as an infallible case study proving the unforgivable sin or final reprobation.

## Puritan/Reformation reception

Later Protestant authors may be cited to show how the case functioned in conscience literature. Their diagnosis is reception history, not inspired access to Spira’s eternal state.

# 11. Church history as a control against two opposite modern errors

## Error A — every lapse = Judas

The historical record contains people who publicly failed and later sought/received restoration, and Cranmer supplies a dramatic recantation/reversal case.

## Error B — pressure makes denial morally unimportant

Cyprianic discipline and martyr literature treat public denial/sacrifice as gravely serious.

## Frozen synthesis

> **Historical Christianity repeatedly had to hold together the gravity of public denial and the reality of repentance/restoration.**

This mirrors, but does not establish, the biblical categories already grounded in Peter, James 5, Gal 6, 2 Tim 2 and the warning texts.

# 15. Editorial freeze list

1. History illustrates biblical categories; it does not create inspired soteriological categories.
2. Public profession/denial/restoration can be historically documented; regeneration/election cannot be archived.
3. Pliny proves a visible “former Christian” category, not prior regeneration.
4. Quintus documents collapse under fear, not an explicit final verdict.
5. Cyprianic `lapsi` evidence preserves both gravity and restoration.
6. Ninus/Clementianus/Florus are a high-value documented fall-after-endurance + prolonged-repentance case.
7. Novatian controversy must not be anachronistically converted into later sacramental polemics.
8. Julian is a strong public-repudiation case; disputed formation details must be attributed.
9. Cranmer is a strong recantation-and-reversal case; Foxe requires source-character disclosure/corroboration.
10. Spira is not to be used as an inspired verdict on the unforgivable sin or election.
11. No historical case may be assigned greater soteriological certainty than its sources permit.
12. Primary documents outrank platform summaries.
