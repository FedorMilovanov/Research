## 1. Historical method

Church history can illustrate biblical categories but cannot create new inspired categories.

For every case distinguish:

1. **documented public profession / Christian identification**;
2. **documented denial, sacrifice, recantation or doctrinal repudiation**;
3. **documented repentance/restoration, if any**;
4. **church judgment or later reception**;
5. **what the sources do not tell us about the person’s regenerate/final state**.

### Evidence grades

- `A — PRIMARY / NEAR-PRIMARY`: contemporary text or official document.
- `B — STRONG MODERN CRITICAL`: modern academic historical analysis controlling partisan sources.
- `C — LATER CONFESSIONAL / MARTYROLOGICAL RECEPTION`: useful for reception, not transparent biography.
- `D — DEVOTIONAL LEGEND / UNSOURCED`: not admissible for factual claims.

### Governing rule

> **Historical apostasy is observable at the level of profession, worship and public allegiance. Regeneration and final election are not archival categories.**

# 2. Pliny the Younger, Letters 10.96–97 — former Christians under Roman examination

## Evidence grade

`A`.

Pliny is a non-Christian Roman governor writing to Trajan, c. AD 112.

Primary Latin:
https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0139%3Abook%3D10%3Aletter%3D96

Accessible translation:
https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/pliny1.asp

Attalus translation with textual notes:
https://www.attalus.org/pliny/ep10b.html

## 2.1. Pliny’s legal test

For persons accused of being Christians, Pliny describes tests involving:

- invoking Roman gods;
- worship/offerings before the emperor’s image and divine statues;
- cursing Christ.

He remarks that those who are truly Christians were said not to be compelable to curse Christ.

## 2.2. Former-Christian group

Pliny’s Latin is especially valuable:

`fuisse quidem sed desisse, quidam ante triennium, quidam ante plures annos, non nemo etiam ante viginti`

Meaning:

- they said they **had been** Christians;
- but had **ceased**;
- some three years earlier;
- some many years earlier;
- at least one around twenty years earlier.

They then performed the Roman cultic test and cursed Christ.

## 2.3. What this historically proves

`GREEN`:

> By the early second century Roman authorities encountered people who claimed a former Christian identity but now publicly repudiated it in conduct acceptable to Roman pagan authorities.

## 2.4. What it does not prove

Do not say:

- Pliny verified prior regeneration;
- all such people were true Christians who lost justification;
- all were permanently apostate until death;
- Pliny understood Christian theology sufficiently to adjudicate saving faith.

### Reader-facing value

This is one of the cleanest external historical witnesses that “former Christian” was not merely a later theological abstraction.

Status: `GREEN`.

# 3. *Martyrdom of Polycarp* 4 — Quintus: zeal, fear and sacrifice

## Evidence grade

`A/B`.

Primary/public texts:

- CCEL, ch.4: https://ccel.org/ccel/polycarp/martyrdom_of_polycarp/anf01.iv.iv.iv.html
- New Advent: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0102.htm

## 3.1. Narrative

The text reports that Quintus, a Phrygian:

- voluntarily presented himself, encouraging others to do likewise;
- saw the wild beasts and became afraid;
- was persuaded by the proconsul to swear and sacrifice.

The author’s explicit pastoral inference is not merely “Quintus was bad.” It is:

> Christians should not rashly volunteer themselves for persecution, because the Gospel does not teach such self-presentation.

## 3.2. Project use

Safe claim:

> Early Christian martyr literature already knew a case in which conspicuous zeal before trial did not guarantee endurance under terror.

This is valuable alongside:

- Peter’s overconfident pledge before denial;
- 1 Cor 10:12;
- warning against presumption in one’s own courage.

## 3.3. Guardrails

Do not infer:

- Quintus’ final eternal state;
- that he never repented;
- that voluntary surrender itself proves hypocrisy;
- that every martyr narrative is neutral modern biography.

The later-life record is absent from this text.

Status: `GREEN / FINAL-STATE UNKNOWN`.

# 4. Decian persecution and the *lapsi* — public lapse was not one flat category

## Evidence grade

- Cyprian primary material: `A`.
- later terminology summaries: `B/C` depending source.

Primary anchor:

Cyprian, *De Lapsis / On the Lapsed*:
https://ccel.org/ccel/cyprian/treatises/anf05.iv.v.iii.html
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050703.htm

## 4.1. Historical context

Under Decius’ empire-wide sacrificial requirements (AD 249–251), Christians faced pressure to conform publicly to Roman cult.

Cyprian’s writings distinguish people who stood fast from those who fell and then sought peace/communion again.

## 4.2. Common later labels

Historical summaries commonly distinguish:

- `sacrificati` — those who sacrificed;
- `thurificati` — those who offered incense;
- `libellatici` — those who obtained certificates attesting compliance, in some cases without actual sacrifice.

Useful secondary taxonomy control:
https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09001b.htm

But flagship should not imply every label occurs as a complete formal taxonomy in one sentence of *De Lapsis*.

## 4.3. Cyprian’s two-front concern

Cyprian opposes two opposite errors:

1. treating public idolatrous lapse as trivial and granting cheap peace without repentance;
2. closing the path of repentance so completely that the lapsed are driven to despair.

*De Lapsis* 34–35 explicitly combines:

- severity about the sin;
- insistence on penitence;
- rejection of false safety;
- warning **not to despair of the Lord’s mercy** while also not presumptuously claiming immediate pardon.

Primary text:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050703.htm

### Project synthesis

> **The early church’s lapse crisis did not reduce to “once denied = irrecoverable” or “fear excuses everything.” Serious public denial generated serious discipline, but repentance and restoration remained live categories.**

Status: `GREEN`.

# 5. Ninus, Clementianus and Florus — fall under torture and long repentance

## Evidence grade

`A`.

Cyprian, Epistle 52 in ANF numbering:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050652.htm

Alternate ANF mirror:
https://www.tertullian.org/fathers2/ANF-05/anf05-77.htm

### Numbering control

Ancient/modern editions of Cyprian’s correspondence may use differing letter numbering systems. For publication use:

> **Cyprian, ANF Epistle 52, “To Fortunatus and His Other Colleagues, Concerning Those Who Had Been Overcome by Tortures.”**

Do not cite simply “Cyprian Letter 52” without edition convention.

## 5.1. Sequence stated in the letter

Cyprian is told that Ninus, Clementianus and Florus:

1. were seized in persecution;
2. confessed the Lord’s name;
3. initially endured magistrates and hostile crowd;
4. were later subjected to severe, prolonged torture before the proconsul;
5. were overcome and fell;
6. then continued repentance for **three years**.

Cyprian’s own judgment is that mercy should not be denied them and that reception to communion is appropriate, while he also proposes conciliar consideration.

## 5.2. Why this case matters

This is an unusually strong historical analogue for the project’s Peter/restoration distinction.

The three were not described as people who had never made any costly confession. They had already endured substantial pressure and then failed later under prolonged torture.

Yet the surviving church document treats their later repentance as meaningful and restoration as possible.

### Publication-safe thesis

> **Early Christian discipline could regard a genuine public lapse under torture as gravely serious while still recognizing prolonged repentance and ecclesial restoration.**

### What not to infer

Cyprian cannot tell us infallibly:

- their regenerate status before lapse;
- their final election;
- whether every lapsed Christian should follow the same three-year timeline.

Status: `GREEN / HIGH-VALUE HISTORICAL CASE`.

# 6. Cyprian, Cornelius and Novatian rigorism — restoration was a church-defining controversy

## Evidence grade

`A/B`.

The post-Decian church disputed how the lapsed should be restored.

Safe historical formulation:

- Cyprian and Cornelius opposed indiscriminate instant restoration;
- they also opposed the rigorist exclusion associated with Novatian’s schism;
- ecclesial penitence and eventual restoration remained possible in the Catholic/Cyprianic line.

### Method constraint

This history illustrates pastoral categories; it does not itself adjudicate Reformed versus Roman Catholic sacramental theology.

Do not import later penitential-system theology into the third-century evidence without argument.

Status: `GREEN-PARAPHRASE`; direct historical quotations require exact letter/edition locator.

# 7. Julian — from Christian formation to explicit anti-Christian imperial program

## Evidence grade

- Julian’s surviving writings: `A`.
- Ammianus: `A/near-primary`, non-Christian and broadly favorable to Julian.
- Gregory Nazianzen: `A/near-primary but openly hostile`.
- modern critical synthesis: `B`.

## 7.1. Christian formation

Modern scholarship broadly recognizes Julian’s Christian upbringing/education before his turn toward pagan Hellenism.

Henry Chadwick summarizes that Julian inwardly abandoned Christianity and converted to polytheism around age 20 (351).

Source:
https://academic.oup.com/book/27035/chapter-abstract/196315749

Recent Classical Quarterly discussion notes:

- education under bishops;
- Christian Scriptures;
- Cyril’s report of baptism;
- Gregory’s report that Julian served as a church reader;
- Julian’s own account of departure around age twenty.

Source:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/julian-the-apostate-and-the-of-abraham/DBB63549F20D079C9CCA56C44AF7DF8D

### Caution

Individual details such as baptism/reader status come through Christian witnesses and should be attributed, not silently treated as equally documented autobiographical facts.

---

## 7.2. Julian’s own anti-Christian polemic

Surviving fragments of *Against the Galileans* openly attack Christianity as fabricated/false and defend Hellenic religion.

Primary/public text:
https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/julian_apostate_galileans_1_text.htm

This is sufficient to establish **public doctrinal repudiation of Christianity** without relying on Gregory’s invective.

---

## 7.3. Pagan restoration as imperial policy

Ammianus Marcellinus, Book 22.5, reports that Julian:

- had practiced pagan rites secretly;
- once secure, openly professed worship of the gods;
- ordered temples opened;
- restored sacrifices;
- pursued policies affecting Christians.

Primary/near-primary text:
https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/22%2A.html

Ammianus is especially valuable because he was not a Christian polemicist and often admired Julian.

## 7.4. Gregory as hostile witness

Gregory Nazianzen’s invectives repeatedly call Julian an apostate and portray his policy as rebellion against Christ.

Primary/public text:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/pearse/morefathers/files/gregory_nazianzen_3_oration5.htm

Use Gregory for **Christian contemporary reception**, not as sole factual control.

## 7.5. Safe classification

> **Julian is a historically strong case of public movement from Christian formation/identity into explicit doctrinal rejection of Christianity and state-backed restoration of pagan worship.**

### What cannot be historically proven

- prior saving faith;
- regeneration;
- final divine verdict beyond biblical general principles.

Status: `GREEN / PUBLIC APOSTASY; SOTERIOLOGICAL PRIOR STATE UNPROVEN`.

# 8. Thomas Cranmer — recantation under fear and repudiation of recantation at death

## Evidence grade

- 1556 printed recantation collection: `A`, but produced under Marian authorities and must be contextually controlled.
- final speech tradition / Foxe and eyewitness streams: `A/B`.
- modern historical scholarship: `B`.

## 8.1. The recantations are not Protestant legend

A 1556 printed volume exists under Cranmer’s name:

*All the submyssyons, and recantations of Thomas Cranmer... agreable to the originalles, wrytten and subscribed with his owne hande.*

Bibliographic record / Bodleian reproduction metadata:
https://openlibrary.org/books/OL21841144M/All_the_submyssyons_and_recantations_of_Thomas_Cranmer_late_Archebyshop_of_Canterburye

Modern scholarship specifically studies the Marian publication of his recantations and cites the 1556 edition:
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13574175.2021.1898235

Thus the fact that Cranmer signed substantive recantations/submissions is historically secure.

---

## 8.2. Final repudiation

In the received text of Cranmer’s final speech he says the writings signed after his degradation were contrary to the truth he held in his heart and were written:

- from fear of death;
- in hope of saving his life.

He then repudiates them and declares that the hand that wrote them should be punished first.

Accessible text tradition:
https://www.luminarium.org/renlit/cranmerspeech.htm

Foxe-derived version:
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_World%27s_Famous_Orations/Volume_3/On_the_Eve_of_His_Execution

Folger confirms the basic historical reception of the episode while explicitly warning that Foxe is not an evenhanded source:
https://www.folger.edu/explore/collection-highlights/the-book-of-martyrs/

## 8.3. Modern control

Modern Cranmer scholarship (especially Diarmaid MacCulloch) treats the sequence of multiple submissions/recantations and the final reversal as historical, while reconstructing their chronology with more precision than Protestant martyrology.

A modern article on the recantation texts cites MacCulloch and the 1556 primary edition:
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13574175.2021.1898235

## 8.4. Project classification

> **Cranmer is a high-value historical case of serious public recantation under mortal pressure followed by equally public repudiation of that recantation and renewed confession immediately before death.**

This makes him useful as a historical analogue to **fall and restoration/confession**, not as proof that every recanter is regenerate or will return.

Status: `GREEN / PRIMARY-DOCUMENTED RECANTATION + FINAL REVERSAL`.

# 9. Francesco Spiera/Spira — recantation, despair and the danger of turning propaganda into diagnosis

## Evidence grade

- Matteo Gribaldi 1549 account: `A/near-primary, participant/polemic context`.
- Vergerio/Curione and other early Protestant accounts: `A/near-primary but confessional/polemical`.
- later Nathaniel Bacon 1638: `C` for historical detail unless traced to earlier sources.
- modern Overell/MacDonald: `B` critical reception/history controls.

## 9.1. Stable historical core

Modern historians agree on the core:

- Francesco Spiera was an Italian lawyer with Protestant convictions/associations;
- under Inquisition pressure in 1548 he publicly recanted/abjured;
- the event was followed by profound despair;
- he became a famous Protestant cautionary narrative about recantation.

Modern critical controls:

- M. A. Overell, “Recantation and Retribution: ‘Remembering Francis Spira’, 1548–1638”: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-church-history/article/abs/recantation-and-retribution-remembering-francis-spira-15481638/8B4F94D329E7CD69A0E22233AF84A4B2
- M. A. Overell, “The Exploitation of Francesco Spiera”: https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/pdf/10.2307/2543142
- Michael MacDonald, “The Fearefull Estate of Francis Spira”: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-british-studies/article/abs/fearefull-estate-of-francis-spira-narrative-identity-and-emotion-in-early-modern-england/E9DE3981EA81D57F153DCDC4A037E6B0

## 9.2. Near-primary Gribaldi text

Matteo Gribaldi’s 1549 *Historia de quodam quem hostes Evangelii in Italia coegerunt abiicere agnitam veritatem* is digitally preserved.

Critical digital bibliography / scan access:
https://www.controversia-et-confessio.de/cc-digital/quellen/modus/ls/10/70/10/ansicht/4172-historia-de-quodam-quem-hostes-evangelii-coegerunt.html

Scan/OCR access:
https://sourcelibrary.org/book/historia-de-quodam-quem-hostes-evangelii-in-italia-gribaldi

A 1549 German version is also catalogued with detailed editorial description:
https://www.controversia-et-confessio.de/cc-digital/quellen/modus/ls/10/70/10/ansicht/3768-historia-von-einem-doctor-in-italia-historia-de-quodam-quem-hostes-evangelij-in-italia-coeger.html

## 9.3. The key source-critical warning

Overell shows that the Spiera narratives were **not disinterested clinical reportage**.

They were shaped by:

- anti-Nicodemite polemic;
- Protestant exhortation against dissimulation/recantation;
- older ars moriendi traditions concerning despair;
- subsequent confessional reuse.

Therefore flagship must distinguish:

### Historically secure

- recantation under pressure;
- ensuing severe despair;
- rapid formation of a Protestant cautionary tradition.

### Not secure enough for dogmatic assertion

- “God visibly reprobated him at the moment of recantation”;
- “his despair proves he committed the unpardonable sin”;
- every reported inner speech as stenographically exact;
- later devotional embellishments as independent facts;
- a final eternal-state verdict.

## 9.4. An unexpectedly useful guardrail inside early reception

The 1549 German Gribaldi tradition is described as warning not only against denying known truth but also against those who **flee from God and exclude themselves from the number of the elect**, emphasizing God’s promise rather than private fantasies of damnation.

This is important for the project:

> Spiera’s reception can be used against cowardly recantation **and** against making despair a pious response to sin.

## 9.5. Project status

> **Spiera is usable as a history-of-recantation-and-despair case, but not as transparent proof of divine reprobation.**

Status: `GREEN-PARAPHRASE / SOURCE-CRITICISM REQUIRED`.

# 11. What church history adds to the biblical taxonomy

## 11.1. Public apostasy can be historically observable

Pliny and Julian demonstrate that one can document:

- former Christian identification;
- later public repudiation.

But history cannot open the decree of election.

## 11.2. Severe public lapse can be recoverable

Ninus/Clementianus/Florus and Cranmer strongly undermine a simplistic rule:

> “Once publicly denied, always to be treated as terminal apostate.”

This does not weaken Matt 10 warnings; it prevents humans from claiming more diagnostic authority than Scripture grants.

## 11.3. Zeal before testing is not perseverance

Quintus is a historical caution against presumption in courage.

## 11.4. Church discipline must hold severity and restoration together

Cyprian’s lapse crisis shows how quickly churches can fall into opposite errors:

- cheap peace;
- irrecoverable rigorism.

## 11.5. Despair is not repentance

Spiera’s reception history is especially useful when critically handled:

- grave sin must not be minimized;
- but the sinner is not authorized to convert subjective despair into God’s final decree.
