## 1. Главный вопрос

2 Пет. 2:1 говорит о лжеучителях:

`τὸν ἀγοράσαντα αὐτοὺς δεσπότην ἀρνούμενοι`

буквально:

> «отвергая/отрекаясь от Владыки, купившего их».

Текст неудобен сразу для двух упрощений:

1. **строгий particular-redemption shortcut:** «`bought` не может означать реальную искупительную покупку, потому что эти люди погибают»;
2. **conditional-security shortcut:** «раз Христос купил их, значит они были возрождены/оправданы и затем потеряли спасение».

Оба вывода бегут дальше грамматики.

Нужно отдельно решить четыре вопроса:

1. Кто `δεσπότης` — Отец или Христос?
2. Что означает `ἀγοράζω` в конструкции?
3. Есть ли прямой Deut 32:6 intertext?
4. Что purchase-language доказывает — и чего не доказывает — о regeneration, justification, atonement extent и perseverance?

---

# 2. DIRECT EXEGESIS — синтаксис утверждает purchase, а не appearance

Грамматика проста:

- `δεσπότην` — accusative singular, объект `ἀρνούμενοι`;
- `ἀγοράσαντα` — aorist active participle acc. masc. sg., модифицирует `δεσπότην`;
- `αὐτούς` — direct object participle: «купившего **их**»;
- `ἀρνούμενοι` — «отрекаясь/отвергая».

### DIRECT EXEGESIS conclusion

Narrator grammatically characterizes Master as **the one who bought them**.

The sentence does **not** explicitly say:

- «который, как им казалось, купил их»;
- «которого они исповедовали как купившего их»;
- «который купил только их общину, но не их».

Those may be proposed as contextual/systematic explanations, but they are not encoded in the participle itself.

This distinction is mandatory when weighing phenomenological readings.

---

# 3. DIRECT EXEGESIS — `ἀγοράζω` is real purchase/ownership language

The core sense of `ἀγοράζω` is buy/purchase/acquire in the marketplace.

Relevant NT theological uses include:

- 1 Cor. 6:20 — «вы куплены дорогою ценой»;
- 1 Cor. 7:23 — same purchase formula;
- Rev. 5:9 — Lamb purchased people for God with His blood;
- Rev. 14:3–4 — the redeemed/purchased from humanity;
- 2 Pet. 2:1 — Master who bought them.

Technical lexical/commentary tradition commonly places 2 Pet. 2:1 in this purchase/redemption field.

### Thomas Schreiner’s concession

In his chapter “Problematic Texts for Definite Atonement,” Schreiner explicitly says that a non-soteriological interpretation faces a **severe lexical problem** because NT purchase/redemption language associated with Christ is soteriological.

Direct accessible HTML:
- https://www.monergism.com/%E2%80%9Cproblematic-texts%E2%80%9D-definite-atonement-pastoral-and-general-epistles-0
- continuation: https://www.monergism.com/%E2%80%9Cproblematic-texts%E2%80%9D-definite-atonement-pastoral-and-general-epistles?page=1

Original chapter source:
- Thomas R. Schreiner, “Problematic Texts for Definite Atonement in the Pastoral and General Epistles,” in *From Heaven He Came and Sought Her*.

### Closure

Do **not** publish:

> «`ἀγοράζω` здесь не искупительное слово».

That is too strong and is rejected by a major conservative Reformed NT scholar.

### Equally important

Do not publish the reverse lexical equation:

> `ἀγοράζω` = regeneration + justification + final saving application.

The verb establishes purchase/ownership/redemption language. It does not itself encode the complete ordo salutis.

# 4. `δεσπότης` — Christ is the leading technical reading

`δεσπότης` means master/owner/sovereign.

Its use does not independently force Father or Son.

The direct phrase is especially natural with purchase imagery:

> an owner/master who bought slaves.

The technical-commentary acquisition already showed a broad vote for **Christ** as referent across commentary tradition, including WBC/Bauckham as summarized in SIL Exegetical Summary.

Direct older exegetical witnesses likewise read Christ:

- Meyer;
- Expositor/Cambridge tradition;
- many modern translations/commentaries.

MacArthur also takes the sovereign Master as Jesus Christ in his exposition of 2 Pet. 2:1–3.

GTY:
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/61-17/a-portrait-of-false-teachers-part-2

### DIRECT/CUMULATIVE conclusion

`δεσπότης = Christ` is currently the **preferred project reading**.

`Father` remains grammatically possible, especially within a Deut 32/covenant model, but cannot be claimed from the noun alone.

---

# 5. Deuteronomy 32:6 — conceptual parallel, not direct lexical echo

This is a major correction to a common Reformed/pastoral argument.

## Hebrew

Deut 32:6:

`אָבִיךָ קָּנֶךָ`

YHWH is Israel’s Father who acquired/created/possessed the people.

## LXX

The Greek is:

`οὐκ αὐτὸς οὗτός σου πατὴρ ἐκτήσατό σε ...`

The verb is **`κτάομαι / ἐκτήσατο`**, not `ἀγοράζω`.

Text witnesses:
- https://www.biblindex.org/en/bible/septuaginta/dt-32%2C6
- https://www.fellowshipbook.org/en/deuteronomy/32/6

### Consequence

MacArthur’s Deut 32 parallel is **conceptually substantial**:

- a covenant people corporately acquired/redeemed by God;
- many within that people are corrupt/apostate;
- privilege/ownership does not establish saving status of every member.

GTY exposition:
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/80-356/the-atonement-real-or-potential

But it is not correct to say:

> «Peter simply quotes the same Greek purchase verb from Deut 32:6.»

He does not.

### Classification

- Exodus/covenant-acquisition analogy: `CANONICAL INFERENCE — STRONG ENOUGH TO CONSIDER`.
- direct lexical allusion via `ἀγοράζω`: `NOT ESTABLISHED`.

This significantly lowers the evidential weight of a simplistic Deut-32-only solution.

---

# 6. MacArthur’s corporate-redemption solution

MacArthur argues:

- Deut 32 addresses Israel as a corporately bought/redeemed people despite apostates within it;
- similarly false teachers are “among you,” located in the redeemed church-community;
- they can therefore be described in the corporate/superficial sense as bought while not being truly savingly redeemed.

Direct source:
- https://www.gty.org/sermons/80-356/the-atonement-real-or-potential

He explicitly concludes that their destruction proves they were not real believers.

### Strengths

1. Takes seriously corporate covenant categories.
2. Integrates “among you” and apostasy within visible people of God.
3. Fits strong whole-Bible distinction between corporate privilege and individual saving reality.
4. Preserves definite atonement/perseverance coherently.

### Pressure points

1. Deut 32 LXX uses `κτάομαι`, not `ἀγοράζω`.
2. Peter grammatically says the Master bought **them**, not merely their community.
3. Technical commentary tradition frequently hears Christ/redemptive purchase directly.
4. The explanation risks letting systematic theology determine a lexical result before the sentence has been heard.

### Weight

`SERIOUS CONSERVATIVE CANONICAL MODEL / MEDIUM TECHNICAL WEIGHT`.

Useful for canonical synthesis; insufficient as lexical closure by itself.

---

# 7. Schreiner’s phenomenological solution

Schreiner takes an unusually candid route.

He grants:

- `δεσπότης` is Christ;
- `ἀγοράζω` is genuinely soteriological/redemptive language;
- a purely nonsoteriological reading has a severe lexical problem.

Then he argues that Peter speaks **phenomenologically**:

- false teachers were insiders;
- they initially embraced/professed Christian faith;
- they appeared to be bought by Christ;
- appeared to know Him as Lord and Savior;
- their later apostasy revealed that they were never truly redeemed/regenerated.

Direct HTML:
- https://www.monergism.com/%E2%80%9Cproblematic-texts%E2%80%9D-definite-atonement-pastoral-and-general-epistles?page=1

Schreiner explicitly appeals to:

- Mark 4;
- 1 Cor. 11:19;
- 2 Tim. 2:19;
- 1 John 2:19;
- Matt. 7:23;
- dog/sow imagery in 2 Pet. 2:22.

### Strength

This model does not falsify the lexical field of `ἀγοράζω` merely because definite atonement is true.

It also integrates vv.20–22 rather than isolating v.1.

### Major pressure point

The phenomenological qualification is **not expressed in v.1 grammar**.

Peter does not say:

> «the Master who seemed to have bought them».

Therefore:

> phenomenological language is a **CANONICAL/SYSTEMATIC EXPLANATION**, not DIRECT EXEGESIS of the participle.

### Required project judgment

Schreiner’s reading is serious and internally coherent, but should **not** be presented as if the Greek participle itself means “appeared to buy.”

### Weight

`MAJOR REFORMED MODEL / HIGH SYSTEMATIC COHERENCE / MEDIUM LOCAL-GRAMMATICAL DIRECTNESS`.

---

# 8. Important internal tension in Schreiner’s case

Schreiner correctly says the nonsoteriological approach has a severe lexical problem.

But his own solution then relocates the semantic pressure:

- lexical meaning stays redemptive;
- factual force of the narrator’s assertion becomes phenomenological.

In effect:

> the verb means real purchase, but Peter is said not to be asserting actual purchase.

This is possible as discourse phenomenon, but it needs positive contextual evidence.

Schreiner’s evidence is primarily:

- insider status;
- prior profession;
- vv.20–22;
- final dog/sow disclosure;
- broader perseverance texts.

### Research verdict

The model is **plausible but inferential**.

It should not be used as a proof that “2 Pet 2:1 obviously supports definite atonement.”

Indeed, Schreiner himself acknowledges that the straightforward Arminian/general-atonement reading has obvious appeal.

---

# 9. View C — actual atoning purchase without proving saving application

A third model deserves more weight than it often receives in Calvinist-Arminian polemics.

It accepts:

1. `δεσπότης` = Christ;
2. `ἀγοράζω` = real redemptive purchase;
3. false teachers genuinely fall within the objective scope described by that purchase;
4. yet `purchase` is not automatically identical to regeneration, justification or final saving union.

This can be developed in several ways:

- general atonement / objective provision;
- universal purchase/ownership of humanity or visible covenant community;
- redemption language broader than applied salvation;
- purchase conferring accountability/ownership without final efficacy in every individual.

### Andrew D. Chang

“Second Peter 2:1 and the Extent of the Atonement,” *Bibliotheca Sacra* 142 (1985): 52–63.

Chang argues for actual purchase and uses the verse against strict limited-atonement readings.

### Douglas W. Kennard

“Petrine Redemption: Its Meaning and Extent,” *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 30.4 (1987): 399–405.

Kennard separates Petrine “redemption” from final salvation more sharply than standard Reformed theology, arguing that real redemptive transformation need not equal final salvation.

### Evaluation

This model’s chief strength is that it leaves the narrator’s assertion fully factual.

Its pressure point is systematic:

> what exactly does Christ accomplish for those bought, and can objective purchase be separated from final saving efficacy without weakening NT redemption theology elsewhere?

### Weight

`STRONG EXEGETICAL COMPETING MODEL / ATONEMENT SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS AMBER`.

It may challenge a simple form of definite atonement more directly than it challenges perseverance.

# 10. Critical distinction: extent of purchase ≠ application ≠ regeneration

This distinction is essential.

Suppose one grants the strongest local reading:

> Christ truly bought these false teachers in an atoning sense.

What follows directly?

### Follows

- they are described as objects of Christ’s purchase;
- their denial is therefore aggravated by ownership/redemptive privilege;
- the verse creates a real question for the **extent/efficacy model of atonement**.

### Does not follow automatically

- they had been regenerated;
- they had been justified;
- they were elect unto final salvation;
- they possessed irreversible union with Christ;
- they lost a previously possessed eternal-life status.

Those require additional premises.

### Therefore

2 Pet 2:1 is more directly a hard text for **atonement extent/efficacy** than a standalone proof of **loss of regeneration**.

This is one of the most important conclusions of the dossier.

---

# 11. The dog/sow proverb cannot erase v.1

2:22:

- dog returns to vomit;
- washed sow returns to mud.

Classic Reformed inference:

> final return reveals unchanged nature.

That inference is legitimate as a strong theological reading.

But it must not be used to rewrite earlier statements:

- v.1 says bought;
- v.20 says escaped defilements through knowledge of Christ;
- v.21 says knew the way of righteousness and turned back;
- v.22 explicitly says the sow was washed.

### Correct use of the proverb

The proverb supports:

> real change/cleansing/privilege need not equal an irreversible new nature.

It does **not** support:

> nothing real ever happened to them.

This sets up H4.

---

# 12. “They were once Christians” — define the phrase carefully

Technical commentators can say the false teachers were “once Christians” in at least three senses:

1. members/professors in Christian community;
2. persons with real Christian knowledge, moral reform and covenant privilege;
3. regenerate/justified persons savingly united to Christ.

Those are not interchangeable.

Secondary commentary synthesis attributes to Green/Davids/Bauckham the view that the teachers arose from within the churches and had meaningful prior Christian standing.

Locator leads:

- Gene L. Green, BECNT, around p.105;
- Peter H. Davids, PNTC, around pp.219–221;
- Richard Bauckham, WBC 50, around p.240 and related pp.274–281.

Research summary:
- https://biblicalscholarship.wordpress.com/2012/06/26/commentary-on-2-peter-2/

### Publication rule

Do not quote these pages until original editions are checked.

But at the argument level they strengthen this conclusion:

> **the opponents are insiders/defectors, not pagans who merely heard Christianity from afar.**

# 13. Older Reformed commentators are more diverse than later slogans

## Matthew Poole

Poole preserves multiple possibilities:

- Father/covenantal-acquisition reading;
- Christ reading as more probable;
- alternative senses of Christ’s purchase.

This is useful historically because it shows Reformed exegesis did not have one effortless answer.

Research access:
- https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/150/matthew-pooles-concise-commentary-on-the-bible/2-peter/2/1

## John Gill

Gill strongly prefers Father / Deut 32 style temporal-covenantal purchase.

Value:
- robust particular-redemption consistency;
- real corporate-redemption category.

Pressure:
- weaker against Christ-referent and `ἀγοράζω` lexical evidence than modern technical literature.

## Calvin

Calvin’s broader treatment of 2 Pet 2 emphasizes that false teachers can undergo substantial change and then reveal unchanged underlying nature; his pastoral/theological value is stronger in vv.20–22 than as a simple lexical solution to v.1.

### Historical conclusion

Do not present “the Reformed interpretation” in singular.

At minimum distinguish:

- covenantal acquisition;
- phenomenological purchase;
- objective purchase without saving application;
- broader extent models.

# 15. Strongest conditional-security reading

The strongest loss-of-salvation argument combines the whole paragraph:

1. Christ actually bought them (2:1);
2. they really escaped world defilements through knowledge of Christ (2:20);
3. they knew the way of righteousness (2:21);
4. then they turned back and became worse than before;
5. they perish in swift destruction;
6. therefore truly redeemed believers can finally apostatize.

### Strength

This reading takes every positive expression factually and integrates vv.1, 20–22.

### Its hidden extra premise

It assumes:

> actual purchase + real escape + real knowledge = regeneration/justification/election unto final life.

That equivalence is not directly stated.

Therefore the conditional-security reading is serious but still makes a **SYSTEMATIC SYNTHESIS** beyond lexical facts.

# 17. Preferred project synthesis

The project should **not** make the Father/Deut 32 reading its first-line escape hatch.

It should also **not** make Schreiner’s phenomenological qualifier sound as if it were simply the meaning of `ἀγοράζω`.

### Current strongest formulation

> **Second Peter 2:1 most naturally presents Jesus Christ as the sovereign Master who “bought” the false teachers, and `ἀγοράζω` belongs to genuine purchase/redemption language. This wording should not be weakened merely to protect a doctrine of definite atonement. At the same time, “bought” is not itself a technical synonym for regeneration, justification, or final saving union. Deuteronomy 32 supplies a real canonical analogy of a corporately acquired people containing apostates, but its Septuagint uses `κτάομαι`, not Peter’s `ἀγοράζω`, so the parallel is conceptual rather than a direct lexical quotation. Schreiner therefore takes a different Reformed route: he grants the redemptive force of the verb but reads Peter phenomenologically, as describing insiders according to the Christian status they appeared to possess; the dog/sow ending reveals their unchanged nature. That solution is coherent but inferential, because the verse itself says the Master bought them rather than merely seeming to do so. Consequently 2 Peter 2:1 remains a genuine hard text for the extent and efficacy of the atonement. Even on the strongest actual-purchase reading, however, the verse does not by itself prove that regenerated and justified elect people lose salvation; that further conclusion depends on how purchase, application, regeneration, vv.20–22, and the whole canon are integrated.**

This is currently preferable to all simpler slogans.

# 18. Pastoral theology

## 18.1. Greater privilege intensifies guilt

Whether purchase is interpreted as objective atoning scope, visible-covenant ownership or phenomenological Christian status, Peter’s rhetorical point is severe:

> these teachers deny the very Master to whom they owed allegiance.

Apostasy is not ignorance from afar. It is revolt after extraordinary light and privilege.

## 18.2. Church insider status is not final assurance

False teachers arise `among you`.

The church must not confuse:

- teaching office;
- Christian vocabulary;
- ministry success;
- past moral reform;
- public profession

with indefectible evidence of saving union.

## 18.3. Do not use perseverance to neutralize the warning

The warning against destructive teaching and practical denial is a means by which the church perseveres.

## 18.4. Do not use the warning to terrorize every struggling believer

The subjects here are not believers mourning sin and clinging to Christ. They are false teachers who introduce destructive heresies, exploit others and deny the Master.
