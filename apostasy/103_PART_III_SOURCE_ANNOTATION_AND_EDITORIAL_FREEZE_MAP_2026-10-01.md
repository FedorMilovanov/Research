## §1 — Christ predicts both Peter’s fall and return

### Primary owner

Luke 22:31–32.

Key elements:

- satanic sifting is real;
- Christ’s intercession is explicit;
- Peter’s faith is the object of prayer;
- Peter’s future `ἐπιστρέψας` is anticipated;
- his restored function is to strengthen the brothers.

### Canonical reinforcement

- John 17:6–19;
- John 21:15–19;
- Heb 7:25 as broader Christ-intercession theology, without claiming Luke depends on Hebrews.

### Reformed theological control

R. C. Sproul is useful for the broad principle that perseverance rests finally on divine preservation and Christ’s intercession, but Scripture remains the claim owner.

Ligonier resource map:

https://learn.ligonier.org/topics/apostasy

### Editorial wording

Prefer:

> “Christ predicts Peter’s real fall, prays that his faith not finally fail, and anticipates his return.”

Avoid:

> “Peter never lost faith in any meaningful sense.”

The narrative makes the fall severe.

## §2 — Peter’s denial must not be softened

### Primary owners

- Matt 26:69–75;
- Mark 14:66–72;
- Luke 22:54–62;
- John 18:15–27.

### Claim freeze

The denial is:

- repeated;
- escalating;
- socially costly/fear-driven;
- explicitly distancing Peter from Jesus.

### Practical-theology reinforcement

Thomas Brooks is useful here against using saints’ falls to normalize sin. His practical point is not “saints fall, so relax,” but that their falls are narrated together with sorrow, discipline and recovery.

Primary/source map: `89_...`, Brooks §5.4.

### Guardrail

Perseverance must not be protected by making grievous sin semantically small.

## §3 — tears, remorse and repentance

### Primary owners

- Peter: Matt 26:75; Luke 22:61–62;
- Judas: Matt 27:3–5.

### Lexical guardrail

`μεταμεληθείς` in Matt 27:3 must not be treated as a magic technical word proving that repentance is necessarily false whenever this lexeme appears.

The distinction between Peter and Judas is established by:

- prior canonical characterization;
- direction after the act;
- relation to Christ/community;
- narrated restoration vs terminal trajectory;
- Christ’s own words.

### Puritan reinforcement — Thomas Watson

Watson’s repentance analysis is highly useful because he distinguishes:

- natural tenderness;
- legal terror;
- superficial sorrow;
- repentance involving changed judgment/affections/life and return toward God.

Primary public text:

https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/commandments.vi.ii.html

### Correct use

Watson illustrates the anatomy of repentance **after** Matthew has been exegeted.

### Incorrect use

Do not use Watson to claim the Greek lexeme itself settles Judas’s spiritual state.

### Claim freeze

### Critical distinction

`chosen as one of the Twelve` ≠ automatically `savingly elected to eternal life`.

### Guardrail

## §5 — Judas’s hidden trajectory: greed under religious language

### Primary owner

John 12:1–8.

### Claim freeze

John explicitly supplies a hidden moral trajectory before the public betrayal:

- stated concern for the poor;
- narrator’s disclosure of theft/greed;
- money later becomes part of betrayal.

### Puritan reinforcement — Brooks

Useful mechanisms from *Precious Remedies*:

- bait displayed, hook concealed;
- vice painted in virtue’s colors;
- tolerated “small” sin becomes encroaching.

Owner: `89_...` §§5.1–5.3.

### Editorial guardrail

Do not convert Judas into a deterministic psychological ladder applicable in exactly the same order to every apostate.

---

## §6 — real denial ≠ automatic final apostasy

### Primary owners

- Luke 22;
- denial narratives;
- John 21.

### Claim freeze

Strong formulation allowed:

> **A real verbal denial of Christ under fear is not, by that fact alone, identical with final apostasy; Peter is the canonical counterexample.**

### What Peter does not prove

He does not prove:

- denial is safe;
- warnings are empty;
- prolonged conscious repudiation should receive assurance;
- restoration eliminates discipline/consequences.

### Puritan/Reformed reinforcement

Flavel’s distinction between breach/loss of peace and automatic breach of covenant is pastorally useful (`89_...` §6.3), but it must remain downstream from the narrative.

### Primary owner

Do not let v.13 cancel v.12.

## §11 — David: terrible sin, real discipline, real restoration

### Primary owners

- 2 Sam 11–12;
- Psalm 51, with superscription/traditional linkage handled normally.

### Claim freeze

David is a canonical control against two opposite errors:

1. true believers cannot fall gravely;
2. grave fall itself proves terminal apostasy.

### Guardrail

Restoration does not erase temporal consequences.

### Brooks reinforcement

Use Brooks’ treatment of saints’ falls only to reinforce the narrative pattern: sin + grief/repentance/recovery, never sin-as-permission.

## §12 — Manasseh: severity of sin does not set a human limit on mercy

### Primary owner

2 Chr 33:1–20.

### Claim freeze

Manasseh provides narrated:

- extreme idolatry/evil;
- humiliation;
- prayer;
- restoration;
- reform.

### Guardrail

Do not infer from Manasseh that every severe apostate will repent.

The narrower point is:

> magnitude of sin alone is not a biblical proof that repentance has become metaphysically impossible.

## §§15–16 — what to say to the denier / current unbeliever

### Biblical principle

The response must track present posture:

- repentant denier → call to Christ, serious restoration, not despair;
- conscious ongoing repudiation → no detached assurance, real warning, continuing call to repent.

### Watson reinforcement

Watson is useful for both halves:

- counterfeit repentance must not be mistaken for conversion;
- enormous sin does not place a repentant sinner outside promised mercy.

Primary public text:

https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/commandments.vi.ii.html

### Guardrail

Do not diagnose psychiatric/medical causes of despair in this article.

## §20 — final two-sentence balance

The draft’s governing pair should remain:

> **Final rejection of Christ is a terrible reality; biblical warnings must not be neutralized.**

and

> **A grievous fall, even a real denial, must not be declared irreversible where Scripture still gives the category of repentance and return.**

### Final pastoral vector

The conclusion should send readers toward:

- watchfulness;
- humility;
- repentance;
- rescue;
- sober warning;
- Christ’s keeping power.

Not toward speculative identification of “secret apostates.”
