# 5. 2 Timothy 2 status

## GREEN

- 2:11–13 belongs to perseverance/exhortation context;
- v.12 contains a genuine denial warning;
- `ἀρνέομαι` cannot by itself prove terminal apostasy because Peter is a canonical restored-denial control;
- v.13 explicitly grounds Christ’s faithfulness in His inability to deny Himself;
- grammar does not require “faithful only to punish”;
- grammar does not permit v.13 to erase v.12;
- real false teaching can `upset the faith of some`;
- `μέντοι` answers real damage with divine stability, not with denial of damage;
- first seal inscription has Numbers 16 background;
- divine knowledge/ownership and ethical departure from unrighteousness stand together;
- 2:24–26 requires hopeful gentle correction because God may grant repentance even to serious opponents.

## Important methodological closure

The project statement:

> Christ’s faithfulness guarantees both His saving promises and His warnings

is classified as:

`CANONICAL / SYSTEMATIC REFORMED SYNTHESIS`

not:

`LEXICAL GLOSS OF πιστός`.

This distinction is now mandatory.

# 6. Titus status

## GREEN

- Titus 1:16 is profession/deeds contradiction: profess God, deny by works;
- this is a settled false-teacher profile, not a formula equating every Christian sin with apostasy;
- `ἀδόκιμος` is unfit/failing test, not automatic later decretal `reprobate` terminology;
- `αἱρετικός` in 3:10 is best controlled initially by divisive/factious sense;
- first and second admonition precede real ecclesial boundary;
- `ἐξέστραπται` presents an established warped condition but not metaphysical proof of irreversible future;
- `αὐτοκατάκριτος` = self-condemned in the context of persistence after correction;
- Titus 3:5 blocks works-righteousness while the whole letter makes good works fruit/evidence of grace;
- Charnock/Owen are strong post-exegetical practical theology controls.

Questions:

- force of `ἀδύνατον`;
- full force of `φωτισθέντας`, `γευσαμένους`, `μετόχους γενηθέντας πνεύματος ἁγίου`;
- Hebrews’ own use of `μέτοχος`;
- `παραπεσόντας`;
- syntactic force of the participial clauses around “crucifying again”;
- agricultural analogy vv.7–8;
- decisive force of v.9: `better things — and things belonging to salvation`;
- whether previous privileges are real but distinguished from “things belonging to salvation.”

Modern conservative priority:

- Thomas Schreiner;
- Peter O’Brien where usable;
- George Guthrie;
- Paul Ellingworth for technical grammar with doctrinal filtering;
- Dennis Johnson as Reformed accessible control;

## P0-H3 — 2 Peter 2:1

Main problem:

> `denying the Master who bought them`.

Thomas Schreiner himself notes the lexical difficulty for readings that make `ἀγοράζω` wholly non-soteriological: NT usage strongly favors purchase/redemption language.

Therefore the dossier must compare:

1. true redemption later lost;

3. covenant/exodus master-purchase background;

No easy Calvinist shortcut is allowed.
