# 4. What “quotation-free drafting” means

A draft may now use:

1. Scripture directly under the project’s translation/citation policy;
2. primary historical facts with exact source attribution;
3. paraphrases of technical scholars where the claim has already been independently established;
4. paraphrases of Puritan practical theology from exact work/section custody;
5. fair summaries of Lutheran/Arminian/Free Grace alternatives;
6. explicit variant notes from Jude/Revelation dossiers.

It may **not yet** use quotation marks around:

- an unverified page from a modern commentary;
- a quote copied from TGC without checking the author and source trail;
- a viral Puritan saying without primary text;
- a historical phrase whose translation/source chain is unclear;
- a secondary historian where edition/page has not been frozen.

### Editorial principle

> **Paraphrase is not a lower standard when source custody is strong; invented precision is.**

# 9. Part V drafting gate — GREEN core

## Core pastoral propositions allowed

1. A person whom Scripture tells us to restore/rescue must not be prematurely pronounced terminal.
2. Restoration does not mean minimizing sin.
3. Church discipline and correction can be restorative rather than identical with final condemnation.
4. Warning texts are means of wakefulness, not raw material for diagnostic cruelty.
5. Assurance and presumption must be distinguished.
6. Despair and presumption are opposite errors.
7. Perseverance theology should generate watchfulness, prayer, exhortation, mortification, doctrinal vigilance and rescue.
8. A visible church cannot read the decree of election directly from one frightening episode.

- exact timetable for restoring a public denier;

- automatic church-office restoration after public lapse;

These require text/category-specific sourcing and should not be improvised in the initial flagship.

### Drafting rule

Part V may be drafted now if it remains **biblical-principle driven**, not pseudo-canonical procedure manual.

# 12. Models that must remain fair

When the reader-facing prose explicitly answers:

> “Can a truly regenerate believer finally lose salvation?”

it must not pretend the only serious alternatives are liberal.

At minimum, where relevant:

- Confessional Lutheran;
- conservative/classical Arminian;
- Reformed warning-as-means;
- Free Grace only where materially different.

### Reformed conclusion may be firm

Fairness does not require agnosticism.

But the article must demonstrate why the Reformed synthesis best accounts for:

- positive warning language;
- perseverance promises;
- cases like Judas/1 John 2:19;
- recoverable cases like Peter/James 5;
- covenant privileges that are real without automatically proving saving union.
