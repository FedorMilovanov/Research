# 1. Почему нужен отдельный closure

Предыдущий dossier хорошо закрыл 1 Тим. 1:18–20 и 4:1–16, но P1-очередь требует прочитать **всю эпистолу как единую карту отклонения от веры**.

Ключевые узлы:

- 1:6 — `ἀστοχήσαντες` — некоторые **уклонились / промахнулись мимо цели**;
- 1:19 — `ἀπωσάμενοι` добрую совесть — **оттолкнули / отвергли** её;
- 1:19 — `περὶ τὴν πίστιν ἐναυάγησαν` — **потерпели кораблекрушение относительно веры**;
- 4:1 — `ἀποστήσονταί τινες τῆς πίστεως` — **некоторые отступят от веры**;
- 5:15 — `ἐξετράπησαν ὀπίσω τοῦ σατανᾶ` — **некоторые уже свернули вслед сатаны**;
- 6:10 — `ἀπεπλανήθησαν ἀπὸ τῆς πίστεως` — некоторые через любовь к деньгам **заблудились / ушли от веры**;
- 6:21 — `περὶ τὴν πίστιν ἠστόχησαν` — некоторые, исповедуя лжеименное знание, **промахнулись относительно веры**.

Это не случайные метафоры, разбросанные по письму. Они образуют литературно-богословскую сеть вокруг главного поручения Тимофея:

> **сохранять апостольскую истину, добрую совесть и благочестивую жизнь в церкви, где часть людей уже отклонилась, а новые отступления ещё ожидаются.**

---

# 2. Главный вывод заранее

> **1 Тимофею учит реальности серьёзного doctrinal-and-moral defection внутри сферы христианского исповедания. Павел не сводит отступление к одному психологическому акту: люди могут уклониться от цели, сознательно оттолкнуть добрую совесть, потерпеть кораблекрушение относительно веры, отступить от веры под воздействием ложного учения, свернуть вслед сатаны в поведении, заблудиться от веры через любовь к деньгам и промахнуться относительно веры через псевдознание. Но эти выражения неодинаковы по силе и не дают права автоматически объявить каждое падение необратимой final apostasy или восстановить скрытую историю regeneration каждого человека.**

# 3. Каркас письма: цель поручения и повторяющийся глагол `ἀστοχέω`

## 3.1. 1 Тим. 1:5

Цель (`τέλος`) поручения:

- любовь;
- из чистого сердца;
- доброй совести;
- нелицемерной веры.

Это важно: Paul задаёт одновременно **ethical, conscience and faith** frame.

## 3.2. 1 Тим. 1:6

`ὧν τινες ἀστοχήσαντες ἐξετράπησαν εἰς ματαιολογίαν`

Некоторые, **уклонившись / промахнувшись** от этих вещей, свернули к пустословию.

`ἀστοχέω` буквально связан с промахом мимо цели; в Pastorals он становится важным словом doctrinal deviation.

## 3.3. 1 Тим. 6:21

Письмо заканчивается тем же semantic family:

`ἥν τινες ἐπαγγελλόμενοι περὶ τὴν πίστιν ἠστόχησαν`

Некоторые, исповедуя «знание» ложно так называемое, **промахнулись относительно веры**.

### Inclusio-like observation

Не нужно слишком формально доказывать строгую rhetorical inclusio, но композиционный эффект очевиден:

- начало: отклонение от цели поручения;
- конец: отклонение относительно веры.

В центре письма Paul показывает несколько механизмов такого отклонения.

# 4. 1 Тим. 1:18–20 — сознательный отказ от совести и «кораблекрушение»

## 4.1. `ἔχων πίστιν καὶ ἀγαθὴν συνείδησιν`

Timothy должен держать вместе:

- веру;
- добрую совесть.

Paul не знает здоровой doctrine, которая может долго сосуществовать с сознательным подавлением нравственной совести без духовного риска.

## 4.2. `ἥν τινες ἀπωσάμενοι`

Antecedent по ближайшему согласованию — `ἀγαθὴν συνείδησιν`.

`ἀπωθέομαι / ἀπωθέω` имеет сильную волевую окраску:

- оттолкнуть;
- отвергнуть;
- отказаться принять.

Следовательно, это не просто слабость или случайное падение.

## 4.3. `περὶ τὴν πίστιν ἐναυάγησαν`

`ναυαγέω` — терпеть кораблекрушение.

Метафора катастрофична.

Mounce Reverse Interlinear сохраняет структуру: некоторые, отвергнув совесть, **shipwrecked the faith / suffered shipwreck concerning the faith**.

Knight, NIGTC, около pp.109–111, связывает выражение с серьёзным разрушением их отношения к вере и рассматривает v.20 как церковно-дисциплинарную меру.

### Что доказано

- моральное отвержение совести и doctrinal catastrophe связаны;
- состояние серьёзное, а не «minor stumble»;
- церковь не должна относиться к нему как к нейтральной ошибке.

### Что НЕ доказано одним образом кораблекрушения

- что каждый названный мужчина был прежде regeneration-certain;
- что forensic justification был «аннулирован» в данный момент;
- что final damnation уже стала необратимой.

# 5. 1 Тим. 1:20 — «предал сатане»: severe discipline с педагогической целью

`οὓς παρέδωκα τῷ Σατανᾷ ἵνα παιδευθῶσιν μὴ βλασφημεῖν`

Два элемента нельзя разрывать.

## 5.1. Severity

«Предать сатане» естественно сопоставляется с 1 Кор. 5:5 и наиболее вероятно включает исключение из защищённой церковной fellowship в мир, где Satan действует как ruler/usurper.

Knight, p.111, прямо понимает это как дисциплину через удаление из fellowship.

Mounce, pp.69, 72, также рассматривает ecclesial-exclusion / disciplinary background.

## 5.2. Corrective aim

Purpose clause:

`ἵνα παιδευθῶσιν μὴ βλασφημεῖν`

— чтобы они **были научены / дисциплинированы** не богохульствовать.

Следовательно:

> **даже после “shipwreck” апостольское действие ещё формулируется педагогически.**

Это чрезвычайно важно для общей taxonomy:

`severe church discipline ≠ automatic declaration of irreversible final apostasy`.

### Но не обратная ошибка

Corrective purpose не означает гарантированный repentance outcome.

Если Именей 2 Тим. 2 — тот же человек, его позднейшая деятельность показывает, что restorative intent дисциплины не гарантирует видимого восстановления.

---

# 6. 3:14–16 → 4:1: pillar of truth против departure from the faith

Непосредственно перед 4:1 Paul говорит:

- церковь — дом Бога живого;
- pillar and support of truth;
- confessing mystery of godliness centered in Christ.

Затем:

`Τὸ δὲ Πνεῦμα ῥητῶς λέγει...`

**Но Дух ясно/прямо говорит**, что в later times некоторые отступят от веры.

Контраст важен:

> proximity to the church as pillar of truth does not create automatic immunity from apostasy.

False teaching возникает внутри visible ecclesial sphere и должен быть встречен верным teaching ministry.

# 7. 1 Тим. 4:1 — `ἀποστήσονταί τινες τῆς πίστεως`

## 7.1. Grammar

- lemma: `ἀφίστημι`;
- `ἀποστήσονται`: future middle indicative, third plural;
- genitive: `τῆς πίστεως`.

Значение в контексте:

> **некоторые отойдут / отступят от веры.**

Здесь слово `ἀφίστημι` получает явно духовно-доктринальный объект.

## 7.2. Knight exact control

Knight, *Pastoral Epistles* (NIGTC), pp.187–189, обсуждает 4:1–2 и seared/branded conscience.

NET technical note также ссылается на Knight p.189 по спору:

- branded as ownership;
- branded as penal mark;
- seared/desensitized conscience.

### Exact conclusion

Текст несомненно учит **реальному departure from Christian faith**.

Он не позволяет сказать:

> «никакого настоящего отступления в церкви вообще не бывает; только внешне кажется».

Но он также не говорит в одной строке:

> «каждый такой человек был regeneration-certain, justified и elect, а затем перестал быть таковым».

Это последующий systematic inference.

---

# 8. `τῆς πίστεως`: objective “the faith” и personal allegiance нельзя разорвать

В Pastoral Epistles `πίστις` используется гибко:

- personal faith/trust;
- faithfulness;
- sphere/content of Christian faith;
- perhaps combinations where subjective/objective distinction is too modernly rigid.

В 4:1 `τῆς πίστεως` очень естественно означает **the Christian faith**.

В 6:21 `περὶ τὴν πίστιν` — отклонение относительно faith.

Но из этого нельзя сделать вывод:

> «раз речь об objective doctrine, значит личные спасительные отношения вообще не затронуты».

В 1 Тимофею doctrine и allegiance соединены:

- false doctrine служит другим spiritual powers;
- truth produces godliness;
- false teachers’ consciences damaged;
- money pulls people away from faith;
- Timothy’s perseverance in life/doctrine instrumentally saves himself and hearers.

### Better formulation

> **“The faith” обозначает апостольскую Christian truth/confession, но departure from that faith является personal religious defection, а не только ошибкой в экзамене по богословию.**

---

# 9. 4:1–5 — apostasy может быть не либеральной, а аскетически «строгой»

False teaching включает:

- запрет брака;
- воздержание от foods, которые God создал для thanksgiving.

Это разрушает популярный stereotype:

> apostasy = только loosening morality.

Paul показывает другую форму:

> **false holiness может отойти от gospel by denying God’s good creation and binding consciences where God has not bound them.**

Fee’s treatment of 4:1–5 (public secondary locators place his discussion around pp.60–62 in the older NIBC/UBCS edition) correctly situates error inside larger Ephesian false-teacher problem.

### Source-policy qualification on Fee

Fee используется здесь как серьёзный Greek-aware exegete.

Его denominational/systematic conclusions не принимаются автоматически там, где спор выходит за text-level exegesis.

---

# 10. 4:6–16 — Paul’s answer to apostasy: ordinary means, не panic

Against demonic teaching Timothy должен:

- питаться словами веры и доброго учения;
- reject myths;
- упражняться в благочестии;
- быть примером;
- заниматься public reading;
- exhort;
- teach;
- watch himself and teaching;
- persist.

Кульминация 4:16:

`ἔπεχε σεαυτῷ καὶ τῇ διδασκαλίᾳ ... ἐπίμενε αὐτοῖς`

и:

`τοῦτο γὰρ ποιῶν καὶ σεαυτὸν σώσεις καὶ τοὺς ἀκούοντάς σου`.

### Preservation-as-means

Paul свободно говорит, что faithful ministry **спасёт** Timothy и hearers, не делая Timothy atoning savior.

Это одна из самых ясных Pauline формулировок instrumentality:

> **God preserves through means: watchfulness, sound teaching, godly example, persistence and hearing.**

Это созвучно:

- Иак. 5:19–20;
- Иуд. 22–23;
- 1 Кор. 9–10;
- Евр. 3:12–14.

# 11. 1 Тим. 5:15 — `ἐξετράπησαν ὀπίσω τοῦ σατανᾶ`

Это P1-пробел старого dossier.

## 11.1. Immediate context

Paul обсуждает younger widows:

- strong desires in conflict with a prior commitment to Christ (5:11–12);
- idleness;
- house-to-house wandering;
- gossip/busybodiness;
- danger giving the adversary occasion for reproach;
- therefore he counsels younger widows to marry, bear children, manage household.

Then:

`ἤδη γάρ τινες ἐξετράπησαν ὀπίσω τοῦ σατανᾶ`

— «ибо некоторые уже свернули вслед сатаны».

## 11.2. `ἐκτρέπω` family

Same family appears in 1:6:

- 1:6 `ἐξετράπησαν εἰς ματαιολογίαν` — turned aside into vain discussion;
- 5:15 `ἐξετράπησαν ὀπίσω τοῦ σατανᾶ` — turned aside after Satan;
- 6:20 Timothy is to **turn away from / avoid** (`ἐκτρεπόμενος`) profane chatter.

This creates another letter-wide contrast:

> **wrong turning vs ministerial turning-away from error.**

## 11.3. How severe?

Very severe:

- Satan is not neutral rhetoric;
- moral direction is explicitly anti-Christian/adversarial.

But context does **not require** formal doctrinal repudiation of Christianity.

Older Greek commentary correctly notes that it can denote:

- un-Christian/carnal conduct;
- perhaps false-teaching alignment;
- but not necessarily a fully formal renunciation of the faith.

### Classification

`5:15 = STRONG MORAL/RELIGIOUS DEFECTION LANGUAGE, NOT AUTOMATIC TERMINAL VERDICT.`

This is exactly why the project needs graded categories.

# 12. 5:12 and “first faith/pledge” — do not smuggle a conclusion

The immediately preceding phrase:

`τὴν πρώτην πίστιν ἠθέτησαν`

has been translated/interpreted as:

- abandoning first faith;
- breaking first pledge/commitment;
- violating a prior commitment associated with enrolled widowhood / devotion to Christ.

The context is notoriously disputed.

### Guardrail

Do **not** build doctrine of loss of regeneration on English “cast off their first faith” without first settling:

- widow enrollment question;
- nature of the pledge;
- meaning of `πίστις` here;
- relation of marriage desire itself to prior commitment.

P1-8 closure does not require solving every detail of widow enrollment.

The secure point is narrower:

> some conduct among the younger widows had already taken a direction Paul describes as following Satan.

# 13. 1 Тим. 6:3–10 — greed inside false-teacher pathology

## 13.1. 6:3–5

False teacher:

- teaches differently;
- does not consent to healthy words of Jesus Christ;
- rejects teaching according to godliness;
- is conceited;
- has diseased craving for controversies;
- is corrupted in mind;
- deprived of truth;
- supposes godliness to be `πορισμός` — a means of gain.

Then Paul turns the word `gain` around:

> godliness with contentment is great gain.

## 13.2. 6:9–10

Those wanting to be rich:

- fall into temptation;
- snare;
- many foolish/harmful desires;
- which plunge people into ruin and destruction.

Then:

`ῥίζα γὰρ πάντων τῶν κακῶν ἐστιν ἡ φιλαργυρία`

and:

`ἧς τινες ὀρεγόμενοι ἀπεπλανήθησαν ἀπὸ τῆς πίστεως`

— some, craving/loving money, **wandered away from the faith**.

## 13.3. Grammar

- `ὀρεγόμενοι`: present middle participle, pursuing/desiring;
- `ἀπεπλανήθησαν`: aorist passive-form verb from `ἀποπλανάω`, here “were led/wandered away”;
- `ἀπὸ τῆς πίστεως`: away from the faith.

The following image is equally severe:

`ἑαυτοὺς περιέπειραν ὀδύναις πολλαῖς`

— they pierced themselves through with many pains.

Mounce’s technical discussion is publicly cited around p.348 for this unit; his open Pastoral Epistles course summarizes 6:3–10 as a **downward spiral and eventual destruction of the false teacher especially related to love of money**.

### Major canonical mechanism

Money is not a side moral issue.

It can change the direction of faith itself:

`desire for gain → rationalization → false ministry → wandering from faith → self-inflicted destruction`.

Compare:

- Balaam;
- Gehazi;
- Judas;
- 2 Pet. 2;
- Jude 11;
- Demas / love of present age as adjacent, not identical mechanism.

# 14. Is 6:10 about false teachers only?

Do not overrestrict.

6:3–5 clearly discusses false teachers and profit-minded pseudo-godliness.

6:9 broadens grammatically to:

`οἱ δὲ βουλόμενοι πλουτεῖν`

— **those who want to be rich**.

Therefore 6:10 can apply to more than formally recognized teachers.

### Publication-safe conclusion

> **The false teachers exemplify the money–error nexus, but Paul phrases the danger broadly enough to warn the church against the desire for wealth itself.**

This is pastorally stronger and grammatically safer.

# 15. 6:20–21 — “knowledge falsely so called” and another `ἀστοχέω`

Paul closes:

`Ὦ Τιμόθεε, τὴν παραθήκην φύλαξον`

— guard the deposit.

He must turn away from:

- profane empty talk;
- `ἀντιθέσεις` of `ψευδωνύμου γνώσεως` — oppositions/contradictions of knowledge falsely so called.

Then:

`ἥν τινες ἐπαγγελλόμενοι περὶ τὴν πίστιν ἠστόχησαν`

— some professing it **missed the mark / deviated concerning the faith**.

NET’s technical note translates the sense “have deviated concerning the faith.”

## 15.1. Do not anachronistically say “second-century Gnosticism”

The text definitely speaks of false claim to `γνῶσις`.

It does **not** require the fully developed Valentinian/Marcionite systems of the second century.

Later Gnostics naturally appropriated such terminology, but publication should say:

> **knowledge-claim / speculative false teaching**, not “Paul is certainly naming mature second-century Gnosticism.”

This also avoids allowing a disputed historical reconstruction to control exegesis.

# 16. Whole-letter lexical trajectory

| Text | Greek | Movement | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:6 | `ἀστοχήσαντες ... ἐξετράπησαν` | miss goal → turn to vain talk | doctrinal/moral deviation |
| 1:19 | `ἀπωσάμενοι` | actively reject good conscience | volitional moral rejection |
| 1:19 | `ἐναυάγησαν` | shipwreck concerning faith | catastrophic faith-relation |
| 4:1 | `ἀποστήσονται τῆς πίστεως` | depart from the faith | explicit doctrinal apostasy |
| 5:15 | `ἐξετράπησαν ὀπίσω τοῦ σατανᾶ` | turn after Satan | serious moral/religious defection; context-specific |
| 6:10 | `ἀπεπλανήθησαν ἀπὸ τῆς πίστεως` | wander away from faith | greed-driven departure |
| 6:21 | `ἠστόχησαν περὶ τὴν πίστιν` | miss/deviate concerning faith | pseudo-knowledge-driven doctrinal deviation |

### Major synthesis

1 Timothy has no one-size-fits-all apostasy verb.

Paul deliberately uses several movement/destruction metaphors.

That diversity should be preserved rather than flattened into:

> “every verb means exactly irreversible loss of regeneration”

or:

> “none of the verbs has salvation significance.”

Both are exegetically crude.

---

# 17. Faith + conscience + doctrine + godliness form one system

The letter repeatedly ties together:

- `πίστις`;
- conscience;
- truth;
- doctrine;
- godliness;
- money/desire;
- conduct.

## Negative chain

`bad desire / rejected conscience / pride / greed → doctrinal distortion → departure from faith → harm to self and others`.

## Positive chain

`apostolic truth → good conscience → godliness → contentment → watch life and doctrine → persist → instrumental salvation of self/hearers`.

### Canonical value

This undermines two false dichotomies:

1. “doctrine matters, life is secondary”;
2. “life matters, doctrine is merely intellectual.”

Paul treats corrupted life and corrupted doctrine as mutually reinforcing dangers.

---

# 18. Reformed soteriological synthesis

A strong Reformed reading should avoid the weak move:

> “departure from the faith only means they were never near anything real.”

The text says more:

- they were inside the ecclesial sphere;
- some had real Christian profession;
- some were teachers;
- some experienced disciplinary relation to the church;
- apostolic truth was genuinely known/professed;
- departure is catastrophic.

But Reformed theology distinguishes:

- visible profession/covenant privilege;
- temporary/common operations;
- saving union/regeneration;
- final perseverance.

Thus:

> **the fact of real apostasy from Christian profession/doctrine does not itself prove apostasy from eternal election or irreversible regeneration.**

The perseverance side is not defended by weakening 1 Timothy’s verbs, but by the wider canonical account of God’s keeping and by recognizing warnings/discipline/teaching as appointed means.

# 19. MacArthur / GCC conservative control — useful, but not lexical authority

John MacArthur’s exposition gives a clear conservative confessional reading:

- 1 Tim. 4:1 is deliberate abandonment of truth once outwardly affirmed;
- “the faith” is the Christian body of truth;
- apostasy differs from ordinary doubt;
- demonic deception and human responsibility coexist.

GTY also repeatedly links:

- 1:19 shipwreck;
- 4:1 departure;
- 6:20–21 guarding the deposit;
- 6:10 money-driven wandering.

Permanent examples:

- “Fighting the Noble War, Part 2,” 1 Tim. 1:18–19: https://www.gty.org/sermons/54-8/fighting-the-noble-war-part-2
- “Understanding the Seducing Spirit,” 1 Tim. 4:1–5: https://www.gty.org/resources/study-guides/print/54-29/understanding-the-seducing-spirit
- “The Danger in Loving Money,” 1 Tim. 6:6–10: https://www.gty.org/sermons/54-46/the-danger-in-loving-money
- “Handling Treasure, Part 2,” 1 Tim. 6:20–21: https://www.gty.org/sermons/54-50/handling-treasure-part-2
- “God’s Demand for Discernment,” Shepherds Conference 2019: https://www.gty.org/sermons/TM19-11/gods-demand-for-discernment-john-macarthur

### Qualification

MacArthur’s systematic claim that apostates never possessed saving faith is a **Reformed systematic conclusion**, not a lexical meaning of `ἀφίστημι`.

The dossier therefore uses him for confessional coherence and pastoral force after grammar, not instead of grammar.

# 24. Puritan “solid food”: John Owen on apostasy mechanisms

John Owen’s *The Nature and Causes of Apostasy from the Gospel* is unusually valuable for the practical synthesis because his taxonomy overlaps strikingly with 1 Timothy:

- corrupt doctrine can accommodate unmortified sin;
- false religion can soothe/deceive conscience;
- ignorance exposes professors to error;
- intellectual pride and curiosity create instability;
- love of the world is a cause of defection;
- Satan works through these mechanisms;
- judicial blindness may follow resistance;
- merely outward orthodoxy without gospel holiness does not secure perseverance.

Permanent public text:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.toc.html

Especially relevant chapters:
- causes of doctrinal apostasy: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.x.html
- false religion and conscience: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.viii.html
- ignorance and error: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.ix.html

### Strong practical insight

Owen’s framework explains how 1 Timothy’s apparently different dangers can belong to one spiritual ecology:

`unmortified desire + compromised conscience + pride/curiosity + false doctrine + worldly gain + satanic deception`.

### Guardrail

Owen is not used to determine aorist morphology or the lexical meaning of `ἀφίστημι`; he supplies practical-theological synthesis after exegesis.

# 27. Competing soteriological readings

## 27.1. Reformed perseverance

Strong form:

- the departures are real;
- the people can be genuinely integrated into visible church/profession;
- Christian doctrine can genuinely have been known and affirmed;
- some forms may involve temporary/non-saving faith;
- final apostasy manifests lack of persevering saving faith;
- warnings, teaching and discipline are means God uses to preserve His own.

This reading must not downgrade `shipwreck`, `depart`, `wander` into unreal language.

## 27.2. Wesleyan/Arminian conditional security

Strong form:

- some phrases naturally permit the idea of people genuinely within Christian faith who later abandon it;
- 4:1 can be read as actual believers departing saving faith;
- 6:10 can be read as people once believing who wander away;
- therefore final salvation remains conditional on continuing faith.

This view should not be caricatured as teaching that every single post-conversion sin instantly cancels salvation.

## 27.3. Free Grace

A typical Free Grace response may distinguish:

- loss/corruption of discipleship and doctrinal fidelity;
- temporal discipline/reward;
- from irreversible possession of eternal life.

But the model bears a real exegetical burden:

> 1 Timothy repeatedly uses severe movement/destruction language tied to “the faith,” demonic teaching, Satan, ruin/destruction and instrumental salvation.

A purely rewards-level reading risks under-reading the rhetoric.

### Editorial rule

The flagship should present the strongest form of each model and then adjudicate canonically, not with lexical shortcuts.

# 28. 5:15 is the crucial anti-flattening control

The project must preserve this distinction:

- 4:1 explicitly says **depart from the faith**;
- 5:15 says **turn aside after Satan** in a specific behavioral/widow context;
- 6:10 explicitly says **wander away from the faith**;
- 6:21 says **miss the mark concerning the faith**.

If we call all four “exactly the same terminal apostasy,” we erase Paul’s own lexical and contextual differentiation.

### Better taxonomy

- 4:1 = explicit doctrinal apostasy;
- 5:15 = serious satanward behavioral/religious deviation, final status unstated;
- 6:10 = greed-driven departure from faith;
- 6:21 = pseudo-knowledge-driven doctrinal deviation.

---

# 29. 6:10 is the crucial “desire can move doctrine” control

Paul does not say only:

> “bad theology causes bad behavior.”

He also shows:

> **disordered desire can cause departure from faith.**

Love of money can:

- reshape ministry motives;
- create false teaching for gain;
- silence conscience;
- direct trust toward wealth;
- lead away from Christian faith.

This belongs centrally in the canon-wide mechanism matrix.

---

# 30. 6:20–21 is the crucial “intellectual pride can move faith” control

At the opposite-looking pole from greed stands pseudo-knowledge.

But Paul places both inside one letter.

A person may depart through:

- sensual/material desire;
- or elite intellectual claim.

Owen’s practical theology is especially valuable here: curiosity, pride and desire for novelty can destabilize people who will not submit mind/conscience to revelation.

Thus apostasy is not class-specific:

- greedy minister;
- ascetic rigorist;
- idle moral wanderer;
- speculative intellectual

can all move away from truth through different doors.

---

# 31. What 1 Timothy establishes with high confidence

1. Real defection from Christian faith occurs in the visible church sphere.
2. “The faith” can denote the apostolic Christian truth/confession.
3. Rejection of good conscience and doctrinal shipwreck are connected.
4. Shipwreck is catastrophic language, not a minor discipleship setback.
5. Church discipline can have a restorative/educative aim even in severe cases.
6. The Spirit explicitly predicts departure from the faith.
7. False doctrine may be mediated through satanic/demonic deception and human hypocrisy simultaneously.
8. Apostasy may be ascetic/legalistic, not only sensual/libertine.
9. Turning after Satan in 5:15 is severe but contextually not identical to explicit 4:1 doctrinal apostasy.
10. Love of money can lead people away from the faith.
11. Pseudo-knowledge can lead people to miss the mark concerning the faith.
12. Sound doctrine, godliness, good conscience, contentment and persevering ministry belong together.
13. Paul describes faithful ministry as an instrument by which Timothy and hearers are “saved.”
14. Therefore divine preservation and human watchfulness are not competitors.

---

# 32. What 1 Timothy does NOT establish by itself

It does not establish by lexical force alone:

- that every person who departs had saving regeneration;
- that none of them had any real prior Christian experience;
- that every deviation is irreversible;
- that 5:15 is necessarily formal doctrinal apostasy;
- that “first faith” in 5:12 certainly means lost regeneration rather than violated pledge/commitment;
- that every wealthy Christian is in apostasy;
- that money itself is evil;
- that `ψευδώνυμος γνῶσις` names fully developed second-century Gnosticism;
- that discipline guarantees repentance;
- that Timothy merits salvation by pastoral works;
- that every occurrence of `πίστις` has exactly the same subjective/objective nuance.
