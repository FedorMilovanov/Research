# I. Пс. 118(119):176 — удивительный финал псалма верности

## 1. Последняя строка не звучит как самодовольство

После 175 стихов любви к закону псалмопевец заканчивает:

> «Я заблудился / если заблужусь как потерянная овца; взыщи раба Твоего, ибо заповедей Твоих я не забыл».

### Textual/exegetical nuance

Cambridge отмечает две основные возможности:

- ancient-version-like reading: **«я заблудился»**;
- Masoretic accentuation may support something like: **«если/когда я заблужусь, взыщи меня как потерянную овцу»**.

Также спорно, преимущественно ли речь о:

- нравственном уклонении;
- внешней изгнаннической/преследуемой потерянности;
- образе, намеренно способном вмещать оба измерения.

Sources/discovery:
- Cambridge on Psalm 119
- collected Psalm 119:176 commentaries

### Editorial rule

Do not use Ps 119:176 as a simplistic proof that regenerate believer has definitely committed doctrinal apostasy. The text itself preserves ambiguity of moral/outward wandering.

---

# II. Но одна вещь ясна: «потерянная овца» здесь всё ещё названа Божьим рабом

## 2. `seek your servant`

The speaker does not say:

> «I am autonomous; I will recover myself».

He asks God to **seek** him.

At the same time:

> «I do not forget your commandments.»

### Critical distinction

Even on a moral-wandering reading, the verse distinguishes:

- serious wandering;
- from complete repudiation of God’s word.

Cambridge explicitly sees confession of weakness/failure as compatible with continuing devotion; older Reformed readings likewise emphasize temporary wandering without final forgetting.

### Project significance

This is a strong OT analogue of Peter:

> grievous deviation can coexist with a deeper orientation God restores.

It does not establish hidden regeneration mechanics by itself, but it blocks “all wandering = final apostasy.”

---

# III. Ис. 53:6 — universal wandering and the Servant’s substitution

## 3. `All we like sheep have gone astray`

Here wandering is not a special category of ex-Christians.

It describes the human/covenant sin problem:

- each turned to his own way;
- Yahweh lays iniquity on Servant.

### Importance for 1 Pet 2:25

Peter’s wording `ἦτε ... ὡς πρόβατα πλανώμενοι` sits directly in the Isa 53 passion context he has been citing/alluding to.

Therefore “were straying sheep → now returned” can describe **conversion from sinful wandering**, including Gentiles, without requiring a prior saved state.

### Stronger theological point

The answer to wandering is not self-navigation alone; it is connected to the Servant who bears sin.

---

# IV. Иезекииль 34 — failed shepherds and Yahweh Himself as seeker

## 4. Human shepherds failed in exactly the tasks endangered sheep needed

Ezek 34 condemns shepherds for not:

- strengthening weak;
- healing sick;
- binding broken;
- bringing back strayed;
- seeking lost.

The flock scatters and becomes prey.

### Ecclesial warning

Apostasy/wandering is not only an individual psychology problem.

**Bad shepherding can materially expose the flock to danger.**

This does not remove sheep’s responsibility, but it creates leadership accountability.

### Later NT relevance

- Acts 20 shepherd/overseer warning;
- 1 Pet 5 elders must shepherd/oversee;
- Heb 13 leaders watch over souls;
- Rev 2–3 leadership/church responsibility.

---

# V. Ezek 34:11–16 — divine reversal of failed pastoral care

## 5. Repeated divine first person

God says, in effect:

- I Myself will search for My sheep;
- seek them out;
- rescue from scattering;
- bring them out/gather them;
- feed them;
- make them lie down;
- seek lost;
- bring back strayed;
- bind injured;
- strengthen weak;
- judge fat/strong oppressors.

### Massive preservation/restoration insight

God’s response to failed shepherds is not:

> «the sheep should have found their own way back, therefore I abandon them».

It is personal divine shepherding.

### But mercy includes judgment

The chapter also judges exploitative sheep/shepherds.

So shepherd grace is not sentimental permissiveness.

Source/discovery:
- Ezekiel 34:16 commentaries; Keil/Delitzsch and others emphasize Yahweh doing what bad shepherds omitted.

---

# VI. Ezek 34 and `ἐπίσκοπος` / visitation-overseeing background

## 6. Potential LXX connection to 1 Pet 2:25

Older Greek-commentary traditions note that Ezek 34:11 LXX uses a cognate verb in the semantic neighborhood of `ἐπίσκοπος` / oversight-visitation.

1 Pet 2:25 calls Christ:

- Shepherd (`ποιμήν`);
- Overseer/Guardian (`ἐπίσκοπος`) of souls.

### Caution

This is a **plausible strong intertextual echo**, not yet apparatus-level proven as deliberate lexical quotation.

Need exact LXX and modern Petrine scholarship before headline claim.

Sources/discovery:
- Cambridge / Ellicott / collected commentary on 1 Pet 2:25.

# VII. Ezek 34:23–24 — one Davidic shepherd

God promises one shepherd, “my servant David,” over the flock.

### Canonical development

This creates messianic shepherd expectation:

- Yahweh Himself shepherds;
- Yahweh appoints one Davidic shepherd.

In the NT, Jesus can occupy both lines:

- Davidic Messiah;
- divine shepherd functions.

### Christological significance

1 Pet’s Shepherd/Overseer, John 10 Good Shepherd and Luke 15 seeking shepherd naturally stand within this field.

Do not reduce Christological argument to one title; use whole function/motif cluster.

---

# VIII. Лк. 15:1–7 — the Shepherd seeks until he finds

## 7. Immediate controversy

Jesus receives/eats with tax collectors and sinners; Pharisees/scribes grumble.

The lost sheep parable answers **why divine grace seeks sinners**.

### Crucial dynamic

The shepherd:

- recognizes loss;
- goes after lost sheep;
- seeks **until he finds**;
- carries it on shoulders rejoicing;
- calls for communal joy.

Verse 7 interprets the image in terms of **one sinner who repents**.

Sources/discovery:
- Cambridge, JFB, Calvin and other Luke 15 commentaries.

### What this means

The parable centers divine seeking joy, not an ordo-salutis taxonomy of whether the sheep had previously been regenerate.

### No-go

Do not argue:

> “it belonged to flock before loss, therefore a justified believer lost justification and regained it.”

That is a metaphor-to-metaphysics jump beyond Jesus’ stated interpretive point.

---

# IX. Luke 15 nevertheless does not make lostness cosmetic

## 8. Lost means real danger/estrangement

The shepherd does not say:

> «technically it remains my sheep, therefore its location is irrelevant».

He goes after it.

### Perseverance/restoration implication

Covenant/election language cannot be used pastorally to justify passivity toward wanderers.

The correct response to “one is missing” is **seek**.

This directly supports:

- James 5 turn wanderer back;
- Jude rescue from fire;
- Gal 6 restore caught person;
- Heb 3 exhort daily.

---

# X. 1 Пет. 2:25 — conversion as return to the Shepherd/Overseer

## 9. Peter fuses Isa 53 and shepherd field

Direct context unmistakably depends on Isa 53:

- sinlessness;
- no deceit;
- bearing sins;
- wounds/healing;
- straying sheep.

Then Peter adds:

> now you have turned/returned to Shepherd and Overseer of souls.

### Gentile control

Because `ἐπιστρέφω` commonly describes first conversion of Gentiles (Acts 14:15; 15:19; 1 Thess 1:9), “returned” does not prove prior regeneration.

### Shepherd control

Because Ezek 34 promises Yahweh’s seeking/returning/overseeing shepherd action, the image is richer than mere human self-turning.

See dedicated `29_1_PETER_2_25...`.

# XI. 1 Peter 5 completes the shepherd circle

## 10. Christ’s pastoral care becomes ecclesial means

1 Pet 5:

- elders shepherd (`ποιμάνατε`) God’s flock;
- exercise oversight (`ἐπισκοποῦντες`, textual details need checking);
- under Chief Shepherd (`ἀρχιποίμην`).

### Canonical pattern

Ezek 34:

> bad shepherds fail → Yahweh shepherds → Davidic shepherd promised.

1 Peter:

> Christ = Shepherd/Overseer → elders shepherd under Chief Shepherd.

### Preservation-through-means

Christ’s keeping of souls does not make human shepherding unnecessary. It **grounds and governs it**.

---

# XII. Three different “lost sheep” functions must not be collapsed

## A. Isa 53 / 1 Pet 2

Sinful humanity/covenant people as wandering sheep → Servant bears sin → conversion to Shepherd.

## B. Psalm 119:176

Faithful servant/community in weakness/outward danger or moral wandering asks God to seek him; commandments not forgotten.

## C. Luke 15

Sinner as lost sheep → divine shepherd seeks → repentance → joy.

### Conclusion

Same image-field does not imply identical prior saving status.

**Metaphor is controlled by local narrative/exegetical function.**

---

# XIII. Lost sheep vs final apostate

## 11. What distinguishes trajectories canonically?

Not a single psychological symptom, but clusters:

### Recoverable/wanderer cluster

- remains object of summons/search;
- can hear/return;
- repentance possible;
- church commanded to restore;
- God heals/returns;
- truth not finally repudiated or later received again.

### Terminal-hardening cluster

- great known light;
- deliberate repudiation;
- sustained resistance/hardening;
- texts may state impossibility/judgment/finality explicitly (hard passages need own exegesis).

### Warning

Church usually does not know hidden final-state status in advance. Therefore pastoral vocation toward a living wanderer is normally **seek, warn, call, restore**, not speculate that no return is possible.

---

# XIV. Shepherd theology changes how warning passages are applied

## 12. Warnings are shepherding speech

A shepherd:

- warns of wolves;
- calls wandering sheep;
- retrieves straying;
- binds wounded;
- disciplines dangerous members;
- feeds truth.

Thus warning theology without shepherd theology becomes punitive classification.

Shepherd theology without warnings becomes sentimental permissiveness.

Biblical pastoral preservation requires both.

---

# XV. Relation to John 10

John 10 adds:

- sheep hear Shepherd voice;
- Shepherd knows them;
- they follow;
- Christ gives eternal life;
- they will never perish;
- none snatches from His hand.

### Canonical synthesis

1 Pet 2 / Ezek 34 / Luke 15 emphasize seeking/returning care.

John 10 emphasizes recognition/following/security.

Together:

> the Shepherd who finds is also the Shepherd who keeps.

### P0

Need standalone John 10 exegesis before using as technical proof against every conditional-security argument.

---

# XVI. Relation to Heb 13

Heb 13:17 leaders watch over souls as those who will give account.

### Echo with 1 Pet 2:25

Christ is ultimate Overseer of souls; human leaders watch over souls under accountability.

### Project relevance

Perseverance is ecclesial. Isolation from shepherding/exhortation is not the normative biblical model of security.

---

# XVII. What the shepherd cluster clearly proves

High confidence:

1. Scripture knows serious wandering that is not automatically terminal.
2. Lostness/wandering is dangerous, not cosmetic.
3. God portrays Himself as active seeker/restorer of scattered sheep.
4. Human shepherd failure can contribute to scattering.
5. God’s shepherding includes search, return, healing, strengthening, feeding and judgment.
6. Jesus places divine seeking joy at center of sinner’s repentance.
7. 1 Peter presents conversion as return/turn to Christ the Shepherd/Overseer.
8. The same shepherd vocabulary can apply to first conversion and restoration; context determines function.
9. Christ’s shepherding works through subordinate human shepherds/restorers.
10. Pastorally, living wanderers should be sought rather than prematurely pronounced terminal.

---

# XVIII. What it does NOT prove alone

It does not prove:

- every person called sheep was regenerate before wandering;
- every wanderer will certainly return;
- all lost-sheep texts have identical referent;
- Luke 15 is a technical treatise on losing/regaining justification;
- Psalm 119:176 unambiguously refers only to moral apostasy;
- Ezek 34 cancels judgment on corrupt shepherds/fat sheep;
- church may ignore doctrine because shepherding is relational;
- divine seeking removes human repentance response.

# XX. Working conclusion

> **Библейский образ заблудшей овцы не является мягким эвфемизмом: овца потеряна, уязвима и нуждается в поиске. Но именно поэтому этот образ нельзя автоматически превращать в термин “безвозвратный отступник”. Псалмопевец просит Бога взыскать заблудившегося раба; Иезекииль обещает, что Сам Яхве будет искать потерянное, возвращать отогнанное, перевязывать повреждённое и укреплять слабое; Иисус ищет потерянную овцу до тех пор, пока не найдёт; Пётр говорит обращённым, что они возвратились к Христу — Пастырю и Блюстителю душ. Каноническая пастырская логика поэтому требует не только предупреждать об отпадении, но и активно искать, возвращать и укреплять заблудившихся.**
