## Freeze

# 5. §4 — gentleness does not mean doctrinal vagueness

## Primary owners

- Gal 6:1;
- 2 Tim 2:24–26;
- 2 Tim 2:16–19 for immediate contrast with “gangrene.”

## Freeze

KINDNESS / GENTLENESS refers to manner, posture and self-watchfulness, not to renaming destructive teaching as harmless.

Strong line allowed:

> The same apostolic context that commands gentleness can call false teaching gangrene.

## Pastoral self-diagnosis

Gal 6:1 requires the restorer to watch himself. This authorizes explicit warning against:

- pride in being right;
- enjoyment of another person’s humiliation;
- using discipline to settle personal scores;
- imagining oneself temptation-proof.

## Puritan practical control

Brooks and Flavel are useful for self-watchfulness and the deceitfulness of sin, but Scripture owns the ethical command.

## Freeze

- summon repentance;

### Critical distinction

Allowed:

> “We cannot presently recognize this profession/life/teaching as consistent with Christian fellowship.”

Not automatically allowed:

> “We possess infallible knowledge of this person’s final eternal decree.”

### 1 Tim 1 control

“Handed over to Satan” is severe, yet Paul states a corrective pedagogical purpose: that they may learn not to blaspheme.

# 7. §6 — forgiveness/restoration does not erase consequences

## Primary owners

- David: 2 Sam 12;
- Peter: John 21;
- pastoral qualifications: 1 Tim 3; Titus 1;
- Zacchaeus/repentance fruit may illustrate restitution, if used carefully.

## Freeze

Keep separate questions:

1. Can the person repent and be forgiven in Christ?
2. Can church fellowship be restored?
3. Has trust been repaired?
4. Is restitution needed?
5. Is the person presently qualified for office/ministry responsibility?

These are not synonyms.

## Guardrail

Peter’s exceptional apostolic restoration by Christ cannot be turned into a universal automatic reinstatement rule for every fallen pastor/elder.

Likewise Scripture does not authorize a universal extra-biblical timetable for every case.

# 12. §11 — response to false teachers differs from response to confused sheep

## Primary owners

- Acts 20:28–31;
- Rom 16:17–18;
- Titus 1:10–16;
- Titus 3:9–11;
- 2 Pet 2;
- 2 John 7–11;
- Jude.

## Freeze

The NT distinguishes:

- doubters/endangered persons who need mercy/rescue;
- opponents who may be corrected in hope of repentance;
- divisive/false teachers who, after warning or depending on severity/context, must have influence bounded or fellowship refused.

## Guardrail

“Love everyone” does not entail “give everyone a teaching platform.”

Likewise “protect the flock” does not authorize cruelty or invention of motives beyond evidence.

# 13. §12 — family/friend response: truth without pretending to control conversion

## Biblical controls

- 2 Tim 2:24–26 — God grants repentance;
- 1 Pet 3 may inform conduct in relational witness but should not be imported mechanically outside its context;
- James 5 / Jude rescue language;
- Rom 9–10 can frame grief/prayer for unbelieving kin without turning Part V into a separate election essay.

## Freeze

Pastoral counsel may include:

- clear confession of truth;
- refusal to provide false assurance;
- continuing relational love where morally/safely possible;
- prayer;
- patient availability for honest questions;
- boundaries when the person is destructive.

## Guardrail

Do not promise a specific relative will return. Divine sovereignty is ground for prayer and faithfulness, not prediction.

# 14. §13 — assurance belongs in Christ, not in endless self-autopsy

## Primary owners

- John 5:24;
- John 6:37–40;
- John 10:27–30;
- Rom 5; 8;
- 1 John’s stated assurance purpose;
- Heb 6:9–20;
- Heb 10:19–25, 35–39.

## Reformed control

Sproul/Ferguson may be useful for preservation/assurance, but claim-by-claim.

## Schreiner control

Warnings and assurance are not opposites when warnings function as means of perseverance.

## Puritan balance

- Watson: false peace must be exposed;
- Sibbes: wounded believers should be directed toward Christ;
- Owen: watchfulness is a means, not the ground of justification.

## Freeze

Avoid both:

- assurance detached from present relation to Christ;
- assurance reduced to confidence that one’s introspective performance is good enough.

> **Christ is the ground of assurance; Scripture also directs believers to the evidences and persevering shape of living faith.**

# 15. §14 — “once saved always saved” wording discipline

If this phrase appears, it requires immediate clarification because it is used for incompatible doctrines.

## Unacceptable caricature

> “A past decision guarantees heaven even if the person later permanently repudiates Christ without repentance.”

That is not the project’s Reformed perseverance doctrine.

## Reformed formulation

> Those whom God truly saves He preserves to the end, and He preserves them through living faith, repentance, warnings, Word, Spirit, church means and Christ’s intercession.

## Biblical owners

John 6; 10; 17; Rom 8; Phil 1:6; 1 Pet 1:5; Heb 3; Jude.

## Modern control

Schreiner + Chou as already source-locked.

# 16. §15 — warning people without pretending to know the decree

## Freeze language by present posture

### Repentant/grieved person seeking Christ

Emphasize:

- Christ’s sufficiency;
- real call to return;
- forgiveness/restoration;
- seriousness without hopelessness.

### Confused/endangered person still listening

Emphasize:

- correction;
- biblical clarity;
- patient rescue;
- boundaries proportionate to influence/risk.

### Settled public repudiation demanding assurance

Emphasize:

- warning;
- no detached assurance;
- call to repent/return;
- refusal to use former profession as immunity.

### Destructive false teacher

Emphasize:

- protect flock;
- expose doctrine accurately;
- restrict platform/fellowship as Scripture requires;
- do not invent final-state certainty beyond the text.

# 18. §17 — suicide/despair/Judas: pastoral caution

If Part V refers to despair or self-destruction, maintain strict theological and pastoral restraint.

## Biblical owners

Judas’s suicide is part of his terminal trajectory, but suicide as an act must not be turned into a universal independent proof of damnation for every case.

## Puritan control

Brooks’s anti-despair material is valuable: sin can be minimized before commission and magnified afterward into hopelessness.

## Freeze

The article should call despair away from itself and toward Christ, while keeping Judas’s canonical trajectory distinct from generic mental-health or suicide cases.

Do not make medical/psychiatric diagnoses.

# 22. Editorial freeze list

1. Do not prematurely pronounce a living/recoverable wanderer terminal.
2. Do not give assurance detached from Christ to a settled repudiator.
3. Warnings/exhortations are real preserving means.
4. Gentleness is manner, not doctrinal vagueness.
5. Discipline is serious but is not automatic infallible declaration of the final decree.
6. Forgiveness, fellowship, trust and office qualification are distinct questions.
7. Emotion/remorse alone does not prove repentance; neither does weak emotion disprove it.
8. Weak/bruised grace must not be confused with final apostasy.
9. Presumption and despair are opposite distortions.
10. Early declensions matter; intervene before public collapse where possible.
11. Confused sheep and destructive teachers require different responses.
12. Prayer/faithfulness toward an unbelieving relative does not authorize prediction of return.
13. Assurance rests finally in Christ, not in past experience or endless introspection.
14. Reformed perseverance is preservation through living faith and appointed means, not carnal security.
15. Restoration to fellowship does not automatically restore office.
16. Puritans supply practical anatomy after exegesis; they do not settle Greek/Hebrew disputes.
17. Platform brands do not own claims.
18. Final-state certainty never exceeds Scripture’s disclosure.
