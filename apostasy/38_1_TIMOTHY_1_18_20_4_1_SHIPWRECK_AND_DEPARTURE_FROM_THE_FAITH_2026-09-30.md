> 1 Тим. соединяет несколько разных уровней опасности: человек может отвергнуть добрую совесть и потерпеть «кораблекрушение относительно веры»; конкретные Именей и Александр подвергаются суровой дисциплине с исправительной целью; затем Дух прямо предсказывает, что некоторые `ἀποστήσονται τῆς πίστεως` — отступят/отойдут от веры, следуя обольщающим духам и бесовским учениям. Но ответ Павла — не только классифицировать отступников: Тимофей должен питаться словами веры, упражняться в благочестии, внимать себе и учению, пребывать в этом — и именно так «спасёт и себя и слушающих». Это крупный текст о **доктринальном отступлении и сохранении через верное учение**.

# I. 1 Тим. 1:18–19 — добрый подвиг требует веры и доброй совести

## 1. Павел не противопоставляет doctrine и conscience

Timothy must:

- fight the good warfare;
- hold `πίστιν` — faith;
- hold `ἀγαθὴν συνείδησιν` — good conscience.

### Key relation

`ἥν τινες ἀπωσάμενοι` — some have **pushed away/rejected** the good conscience;

and then:

`περὶ τὴν πίστιν ἐναυάγησαν` — suffered shipwreck **concerning the faith**.

### Mechanism

Paul’s syntax suggests moral rejection and doctrinal catastrophe are related.

A person does not necessarily become heretical because of a purely neutral intellectual mistake. Rejection of conscience can open space for distortion of faith.

### Canonical parallels

- Titus 1:16 — profess to know God but deny by deeds;
- 2 Tim 3 — corrupt life and corrupt teaching;
- 2 Pet 2 / Jude — moral corruption and doctrinal seduction intertwined;
- John 3:19–20 — love of darkness affects relation to truth.

---

# II. `ἀπωσάμενοι` — not accidental drift

## 2. They rejected/pushed away good conscience

The verb carries active resistance/rejection force.

### Important distinction

This is different from Heb 2:1’s drifting through neglect.

Canonical apostasy trajectories can begin in different ways:

- passive neglect/drift;
- deception;
- pressure/persecution;
- desire;
- active rejection of conscience.

### Taxonomy consequence

Do not create one universal psychological path. Different passages expose different entry points.

---

# III. `περὶ τὴν πίστιν ἐναυάγησαν` — shipwreck imagery

## 3. The metaphor is severe

A shipwreck is not minor loss of comfort.

TGC commentary argues the phrase can be rendered as shipwreck **with respect to / concerning the faith**, and treats these persons as spiritually sunk from the apostolic perspective even if outwardly appearing intact.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/1-timothy/

### Translation/inference trap

The metaphor alone does **not** tell us every hidden prior/final ordo-salutis fact.

It clearly proves:

- catastrophic relation to Christian faith;
- moral/doctrinal failure;
- need for church discipline.

It does not by itself prove:

- each named person had been certainly regenerate;
- regeneration was metaphysically lost at the exact moment of shipwreck;
- final damnation was already irrevocably pronounced.

### Nor can it be minimized

A Reformed reading must not say:

> «shipwreck means almost nothing because elect cannot finally perish».

The apostolic metaphor is deliberately catastrophic.

---

# IV. Is `πίστις` subjective trust or objective Christian faith?

## 4. The Pastoral Epistles use `πίστις` flexibly

Possible values include:

- personal faith/trust;
- faithfulness;
- the Christian faith/content/sphere of confession.

In 1:19 `περὶ τὴν πίστιν` naturally allows strong objective/reference nuance: catastrophe concerning the faith.

In 4:1 `τῆς πίστεως` is especially naturally read as **the faith**, the apostolic Christian truth from which false teaching departs.

### P0 caution

Do not solve hidden regeneration-status simply by choosing objective or subjective gloss. A person can depart from Christian doctrine/profession with or without the text telling us exactly when/how internal saving faith related to that departure.

Need full Pastoral `πίστις` concordance before publication.

---

# V. Именей и Александр — named cases but not identical biographies

## 5. Paul names them

`ὧν ἐστιν Ὑμέναιος καὶ Ἀλέξανδρος`.

### Hymenaeus

2 Tim 2:17–18 later names Hymenaeus with Philetus as teachers who say resurrection already happened and overthrow the faith of some.

This likely creates continuity, though identification should be treated carefully as same name/person unless scholarly reasons challenge it.

### Alexander

Could be same Alexander as 2 Tim 4:14 or Acts figures, but **do not identify automatically**. Common names and insufficient data make such harmonization speculative.

### Editorial rule

Named canonical cases are not permission to invent biographies.

---

# VI. «Предал сатане» — severity plus corrective purpose

## 6. `οὓς παρέδωκα τῷ Σατανᾷ`

Parallel commonly compared with 1 Cor 5.

### Purpose clause matters

`ἵνα παιδευθῶσιν μὴ βλασφημεῖν` — **so that they may be taught/disciplined not to blaspheme**.

TGC commentary stresses the restorative/repentance aim: discipline is not exclusion for exclusion’s sake.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/1-timothy/

### Very important project control

Even after “shipwreck” and blasphemy, Paul expresses discipline in a **pedagogical purpose**.

Therefore:

> catastrophic spiritual language ≠ automatic declaration that church should cease seeking repentance.

This stands beside James 5 and Jude 22–23.

### Caution

A corrective purpose does not guarantee successful repentance of each disciplined person.

---

# VII. 1 Tim. 3:15–16 → 4:1 — truth-church vs departure-from-faith contrast

## 7. Literary contrast

Immediately before 4:1:

- church = household of God;
- church of living God;
- pillar/support of truth;
- mystery of godliness confessed in Christological form.

Then:

`Τὸ δὲ Πνεῦμα ῥητῶς λέγει...` — **But the Spirit expressly says...**

some will depart from faith.

Older commentators noticed this sharp contrast between grounded truth/mystery of godliness and coming defection.

### Ecclesiological implication

Apostasy arises **in relation to the church’s truth-confession**, not only outside it.

Institutional proximity to the pillar of truth is not immunity against false teaching.

---

# VIII. 4:1 — `ἀποστήσονταί τινες τῆς πίστεως`

## 8. One of NT’s most explicit spiritual-departure verbs

`ἀφίστημι` can mean depart/withdraw in ordinary senses; here complement `τῆς πίστεως` and false-teaching context make spiritual/doctrinal defection explicit.

Cambridge renders it “fall away,” noting objective “the faith.”

BibleHub lexical/commentary tradition notes this is the verb-family behind apostasy language.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/cambridge/1_timothy/4.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_timothy/4-1.htm

### Strong conclusion

The NT undeniably predicts **real departure from Christian faith**.

### But thin-status warning

The clause does not by itself settle whether every departing individual:

- was regenerate;
- possessed only profession/temporary faith;
- belonged corporately/externally;
- lost saving union.

That question requires wider canonical/systematic synthesis.

---

# IX. Apostasy is not self-generated only: deception has spiritual agency

## 9. `πνεύμασιν πλάνοις` / `διδασκαλίαις δαιμονίων`

They give attention to deceiving spirits and teachings associated with demons.

### Canonical pattern

Apostasy involves:

- internal desire;
- human false teachers;
- spiritual deception.

This parallels:

- Gen 3 serpent;
- Deut 13 false prophet/test;
- Matt 24 false christs/prophets;
- 2 Thess 2 deception;
- 1 John 4 test spirits;
- Rev 12–13 dragon/beast/false prophet.

### Guardrail

Do not use demonic language to remove human responsibility. 4:2 immediately describes human liars/hypocrites and seared conscience.

---

# X. 4:2 — seared conscience: false doctrine becomes embodied hypocrisy

## 10. `ἐν ὑποκρίσει ψευδολόγων, κεκαυστηριασμένων τὴν ἰδίαν συνείδησιν`

The teaching is mediated through hypocritical liars whose conscience is cauterized/seared/marked.

### Relation to 1:19

1:19:

> reject good conscience → shipwreck concerning faith.

4:2:

> false-teaching agents have damaged/seared conscience.

### Major Pastoral thesis

**Doctrine and conscience deteriorate together.**

This is one of the strongest canonical witnesses that apostasy can become a feedback loop:

> moral compromise → doctrinal rationalization → more moral numbness → deeper false teaching.

---

# XI. 4:3–5 — false asceticism can be apostasy

## 11. Apostasy can look stricter than Christianity

False teachers:

- forbid marriage;
- require abstinence from foods God created to be received with thanksgiving.

### Important deception pattern

Apostasy is not necessarily permissive/libertine.

It can be **hyper-religious asceticism** that denies creation’s goodness and distorts gospel truth.

This belongs beside Gal 4–5:

> moving into stricter religious practice can be movement away from Christ.

### Project taxonomy addition

Need category:

**ascetic/legalistic apostasy** — not only sensual apostasy.

---

# XII. 4:6 — antidote begins with nourishment in words of faith

## 12. Timothy combats apostasy positively

He must be nourished on:

- words of faith;
- good doctrine he has followed.

### Means-of-perseverance implication

False doctrine is not defeated merely by labeling enemies.

The church’s preservation requires **positive nourishment in truth**.

This mirrors:

- Deut 6/8 remember/word;
- Psalm 78 teach next generation;
- Heb 3 daily exhort;
- Jude contend/build yourselves;
- 2 Pet grow in grace/knowledge.

---

# XIII. 4:7–10 — train for godliness, hope in living God

## 13. Apostasy avoidance is active formation

Timothy must reject irreverent myths and train (`γύμναζε`) himself for godliness.

### Important correction

The biblical anti-apostasy strategy is not simply:

> “know which doctrines are wrong.”

It includes **habituation in godliness**, embodied fidelity, hope in living God.

### Canonical contrast

Jer 2:

- leave living fountain → broken substitutes.

1 Tim 4:

- false spiritual teachings → Timothy hopes in **living God** and nourishes on truth.

---

# XIV. 4:12–15 — example, reading, exhortation, teaching, progress

## 14. Church-preserving ministry is visible and disciplined

Timothy must attend to:

- public reading;
- exhortation;
- teaching;
- gift;
- practice/immersion;
- visible progress.

### Apostasy prevention via ordinary means

This is important against spectacular models where only extraordinary revival prevents apostasy.

Paul’s answer to demonic error is largely **ordinary faithful ministry**.

---

# XV. 4:16 — `ἔπεχε σεαυτῷ καὶ τῇ διδασκαλίᾳ`

## 15. «Вникай в себя и в учение»

Two inseparable objects:

- yourself/life;
- teaching.

Then:

`ἐπίμενε αὐτοῖς` — persist/remain in them.

### Key perseverance structure

> personal vigilance + doctrinal vigilance + persistence.

This is exactly opposite of 1:19:

> reject conscience + shipwreck faith.

---

# XVI. «Поступая так, и себя спасёшь и слушающих тебя»

## 16. Human instrumentality without autonomous saviorhood

Paul can say faithful pastoral perseverance “will save” Timothy and hearers.

This does not mean Timothy becomes atoning savior.

It means God’s salvation operates through appointed means:

- faithful teaching;
- godly life;
- persistence;
- hearing/responding.

### Canonical parallels

- James 5: one who turns sinner back “saves a soul from death”;
- Jude 23 “save others, snatching from fire”;
- Rom 11 ministry aims to save some;
- 1 Cor 9 Paul disciplines himself and ministry for gospel participation.

### Strong synthesis

> Preservation doctrine that cannot accommodate creaturely “save” language is too thin. Scripture freely describes ministers/believers as real instruments while salvation remains God’s work.

---

# XVII. 1 Tim 1 → 4 full trajectory

| Negative trajectory | Positive counter-means |
|---|---|
| reject good conscience | attend to yourself/life |
| shipwreck concerning faith | nourish on words of faith |
| blasphemy | sound doctrine |
| false spirits | Spirit expressly warns |
| demon-associated teachings | truth confession/mystery of godliness |
| hypocritical lies | exemplary speech/conduct/love/faith/purity |
| seared conscience | good conscience / godliness |
| false ascetic practice | receive God’s creation with thanksgiving/word/prayer |
| departure from faith | persist in doctrine |
| influence others into error | faithful teaching saves hearers instrumentally |

This table belongs in eventual flagship or technical appendix.

# XVIII. Shipwreck vs final apostasy

## 17. Why 1:20 forbids premature terminal verdict

The disciplined named men are handed over **so that they may learn** not to blaspheme.

Thus even shipwreck language still sits inside corrective church action.

### But later Hymenaeus remains dangerous

If 2 Tim 2 Hymenaeus is same man, he appears later still spreading resurrection error.

This demonstrates:

- discipline can have restorative aim without guaranteed visible success;
- prolonged doctrinal rebellion can deepen;
- church cannot infer successful repentance merely from benevolent aim.

### Final-state caution

Scripture never gives us permission to fill all gaps in Hymenaeus’s final biography.

---

# XIX. 1 Tim 4:1 and temporary faith

These are not identical categories.

### Luke 8

Temporary belief revealed by testing/rootlessness.

### 1 Tim 4

Departure from `the faith` under false spiritual/doctrinal influence.

A person in 1 Tim 4 might historically overlap with temporary profession, but text’s primary concern is **doctrinal apostasy**.

### Editorial rule

Do not make every departure passage merely “temporary faith” by definition.

---

# XX. 1 Tim 4 and Heb 6/10

Again, not identical.

1 Tim 4 describes predicted doctrinal defection and mechanisms.

Hebrews adds exceptional experiential/covenantal privilege, irreversible-renewal language (6), and deliberate trampling/insult (10).

### Taxonomy consequence

`ἀφίστημι` from faith is serious but does not automatically carry every terminal feature of Heb 6/10.

---

# XXI. 1 Tim and Galatians: apostasy may be ascetic/legalistic

Galatians:

- circumcision/calendar as move back to slavery when made covenantal necessity.

1 Timothy:

- forbidding marriage/foods in false spirituality.

### Important balance

Not every ascetic discipline or abstinence is apostasy. Paul himself practices self-control and allows voluntary singleness/fast-like disciplines elsewhere.

The problem is **doctrinal prohibition that contradicts God’s creation/gospel order**.

---

# XXII. What 1 Tim clearly proves

High confidence:

1. Christian leaders/communities face real internal doctrinal defection.
2. Good conscience and sound faith are tightly related.
3. Deliberate rejection of conscience can lead to catastrophic shipwreck concerning faith.
4. Church discipline can remain corrective even after severe doctrinal/moral failure.
5. The Spirit explicitly predicts some will depart from the faith.
6. False doctrine may have spiritual/demonic and human/hypocritical dimensions simultaneously.
7. Apostasy can wear strict ascetic/religious clothing.
8. Positive nourishment in apostolic truth is a central antidote.
9. Minister must watch both life and doctrine and persist.
10. God saves/preserves through faithful human teaching as an instrument.

---

# XXIII. What it does NOT prove alone

It does not by itself prove:

- everyone departing from “the faith” had prior saving union;
- nobody departing from “the faith” had prior saving union;
- objective `the faith` makes personal faith irrelevant;
- shipwreck = irrevocable final damnation;
- corrective discipline guarantees repentance;
- every dietary/ascetic practice is demonic;
- Timothy earns salvation by ministry works;
- church leaders are preserved automatically irrespective of life/doctrine;
- one can infer Alexander identities across NT without proof.

# XXV. Working conclusion

> **1 Тимофею показывает doctrinal apostasy как процесс, в котором жизнь, совесть и учение могут разрушаться вместе. Некоторые активно отталкивают добрую совесть и терпят “кораблекрушение относительно веры”; Дух прямо предупреждает, что другие отступят от веры, слушая обольщающие духовные силы и ложные учения. Но апостольский ответ — не фатализм: дисциплина имеет исправительную цель, а Тимофей должен питаться истиной, упражняться в благочестии, следить за собой и учением и пребывать в этом. Таким образом Павел соединяет реальные отступления с реальными средствами, через которые Бог сохраняет и “спасает” служителя и слушающих.**
