# 1. Closure question

Гал. 5:4:

`κατηργήθητε ἀπὸ Χριστοῦ, οἵτινες ἐν νόμῳ δικαιοῦσθε, τῆς χάριτος ἐξεπέσατε.`

Основной вопрос:

> Павел утверждает, что уже оправданные и возрождённые люди потеряли спасительное единение со Христом и благодать, или описывает реальную смертельную траекторию отказа от Христа как единственного основания оправдания, от которой само предупреждение должно удержать церковь?

Второй вопрос:

> Насколько грамматика сама по себе решает systematic perseverance debate?

Рабочий verdict:

> **Гал. 5:4 является настоящим сотериологическим предупреждением. Тот, кто выбирает закон/обрезание как основание или необходимое дополнение к оправданию, тем самым отказывается от Христа как достаточного Спасителя и выходит из принципа/сферы благодати. Грамматика не позволяет ослабить текст до “потери радости, fellowship или rewards”. Но она также сама по себе не утверждает, что завершённое forensic justification конкретного regenerate человека было юридически отменено. Reformed warnings-as-means и conditional-security readings спорят именно на следующем — systematic — уровне.**

# 2. DIRECT EXEGESIS — текст и морфология

## 2.1. `κατηργήθητε`

Форма:

- lemma: `καταργέω`;
- aorist passive indicative, second person plural.

Основной semantic range в NT:

- лишить силы/эффекта;
- сделать недействительным;
- освободить от связи/обязательства;
- прекратить действенную связь.

Ключевой same-author control — Rom. 7:2, 6, где конструкция с `ἀπό` описывает освобождение/разрыв отношения с законом.

NET lexical note, ссылаясь на BDAG, передаёт Gal. 5:4 как **“be estranged from Christ”**.

Mounce Reverse Interlinear передаёт: “You have cut yourself off from Christ.”

### Direct exegetical conclusion

`κατηργήθητε ἀπὸ Χριστοῦ` нельзя честно свести к:

> «Христос остаётся полностью вашим спасительным основанием, но временно менее полезен в sanctification».

Фраза описывает **аннулирование действенной связи с Христом именно в том вопросе, который обсуждается — оправдание/спасительная надежда**.

Но сам `καταργέω` не содержит встроенной lexical информации:

- был ли человек прежде regenerate;
- был ли прежде forensic-justified;
- был ли избран к вечной жизни;
- может ли такое состояние быть восстановлено.

Это должны решить контекст и более широкий канон.

---

## 2.2. `οἵτινες ἐν νόμῳ δικαιοῦσθε`

Форма `δικαιοῦσθε` — present passive indicative.

Контекст делает невозможным значение:

> «вы реально и успешно оправдываетесь законом».

Paul уже установил:

- Gal. 2:16 — делами закона не оправдается никакая плоть;
- 2:21 — если righteousness через закон, Христос умер напрасно;
- 3:10–14 — закон как путь righteousness оставляет под проклятием;
- 3:21 — закон не может дать жизнь.

Поэтому conative rendering (“trying/seeking to be justified”) является контекстуально сильным.

NET прямо отмечает conative present.

### Direct exegetical conclusion

Объект обличения — не человек, который согрешил после обращения, а человек, который **переносит основание праведности перед Богом с Христа/веры/благодати на закон**.

---

## 2.3. `τῆς χάριτος ἐξεπέσατε`

Форма:

- lemma: `ἐκπίπτω`;
- aorist active indicative, second person plural;
- genitive `τῆς χάριτος` обозначает то, из чего произошло падение/выход.

Обычная сила `ἐκπίπτω` — выпасть, утратить положение, выйти из некоторого состояния/сферы.

Поэтому слабые paraphrases вроде:

> «вы просто перестали субъективно чувствовать благодать»

не выдерживают текста.

Robertson удачно описывает контраст как переход из sphere of grace in Christ к sphere of law как надежде спасения.

### Direct exegetical conclusion

Paul описывает **реальную несовместимость двух saving stances**:

`Христос / благодать / вера`

versus

`закон как основание оправдания`.

---

# 3. Вопрос аористов: уже случилось или риторически описывает исход выбранного пути?

В v.4 стоят два сильных aorist indicatives:

- `κατηργήθητε`;
- `ἐξεπέσατε`.

Их нельзя просто переписать будущим временем ради системы.

Но ближайший контекст сложнее простого “все уже окончательно отпали”.

## 3.1. Gal. 5:2

`ἐὰν περιτέμνησθε ... Χριστὸς ὑμᾶς οὐδὲν ὠφελήσει`

Paul использует условную конструкцию и future:

> **если** вы примете circumcision, Christ **не будет** вам полезен.

Это показывает, что по крайней мере для части аудитории опасное решение ещё является реальным будущим выбором.

## 3.2. Gal. 5:3

Paul затем говорит каждому `περιτεμνομένῳ` — человеку, принимающему/подвергающемуся circumcision — что тот обязан исполнить весь закон.

Возможно, риторический фокус сужается от всей колеблющейся аудитории к тем, кто реально переходит на сторону agitators.

## 3.3. Gal. 5:10

Paul пишет:

> `πέποιθα εἰς ὑμᾶς ἐν κυρίῳ ὅτι οὐδὲν ἄλλο φρονήσετε`

Он уверен **в Господе**, что Galatians не будут мыслить иначе.

Это не пустая вежливость после объявления всего сообщества необратимо погибшим.

### Closure

Есть две грамматически серьёзные модели:

1. **factual/subgroup reading:** некоторые уже фактически сделали law-justification своим stance, и v.4 описывает их текущую позицию;
2. **proleptic/rhetorical consequence reading:** Paul настолько живо описывает неизбежный результат принятия legal-justification, что формулирует его как совершившийся разрыв.

Нельзя превращать спор об aspect/aorist в главный аргумент doctrine of perseverance.

### Publication-safe formulation

> **Paul не даёт церкви игрушечного hypothetical. Опасность реальна и, возможно, уже материализовалась у части людей. Но его продолжающееся увещание и уверенность в 5:10 показывают, что письмо функционирует как средство предотвратить окончательное закрепление отступления.**

# 4. Галатам 5:2–6 — вопрос именно окончательного оправдания, а не rewards

Frank Thielman в ESV Expository Commentary правильно подчеркивает:

- принятие Mosaic law как пути к acceptance with God выражает недоверие к sufficient atoning work Christ;
- `κατηργήθητε ἀπὸ Χριστοῦ` означает выбор закона вместо Христа;
- `ἐξεπέσατε` означает отказ от Божьего gracious gift in Christ;
- v.5 противопоставляет этому ожидание Божьего final righteous verdict через Spirit и faith.

Thomas Schreiner также читает `δικαιοσύνη` в 5:5 forensic/eschatologically: верующие ожидают публичного final verdict, который соответствует уже данной праведности во Христе.

Douglas Moo likewise связывает vv.4–5 с justification и eschatological hope.

### Direct exegetical consequence

Reward-only / fellowship-only model недостаточен.

Paul обсуждает:

- оправдание;
- отношение ко Христу;
- благодать;
- Духа;
- веру;
- final righteousness/hope.

Это salvation-level discourse.

---

# 5. Schreiner: warning is real, and warning is a means of perseverance

Thomas Schreiner’s Galatians commentary gives one of the strongest conservative Reformed treatments.

Secondary scholarly citations consistently locate the crucial discussion around:

- pp. 314–315 — severity of vv.2–4 / aorists / apostasy warning;
- pp. 315–316 — relation of falling from grace to perseverance and v.5;
- pp. 318–319 — faith working through love and persevering faith.

A Southern Seminary dissertation citing Schreiner records the core formulation that the v.4–5 relation implies that **true believers will not finally be cut off from Christ or defect from grace**, while the warning itself remains serious.

Schreiner’s broader perseverance work (*Run to Win the Prize*) makes the systematic architecture explicit:

> NT warnings can be addressed to believers, threaten eternal destruction, and be among the means by which God ensures the perseverance of the elect.

Its appendix specifically meditates on Gal. 5:2–6.

### Why this model is stronger than “it does not really concern Christians”

It does not need to deny:

- real Christian church address;
- Gal. 3 Spirit reception language;
- genuine movement toward another gospel;
- salvation-level severity.

It instead distinguishes:

- **warning as real means**;
- **final apostasy as incompatible with saving perseverance**.

### Remaining locator caution

Exact Schreiner pages above are multiply attested in scholarly secondary sources but should be checked against the original ZECNT edition before quotation-ready publication.

# 6. Moo: grace and law as mutually exclusive justification stances

Secondary academic citations locate Moo’s BECNT discussion at approximately pp. 326–328.

The central points attributed to Moo are:

- `οἵτινες ... δικαιοῦσθε` refers to those attempting justification through law;
- grace and legal self-justification are mutually exclusive;
- 5:5 is eschatological and oriented to final righteousness/verdict;
- `Spirit / faith / righteousness / hope` form the positive contrast to v.4.

This supports a strong exegetical conclusion:

> **Paul is not contrasting two styles of sanctification inside an unchanged saving foundation. He contrasts two incompatible ways of seeking a right standing before God.**

### Remaining locator caution

Moo pp. 326–328 are supported by multiple scholarly secondary citations, but original-print verification remains required for quotation custody.

# 7. Timothy George / conservative Baptist control

Timothy George, *Galatians* (NAC), discussion around p.360–361 is repeatedly cited in teaching and academic literature for this unit.

George’s confessional contribution fits the central Pauline antithesis:

- circumcision as a saving requirement is not harmless addition;
- justification by Christ and justification by law cannot be combined;
- grace must remain grace.

George is useful as a conservative Baptist theological/exegetical control but does not replace Moo/Schreiner-level grammar work.

MacArthur’s 1982 Romans 5 sermon/study guide explicitly discusses Gal. 5:4.

His reading:

# 9. Calvin: mixing law into justification renounces Christ’s grace

Calvin’s commentary on Gal. 5:4 is exceptionally clear on the doctrinal center.

His argument:

- Galatians were not necessarily rejecting Christ verbally;
- they attempted to combine Christ with legal righteousness;
- but assigning even a share of justification to law deprives Christ of His saving office;
- Christ and grace cannot be retained as one co-ground among others.

This is highly relevant against a common pastoral mistake:

> apostasy from gospel grace need not begin with the sentence “I reject Jesus.”

It can begin with:

> “Jesus is necessary, **but not sufficient**; my qualifying work must complete the ground of acceptance.”

Primary source:
- John Calvin, *Commentaries on the Epistles of Paul to the Galatians and Ephesians*, on Gal. 5:4.
- CCEL Galatians study/commentary locator: https://www.ccel.org/study/Galatians_5%3A4

# 10. William Perkins: Puritan/Reformed law–gospel precision

William Perkins’ *Commentary on Galatians* is especially useful because it joins rigorous Reformed justification theology with practical application.

His Galatians exposition repeatedly insists:

- grace and human merit cannot function jointly as causes of justification;
- Christ’s obedience cannot be supplemented by human law-performance as co-ground;
- gospel obedience is fruit/testimony of faith and gratitude, not the meritorious basis of salvation.

Permanent public edition:
- https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/perkins/Commentary%20on%20Galatians%20-%20William%20Perkins.pdf

### Practical-theology use

Perkins helps formulate the pastoral danger:

> **Legal apostasy can retain Christian vocabulary while silently changing the answer to “why should God receive me?”**

### Source-policy guardrail

Perkins does not decide verbal aspect or modern lexical questions. He belongs after grammatical adjudication.

# 11. Strong conditional-security reading — represent it at full strength

A serious conditional-security interpretation argues:

1. Galatians were actual Christians, not merely external hearers.
2. Gal. 3:2–5 says they received the Spirit by hearing with faith.
3. Gal. 4:6 speaks of God sending the Spirit of His Son into their hearts.
4. Gal. 5:4 says those turning to law-justification are severed from Christ and fallen from grace.
5. Therefore Paul envisages real believers forfeiting saving relation/grace through gospel apostasy.

This must not be dismissed with:

> “they were obviously never believers.”

That reply is too easy and fails to engage the letter’s positive Christian language.

---

# 12. David deSilva / Wesleyan control

David deSilva provides a useful contemporary Wesleyan/Methodist systematic control.

In a 2026 catechetical exposition he explicitly allows that a believer may **fall from grace**, but makes several distinctions that matter for this project:

- temptation is not falling from grace;
- every individual sin is not automatically final apostasy;
- serious persistence in sin/enduring departure places the believer in danger;
- restoration remains possible by grace;
- Gal. 6:1–2, Jas. 5:19–20, Jude 22–23 and Rev. 2–3 show ongoing rescue/repentance ministry.

This is not a line-by-line technical commentary on Gal. 5:4, but it is a fair contemporary representation of the conditional-security framework into which deSilva places warning/restoration texts.

Permanent source:
- https://seedbed.com/are-those-who-have-fallen-from-grace-forever-condemned-global-methodist-church-catechism/

### Important fairness correction

Conditional security does **not** require the claim:

> “Gal. 5:4 = irrevocable terminal apostasy the moment any Christian sins.”

A sophisticated Wesleyan reading can hold:

- real loss of saving grace is possible;
- ordinary sin is not identical to apostasy;
- restoration may remain possible.

That distinction must appear in publication.

# 13. Why Gal. 3 Spirit-language cannot be neutralized

Gal. 3:2 asks whether they **received the Spirit** by works of law or hearing with faith.

3:3:

> having begun by Spirit, are you now being perfected by flesh?

3:5:

> God supplies the Spirit among them.

4:6:

> because you are sons, God sent the Spirit of His Son into our hearts.

A Reformed treatment that simply says:

> “Paul is talking only to unbelieving legalists who were never part of Christian experience”

fails to take the rhetoric of the letter seriously.

### Better Reformed formulation

Paul addresses the visible churches according to their professed and experienced Christian reality; within that pastoral address he warns that **final transfer to another gospel would be incompatible with perseverance in saving faith**.

The warning is one means by which God prevents that final transfer among His elect.

---

# 14. Gal. 5:10 is equally non-negotiable

Paul’s confidence is:

`ἐν κυρίῳ` — **in the Lord**.

He expects the Galatians not finally to embrace the alien teaching.

This is more than psychological optimism.

It gives Reformed warning-as-means theology an important internal foothold:

- warning is severe;
- danger is real;
- Paul still trusts the Lord to preserve/correct them.

### But do not overclaim

5:10 does not lexically prove unconditional election of every addressee.

It demonstrates pastoral confidence in divine preserving action toward the churches.

---

# 15. Relation to Gal. 1:6 — apostasy is from God, not merely from a doctrine

Gal. 1:6:

`μετατίθεσθε ἀπὸ τοῦ καλέσαντος ὑμᾶς ἐν χάριτι Χριστοῦ`

They are deserting **the One who called them** in Christ’s grace for another gospel.

This matters enormously.

Paul does not treat doctrinal corruption as an abstract exam error.

False gospel produces personal covenantal/religious movement:

`another ground of justification → another gospel → desertion of the Caller → severance from Christ / fall from grace`.

### Canonical implication

Doctrinal apostasy is morally and relationally serious because truth about justification determines **whom a person is trusting**.

---

# 16. Relation to Gal. 4:8–11 — return language without simplistic former-state inference

Gal. 4 says Gentile believers who formerly served non-gods now want to “turn back again” to weak/beggarly elements through legal-calendar bondage.

This demonstrates an important trajectory:

- the former pagan object and the Judaizing object are not identical;
- yet Paul can call the move a “return” because both belong to a comparable enslaving structure when used against Christ.

Thus Galatians itself warns against simplistic word-study reasoning.

`πάλιν / ἐπιστρέφω` does not by itself specify identical historical object.

Likewise `ἐκπίπτω` in 5:4 cannot by itself give an entire ordo salutis.

---

# 17. Relation to Gal. 6:1 — serious danger and restorative ministry coexist

Gal. 6:1:

> if anyone is caught in a transgression, spiritual people should restore such a person gently.

This does not directly interpret 5:4, but it controls pastoral classification.

Paul does not teach:

> “every serious deviation proves terminal apostasy; abandon the person.”

The letter holds together:

- severe gospel warning;
- confidence in the Lord;
- active correction;
- gentle restoration.

This fits the whole project’s distinction between:

- grievous/recoverable wandering;
- doctrinal movement toward apostasy;
- finalized repudiation.

# 19. Best current Reformed synthesis

The strongest Reformed treatment should **not** say:

> “Gal. 5:4 sounds severe but does not really describe a salvation danger.”

It should say:

> **Paul warns Christian churches that if they make law-observance necessary for justification, they thereby abandon Christ as sufficient Savior and fall from the grace-principle in which salvation is found. This is a genuine salvation-threatening warning. God does not preserve His people by making such warnings fictional; He preserves them in part by causing them to hear, fear and heed those warnings. Paul’s confidence “in the Lord” in 5:10 belongs to the same pastoral logic. Final apostasy from the gospel would manifest non-persevering faith; perseverance consists in continuing to rely upon Christ alone.**

This integrates:

- severity;
- church address;
- Spirit language;
- justification context;
- Paul’s confidence;
- wider Pauline perseverance theology.

---

# 20. Best current conditional-security synthesis

The strongest conditional model should be stated just as carefully:

> **Paul addresses people who genuinely received the Spirit and belonged to Christ; adopting law-righteousness as the ground of justification can therefore sever a real believer from Christ and cause a real fall from saving grace. This does not mean every sin instantly causes loss of salvation, nor does Gal. 5:4 say restoration is impossible. The warning summons real believers to remain in faith and may rescue those moving toward apostasy.**

This model takes positive Galatian Christian language and v.4’s severity very seriously.

Its main systematic pressure is explaining how:

- completed forensic justification;
- union with Christ;
- sealing/Spirit language elsewhere in Paul;
- divine election/preservation texts

relate to a reversible saving status.

That pressure comes from the wider canon, not from denying Gal. 5:4 grammar.

---

# 21. Why MacArthur’s “principle of grace” formulation needs supplementation, not rejection

MacArthur is right that:

- v.4 concerns law as a way of salvation;
- grace and works-righteousness are mutually exclusive;
- it is not teaching that any Christian who sins “falls from grace.”

But the phrase:

> “fallen from the principle of grace”

becomes too weak if it implies only an intellectual doctrinal category with no personal relation to Christ.

Paul says both:

- **severed from Christ**;
- **fallen from grace**.

Therefore publication should retain MacArthur’s law/grace insight while using Schreiner/Moo/Thielman to preserve the full relational and eschatological force.

---

# 22. Practical theology — “legalism” can itself be apostasy, not merely spiritual immaturity

The popular use of “legalism” often means:

- strict rules;
- conservative preferences;
- over-discipline;
- excessive scrupulosity.

Galatians 5 is much more precise.

The mortal issue is:

> **What must finally make me acceptable before God?**

A person may affirm:

- Christ died;
- grace matters;
- faith matters;

and yet corrupt the gospel by adding:

> “but Christ’s righteousness is insufficient unless my qualifying obedience supplies the missing ground.”

Calvin and Perkins are especially strong here.

### Pastoral diagnostic

Ask not merely:

> “Do you believe in Jesus?”

but:

> “If God asks why you should be accepted as righteous, what is your final plea?”

If the answer is structurally:

> “Christ **plus** something in me that completes His insufficiency,”

Galatians’ warning is directly relevant.

---

# 23. What Gal. 5:4 establishes with high confidence

1. The warning is salvation-level.
2. The issue is justification, not generic Christian failure.
3. Law-justification and Christ-grace justification are mutually exclusive.
4. `κατηργήθητε ἀπὸ Χριστοῦ` is genuinely severe separation language.
5. `τῆς χάριτος ἐξεπέσατε` is genuinely severe fall-out-of-grace language.
6. The text is addressed inside a real church crisis, not as an abstract philosophical possibility.
7. Paul still acts pastorally to prevent/correct the trajectory.
8. Paul has confidence **in the Lord** that the Galatians will not finally embrace the error.
9. The warning therefore cannot be treated merely as postmortem description of people beyond recovery.
10. Physical circumcision itself is not the problem; its soteriological use is.

---

# 24. What Gal. 5:4 does NOT establish by itself

It does not by itself establish:

- that completed forensic justification is reversible;
- that an elect regenerate believer can become non-elect;
- that every person addressed was regenerate;
- that every serious sin is apostasy;
- that fallen-from-grace here means irreversible Heb. 6/10 apostasy;
- that a person cannot be restored;
- that circumcision as ethnic/medical act damns;
- that Christian obedience itself is legalism;
- that perseverance is human merit.

# 27. Final closure sentence

> **“Отпали от благодати” в Гал. 5:4 нельзя ни ослабить до потери эмоциональной близости, ни автоматически превратить в доказательство отменённого возрождения. Павел описывает реальный спасительный разрыв, который следует из отказа от Христа как достаточного основания оправдания ради закона. Внутри реформатской модели это предупреждение является настоящим средством сохранения: избранные не спасаются вопреки предупреждениям, а Бог сохраняет их, в том числе заставляя услышать их. В conditional-security модели тот же текст читается как возможность реальной утраты saving grace. Спор между моделями начинается после грамматики; сама грамматика требует от обеих сторон сохранить всю серьёзность предупреждения.**
