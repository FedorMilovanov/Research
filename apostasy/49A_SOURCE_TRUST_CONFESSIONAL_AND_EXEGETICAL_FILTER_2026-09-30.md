# Source trust policy — confessional, exegetical and pastoral filter

**Дата:** 2026-09-30  
**Статус:** `CONTROL POLICY / ACTIVE / PUBLICATION_HOLD`  
**Applies to:** all lowercase `apostasy/` research.

---

## 1. Основной принцип

Источник оценивается **по автору, аргументу, методологии и конкретному месту**, а не по бренду площадки.

Поэтому:

- TGC не считается автоматически doctrinally safe;
- статья на консервативном сайте не получает автоматический Tier 1;
- academic prestige не заменяет high view of Scripture;
- confessional agreement не заменяет точную экзегезу;
- пуританская практическая глубина не заменяет morphology/syntax;
- грамматическая компетентность экзегета не означает, что все его systematic conclusions принимаются.

---

# 2. Рабочая иерархия источников

## Tier 0 — сам библейский текст

1. Hebrew / Aramaic / Greek text;
2. morphology and syntax;
3. textual variants where material;
4. immediate literary context;
5. authorial usage;
6. explicit biblical quotation/allusion;
7. canonical reuse.

Никакой commentator не может отменить этот слой.

### Important methodological rule

An exegete can be used **for grammar, lexical range, discourse, historical background or literary structure without importing all of his theology**.

Example: Gordon D. Fee can be highly valuable on Greek syntax, discourse and Pauline exegesis. His Pentecostal/continuationist or other confessional conclusions do not become project conclusions merely because his grammatical observation is useful.

# 3. Puritan pastoral-practical layer — mandatory, not decorative

For the final practical theology, puritans should not appear merely as quotation ornaments.

They are especially valuable for:

- anatomy of temptation;
- deceitfulness of sin;
- heart drift before public apostasy;
- presumption;
- hypocrisy;
- spiritual decay;
- repentance after falls;
- use of warnings;
- watchfulness;
- perseverance through means;
- distinction between falling into sin and lying impenitently in sin.

Priority pool:

- John Owen — apostasy, indwelling sin, temptation, mortification, perseverance;
- Thomas Watson — perseverance, repentance, apostasy, presumption;
- Thomas Brooks — Satan’s devices, precious remedies, assurance;
- John Flavel — keeping the heart, providence, perseverance;
- Stephen Charnock — nature of sin, attributes of God, practical atheism;
- William Gurnall — spiritual warfare;
- Richard Sibbes — bruised reed / restoration and gospel tenderness;
- Jeremiah Burroughs — contentment and worldly desire;
- John Bunyan — perseverance, false profession, pastoral imagery;
- Thomas Goodwin where directly relevant.

Primary-access examples:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity/divinity.ix.x.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/prayer/prayer.viii.html
- https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/owen_natureapostasy.html

### Guardrail

Historical anecdotes repeated by older writers (for example later traditions about Demas) require independent historical verification before being stated as fact.

# 4. TGC policy

TGC is now classified as:

`DISCOVERY / SECONDARY ACCESS / AUTHOR-SPECIFIC TRUST`

It may be used for:

- locating an author’s argument;
- bibliographic discovery;
- accessible commentary where the named author is independently acceptable;
- comparison of interpretations.

It must **not** be used as:

- a blanket confessional authority;
- sole support for a contested doctrinal conclusion;
- substitute for technical commentary on Hebrew/Greek;
- evidence that a position fits the site’s theological policy merely because it appears on TGC.

For every doctrinally material TGC use ask:

1. Who is the author?
2. What is the author’s theological orientation?
3. Is the claim grammatical, historical, literary, or theological?
4. Can the claim be verified in primary text / technical source?
5. Does the author’s conclusion conflict with project confessional boundaries?

If answer to 4 is no on a contested point → keep `PUBLICATION_HOLD`.

---

# 5. Academic sources outside the conservative confessional orbit

They may still be useful for:

- textual criticism;
- philology;
- epigraphy;
- ancient Near Eastern / Greco-Roman background;
- manuscript evidence;
- lexical corpus data;
- history of interpretation.

But they are **evidence providers, not theological adjudicators**.

A source that denies or weakens inspiration/inerrancy must not control the canonical synthesis.

---

# 6. Competing views policy

The project remains fair to:

- Arminian/Wesleyan readings;
- Lutheran readings;
- corporate-election readings;
- Free Grace readings;
- covenantal warning readings;
- other serious evangelical alternatives.

Fairness means:

- state the strongest actual argument;
- name the grammatical/canonical evidence;
- distinguish what the text directly says from theological inference;
- then adjudicate.

Fairness does **not** require theological neutrality in final conclusions.

---

# 7. Publication test for a major claim

A major claim should normally have:

1. exact biblical locator;
2. original-language check if wording matters;
3. canonical control;
4. at least one trusted modern technical exegete;
5. a conservative confessional/systematic control where doctrine is material;
6. puritan/classical practical witness when making pastoral application;
7. competing-view note if the text is substantially disputed.

Only after those layers align should `PUBLICATION_HOLD` be considered for removal.
