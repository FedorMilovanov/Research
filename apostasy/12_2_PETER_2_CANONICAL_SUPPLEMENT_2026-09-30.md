## 1. Главный methodological result

2 Пет. 2 нельзя объяснять одним словом в 2:22 («собака/свинья») и нельзя решать 2:1 системой искупления до экзегезы.

Письмо одновременно говорит о:

- подлинной вере/призвании адресатов (1:1–11);
- необходимости diligence и confirmation calling/election (1:5–11);
- false teachers who deny the Master who bought them (2:1);
- настоящем escape from world-defilements through `ἐπίγνωσις` of Christ (2:20);
- return and defeat worse than beginning (2:20–21);
- dog/sow proverbs explaining final state (2:22);
- warning самим «возлюбленным» не быть увлечёнными error and fall from own steadfastness, but grow in grace/knowledge (3:17–18).

Любое решение, которое делает один из этих элементов фиктивным, недостаточно.

## 2. `ἐπίγνωσις` — слишком сильное слово для дешёвого «они просто знали факты»

В 2 Петра `ἐπίγνωσις` используется положительно:

- 1:2 — grace/peace in knowledge of God/Jesus;
- 1:3 — divine power grants life/godliness through knowledge;
- 1:8 — virtues prevent fruitlessness in knowledge of Jesus Christ;
- 2:20 — escape from world defilements through/in knowledge of Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

Поэтому семантический аргумент «2:20 не может означать серьёзного знания, потому что люди погибли» — circular.

### Что можно утверждать

2:20 описывает **реальное и религиозно содержательное познание Христа**, достаточное для реального выхода из прежних загрязнений.

### Чего слово само не доказывает

`ἐπίγνωσις` не является техническим штампом `regeneration/justification`. Контекст должен решить, является ли это знание saving-union knowledge или сильной церковно-религиозной реальностью, не достигающей нового рождения.

Historical counterpoint: Cambridge/Expositor tradition часто подчёркивает, что `ἐπίγνωσις` здесь очень сильна и видит в тексте проблему для indefectibility. Aggregated access:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_peter/2-20.htm

Не использовать aggregator как final quote source; locate original edition before publication.

## 3. `ἀποφεύγω` — реальный escape нельзя отрицать

Параллель внутри письма:

- 1:4 — believers have `ἀποφυγόντες` corruption in world through lust;
- 2:20 — false teachers / relevant subjects have `ἀποφυγόντες` pollutions of world through knowledge of Christ.

Объекты различаются (`φθορά` vs `μιάσματα`), поэтому нельзя сказать, что два стиха полностью идентичны. Но повтор глагола делает формулу «они вообще никогда реально не вышли из мира» слишком слабой.

Лучший neutral research wording:

> Пётр приписывает им реальный выход из прежней мирской скверны и реальную нравственно-религиозную перемену. Спор идёт не о том, было ли что-то реальное, а о **качестве и сотериологическом статусе этой перемены**.

## 4. Dog/sow: nature argument силён только если не отрицать washing

2:22 не говорит «овца стала свиньёй». Это поддерживает классический nature argument: финальный возврат соответствует неизменившейся определяющей природе.

Но proverb explicitly includes a **washed sow**. Поэтому нельзя превращать текст в:

> «никакого очищения, знания, escape или change вообще не было».

Сильнее:

> внешнее/моральное/религиозное очищение может быть реальным; конечный возврат показывает, что оно не было тем окончательным новым творением, которое делает человека persevering sheep of Christ.

Это harmonizes с Owen/common-operations model лучше, чем denial of all reality.

Calvin 2 Pet 2:20–22:
- https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentaries_on_the_catholic_epistles/2_peter_2_20-22.htm

## 5. 2 Пет. 2:1 — отдельный hard case

Greek:

`τὸν ἀγοράσαντα αὐτοὺς δεσπότην ἀρνούμενοι`

Минимум:

- participle `ἀγοράσαντα` реально относится к `δεσπότην` and object `αὐτούς`;
- false teachers deny the Master characterized as having bought them;
- sentence intensifies their guilt and ends with swift destruction.

Проблема должна быть разделена на два вопроса:

1. **What does “bought” mean here?**
2. **Even if redemptive/atoning, does that equal regeneration/justification/application?**

И только затем задавать третье:

3. **How does this relate to definite/general atonement?**

## 6. Competing-view matrix: 2 Пет. 2:1

| View | Core claim | Strength | Main pressure point |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Nonsoteriological/covenantal purchase | `δεσπότης` often Father; `ἀγοράζω` echoes Deut 32:6 / God acquiring Israel; false teachers belong to covenant/providential sphere without blood-purchase | preserves actual `bought` without claiming saving redemption; strong OT-background possibility | NT redemptive uses of `ἀγοράζω`; whether Deut 32 is sufficiently demonstrable here; whether `δεσπότης` must be Father |
| B. Phenomenological/professional purchase | Peter speaks according to their apparent/professed Christian status; they appeared bought/knowing Christ, later nature revealed | integrates 2:1, 2:20, 2:22 with perseverance/definite atonement | phrase grammatically sounds like narrator assertion, not explicit “they claimed”; must justify phenomenological convention rather than assume theology |
| C. Actual atoning purchase, not necessarily saving application | Christ’s atoning purchase/provision extends to these teachers; they nevertheless perish because redemption benefits are not savingly applied apart from persevering faith | takes `ἀγοράζω` in familiar redemptive sense and Christ as `δεσπότης`; explains scandal of wording naturally | must distinguish purchase/provision/application carefully; challenges strict efficacy/definite-atonement formulations |
| D. Actual saving redemption followed by final apostasy | teachers were genuinely redeemed/saved and later lost salvation | straightforward conditional-security reading if all terms are saving | must explain 2:22 nature imagery, whole-letter election/steadfastness, wider perseverance texts; `bought` alone does not prove prior regeneration |

## 7. View A — Gill/Owen-style nonsoteriological reading

John Gill argues:

- `δεσπότης` here is God the Father rather than Christ;
- `bought` is not redemption by Christ’s blood;
- Deut 32:6 provides background of God “buying”/acquiring Israel;
- thus false teachers can be truly “bought” in a temporal/covenantal/providential sense and still perish.

Primary text:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/doctrinal.vii.iv.html

### Evaluation

**Strength:** it refuses to say the grammar is unreal and gives an OT corporate-purchase category already important in apostasy dossier.

**Pressure:** categorical claim “δεσπότης is not Christ” is too strong without full lexical/contextual proof. Jude 4 and Christian use of δεσπότης complicate a simple rule. Also `ἀγοράζω` has clear redemptive uses elsewhere.

Therefore publish Gill as a serious historical option, **not** as lexical closure.

---

## 8. View B — Schreiner’s phenomenological reading

Thomas Schreiner’s definite-atonement treatment explicitly acknowledges the lexical difficulty of the nonsoteriological solution and instead argues phenomenological language:

- false teachers were vitally inside church;
- they initially appeared Christian;
- “bought” and “know Lord/Savior” describe how they appeared/professed within visible community;
- dog/pig ending shows their true nature was never transformed.

Accessible research text:
- https://www.monergism.com/%E2%80%9Cproblematic-texts%E2%80%9D-definite-atonement-pastoral-and-general-epistles?page=1

The same chapter appears in the literature on definite atonement; publication must identify exact book/edition/pages before direct quotation.

### Evaluation

**Strength:** does not force an otherwise unattested nonsoteriological meaning merely because theology requires it; directly integrates vv.20–22.

**Pressure:** the text itself says `Master who bought them`, not `Master whom they said bought them`. Phenomenological language is an interpretive inference and must be defended from narrative/ecclesial convention, not smuggled into grammar.

This is a stronger Reformed option than pretending the wording is easy, but it remains a model, not a lexical fact.

---

## 9. View C — actual atoning purchase / general-redemption reading

Historical Cambridge/Expositor/Ellicott-type commentators often take:

- `δεσπότης` = Christ;
- `ἀγοράζω` = redemptive purchase;
- Christ’s blood is implied from related NT purchase language;
- false teachers are genuinely within scope of the purchase yet perish.

Research comparison access:
- https://www.biblehub.com/commentaries/2_peter/2-1.htm

Westminster Annotations (1651) are historically interesting because they preserve more than one route, including a sufficient-price reading and profession reading; modern transcription:
- https://calvinandcalvinism.wordpress.com/2009/01/27/the-westminster-annotations-second-edition-on-2-peter-21-and-jude-4/

### Evaluation

This view deserves better representation than «Arminians just read superficially». It has real lexical attraction because `ἀγοράζω` is used for redemptive purchase elsewhere.

But **actual atoning purchase does not by itself prove prior regeneration/justification**. One can hold general atonement/provision while denying that every person for whom provision exists was savingly united to Christ.

Thus 2:1 should not be used as a shortcut from `bought` to `saved and justified then lost`.

---

## 10. View D — conditional-security reading

A Wesleyan/Arminian reader may combine:

- actual redemptive purchase in 2:1;
- strong `ἐπίγνωσις` and escape language 2:20;
- turn-back language 2:21;
- destruction language;

and conclude that genuinely redeemed believers can commit final apostasy.

This is a serious whole-paragraph reading and must be answered at whole-paragraph/canon level, not by saying `ἐπίγνωσις` is weak or escape unreal.

Counter-analysis must ask:

- does `bought` entail applied justification or extent-of-atonement only?
- does `ἐπίγνωσις` entail regeneration?
- what does dog/sow nature imagery contribute?
- how does 1:10 calling/election and 3:17–18 function?
- how do John 10, Rom 8, 1 Pet 1, Jude 1/24, 1 John 2:19 etc. shape systematic synthesis?

---

## 11. `δεσπότης`: do not settle by concordance slogan

Gill-style claim that `δεσπότης` when divine “always” means Father needs careful qualification.

The noun means master/owner/sovereign and can refer to God; Jude 4 is especially relevant because `δεσπότης` occurs in a strongly christological construction whose syntax/translation has been debated historically.

Therefore:

> lexical frequency can inform the referent but cannot independently decide it. Immediate syntax, Peter/Jude relationship, purchase verb, and christological usage must all be weighed.

No publication wording: “Greek proves δεσπότης means Father.”

---

## 12. Relation to perseverance is real but must not prejudge atonement

Schreiner correctly identifies an important theological connection: if 2:1 means Christ **effectually redeemed** specific people in the same sense as final saving purchase and they nevertheless perish, perseverance doctrine is directly implicated.

But the inference can run too fast if it assumes:

`bought` = `effectually applied atonement` = `regenerated/justified`.

That equation is precisely what the verse does not explicitly spell out.

Best research sequence:

1. semantic/referent question;
2. OT/NT intertext;
3. local false-teacher context;
4. relationship to v20–22;
5. then atonement-system implications;
6. then perseverance-system implications.

---

## 13. 2:20–22 and Heb 6 — analogy without identity

Both passages describe people who go extraordinarily far:

- real spiritual/religious privilege;
- real change/escape;
- return/apostasy;
- worse judgment.

But vocabulary and rhetorical settings differ. Do not make them mechanically identical.

Useful shared category:

> **deep non-final participation / common operations / covenant-religious transformation can be real without being identical to the saving grace that perseveres.**

This remains a theological synthesis, not a claim that either Peter or Hebrews uses Owen’s later technical terminology.

## 15. Research verdict

Current best formulation without overclaim:

> 2 Петра 2 deliberately attributes to the false teachers a disturbingly high level of Christian proximity: they are described as having been “bought” by the Master, having escaped worldly defilements through knowledge of Jesus Christ, having known the way of righteousness, and then becoming entangled and overcome. A responsible Reformed reading should not deny that reality. The decisive question is whether these descriptions state regeneration/justification and effectual union, or covenant-professional/moral-spiritual realities short of that saving change. The final dog/sow images strongly support a distinction between real cleansing and unchanged determining nature, but they do not by themselves erase the force of “bought,” `ἐπίγνωσις`, or real escape. Therefore 2:1 remains a genuine hard text that requires an explicit competing-view matrix rather than a slogan.
