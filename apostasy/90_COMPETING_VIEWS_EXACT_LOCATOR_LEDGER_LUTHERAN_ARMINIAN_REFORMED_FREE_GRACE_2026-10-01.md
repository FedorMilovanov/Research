# 2. Карта основных моделей

## Model A — Confessional Lutheran: real faith/grace may be lost through willful falling away

Ключевой официальный источник:

**Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration XI — Election**, especially §§40–43.

Primary official/public text:
https://bookofconcord.org/solid-declaration/election/

### Confessional claim

§42 прямо говорит, используя Лк. 8:13:

- многие принимают Слово с радостью;
- потом «fall away again»;
- причина не в нежелании Бога дать grace for perseverance;
- они сознательно отворачиваются от святой заповеди;
- огорчают/ожесточают Святого Духа;
- снова запутываются в скверне мира;
- последнее состояние становится хуже первого.

Контекст §§40–43 также говорит о сопротивлении Святому Духу и rejection of the Word.

### Почему это важно для проекта

Нельзя представлять классическое лютеранство так:

> «они просто считают, что человек может случайно потерять спасение от любого греха».

Формула строит модель вокруг:

- реальной благодати;
- действенности Слова/Духа;
- человеческого wilful resistance;
- serious falling away;
- сохранения оправдания только верой, а не заслугами.

### Hard-text relevance

- Luke 8:13;
- Eph 4:30;
- Heb 10:26;
- 2 Pet 2;
- Luke 11:24–26.

### Strong point

Лютеранская позиция принимает positive language temporary-faith texts максимально серьёзно и не снижает `believe for a time` до «никогда не верили ни в каком смысле».

### Tension requiring response

Она должна быть сопоставлена с:

- John 6:37–40;
- John 10:27–30;
- Rom 8:28–39;
- Phil 1:6;
- 1 Pet 1:5;
- Jude 1,24;
- 1 John 2:19;

и показать, как promises of preservation relate to cases of alleged loss of true faith.

Status: `PRIMARY CONFESSIONAL EXACT`.

# 3. Conservative Arminian — Robert E. Picirilli

## Source

Robert E. Picirilli, **“The Possibility of Apostasy,”** Leroy Forlines Lectures at Free Will Baptist Bible College, 19–22 Nov 2002, lecture 4.

Primary PDF:
https://www.evangelicalarminians.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/07/Picirilli.-The-Possibility-of-Apostasy.pdf

Related full book:

Robert E. Picirilli, *Grace, Faith, Free Will*.

### Exact PDF structure

The lecture is 5 pages and explicitly states at the beginning that it will treat Heb 6:4–6 in the context of the whole epistle as biblical-theological evidence for the possibility of apostasy.

### Important technical argument — Heb 6:4–6

Picirilli argues:

1. `once-for-all enlightened` is salvation-language;
2. `tasted the heavenly gift` cannot be weakened by making “taste” merely partial, because Heb 2:9 uses the same verb for Christ tasting death;
3. `partakers of the Holy Spirit` is compared with Hebrews’ other `μέτοχος` language;
4. tasting God’s good word/powers of coming age belongs to genuine Christian experience;
5. the fifth participle, `παραπεσόντας`, is grammatically coordinate with the preceding aorist participles.

His strongest grammatical point:

> the final participle cannot simply be turned into an unreal hypothetical “if they should fall away” while the first four are treated as actual experiences.

He cites Homer Kent, an eternal-security interpreter, for the narrower grammatical point that there is no grammatical warrant for treating the last participle differently from the preceding ones.

### His conclusion

Picirilli reads:

- vv.4–5 = genuine regenerate believers;
- v.6 = actual apostasy;
- renewal after the apostasy described = impossible;
- warnings across Hebrews mutually interpret one another.

He explicitly denies that conditional security means salvation is “by faith and kept by works”; he frames preservation as continuing faith/union with Christ.

### Why this is a strong opposing source

This is not popular Arminian rhetoric.

It is:

- written by a conservative Free Will Baptist scholar;
- focused on Greek grammar/context;
- addresses the whole warning system;
- acknowledges assurance;
- distinguishes apostasy from ordinary Christian weakness.

### Project response required

A Reformed response must not say merely:

> “`παραπεσόντας` is hypothetical.”

Even if conditional force is discussed syntactically, the stronger response must address:

- identity of vv.4–5;
- land analogy vv.7–8;
- 6:9 `better things, things belonging to salvation`;
- Hebrews-wide use of `μέτοχος`;
- wilderness-generation typology;
- whether the listed privileges necessarily equal saving union.

### Source-custody note

The PDF text was available for parsed-text inspection; the web screenshot service returned cache-miss during visual verification. No image/layout-dependent claim is used here.

- five participles in 6:4–6 describe believers;

# 6. Wesleyan Arminian — Gareth Lee Cockerill

## Source

Gareth Lee Cockerill, Wesleyan Arminian contribution in *Four Views on the Warning Passages in Hebrews*.

Publisher/source metadata:
https://ebooks.faithlife.com/product/47077/four-views-on-the-warning-passages-in-hebrews
https://www.kregel.com/books/pdfs/excerpts/9780825421327.pdf

### Exact comparative locator

A later academic overview reports:

- Cockerill, p.289: believers can fall away and be severed from God’s saving future / face eternal judgment.

Research overview:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241644684_The_Warning_Passages_in_Hebrews_Revised_Theologies_and_New_Methods_of_Interpretation

### Distinctive Wesleyan concern

Cockerill has a pastoral/theological tension not identical to Osborne’s formulation:

- Wesleyan theology ordinarily allows restoration of backsliders;
- Hebrews appears to describe an apostasy from which there is no restoration.

Reviews of the book note that Cockerill distinguishes **recoverable backsliding** from the terminal repudiation described in the strongest Hebrews warnings.

### Project importance

This is highly compatible with the project’s own taxonomy even though the soteriological conclusion differs:

> **not every grievous fall is apostasy; not every backslider is terminal; a terminal category must be distinguished from recoverable wandering.**

The disagreement is over whether the terminal Hebrews subject was previously regenerate.

# 8. Reformed model A — Schreiner & Caneday: warnings as divinely appointed means

## Source

Thomas R. Schreiner and Ardel B. Caneday, *The Race Set Before Us: A Biblical Theology of Perseverance and Assurance* (IVP, 2001), 344 pp.

Publisher:
https://www.ivpress.net/the-race-set-before-us

Google Books:
https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Race_Set_Before_Us.html?id=9E6-el7-n4gC

### Exact chapter locators

- p.87 — `The Race to Be Run: The Necessity of Obedient Faith`;
- p.142 — `Running to Win the Prize: Heeding God’s Admonitions & Warnings`;
- p.214 — `Reflecting on Fallen Runners`;
- p.245 — `Drawing on God’s Grace`;
- p.268 — assurance;
- p.312 — divine appointment/election;
- p.332 — response to William Lane Craig on apostolic warnings.

### Position

Their model refuses two simplifications:

1. warnings are not fake hypotheticals with no pastoral force;
2. warnings do not prove that God’s elect finally perish.

They argue that admonitions/warnings are among the means by which God preserves His people in persevering faith.

### Strong point

This gives warnings **instrumental reality**:

> the elect persevere not despite warnings but in part through hearing and heeding them.

### Required guardrail

Do not turn “warnings as means” into a shortcut that bypasses exegesis of the identity/privileges/judgment in each text.

A means-model still has to explain what the author says would happen under the warned condition.

Status: `PRIMARY EXACT CHAPTER LOCATORS`.

# 10. Reformed model C — Wayne Grudem: Heb 6 privileges do not establish regeneration

## Source

Wayne Grudem, “Perseverance of the Saints: A Case Study from Hebrews 6:4–6 and the Other Warning Passages in Hebrews” (1995), later in *Still Sovereign*.

Author-hosted paper:
https://www.waynegrudem.com/perseverance-of-the-saints-a-case-study-from-hebrews-64-6-and-the-other-warning-passages-in-hebrews

PDF:
https://www.waynegrudem.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/perseverance-of-the-saints-hebrews-6.pdf

Published-book locator:

- pp.133–182 in the 1995/2000 collected versions commonly cited.

### Position

Grudem argues:

- descriptions in Heb 6:4–5 are substantial privileges;
- they do not conclusively establish internal saving state;
- falling away reveals the thorn-bearing character depicted by vv.7–8;
- 6:9’s “better things… belonging to salvation” distinguishes the readers’ hoped-for saving reality from the preceding apostate case.

### Strong point

Grudem forces careful attention to **the author’s own land analogy and v.9**, not only the vocabulary of vv.4–6.

### Weak point / caution

A fair project must acknowledge that some critics think his distinction relies too heavily on the prior systematic doctrine of perseverance.

Therefore the project should use Grudem alongside:

- Hebrews-wide vocabulary;
- OT wilderness analogy;
- technical commentaries;
- not as sole authority.

Status: `PRIMARY AUTHOR-HOSTED EXACT`.

# 11. Free Grace — true believers, temporal discipline and loss of reward

Free Grace is not merely another version of Calvinism.

Its typical warning-text model says:

- the addressees can be truly regenerate;
- eternal life cannot be lost;
- severe warnings concern temporal discipline, physical death, loss of inheritance/reward, or millennial privilege rather than hell.

This must be represented in its own terms.

---

## 11.1. Hebrews 6: true believers, field remains, works burned

Grace Evangelical Society article, “New Testament Repentance: Repentance in the Epistles and Revelation”:
https://faithalone.org/journal-articles/part-5-new-testament-repentance/

The article explicitly argues:

- Heb 6:4–5 describes genuine believers;
- believers cannot be eternally condemned;
- the field remains while worthless production is burned;
- fire represents temporal judgment;
- other Hebrews warnings likewise concern temporal judgments / loss of eternal rewards.

### Strong point

This view takes the positive believer-language seriously and does not make apostasy merely imaginary.

### Main exegetical burden

It must show that the severity language in:

- Heb 6:7–8;
- 10:26–31;
- 12:25–29;

really denotes temporal/reward judgment rather than eschatological destruction.

Status: `PRIMARY MOVEMENT SOURCE`.

# 12. Free Grace must not be confused with the Reformed “warnings-as-means” model

Both affirm eternal security, but their explanatory structures differ.

## Schreiner/Caneday

- final salvation necessarily includes persevering faith;
- warnings are means God uses to preserve the elect;
- final apostasy manifests non-saving/non-enduring faith.

## Free Grace loss-of-reward model

- regenerate status can remain despite severe/nonpersevering failure;
- warnings may target temporal discipline / reward / inheritance rather than eternal destiny;
- perseverance affects reward, fellowship, temporal experience and/or reign, not possession of eternal life.

### Editorial rule

Never label both simply:

> “eternal security view.”

Their doctrines of saving faith, perseverance, assurance, inheritance and reward can be materially different.

# 14. What the project currently concludes after fair comparison

This section records the current Reformed/Baptist synthesis **after** hearing the competing views.

## 14.1. Things conditional-security readings rightly force us to admit

1. Warning texts are not rhetorical toys.
2. `believe for a time` must not be rewritten as “never believed in any meaningful sense.”
3. Heb 6 privileges are real and high-level.
4. `παραπεσόντας` must be grammatically respected.
5. 2 Pet 2 describes real escape/knowledge, not mere external proximity with no change at all.
6. Visible covenant/community participation can be profound.
7. Apostasy language should retain genuine horror/finality.

## 14.2. Things the Reformed reading sees as decisive controls

1. Scripture itself distinguishes great privileges from “things belonging to salvation” (Heb 6:9).
2. Temporary faith/rootless reception is a biblical category.
3. John’s theology gives strong divine-preservation promises and distinguishes final secession from true belonging (1 John 2:19).
4. Hebrews joins severe warning with strong preservation/perfection language.
5. Warning can be a divinely appointed means rather than evidence that God’s decree fails.
6. Whole-canon preservation texts must be integrated rather than bracketed.

## 14.3. Things Free Grace rightly forces us to distinguish

1. discipline ≠ eternal condemnation;
2. physical death ≠ eternal damnation;
3. loss of reward ≠ loss of justification;
4. true believers can grievously fail;
5. church discipline can aim at restoration/salvation from temporal ruin.

## 14.4. Where Free Grace often creates pressure

The strongest Hebrews warning texts use language whose eschatological severity can exceed a simple reward-loss category.

Therefore each proposed temporal/reward reading must be demonstrated from the passage rather than assumed from eternal-security doctrine.

# 15. Claim-level fairness rules for final articles

When presenting another view:

### Do

- identify the named scholar/confession;
- cite the actual primary source;
- state the best argument;
- acknowledge where it reads the local grammar naturally;
- identify the exact point of disagreement;
- respond with text/canonical evidence.

### Do not

- say “Arminians think works save you”;
- say “Lutherans deny assurance”;
- say “Calvinists think warnings are fake”;
- say “Free Grace thinks sin does not matter”;
- cite a hostile critic as the only source for an opponent;
- mix Wesleyan backsliding/restoration with terminal apostasy as though they were identical.
