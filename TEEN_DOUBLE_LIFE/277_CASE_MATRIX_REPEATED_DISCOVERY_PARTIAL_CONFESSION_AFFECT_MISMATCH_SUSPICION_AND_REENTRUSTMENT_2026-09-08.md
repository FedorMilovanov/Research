## 0. Core rule

The future article must distinguish at least three axes:

1. **How did the truth become known?**
2. **What did the person do with facts parents did not already know?**
3. **What happened after pressure decreased?**

Mood is secondary.

### Locked formula

> **DISCOVERY SOURCE + TRUTH INITIATIVE + POST-CRISIS TRAJECTORY > FACIAL EXPRESSION.**

---

# Case A — Parents discover one fact; child immediately admits only that fact

### Example pattern

`parent sees evidence A`
→ `child says yes, A happened`.

### What can be said

- explicit denial stopped;
- admission is factual movement toward truth;
- this is **not** voluntary disclosure of A;
- repentance remains unproven.

### What cannot be said

- `the admission means nothing`;
- `because it came after proof, genuine repentance is impossible`.

### Parent posture

- name A truthfully;
- ask one clear material-completeness question;
- do not instantly restore trust;
- watch whether truth moves beyond A without another discovery.

### Credibility effect

`small positive for factual admission / neutral-to-low for repentance credibility alone`.

---

# Case B — Parents discover A; child voluntarily discloses B and C that parents did not know

### Pattern

`evidence A`
→ `admission A`
→ `voluntary B + C beyond evidence`.

### Why this matters

The person has surrendered a concealment advantage.

That is materially different from simply confirming A.

### What can be said

- stronger observable truth initiative;
- credibility increases relative to Case A;
- still not proof of regeneration;
- B/C require ordinary verification where material.

### Parent posture

- explicitly recognize that voluntary truth matters;
- do not promise no consequences;
- distinguish the original sin from the new truthful act.

### Credibility effect

`meaningful positive`.

---

# Case C — Child says “that is everything”; later remembers a genuinely forgotten detail

### Pattern

`completion claim`
→ `later memory returns`
→ `child self-corrects before new external discovery`.

### Key distinction

Later-added information does not automatically prove the earlier completion statement was a lie.

### Positive indicators

- detail was plausibly forgotten/confused;
- child returns voluntarily;
- no evidence child knowingly withheld it earlier;
- correction happens before being cornered.

### Parent posture

- receive the correction as truth movement;
- clarify material relevance;
- do not weaponize every memory imperfection as deception.

### Credibility effect

`neutral-to-positive depending on context`.

---

# Case D — Child says “that is everything”; parents later discover a known material fact that existed before the conversation

### Pattern

`completion claim`
→ `later external discovery B`
→ evidence indicates child knew B and B was materially relevant.

### What can be said

This is real negative credibility evidence.

The original problem is now twofold:

1. the underlying conduct;
2. the false completion representation / continued concealment.

### Parent posture

- do not reset the clock because the child now cries and admits B;
- distinguish new admission from prior voluntary truth;
- require trust restoration over time;
- preserve hope without pretending current credibility is high.

### Credibility effect

`meaningful negative`.

---

# Case E — Case D repeats several times

### Pattern

`A found → “everything”`
→ `B found → “now everything”`
→ `C found → revised story`
→ repeat.

### Project term

`DRIP_DISCLOSURE / EVIDENCE-BOUNDED CONFESSION`.

### What the repetition adds

The inference is no longer based on one imperfect conversation.

The person’s story repeatedly tracks the evidence envelope.

### Strong pastoral conclusion

> **Parents are not required to treat the next “now you know everything” as highly reliable merely because the latest admission is tearful.**

### What cannot be concluded

- `repentance is impossible`;
- `every future denial is false`;
- `parents know there must always be another layer`.

### Parent posture

- lower present factual confidence;
- stronger but proportionate stewardship;
- no theatrical sorrow requirement;
- invite unknown truth;
- do not invent allegations.

### Credibility effect

`strong cumulative negative until counter-pattern emerges`.

---

# Case F — Child cries intensely, then later laughs, eats, plays, or writes ordinary cheerful messages

### Pattern

`hard confession conversation`
→ `visible acute sorrow`
→ `ordinary affect returns`.

### What this proves

Very little by itself.

Possible meanings include:

- relief;
- emotional rebound;
- ordinary regulation;
- avoidance;
- immaturity;
- genuine repentance plus restored calm;
- shallow sorrow.

### Parent-side temptation

> `If you cared as much as we do, you could not be laughing.`

### Research verdict

That is emotionally understandable but evidentially weak.

### Parent posture

Do not demand performance of sadness.

Look instead at:

- truthfulness;
- forsaking;
- repair;
- future disclosure;
- conduct when unsupervised.

### Credibility effect

`near-neutral alone`.

---

# Case G — After tearful confession, later messages show coordinated concealment

### Pattern

`confession/tears`
→ later messages reveal:

- story coordination;
- deletion of evidence;
- another hidden channel;
- celebration that parents do not know the rest;
- immediate continuation of defended conduct.

### What changes

Now the issue is no longer emotional mismatch.

It is new behavioral evidence contradicting the claimed movement toward truth.

### Parent posture

- treat the new conduct as a separate serious truth/repentance problem;
- do not argue about whether the tears were “fake”;
- say instead: the subsequent deception materially lowers credibility.

### Credibility effect

`strong negative`.

---

# Case H — Child seems emotionally flat but voluntarily discloses unknown damaging facts

### Pattern

- little visible crying;
- calm speech;
- but child tells parents materially important facts they could not prove;
- accepts consequences;
- corrects prior lies;
- begins forsaking channels.

### Parent-side temptation

> `You do not look sorry.`

### Research verdict

Visible affect cannot outweigh stronger truth/forsaking evidence automatically.

### Parent posture

- do not confuse temperament with repentance;
- weigh costly truth seriously;
- still use time/fruit.

### Credibility effect

`meaningful positive despite muted affect`.

---

# Case I — Child is highly emotional but disclosure never moves beyond evidence

### Pattern

- intense crying;
- vows;
- religious language;
- story remains exactly at known facts;
- no voluntary expansion;
- no self-correction;
- same defenses remain.

### Theological category

Compatible with worldly sorrow / crisis sorrow.

Not enough for confident repentance attribution.

### Parent posture

- do not despise the emotion;
- do not crown it as repentance;
- look for truth/forsaking movement over time.

### Credibility effect

`emotion = ambiguous; lack of truth movement = concern`.

---

# Case J — Parents strongly suspect another act but have no evidence

### Pattern

History of lying makes suspicion plausible.

Child denies the new suspected act.

### Correct status

`LOW CREDIBILITY + UNRESOLVED FACT`.

### Parent posture

- maintain boundaries based on established history if needed;
- do not claim the suspected act is proved;
- do not interrogate until the answer changes;
- do not bluff evidence;
- leave possibility of later corroboration/disclosure.

### Credibility effect

The denial may be hard to trust, but the fact remains unresolved.

---

# Case K — Parents find no new evidence for a long period under total surveillance

### Pattern

- strong restrictions;
- little opportunity;
- no new misconduct found.

### What can be said

This is not nothing.

It may show compliance with boundaries.

### What cannot be said

- `therefore heart changed`;
- `therefore trust fully restored`;
- `therefore no risk remains`.

### Stronger evidence emerges when

- opportunity/autonomy gradually returns;
- truth remains stable;
- child self-discloses;
- no migration to hidden channels appears.

### Credibility effect

`weak-positive under maximal control; stronger as integrity persists with increasing freedom`.

---

# Case L — Child voluntarily seeks help before a new fall

### Pattern

> `I am tempted / contact resumed / I found a route around the boundary / I want help before I use it.`

### Why this matters

This reverses the old sequence.

Old:

`sin → conceal → discovery → admission`.

New:

`temptation/opportunity → voluntary truth → intervention before act`.

### Parent posture

- take this seriously as strong positive truth evidence;
- do not promise zero consequences if safety/access changes are needed;
- distinguish help-seeking from completed misconduct.

### Credibility effect

`strong positive prudential evidence`.

---

# Case M — Child voluntarily confesses more but parents demand every graphic detail

### Pattern

Truth begins to emerge, but parents/pastors escalate into exhaustive sexual questioning.

### Risk

The project would betray `165` if `full disclosure` became voyeuristic completeness.

### Correct limit

Material truth relevant to:

- ongoing conduct;
- safety;
- victims;
- coercion;
- other minors;
- access/channels;
- restitution;
- accountability.

Not every explicit detail.

### Parent posture

Ask only what legitimate stewardship requires.

### Credibility effect

Do not make refusal of unnecessary graphic detail proof of deception.

---

# Case N — Child says “I don’t remember” after a history of lies

### Two possibilities

1. evasive `I don’t remember`;
2. genuine uncertainty.

### Correct parent response

> `If you truly do not remember, do not invent an answer. If you later remember something materially important, bring it to us before we discover it.`

### Why this matters

It creates a future truth test without forcing false certainty.

### Credibility effect

`unresolved until corroboration/trajectory`.

---

# Case O — Child confesses unknown facts but immediately demands restored trust

### Pattern

Real positive disclosure occurs, followed by:

> `I told you everything, so give the phone back / stop checking / trust me now.`

### Interpretation

Voluntary truth remains genuinely positive.

But one act of disclosure does not erase a long history of unreliability.

### Parent posture

- praise truth without buying the conclusion;
- explain domain-specific trust;
- use gradual re-entrustment.

### Credibility effect

`positive truth evidence + immaturity/possible bargaining; not a full reset`.

---

# Case P — Child confesses after exposure and sincerely repents

### Important anti-cynicism case

True repentance can begin under painful exposure.

The fact that parents discovered first does not make the later repentance false by definition.

### What may become visible

- confession expands beyond evidence;
- previous lies are corrected;
- protected channels are surrendered;
- Godward moral language becomes more than performance;
- consequences are accepted;
- help is sought proactively;
- conduct remains changed after pressure decreases.

### Parent posture

- remain hopeful;
- do not demand perfect certainty immediately;
- allow fruit/time;
- do not permanently stigmatize.

### Credibility effect

`can rise substantially over time`.

# Product compression rules

## Must survive

- admission after proof and voluntary unknown-fact disclosure are not equivalent;
- repeated false completeness claims matter;
- affect duration is not a repentance metric;
- renewed deception after confession is much stronger evidence than smiling after confession;
- prior lying lowers credibility but does not prove every suspicion;
- full truth means material truth, not graphic exhaustiveness;
- being caught can still become the occasion of real repentance;
- trust rebuilds through domain-specific fruit/time.

# Final drafting lock

> **Do not ask whether the teenager looked repentant enough. Ask what relationship to truth is now observable. Did the story merely follow the evidence, or did truth begin to move ahead of discovery? Did hidden conduct resume when pressure dropped? Did the child voluntarily correct what parents could not know? Did parents preserve the distinction between low credibility and unproved suspicion? Those questions are more faithful than measuring tears, facial expression, or how quickly ordinary conversation returned.**

`TRUTH_INITIATIVE > EMOTIONAL_PERFORMANCE`.

`RENEWED_DECEPTION > AFFECT_MISMATCH` as negative evidence.

`LOW_CREDIBILITY ≠ OMNISCIENCE`.
