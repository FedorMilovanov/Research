# Библейская радикальность: «вырви глаз», бегство от похоти и родительская мудрость

**Статус:** RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Дата:** 2026-09-07

---

## 1. Центральный вопрос

Как использовать слова Христа о правом глазе и руке так, чтобы:

- сохранить их реальную страшную серьёзность;
- не превратить их в literal self-harm;
- не использовать их как оправдание произвольному родительскому контролю;
- показать их прямую релевантность digital temptation, pornography и repeated sexual sin.

---

## 2. Мф. 5:27–30: контекст — lust of the heart

Jesus moves from external adultery to internal lust.

Immediately after this heart-level diagnosis comes radical language:

- right eye causes you to stumble/sin → remove it;
- right hand causes you to stumble/sin → cut it off;
- better to lose a member than whole body be cast into Gehenna.

Greek `σκανδαλίζει` carries the idea of causing to stumble/entrap/occasion to sin.

Lexical source:
- https://biblehub.com/lexicon/matthew/5-29.htm

### Key interpretive point

The eye/hand are not the ultimate source of sin. Jesus has just located adultery in the heart.

Therefore literal mutilation cannot cure lust.

Reformed exposition at Ligonier explicitly makes this point and interprets the saying as ruthless mortification, including willingness to surrender good things if they become instruments/pathways of sin.

Source:
- https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/kill-your-sin

---

## 3. The saying destroys a common modern excuse

Modern argument:

> «A smartphone is normal and useful, therefore taking it away is extreme.»

Jesus’ logic:

> even something as precious/useful as an eye or hand is not worth preserving if it is functioning as a persistent path into damnable sin.

This does **not** mean phones are equivalent to eyes or that every child should lose one.

It means:

> **usefulness is not sufficient reason to preserve access to a tool that has become a repeated occasion of grave sin.**

---

## 4. Radical mortification is personal before it is parental

The command is addressed to the disciple.

The end-goal of Christian parenting therefore cannot be:

> «Father forever removes every possible temptation.»

Eventually the son/daughter must learn to say:

- I will not keep this app;
- I will not sleep with this device;
- I will leave this chat;
- I will end this relationship;
- I will not go to this place;
- I will not preserve this privacy pattern;

because **Christ is worth more than the opportunity**.

This is the mature form of the principle.

---

## 5. 2 Tim. 2:22: not negotiate with youthful passions, but flee

Paul’s counsel is not only cognitive resistance.

He tells Timothy to **flee youthful passions** and pursue righteousness, faith, love and peace together with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart.

The structure matters:

`flee from` + `pursue toward` + `with godly people`.

This matches our empirical corpus surprisingly well:

- remove dangerous opportunity;
- create positive alternative behavior;
- create a better peer community.

### Pastoral synthesis

Simply taking away pornography access without replacing the social/spiritual world may leave a vacuum.

Biblical mortification is not merely subtraction; it includes redirected pursuit.

---

## 6. 1 Cor. 6:18: flee sexual immorality

Again, the New Testament response to sexual sin is not:

> «test your strength by staying close».

It is:

> flee.

This gives strong theological warrant against the naïve idea that deliberate exposure to pornography or sexually charged situations is required to develop resilience.

### Important distinction

A child must eventually live in a fallen world.

But living in a fallen world ≠ voluntarily practicing proximity to every temptation.

Maturity includes knowing **which battles are fought by leaving**.

---

## 7. Proverbs’ pattern: avoid the path before the crisis

Proverbs repeatedly emphasizes path wisdom:

- do not enter path of wicked;
- turn away;
- pass on;
- keep way far from forbidden woman;
- do not go near her house.

The wisdom model is preventive, not merely reparative.

### Relevance

Parents often begin serious controls only after discovery.

Biblical wisdom suggests asking earlier:

> what path predictably leads there?

Not every barrier is fear-based overcontrol. Some are ordinary prudence.

---

## 8. Romans 13:14: make no provision for the flesh

This text is extremely useful for digital application.

The Christian is told not to arrange/provide for the flesh to gratify its desires.

Application principle:

> do not intentionally build a logistics chain that makes your known recurring sin easy.

Examples:

- unrestricted device alone at night after repeated pornography relapse;
- maintaining secret account used for sexual communication;
- keeping saved material;
- maintaining contact with a person whose relationship consistently leads to grave sin.

Again, these are applications, not exact biblical equivalents.

---

## 9. External restraint and internal mortification must remain distinct

A parent can remove a phone.

A parent cannot remove lust.

A filter can block a domain.

A filter cannot create love for holiness.

A curfew can reduce opportunity.

A curfew cannot regenerate.

### But therefore restraints are not useless

This is a common theological mistake.

The fact that external restraint cannot save does not mean it cannot:

- protect;
- delay;
- reduce harm;
- prevent victimization;
- weaken habit opportunity;
- preserve sleep/attention;
- create space for counsel and repentance.

God’s providence ordinarily works through means.

---

## 10. Matt. 5 and individualized boundaries

The text says **if your** eye/hand causes you to stumble.

At application level this supports specificity.

Not every Christian needs identical restrictions.

For one person:

- Instagram may be manageable.

For another:

- it may be a reliable pathway into lust/comparison/secrecy.

For one teenager:

- smartphone in bedroom may not presently produce misuse.

For another:

- it may be the repeatedly documented setting of relapse.

### Working principle

> **The more specific the known pathway to sin, the less wise it is to defend that pathway in the name of abstract freedom.**

---

## 11. Parents must distinguish precaution from punishment

Some boundaries should exist before wrongdoing:

- device-free sleep;
- age-appropriate filters;
- declared account transparency for younger teens;
- no unsupervised adult-stranger contact;
- age-specific platform rules.

These are **precautionary**, not evidence that child is guilty.

Other restrictions may be consequence/restoration plans after discovered misuse.

Mixing the two produces resentment:

> ordinary safety begins to feel like perpetual criminal probation.

The future article should teach parents to state which kind of boundary they are using.

---

## 12. Strong restriction after serious misuse can be loving

Modern therapeutic language can make parents fear decisive action.

But if evidence shows:

- pornography is established and repeated;
- sexual messaging is escalating;
- secret accounts are systematically used to evade safeguards;
- a predatory contact exists;
- exploitation risk is active;

then temporary radical restriction may be morally required.

### Strong line

> **Love does not prove itself by preserving every freedom while the child uses that freedom to move toward destruction.**

This is theological/pastoral reasoning, not clinical protocol.

---

## 13. Yet discipline must aim at restoration, not domination

Heb. 12 frames fatherly discipline as purposeful.

Eph. 6:4 warns fathers not to provoke children to anger but to bring them up in discipline/instruction of Lord.

Therefore strong boundaries should have:

- rationale;
- proportionality;
- review points;
- clear path toward restored trust;
- distinction between safety and shame;
- truthfulness from parent;
- attention to child’s repentance and actual reliability.

Infinite punishment is not the same as faithful discipline.

---

## 14. The parent must also practice radical amputation

The article should turn the text toward adults too.

A father cannot preach:

> «your phone is dangerous»

while being unreachable behind his own screen.

A mother cannot demand:

> «tell me everything»

while reacting to every confession with panic/humiliation.

Parents may need to cut off:

- their own compulsive phone habits;
- pornography;
- secret digital life;
- hypocrisy;
- entertainment patterns they would not tolerate in children;
- relational absence.

Otherwise household boundaries can look like arbitrary power rather than shared discipleship.

---

## 15. Radical separation without gospel can produce only cleaner hypocrisy

A teenager may obey because:

- afraid of punishment;
- lacks opportunity;
- wants device returned;
- fears public shame.

None equals repentance.

Therefore restoration conversations must continually return to:

- what was loved;
- what lie was believed;
- whom was harmed;
- what truth God says;
- whether there is confession without minimization;
- whether fruit consistent with repentance appears over time;
- whether the child is willing to surrender pathways voluntarily.

### Important marker

A very meaningful sign is not merely:

> «he stopped because filter works».

But eventually:

> **«he himself agrees that this access is dangerous and participates in cutting it off.»**

Not proof of regeneration, but significant fruit.

---

## 16. Relationship to sovereignty

If regeneration is sovereign, radical boundaries remain meaningful because:

- God ordains means;
- parents have commands/duties;
- external restraint is real common grace;
- Word, warning, discipline and community are real instruments;
- human responsibility remains.

A Calvinistic parent should be among the least fatalistic, not most fatalistic.

He cannot create life, so he prays.

He is commanded to use means, so he acts.

---

## 17. A future article should distinguish four levels of intervention

### Level 1 — Formation
Before crisis: Scripture, family relationship, sexual language, digital literacy, godly peers.

### Level 2 — Precaution
Age/risk-appropriate boundaries before known misuse.

### Level 3 — Radical interruption
After serious/repeated danger: remove access/pathway decisively.

### Level 4 — Restoration toward mature self-government
Rebuild trust, accountability and responsible freedom.

The mistake is to live forever only at Level 2 or only at Level 3.

---

## 18. The central line

> **Христос не учит нас доказывать зрелость близостью к греху. Он учит настолько серьёзно относиться к греху, что хорошая вещь может быть принесена в жертву, если для меня она стала дорогой в погибель. Родитель не может вырвать похоть из сердца ребёнка, но он обязан не выдавать ему бездумно инструменты, которые уже стали продолжением этой похоти.**

---

## 19. Publication guards

Do not use Matt. 5:29–30 to justify:

- bodily harm;
- abuse;
- arbitrary permanent deprivation;
- treating all teens as addicts;
- equating technology with sin itself.

Use it to establish:

- seriousness of sin;
- priority of soul over convenience/status/access;
- legitimacy of costly avoidance;
- necessity of heart-level mortification;
- personalized identification of occasions of sin.

---

## 20. Conclusion

The biblical alternative to both naïveté and panic is **ruthless wisdom**:

- do not worship freedom;
- do not worship control;
- know the child;
- know the pathway;
- cut off what truly feeds destruction;
- build what feeds righteousness;
- restore freedom as character and trust grow;
- never confuse a locked device with a new heart;
- never confuse sovereign grace with passivity.
