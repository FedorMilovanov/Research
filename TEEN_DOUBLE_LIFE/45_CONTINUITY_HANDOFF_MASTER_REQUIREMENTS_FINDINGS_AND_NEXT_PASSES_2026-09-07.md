User explicitly corrected any impression that:

> “regeneration is random, so upbringing barely matters.”

Required balance:

- God alone regenerates;
- regeneration is sovereign;
- good parenting cannot guarantee regeneration;

# 4. Correct model of hardening / “he already doesn’t fight”

User corrected an overly cognitive sequence such as:

> “Eventually he decides this is normal sexuality.”

The user’s intended observation is subtler:

> A teenager may still know intellectually that pornography/blasphemy/sexual sin is wrong, but eventually **stop practically debating it**. He desires, acts, hides, repeats. The moral proposition remains somewhere in memory, but it no longer meaningfully governs the moment.

Preferred biblical framing:

- lives according to passions/desires;
- enslaved to various lusts/pleasures (Titus 3:3);
- callousness/loss of sensitivity (Eph. 4:17–19);
- deceitfulness of sin hardens (Heb. 3:12–13);
- desire entices (James 1:14–15);
- can retain knowledge while suppressing truth (Rom. 1 themes).

> “brain switched off”

except as obvious metaphor, never diagnosis.

Owen is key:

- a powerful indwelling sin can defile conscience and expose to hardening;
- temporary post-exposure suppression is not mortification.

# 5. Why sin is attractive — required biblical model

Do not tell readers that sin offers no pleasure.

Scripture recognizes **temporary pleasure**:

- Heb. 11:25 — fleeting pleasures of sin;
- Prov. 9:17–18 — stolen waters sweet, hidden death;
- James 1:14–15 — desire entices/drags;
- broad path vs narrow path imagery must be handled in Matt. 7 context, not reduced to self-control psychology.

Preferred formulation:

> **Sin often gives exactly enough immediate pleasure to make its lie persuasive while concealing the end of the path.**

Brooks (*Precious Remedies*) is especially important:

- Satan shows bait and hides hook;
- minimizes sin;
- shows sins of saints while hiding their sorrow/repentance;
- presents God as mercy without holiness;
- persuades that repentance later will be easy;
- makes people bold near occasions of sin;
- emphasizes difficulty of holiness;
- uses wicked company.

Modern digital environment can intensify access to these old devices, but **technology is not a fourth source of sin** alongside world/flesh/devil.

# 8. Discovery is not repentance

Major distinction:

- being exposed;
- crying;
- fear of punishment;
- temporary perfect behavior;
- deleting apps;
- apologizing

are not automatically repentance.

Owen explicitly warns about temporary suppression after scandal/terror.

Watson should be used for counterfeit vs true repentance.

Better indicators to research pastorally:

- naming sin without euphemism;
- ceasing defense;
- willingness to bring hidden facts to light;
- accepting consequences;
- removing access/occasions;
- seeking help;
- restitution where applicable;
- change persisting after immediate crisis;
- new war against sin rather than merely new skill at concealment.

Guard:

Repeated struggle does not automatically equal settled impenitence.

Important contrast:

`recurring struggle + truth + warfare` ≠ `settled peace + defense + planned concealment + refusal of correction`.

# 29. Possible eventual central theses (WORKING, not final copy)

> **Digital culture did not create the sinful heart; it gave old lusts a portable infrastructure.**

> **Parents cannot regenerate a child, but they are not spectators waiting for regeneration.**
