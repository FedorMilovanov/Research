# Unregenerate Minor — Required Worship, Attendance, Family Instruction, and No Forced Profession

**Date:** 2026-09-07

# 0. The practical question

Once parents conclude that a child may be unregenerate, a dangerous counsel can appear:

> “Then stop requiring church, Scripture, prayer, family worship, or moral obedience. Otherwise you are forcing hypocrisy.”

The opposite error also exists:

> “Force the child to speak as though he believes, take Christian privileges, give testimonies, sing representative roles, or make professions he does not actually own.”

Both are confused.

### Governing thesis

> **Parents may require an unregenerate minor to live under the ordinary religious and moral order of a Christian household without requiring him to make a false profession of saving faith.**

The distinction is:

`required exposure / instruction / household participation`  
≠  
`coerced profession / counterfeit conversion`.

---

# 1. 1689 19.5 — moral law binds the unregenerate too

The moral law binds all because of God's authority as Creator.

Therefore:

> “He is unsaved”

does not imply:

> “He is exempt from truthfulness, sexual purity, honor, sobriety, obedience to lawful authority.”

This is already locked elsewhere but controls this question.

---

# 2. Ephesians 6:1–4 — children are addressed as moral agents under parental authority

Paul does not say:

> “Children, obey only after evidence of regeneration.”

He commands children to obey parents in the Lord and commands fathers to bring them up in the Lord's discipline/instruction.

### Consequence

Parents may require:

- attendance at family instruction;
- ordinary household obedience;
- church attendance as a family practice;
- respectful participation in the household's schedule.

### Guard

Parental authority is not unlimited and must itself remain under Christ.

---

# 3. 1689 22.6 — worship in private families daily and public assemblies not wilfully neglected

Primary source:

- https://www.the1689confession.com/chapter-22

1689 22.6 says God is to be worshipped:

- everywhere in spirit and truth;
- in private families daily;
- secretly by individuals;
- solemnly in public assemblies, which are not carelessly or wilfully neglected when God calls to them.

### Project significance

The confession does not imagine Christian parents saying:

> “Because one child may not yet be regenerate, the household must cease family worship or exempt him from all exposure.”

Family worship belongs to the ordered life of the Christian household.

### Guard

The paragraph describes Christian worship broadly; it is not a detailed coercion manual for resistant minors.

Application must preserve Eph. 6:4 and the distinction between presence and profession.

---

# 4. 1689 22.3 — prayer required of all men

Prayer with thanksgiving is required by God of all men.

This prevents the crude statement:

> “If you are unregenerate, you have no business praying.”

The proper call is:

> **seek God truly, repent, pray, do not use prayer as camouflage.**

### Important nuance

A parent may teach/lead a child to pray.

But forcing a child to say:

> “Thank you God that I am saved”

when the child does not believe that is manufacturing falsehood.

---

# 5. 2 Timothy 3:14–15 — childhood Scripture exposure is a means, not proof of regeneration

Timothy knew the sacred writings from childhood.

The passage commends early biblical instruction as able to make wise for salvation through faith in Christ.

### Project principle

> **Teaching Scripture before conversion is not pointless because Scripture is one of God's appointed means through which sinners are brought to faith.**

### Guard

The text does not say early instruction guarantees conversion.

---

# 6. Baxter — household government includes the unrenewed

Richard Baxter's *Christian Directory* explicitly distinguishes within families:

- ungodly/carnal/unrenewed;
- godly/in grace.

Yet he continues to treat the household as a field of:

- instruction;
- catechesis;
- discipline;
- prayer;
- ordered worship.

Primary accessible text:

- Project Gutenberg / public-domain editions of *A Christian Directory*;
- historical edition mirrors.

Baxter does not reason:

> “Unrenewed household member = exempt from household religious government.”

### Project use

This gives strong historical Reformed support for:

> **means before regeneration without confusing means with regeneration.**

---

# 7. Requiring presence is not the same as certifying faith

A parent can say:

> “Ты живёшь в нашем доме. По воскресеньям наша семья идёт на богослужение; вечером мы вместе читаем Писание. Я не буду заставлять тебя говорить, что ты веришь, если ты не веришь. Но я и не буду воспитывать тебя так, будто Бог не имеет к тебе никакого отношения.”

This is internally coherent.

### Distinguish

## May require

- be present at family Scripture reading;
- listen respectfully;
- attend public worship with family;
- learn/catechize age-appropriately;
- obey household moral rules.

## Must not manufacture

- fake testimony;
- baptism profession without credible faith;
- false assurance;
- forced claim “I love Christ”;
- church membership by family inheritance.

---

# 8. Congregational singing needs nuance

Earlier modules distinguish:

- general worship duty;
- ordinary congregational presence;
- formal choir/platform entitlement.

A parent should not turn a resistant child into a public performer to preserve appearances.

But neither should parents teach:

> “Because you are unsaved, God's praise and claims are irrelevant to you.”

### Safe approach

The child may be present and learn the content of Christian worship.

Formal representative singing/choir remains a church-stewarded privilege subject to the Part III controls.

---

# 9. The Lord's Supper and baptism are different from mere attendance

This distinction is essential in a Baptist context.

## Attendance

A household practice / means exposure.

## Baptism

1689 29.2 requires actual profession of repentance, faith and obedience.

## Membership / Table

Ecclesial privileges connected to credible profession and church order.

### Therefore

> **Parents can require church attendance without requiring baptism.**

This prevents both:

- moral laissez-faire;
- counterfeit profession.

---

# 10. What if the child openly says “I don't believe”?

The parent should not demand:

> “Say you believe anyway.”

Better:

- ask what he means;
- distinguish honest doubt from rebellion used as license;
- keep teaching;
- maintain household moral boundaries;
- require respectful attendance if prudentially appropriate while he remains a dependent minor;
- avoid making attendance the sole measure of spiritual health.

### Strong line

> **Honesty about unbelief is spiritually safer than a polished false profession; honesty about unbelief does not create moral independence from God or parents.**

---

# 11. What if forced attendance only produces anger?

This requires wisdom, not automatic surrender.

Questions:

- Is the parent provoking through needless humiliation?
- Is the child merely angry because authority limits him?
- Is the setting physically/safeguardingly safe?
- Is attendance being used as punishment rather than ordinary family order?
- Is there room for genuine questions?

Eph. 6:4 forbids provoking; it does not abolish instruction when a child dislikes instruction.

### Guard

Do not use:

> “He resents church, so stop taking him.”

as a universal rule.

Do not use:

> “More coercive hours in church always produce more faith.”

as the opposite universal rule.

---

# 12. Why releasing all expectations can reinforce the wrong theology

If parents say:

> “Since you are not converted, live as you like until God changes you,”

they may unintentionally teach:

- God's law binds only believers;
- repentance is not presently required;
- household authority depends on conversion;
- sin is expected but not confronted until regeneration.

This contradicts the project's controlling doctrine:

> unregenerate ≠ unaccountable.

---

# 13. Why forcing a Christian persona is equally destructive

If parents require:

- expected testimony;
- scripted emotions;
- outward spiritual language rewarded as proof;
- hiding doubts to preserve family reputation,

they may train the exact double life the project is trying to expose.

### Locked line

> **Christian parents must require truth, not religious theater.**

A child should be free to say:

> “I do not know whether I believe,”

without being free to say:

> “Therefore God's moral law and household authority no longer apply to me.”

---

# 14. Final synthesis

> **Невозрождённый несовершеннолетний ребёнок не должен быть оставлен в религиозно-нравственной нейтральной зоне. Родители вправе и обязаны воспитывать его под Словом, семейным богослужением, церковным собранием и нравственной дисциплиной. Но они не должны покупать внешнее спокойствие ценой ложного исповедания — заставлять его изображать веру, крещение или духовный опыт, которых он не имеет. Библейская цель — не религиозный спектакль и не моральная бесхозность, а правдивая жизнь под данными Богом средствами.**
