# 0. Part II promise

Part II must answer:

> **If only God can give a new heart, what are parents actually responsible to do—and how can they combine formation, serious boundaries, trust, voluntary accountability, correction, and gospel hope without either abandoning an unregenerate child to sin or treating external control as regeneration?**

This part should contain little modern psychology.

Primary movement:

`God gives life`  
`→ parents still have commanded means/duties`  
`→ children remain morally accountable`  
`→ parents must know/guard the particular child`  
`→ radical boundaries can be loving`  
`→ harsh provocation is also sin`  
`→ truth before and after discovery`  
`→ exposure is not repentance`.

# 1. Biblical source packet

## A. Deuteronomy 6:4–9 — ordinary-life transmission

### Direct claim

Israel is commanded to love Yahweh wholly, keep his words on the heart, and diligently teach them to children in the ordinary rhythms of home and life.

### Publication use

Establish that biblical parenting is not mere emergency discipline after scandal.

It is repeated formative instruction woven into life.

### Redemptive-historical guard

This is covenant Israel. Do not simply equate a New Covenant Baptist household with old-covenant national membership.

The moral/formative principle of parents teaching God’s truth remains strongly relevant, but do not build hereditary regeneration from the passage.

---

## B. Proverbs 22:6 — real wisdom pattern without mechanical guarantee

### Direct/wisdom claim

Early formation has lasting significance.

### Project use

The user explicitly rejects both:

- reducing Prov. 22:6 to meaningless “sometimes parenting matters”;
- treating it as a contractual guarantee of regeneration.

### Publication formulation

> Proverbs speaks in genuine wisdom-patterns: early formation is not neutral and normally leaves durable tracks, while the genre does not promise that every well-taught child will certainly be regenerate in old age.

### Guard

Avoid making translation/exegesis controversy carry more weight than necessary. Verify the selected translation/commentary at drafting time.

---

## C. Ephesians 6:1–4 — child duty + parental non-provocation + nurture

### Direct claim

- children are commanded to obey parents in the Lord;
- duty is grounded as right and connected to the fifth commandment;
- fathers are forbidden to provoke children to anger and commanded to bring them up in the discipline/instruction of the Lord.

### Publication use

This is the central NT parenting text for the entire article.

It destroys two errors simultaneously:

1. child moral neutrality;
2. parental tyranny.

### Strong synthesis

> The same passage that gives parents a real duty to discipline also places a moral boundary around how that authority is exercised.

---

## D. Colossians 3:20–21 — obedience + discouragement guard

### Direct claim

Children are commanded to obey; fathers must not provoke/embitter them lest they become discouraged.

### Publication use

Supporting text for the emotional/moral effects of arbitrary severity without psychologizing the category.

---

## E. Matthew 5:27–30 — radical removal in the context of lust

### Direct claim

Jesus treats lust/adultery at heart level and commands radical dealing with what causes/stumbles into sin using eye/hand imagery, because eternal stakes outweigh preserving useful members.

### Publication use

Governing image for:

- phone/app/account removal;
- losing a convenience rather than preserving a repeated occasion of grave sin;
- treating sexual temptation as serious before the act.

### Guard

- not literal self-mutilation;
- not parental abuse;
- parent should not claim every personally disliked activity is the child’s “eye to pluck out.”

The application must be tied to a demonstrable occasion/pattern of serious sin.

---

## F. 2 Timothy 2:22 — flee youthful passions + pursue with holy company

### Direct claim

Timothy is told not merely to withstand youthful passions but to flee them and positively pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace **with** those who call on the Lord from a pure heart.

### Publication use

Important corrective to a purely negative boundary model:

`remove occasion` + `replace company/direction`.

### Guard

Timothy is a minister, not a generic teenager. The command nevertheless articulates a broader Christian strategy of fleeing lust and pursuing holiness with godly companions.

---

## G. 1 Corinthians 6:18–20 — flee sexual immorality

### Direct claim

Paul commands flight from sexual immorality and grounds bodily holiness in belonging to God / being bought with a price.

### Publication use

Supports “distance can be wisdom, not cowardice.”

### Guard

Do not reduce whole passage to a device-filter rule.

---

## H. Romans 13:14 — make no provision for flesh to gratify desires

### Direct claim

Put on Christ and do not arrange/provide for flesh toward lusts.

### Publication use

Excellent concise text for opportunity engineering:

> wisdom includes not stocking the road to the temptation one says he wants to escape.

---

## I. 2 Corinthians 7:8–11 — godly sorrow vs worldly sorrow

### Direct claim

Paul distinguishes sorrow according to God that produces repentance from worldly sorrow, and describes earnest fruits/effects in the Corinthian response.

### Publication use

Central after-discovery text.

### Guard

Do not create an infallible seven-item checklist from v.11.

The passage concerns a concrete church situation and Paul’s letter; use its distinction and fruit orientation, not as a laboratory diagnostic.

---

## J. Proverbs 28:13 — concealment vs confession/forsaking

### Direct claim

Concealing transgression does not prosper; confession and forsaking are associated with mercy.

### Publication use

Compact bridge from secrecy to repentance.

### Guard

Do not teach that verbal confession without turning exhausts the verse; `forsakes` matters.

---

## K. 1 John 1:5–10 — light / confession / no sinless perfectionism

### Direct claim

Walking in light and truthful confession coexist with acknowledgement that believers still sin.

### Publication use

Supports a family truth-culture without demanding sinless children.

### Guard

Walking in light ≠ “never relapses.”

# 2. 1689 confessional packet

Primary stable text:

- https://baptistconfession.org/

Use direct sections only after reopening exact paragraph at drafting time.

## 2.1 Chapter 10 — Effectual Calling

### Verified core

God effectually calls by Word and Spirit, enlightens the mind, removes heart of stone, gives heart of flesh, renews will, and draws to Christ; the sinner comes freely because made willing by grace.

### Publication function

Parents **cannot regenerate**.

### Equal guard

The chapter explicitly includes Word/Spirit means; sovereign calling is not a warrant for parental passivity.

---

## 2.2 Chapter 14 — Saving Faith

### Function

Saving faith is Spirit-wrought and ordinarily tied to the ministry of the Word; faith grows/strengthens through appointed means.

### Publication function

The means matter precisely because God has appointed means.

Do not describe faith as a predictable product of a parenting method.

---

## 2.3 Chapter 15 — Repentance unto Life and Salvation

### Verified core

Saving repentance involves Spirit-produced sense of sin’s evil, faith in Christ, godly sorrow, detestation of sin, prayer for pardon/strength, purpose and endeavor to walk pleasing to God.

### Publication use

Strong confessional synthesis after discovery.

### Guard

Do not convert it into “if all observable indicators appear, we have infallible knowledge of regeneration.”

---

## 2.4 Chapter 17 — Perseverance

### Function

True believers cannot finally fall away, yet real believers may experience grievous falls/obscured assurance and need renewed repentance.

### Publication use

Protects against:

> “he fell again, therefore he was never converted.”

### Equal guard

Perseverance is not permission to reassure a person peacefully defending sin.

---

## 2.5 Chapter 19 — Moral Law

### Core project use

Moral law binds **all**, not only justified people.

### Publication use

Direct answer to:

> “He is unregenerate, what do you expect? Leave him alone until conversion.”

### Strong synthesis

> Regeneration is necessary for spiritual obedience; lack of regeneration is not a license to disobey God or lawful parental authority.

# 3. Classical source packet

## 3.1 Richard Baxter — *A Christian Directory*, Part II / *Christian Economics*

Primary accessible text:

- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43800/43800-h/43800-h.htm

Historical/source note:

The Gutenberg text transcribes Part II of Baxter’s *Christian Directory* from the nineteenth-century collected edition; primary EEBO should be preferred if an exact historical quotation is central.

### High-value material verified

Baxter includes dedicated chapters on:

- holy government of families;
- careful education of children;
- duties of parents;
- duties of children;
- duties of children/youth toward God;
- right teaching of children.

Especially relevant observations:

- children have less mature capacity, but the measure of reason they do possess is to be exercised;
- parents should instruct children about God/salvation;
- Baxter strongly warns against licentious company that teaches cursing, lying, filthy talk and disobedience;
- he sees parental neglect of education as morally serious.

### Paedobaptist guard

Baxter’s baptism/covenant assumptions are **not controlling** for a 1689 Reformed Baptist article.

Use his practical family counsel where biblically sound; do not import his covenant-child conclusions uncritically.

---

## 3.2 Thomas Watson — *The Ten Commandments*, Fifth Commandment

Primary accessible text:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/commandments/commandments.iv.v.html

### Verified high-value balance

Watson tells parents to act lovingly and prudently.

He identifies provocation through:

- abusive/opprobrious language;
- causeless or excessive correction;
- denying genuine necessities;
- partiality;
- parental disgraceful conduct/hypocrisy;
- commands that violate conscience.

### Publication use

One of the best classical correctives against confusing strict biblical discipline with arbitrary authoritarianism.

### Strong synthesis

> Puritan strictness was not supposed to mean parental license to humiliate or punish beyond the fault.

Synthesis, not quotation.

---

## 3.3 John Owen — *Of Temptation*

Primary:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.x.html

### Part II use

Owen’s strongest contribution is **individualized protection**:

- know the child/person’s particular frame;
- recognize peculiar lust/weakness;
- avoid corresponding occasions/companies/opportunities.

### Translation to parenting

The parent does not know the heart infallibly, but may know observed vulnerabilities:

- secrecy;
- sexual curiosity;
- peer dependence;
- inability to stop nighttime use;
- repeated circumvention;
- a particular corrupting relationship.

### Guard

Owen gives individual Christian watchfulness, not a direct parenting statute. Parental application is pastoral inference.

---

## 3.4 Stephen Charnock — regeneration vs outward restraint

Primary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nec_regen/nec_regen.ii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nat_regen/nat_regen.i.html

### Verified key distinction

Charnock explicitly distinguishes **outward reformation/restraint** from regeneration.

He argues:

- outward reformation can exist without inward renewal;
- fear/force can restrain action without changing inclination;
- outward reformation can still be of real/useful significance while remaining insufficient for new birth.

### Publication use

Excellent for:

> taking the phone away is not regeneration—and that does not make taking the phone away pointless when it is functioning as an occasion of serious sin.

### Guard

Do not use coercion as the positive model of sanctification; Charnock’s point is precisely insufficiency of outward restraint.

---

## 3.5 Charles Spurgeon — *Do Not Sin Against the Child*

Primary institutional source:

- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child

### Verified high-value balance

Spurgeon argues:

- children genuinely need supernatural conversion;
- conversion should be sought now, not postponed to adulthood;
- children should not be admitted on a lower standard of real faith/repentance;
- childhood terror is not automatically repentance;
- childhood religious joy is not automatically faith;
- children should not be burdened with needless suspicion or adult mannerisms.

### Publication use

This is the strongest Baptist pastoral source against both:

- hereditary/automatic Christianity;
- “wait until he grows up; no serious spiritual expectations yet.”

### Short direct-quote candidate

Select later. Product policy requires Russian translation of quoted thought in Russian article; exact English source remains citation.

# 9. Part II theological backbone in one paragraph

Not publication prose; editorial logic test:

> Parents cannot give the child a new heart because effectual calling belongs to God. Yet Scripture therefore does not make them spectators: they must teach, discipline, model, guard, and bring the child repeatedly under truth. The unregenerate child is not morally neutral; God’s law still commands him. Because external restraint is not regeneration, parents must never mistake compliance for conversion—but neither should they leave fitting occasions of serious sin untouched. Jesus’ radical language about lust legitimizes real sacrifice of freedoms where necessary, while Eph. 6 and Col. 3 forbid parental authority from becoming humiliating provocation. The goal is not a child who hides better but one who can bring temptation and sin into the light. When discovery happens, fear and tears are not enough: biblical repentance names, hates, confesses, forsakes, seeks mercy and strength, and begins to bear fruit. Even then, parents must remember that true believers can fall grievously and that only God knows the heart perfectly.

If future draft loses any paired tension in this paragraph, revise before publication.
