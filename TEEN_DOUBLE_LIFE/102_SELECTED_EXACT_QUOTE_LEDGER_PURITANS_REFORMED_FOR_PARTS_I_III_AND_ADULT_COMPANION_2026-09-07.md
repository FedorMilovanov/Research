# PART II — Parents / boundaries / repentance

## Q3. Thomas Watson — repentance category

Candidate:

> “Repentance is a pure gospel grace.”

Primary HTML:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/commandments.vi.ii.html

Work:
- Thomas Watson, *The Ten Commandments*, section “Repentance.”

### Use

Discovery/repentance section.

### Status

**GREEN — exact wording verified in HTML.**

### Why useful

It prevents repentance from being framed as:
- self-punishment;
- payment for forgiveness;
- merely legal terror.

## Q4. Thomas Watson — tears are not enough

Candidate:

> “The eye may be watery, and the heart flinty.”

Primary HTML:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/commandments.vi.ii.html

### Use

`being caught / crying ≠ automatically repentance`.

### Status

**GREEN — exact wording verified.**

### Guard

Do not use it cynically to dismiss every tear. Watson’s point is insufficiency as proof, not that true repentance cannot weep.

## Q5. C. H. Spurgeon — same saving reality in children

Candidate:

> “the same repentance which is needful in an adult in order to salvation, is indispensable in a child”

Primary HTML:
- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child

Sermon:
- “Do Not Sin Against the Child,” 1868.

### Use

Part II or Part III when rejecting:
- inherited Christianity;
- lower essence of repentance merely because a person is young.

### Status

**GREEN — exact phrase verified in Spurgeon Library HTML.**

### Critical context guard

Spurgeon immediately balances this by refusing needless suspicion and adultized expectations. Same saving reality **in kind** does not mean same maturity or vocabulary.

# ADULT COMPANION — adulthood / family duties / discipline

## Q7. John Gill — parental power changes with maturity

Candidate:

> “though the power of parents over children is less when grown up, the duty of observance, gratitude, and filial reverence does not cease”

Primary HTML:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html

Work:
- John Gill, *Body of Practical Divinity*, duties of parents and children.

### Use

Excellent opening classical witness for:

> adulthood changes jurisdiction/form of authority without erasing filial relation.

### Status

**GREEN — exact wording verified.**

### Guard

Gill retains a historically strong account of filial obedience. Do not use one sentence to erase other biblical signals of adult household formation/changed jurisdiction (Gen. 2:24 etc.).

---

## Q8. Jonathan Edwards — excommunication does not abolish parental care

Candidate:

> “nor parents from parental affection and care toward their children.”

Primary HTML:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/edwards/works2/works2.v.html

Context:
Edwards says natural/civil relation duties remain even toward the excommunicated.

### Use

Adult companion section rejecting total `family excommunication` as automatic response to rebellion.

### Status

**GREEN — exact wording verified.**

### Guard

Edwards is not saying ecclesial fellowship is unchanged. His point is that discipline does not dissolve antecedent natural relations.

---

## Q9. John Owen — same natural-duty principle

Candidate:

> “No suspension of duties antecedently necessary by virtue of natural or moral relation is allowed”

Primary HTML:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/evangelicalchurches/evangelicalchurches.iii.xiv.html

### Use

Optional alternative to Edwards, not both in full prose unless footnote/source note needs historical convergence.

### Status

**GREEN — exact wording verified.**

### Guard

Owen also restricts ordinary chosen fellowship with an excommunicated member. Preserve the whole distinction.
