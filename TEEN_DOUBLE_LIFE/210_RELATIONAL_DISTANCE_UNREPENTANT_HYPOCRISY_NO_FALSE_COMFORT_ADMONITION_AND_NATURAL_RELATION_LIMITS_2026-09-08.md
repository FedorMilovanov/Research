# Relational distance under unrepentant hypocrisy — no false comfort, continued admonition, and natural-relation limits

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** research-only / biblical-pastoral / adult-child + church interface / not publication approval  
**Owner question:** When a person knowingly chooses defended sin, how should relational closeness change so believers do not communicate moral normalcy, while avoiding an unbiblical total severing of natural family duties?

---

## 0. Governing thesis

Scripture contains a real category of **reduced voluntary fellowship / familiarity** with persistently disobedient professing Christians.

But it also contains equally real limits:

- do not treat an offending brother as an enemy;
- continue admonition;
- natural family duties survive even formal church excommunication;
- unbelievers/outside persons are not governed by the identical church-discipline rule;
- ordinary mercy and humanity remain duties;
- restoration, not humiliation for its own sake, is the proper end.

### Central formula

> **Do not manufacture hostility; withdraw false normalcy.**

And:

> **Changed fellowship is not annihilated relationship.**

---

# 1. 2 Thessalonians 3:14–15 gives the cleanest biblical pair

Paul says that a person refusing apostolic instruction is to be marked and believers are not to keep ordinary company with him so that he may be ashamed.

Immediately:

> do not regard him as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother.

Source text:
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Thessalonians+3%3A14-15&version=KJV

### Classic commentary

Gill explains the reduced company as preventing:

- encouragement to continue the sin;
- public impression that the conduct is connived at.

He still preserves admonition and restoration as the goal.

Source:
https://www.biblestudytools.com/web/2-thessalonians/3-14.html

Poole likewise says familiarity is restricted while enough contact remains to admonish the offender privately or publicly.

Source:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_thessalonians/3-15.htm

### P0

This is not:

> `IGNORE UNTIL HE SUFFERS ENOUGH`.

It is:

> `REDUCED FAMILIARITY + CONTINUED BROTHERLY ADMONITION + RESTORATIVE END`.

---

# 2. The text addresses church disobedience, not directly a parent-child eviction protocol

2 Thess. 3 concerns apostolic instruction within the Christian community.

Therefore it cannot be mechanically converted into:

> "My unbelieving adult daughter sinned; therefore Scripture commands me to stop eating with her."

### Transfer that is safer

The text establishes moral principles:

1. normal familiar association can sometimes communicate false approval/connivance;
2. shame can have a restorative social function;
3. reduced association need not equal hatred;
4. admonition remains possible precisely because the offender is not treated as an enemy.

Exact family application requires natural-relation controls.

---

# 3. 1 Corinthians 5 sharply distinguishes professing insider from outsider

Paul's `not even to eat` rule in 1 Cor. 5:11 is directed to someone **called a brother** while living in specified gross unrepentant sins.

Paul explicitly says he is **not** demanding the same separation from immoral people of the world, because then believers would have to leave the world.

Calvin preserves this insider/outsider distinction.

Source:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom39.xii.iii.html

### P0

Do not write:

> "The Bible says Christians must never eat with any sexually immoral unbelieving relative."

That contradicts the scope distinction in the chapter.

### But

The chapter also destroys the opposite claim:

> "Christian love always requires maintaining familiar normal fellowship with a professing believer who insists on gross unrepentant sin."

It does not.

---

# 4. 2 Timothy 3:1–5 applies directly to religious form without power

The catalogue culminates in people who:

- love pleasures rather than God;
- have a form of godliness;
- deny its power;

and Paul says `from such turn away`.

Classic commentators commonly understand the direction as avoiding church fellowship/intimate association with people whose professed godliness is contradicted by their life.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_timothy/3-5.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/poole/2_timothy/3.htm

### High relevance to series

This text belongs near the project's scenario:

`religious form + protected lust/profanity/deceit + no practical rule of godliness`.

### Guard

Do not declare every struggling believer described by 2 Tim. 3:5.

The issue is a settled pattern where profession and power are being denied by life, not merely the existence of serious remaining sin.

---

# 5. Owen gives the crucial natural-relation limit

John Owen, discussing excommunication, distinguishes:

- ordinary voluntary conversation of choice;
- spiritual admonition, which may continue;
- dealings already required by justice/equity;
- duties arising from natural/moral relations.

He says excommunication does **not** release duties of:

- husband/wife;
- parents/children;
- magistrate/subject;
- master/servant;
- neighbor/blood relation.

Primary source:
https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/evangelicalchurches/evangelicalchurches.iii.xiv.html

EEBO locator:
https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A53732.0001.001/1%3A13?rgn=div1%3Bview%3Dfulltext

### P0

> **If formal excommunication does not erase parent-child natural duties, family members cannot invent a harsher total-shunning doctrine merely from disappointment or anger.**

This is a major control against family excommunication.

---

# 6. Jonathan Edwards reaches the same conclusion

Edwards says excommunicated persons should still receive:

- prayer;
- admonition;
- common duties of humanity;
- relief in sickness/distress;
- necessary help protecting life/property;
- natural/civil relational duties.

He explicitly says parents are not released from parental affection/care toward children, nor children from duties to parents.

Source:
https://www.ccel.org/ccel/edwards/works2.v.html

### Result

Historic Reformed discipline is **not** total social annihilation.

---

# 7. Therefore family contact has multiple adjustable layers

Do not use a binary model:

`FULL NORMALITY`

vs

`NO CONTACT`.

Possible layers include:

1. **natural relation** — son/daughter remains son/daughter;
2. **ordinary humanity/mercy** — illness, crisis, food, necessary help;
3. **basic civil contact** — practical communication;
4. **family affection** — expressions of genuine love;
5. **spiritual admonition** — truth, warning, invitation to repentance;
6. **voluntary familiarity/social ease** — meals, leisure, constant hanging out;
7. **trust** — access to money/home/children/private matters;
8. **shared Christian fellowship** — praying/singing/acting as if common profession is sound;
9. **church privileges/role** — membership/Table/platform/trusted access.

These can change differently.

### P0

`RELATION ≠ FAMILIARITY ≠ TRUST ≠ CHRISTIAN FELLOWSHIP ≠ CHURCH PRIVILEGE`.

---

# 8. What "do not communicate normalcy" can mean for an adult child

Suppose an adult son/daughter openly says in substance:

> "I intend to continue sexual immorality / serious deceit / intoxication / corrupting others; do not address it; treat my life as normal."

Parents do not necessarily need to:

- stop answering messages;
- stop saying they love the child;
- refuse emergency help;
- refuse all meals forever.

But they may need to stop practices whose **function** has become:

- pretending the moral conflict is gone;
- financing the conduct;
- supplying a venue;
- giving romantic partner/spiritual claim family endorsement;
- presenting the person in church/family as if all is well;
- repeatedly restoring privileges without truth;
- allowing the child to use parental warmth as proof that the parents have accepted the sin.

### Product-safe formula

> **Любовь не требует каждый раз повторять обличение. Но любовь также не требует постоянно создавать такую атмосферу, будто предмета для покаяния больше нет.**

---

# 9. "It should be uncomfortable among believers" — refine the statement

Do not make social discomfort the goal.

A church/family can sin by:

- humiliation;
- coldness;
- mockery;
- retaliation;
- coordinated social punishment;
- gossip.

A better thesis is:

> **A person at peace with defended sin may naturally experience tension among believers who refuse to call darkness light. Believers need not manufacture that tension; they must refuse to remove it by false moral reassurance.**

This preserves the owner's insight without turning discomfort into a pastoral technique.

---

# 10. If the person is an unbeliever rather than a professing Christian

1 Cor. 5 explicitly prevents applying insider discipline mechanically to outsiders.

Therefore:

- invite to public Word hearing where safe;
- speak truth about sin;
- call to Christ and repentance;
- protect church/youth from corrupting influence;
- do not give trusted Christian role/status merely to make the person comfortable;
- do not pretend Christian fellowship exists where there is no Christian profession;
- ordinary social contact can be legitimate.

### P0

> **Unbeliever = outside formal discipline, not outside God's moral law or all pastoral admonition.**

---

# 11. If the person professes faith but is under church discipline

Church jurisdiction becomes more explicit.

Potential consequences may include:

- membership discipline;
- Table restriction/removal according to polity;
- removal from ministry/platform;
- changed Christian fellowship;
- safeguarding restrictions.

Family must coordinate rather than contradict church discipline where the church acts biblically.

### But

Family cannot say:

> "The church excommunicated you, therefore you are no longer our child and we owe you no ordinary care."

Owen/Edwards prohibit that inference.

---

# 12. If the church is wrongly permissive

The owner's earlier scenario is also possible:

- leaders know the person's defended gross sin;
- fear losing attendance;
- continue choir/youth/private-group status;
- avoid admonition;
- present hospitality as the only Christian response.

### Biblical problem

2 Tim. 3:5, 1 Cor. 5 and 2 Thess. 3 all show that Christian communities possess categories for **truthful relational consequence**, not endless symbolic approval.

### P0

> **Retention of attendance is not a higher good than truthful admonition.**

But exact formal discipline still depends on whether the person is a professing insider/member.

---

# 13. If the church is wrongly harsh

Opposite error:

- public humiliation without due process;
- discipline of a never-member as if member;
- forcing victims into reconciliation/proximity;
- treating every relapse as apostasy;
- total shunning of family members;
- using 1 Cor. 5 to erase natural duties.

Owen/Edwards and 2 Thess. 3:15 strongly guard against this.

### P0

> **Biblical separation is jurisdictional and restorative, not annihilating.**

---

# 14. Post-departure adult child — contact matrix

## A. Writes calmly, does not demand moral approval

Ordinary contact can remain substantial. Truth remains known. Do not turn every message into a sermon.

## B. Writes mainly for money/resources supporting defended sin

Change resource mode; offer non-enabling mercy where appropriate.

## C. Repeatedly demands parents affirm sin as condition of contact

Parents can refuse false affirmation and allow the child to decide whether contact continues.

## D. Uses contact to abuse/threaten/manipulate family

Stronger communication limits may become necessary for safety/peace.

## E. Wants to discuss repentance / return

Make the road to truthful conversation genuinely open; do not punish initiative by requiring additional wandering.

## F. Blocks parents

Do not create fake accounts/stalking as ordinary practice. Preserve prayer, appropriate indirect welfare checks where genuinely necessary, and openness if contact resumes.

### P0

There is no universal contact quota.

---

# 15. Company texts involving moral formation

Proverbs repeatedly warns that companions shape conduct (e.g., walking with wise vs fools; friendship with angry person). 1 Cor. 15:33 likewise warns that bad company corrupts good morals.

These provide a **formation principle**, especially for protecting younger siblings/youth groups.

They do not create a direct command to abandon every sinful relative.

### Application

Greater restriction is especially justified where contact is not merely with a sinner but is functioning as:

- recruitment;
- instruction in concealment;
- pornographic/sexual exposure;
- normalization of intoxication/profanity;
- romantic/sexual predation;
- undermining parents/church.

The safeguarding/formation interest of other children matters.

---

# 16. Restoration must be real, not theatrical

If changed familiarity had a restorative purpose, then genuine repentance must have a meaningful path toward renewed fellowship.

Do not use separation as permanent punishment after the stated moral condition has changed.

At the same time:

- repentance ≠ instant full credibility;
- forgiveness ≠ instant trust;
- restored affection ≠ restored unsupervised access;
- restored church membership/fellowship follows church polity and safeguarding facts.

### P0

> **Restoration can be warm without being naive.**

---

# 17. Product-safe synthesis

> **Иногда любовь должна изменить близость. Павел умеет сказать одновременно: «не сообщайтесь с ним» — и «не считайте его за врага, а вразумляйте как брата». Это не язык ненависти. Это отказ сделать привычное тёплое общение знаком того, будто защищаемый грех ничего не меняет. Но даже церковное отлучение, по классическому реформатскому пониманию, не уничтожает естественные обязанности родителей и детей.**

And:

> **Христианину не нужно специально делать грешнику больно и неуютно. Ему нужно перестать делать ложь уютной.**
