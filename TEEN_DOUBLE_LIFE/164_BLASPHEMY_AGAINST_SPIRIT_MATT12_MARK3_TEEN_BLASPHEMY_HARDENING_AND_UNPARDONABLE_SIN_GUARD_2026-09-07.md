# Blasphemy Against the Holy Spirit and Teen Blasphemy
## Matthew 12, Mark 3, Hardened Speech, and the Unpardonable-Sin Guard

**Status:** P0 EXEGESIS / PASTORAL DIAGNOSTIC GUARD / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Depends on:** `109`, `150–163`.

---

# 0. Why this file is needed

The project addresses church-raised youth who may become:

- profane;
- blasphemous;
- openly hostile toward God;
- contemptuous of Christ;
- willing to shock parents/peers with deliberately irreverent speech.

A terrified parent may then conclude:

> “He blasphemed the Holy Spirit. The Bible says that sin is never forgiven. It is over.”

That conclusion can be exegetically false and pastorally devastating.

### Governing rule

> **Blasphemous speech can be a grave sign of rebellion and hardening, but not every blasphemous statement is the specific blasphemy against the Holy Spirit in Matthew 12 / Mark 3.**

---

# 1. Immediate narrative setting — Jesus' works by the Spirit

Primary texts:

- Matt. 12:22–32;
- Mark 3:22–30.

Jesus casts out demons / performs works manifesting God's kingdom.

The scribes/Pharisees respond by claiming that his power is demonic:

> Beelzebul / the prince of demons / an unclean spirit.

Jesus first exposes the absurdity:

> Satan divided against Satan cannot stand.

Then comes the severe warning about blasphemy against the Holy Spirit.

---

# 2. Mark 3:30 is an inspired interpretive control

Mark explicitly explains:

> “because they were saying, He has an unclean spirit.”

Commentary controls:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/mark/3-30.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/mark/3-28.htm

This anchors the warning in the concrete controversy:

> the opponents attribute the Spirit-wrought work of Christ to an unclean/demonic spirit.

### Strong project consequence

Do not define the unpardonable sin merely as:

- using a swear word involving God;
- saying “I hate God” in rage;
- saying something atheistic;
- mocking church;
- any serious blasphemous joke;
- any knowingly committed sexual sin.

All may be sinful.

They are not automatically the Matt. 12 / Mark 3 offense.

---

# 3. Gill's explicit guard — not every knowing/wilful sin

John Gill on Matt. 12:31–32 explicitly rejects definitions such as:

- every ignorant denial/opposition;
- every resistance to the Spirit in external ministry;
- **every sin knowingly and wilfully committed**.

He connects the specific offense with malicious, obstinate opposition against known light, attributing Spirit-wrought works to the devil.

Source:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/matthew/12.htm

### P0 project rule

> **Never infer “unpardonable sin” from the mere presence of knowledge + voluntariness.**

That is the same category-control problem addressed in `163` for Heb. 10:26.

---

# 4. Matthew 12:31–32 distinguishes blasphemies rather than making all blasphemy unforgivable

Jesus astonishingly says:

- every sin and blasphemy may be forgiven;
- even a word against the Son of Man can be forgiven;
- the specific blasphemy against the Spirit is uniquely warned as unforgivable.

### Pastoral significance

The text itself prevents the conclusion:

> “He said something blasphemous; therefore forgiveness is impossible.”

Jesus' words require a **specific category**, not generic profanity.

---

# 5. Why the scribes' condition is so grave

The pattern includes:

- extraordinary gospel/light exposure;
- clear works associated with Christ and God's kingdom;
- sustained hostile interpretation;
- moral inversion: calling holy power demonic;
- opposition aimed at discrediting Christ.

This fits the project's broader theme:

> greater light can be rejected in progressively hardened ways.

### But

Do not turn our uncertainty about exact subjective knowledge into certainty.

Historical commentators differ over precisely how knowingly the scribes perceived the Spirit's work and over the metaphysical explanation of unpardonability.

The narrative facts remain controlling.

---

# 6. What about a teenager saying “God is evil” / “Jesus is demonic”?

These statements can be extremely grave.

Ask:

- Is this a single rage/shock utterance?
- Is it repeated after calm reflection?
- Does he retract it?
- Is it peer-performance blasphemy?
- Has he developed sustained conscious repudiation of Christ?
- Is he deliberately calling recognized good evil to justify sin?

### Do not minimize

A church-raised child who repeatedly mocks God and knowingly calls holiness evil may be moving into serious hardening.

### Do not overdiagnose

One statement does not grant us omniscience about whether Matt. 12's specific irreversible offense has occurred.

---

# 7. A person terrified that he committed the unpardonable sin should not be counseled by fatalistic guessing

The pastoral problem is common:

> tender/conscience-stricken person remembers a terrible statement and concludes there is no path back.

The Gospel narratives themselves begin with the astonishing breadth:

> sins and blasphemies may be forgiven.

### Safe counsel

If the person now:

- desires mercy;
- confesses Christ;
- repents of blasphemy;
- fears having rejected God,

we should direct him to Christ rather than certify an unobservable irreversible state from the past utterance.

### Guard

Do not use psychological comfort as exegesis.

The basis is that the specific biblical offense has a defined narrative context, and we are not authorized to broaden it beyond that context.

---

# 8. Better texts for ordinary profane/blasphemous speech

For a youth using filthy or blasphemous speech, more direct controls often include:

- Eph. 4:29;
- Eph. 5:3–4;
- Col. 3:8;
- James 3:9–12;
- Exod. 20:7 / broader reverence toward God's name;
- Ps. 49:16–21 Synodal numbering where religious speech + rebellion is relevant.

These texts permit direct correction without prematurely invoking the unpardonable-sin category.

---

# 9. Relationship to apostasy warnings

Repeated blasphemy can be part of an apostasy-like trajectory when it expresses:

- settled repudiation of Christ;
- contempt for known truth;
- reversal of prior profession;
- persistent resistance to correction.

Then:

- Heb. 3;
- Heb. 6/10 with `152/163` guards;
- 2 Pet. 2 with context guards

may become relevant.

### But

> `blasphemous speech` ≠ automatically `blasphemy against the Holy Spirit` ≠ automatically `final apostasy`.

These categories overlap only when facts warrant it.

---

# 10. Article-ready pastoral language

To a rebellious youth:

> “Твои слова о Боге не безобидная бравада. Писание требует от тебя страха Божия и предупреждает, что человек может ожесточаться настолько, что начинает называть зло добром, а Божье дело — злом. Но я не буду лгать тебе в другую сторону и заявлять, будто одно твоё страшное слово уже доказало, что прощение для тебя невозможно. Тебе нужно покаяться сейчас и перестать ожесточать сердце.”

To terrified parents:

> “Не называйте каждый кощунственный подростковый выкрик непростительным грехом. Назовите богохульство богохульством, увещевайте серьёзно, наблюдайте направление сердца — и продолжайте звать ко Христу.”

---

# 11. P0 guards

Never write:

> “Any blasphemy = blasphemy against the Holy Spirit.”

Never write:

> “If a church-raised teen said Jesus/God is evil once, forgiveness is impossible.”

Never write:

> “Only literal attribution of one miracle to Satan can ever be spiritually analogous, therefore modern hardened moral inversion is irrelevant.”

Prefer:

- exact Gospel narrative for the unpardonable-sin definition;
- ordinary speech texts for ordinary profanity/blasphemy;
- hardening/apostasy texts for trajectories;
- continued gospel call without false assurance.

---

# 12. Final locked formulation

> **Богохульство подростка может быть тяжёлым признаком ожесточения, но Мф. 12 и Мк. 3 не дают родителям права превращать всякую кощунственную речь в безошибочный диагноз непростительного греха. Христос предупреждает о конкретной злонамеренной инверсии света — приписывании явного дела Духа нечистому духу. Поэтому церковь должна серьёзно обличать богохульство и одновременно не закрывать дверь покаяния там, где Сам текст не дал ей такого права.**
