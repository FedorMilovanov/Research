# 1689 Confessional Control
## Children, Worship, Visible Saints, Church Privileges, and Owen Transfer

**Status:** CONFESSIONAL GUARD / RESEARCH ONLY  
**Date:** 2026-09-07

Sources:

Purpose: preserve the project’s conservative Reformed-Baptist stance while using valuable Puritan sources such as John Owen.

---

# 1. Chapter 26 — a local church is constituted of visible saints

1689 26.2 describes visible saints as persons:
- professing gospel faith;
- professing obedience to God through Christ;
- not destroying that profession by foundational error or unholiness of life.

1689 26.6 says church members visibly manifest/evidence obedience to Christ’s call in profession and walk.

### Project implication

The confession does **not** permit the visible church to treat outward profession as completely disconnected from outward life.

### Strong use

> **A church cannot claim that visible holiness is irrelevant to visible church standing merely because only God knows the heart perfectly.**

The confession itself distinguishes:
- invisible church/internal grace known perfectly to God;
- visible profession/walking relevant to visible church order.

---

# 2. This supports the project’s “no heart-reading, but real stewardship judgments” formula

Wrong extreme A:

> `His hidden sexual sin proves with certainty that he is reprobate.`

Wrong extreme B:

> `Because we cannot know election, known unholiness can never affect visible church privileges.`

1689 ch. 26 supports neither.

### Better formula

> **The church does not judge the secret decree; it judges what it must judge for visible order from profession and observable walk.**

---

# 3. Chapter 22 — singing is worship, not spiritually neutral performance

1689 22.5 includes:
- Scripture reading;
- preaching/hearing;
- teaching/admonishing in psalms/hymns/spiritual songs;
- singing with grace in the heart;
- baptism;
- Lord’s Supper;

as parts of religious worship.

These are to be performed with:
- obedience;
- understanding;
- faith;
- reverence;
- godly fear.

### Project importance for children’s choir

A formal church choir is not merely:

> `children doing a cute cultural activity on a stage`.

If it functions within corporate worship, its act of singing belongs to worship context.

Therefore the church should teach children:

> God is not impressed by the contrast between holy lyrics and a deliberately protected rebellious life.

### Guard

1689 22.5 does **not** contain a children’s-choir admission rubric.

Do not invent one.

---

# 4. Chapter 22.3 — prayer required of all men prevents another overcorrection

1689 22.3 says prayer with thanksgiving, as part of natural worship, is required of all men, though acceptable worship requires the divinely appointed manner/graces.

This protects the project from saying:

> `Until you are converted, stop praying, stop singing, stop speaking to God.`

The better summons remains:

> **Do not stop because God has no claim on you; stop using religious form as camouflage. You owe God true worship and therefore need repentance and faith.**

---

# 5. How 1689 corrects Owen’s paedobaptist church-child framework

Owen’s valuable principles:
- church attention to children;
- catechesis;
- prayer;
- parent-elder cooperation;
- admonition;
- differentiated privileges;
- opposition to careless world-conformity.

But Owen also grounds part of his scheme in paedobaptist covenant/church standing.

1689 Baptist ecclesiology should **not** import:
- infant-baptism membership assumptions;
- baptized-child covenant rights as Owen states them;
- automatic child inclusion in local-church membership.

### Re-ground transferable principles

For a 1689 Baptist project, say instead:

1. believers have duties to teach children;
2. churches have ministry responsibilities toward children who attend/are entrusted to their care;
3. children need gospel preaching and catechesis;
4. moral law applies to them;
5. nonmembership does not make leaders unable to admonish conduct in church/youth settings;
6. formal church membership requires credible profession and visible walk according to Baptist ecclesiology;
7. ministry/access policies may prudentially require standards stricter than mere public attendance.

---

# 6. Public hearing of Word remains distinct from membership

1689 26 describes the constitution of the local church in terms of visible saints/profession.

This does not mean unbelievers must be barred from hearing preaching.

The project already controls this through 1 Cor. 14:23–25.

Therefore maintain:

`PUBLIC WORD`  
≠ `LOCAL CHURCH MEMBERSHIP`  
≠ `TABLE`  
≠ `REPRESENTATIVE MINISTRY`  
≠ `LEADERSHIP`  
≠ `TRUSTED ACCESS TO MINORS`.

This distinction is central and should survive final compression.

---

# 7. Children’s choir: what may be said confessionally

### Strongly allowed

- singing in gathered worship is a religious act, not morally meaningless performance;
- the church should teach children reverence and holiness;
- visible ministry should not become deliberate camouflage for known protected rebellion;
- a church may prudentially suspend/restrict a child from representative ministry for serious conduct while still teaching, loving and evangelizing the child.

### Must be labelled prudence

- exact disqualifying sins;
- exact restoration period;
- age thresholds;
- exact private-conversation process;
- whether every choir functions as `representative ministry` in the same sense.

### Forbidden

- `1689 explicitly says immoral children cannot sing in choir.`

It does not.

---

# 8. Unbelieving child and worship

The project needs a nuanced paragraph later.

### Wrong

> `You are unbelieving, therefore God has no moral claim on your worship until conversion.`

### Wrong

> `Your singing proves your relationship with God is fine regardless of protected rebellion.`

### Better

> **God commands all people to honour, fear and call upon Him. Yet acceptable worship cannot be reduced to lips and music while the heart knowingly protects rebellion. The answer is not to create a morally neutral waiting room until conversion, but to call the child to repent, believe and worship God truly.**

---

# 9. Visible ministry and visible walk

1689 26.2/26.6 gives the project a confessional vocabulary:

- profession;
- walking;
- visible evidence;
- obedience;
- church order.

Therefore a church need not claim certainty about regeneration in order to ask:

> `Is the person’s present visible life compatible with this visible role?`

Examples:
- choir;
- testimony;
- worship team;
- youth leadership;
- mentoring younger children.

### Important guard

The higher the role’s:
- spiritual representation;
- authority;
- confidentiality;
- access to vulnerable persons,

the stronger the stewardship rationale for visible-conduct requirements.

This is prudential inference, not wording of 1689.

---

# 10. Restoration

1689 church discipline is ordered toward Christ’s holiness and, biblically, restoration where repentance appears.

Therefore children/youth who were restricted should not be marked forever.

A good policy needs:
- clear reason for restriction;
- truthful explanation;
- pastoral help;
- observable restoration criteria appropriate to the issue;
- review rather than indefinite limbo;
- protection against premature restoration in safeguarding cases.

---

# 11. Publication formulas

> **The Baptist answer to Owen is not to make children invisible. It is to separate child ministry from child membership while retaining real instruction, admonition, evangelism and pastoral care.**

> **Only God sees regeneration infallibly; churches still have to administer visible roles using visible evidence.**

> **A child does not need to be a church member for a pastor to tell him that pornography, obscenity, deceit and sexual corruption are sins against God.**

> **The alternative to forcing a false profession is not moral neutrality. It is law and gospel: name the sin, refuse the mask, preach Christ, call for repentance and guard the church wisely.**

---

# 12. Final confessional verdict

**Owen child-care principles:** useful after Baptist re-grounding.  
**Owen paedobaptist covenant-child standing:** do not import.  
**1689 26 visible holiness/profession:** strong control for church membership/visible saints.  
**1689 22 singing as worship:** strong control against treating church singing as spiritually neutral.  
**Children’s choir exact admission standards:** prudential, not confessional statute.  
**Unbelieving child moral responsibility:** preserved; no neutral waiting room.
