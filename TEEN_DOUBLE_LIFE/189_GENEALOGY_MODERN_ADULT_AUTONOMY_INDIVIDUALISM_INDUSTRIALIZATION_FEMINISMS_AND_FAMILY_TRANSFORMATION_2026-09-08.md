# Genealogy of Modern Adult Autonomy
## Individualism, industrialization, feminisms, mobility and family transformation

**Status:** RESEARCH / CULTURAL-HISTORICAL CONTROL / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08

## Purpose

The project may critique contemporary assumptions such as:

> `adult = independent self whose inherited family relations have no authority beyond optional advice`.

But serious history must not reduce that assumption to one cause (`feminism`). Modern autonomy is produced by several interacting developments.

## 1. Philosophical autonomy predates modern feminism

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, feminist autonomy overview:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-autonomy/

Autonomy means self-government/self-direction in modern moral philosophy.

Important corrective: feminist theorists themselves have often criticized an **atomistic/self-sufficient** model of autonomy and developed `relational autonomy` precisely because humans are socially embedded.

### Project use

Do not claim:

> `feminism uniquely invented autonomy`.

Autonomy is a broader modern philosophical inheritance; feminist movements interact with it in different ways.

## 2. Industrialization changes the household/economy relationship

Cambridge:
- Angelique Janssens, *Family and Industrialization*: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/family-and-social-change/family-and-industrialization/154AB7E64345307C9324F4795B983510
- Joyce Burnette, women's labour participation: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/gender-work-and-wages-in-industrial-revolution-britain/womens-labor-force-participation/F9C8E4C5EE416CC8291D37C317AE8D04

Traditional households were often units of production as well as consumption. Industrialization/wage labour increasingly separates workplace from household and makes individual wages a more visible source of livelihood.

Some nineteenth/twentieth-century accounts explicitly linked wage earning outside the family household with female `economic emancipation` and social independence.

### Guard

Historical research complicates the simple story:

- women already did extensive productive work before industrialization;
- industrialization could remove some forms of household production as well as create factory work;
- patterns differed by class/region.

Therefore:

> `paid employment = first time women became economically productive`

is historically false.

## 3. Mobility and urbanization weaken immediate household control

Industrial/modern economies increase:

- geographic mobility;
- education away from family;
- wage work outside household;
- urban anonymity;
- ability to rent/live apart;
- social networks not mediated by kin.

These structural facts make adult independence practically easier whether or not a person holds feminist ideology.

### Project implication

A daughter who can earn, rent and communicate privately in 2026 occupies a materially different environment from an ancient Israelite daughter embedded in `bet ha-av`.

That difference does not decide God's moral law; it changes prudential enforcement/dependence and therefore must be acknowledged.

## 4. Modern contract/legal adulthood

Modern states formalize capacities around civil adulthood:

- contracts;
- employment;
- property;
- residence;
- marriage rules;
- privacy rights;
- legal personhood.

Civil age `18` is not a biblical sacrament, but it changes the lawful power available to parents and adult children.

### Project rule

Never confuse:

`civil capacity`
with
`moral autonomy from God/family duties`.

But never ignore civil capacity when giving household advice.

## 5. Feminism changes the moral interpretation of dependence

Stanford family/feminism overview:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-family/

Major feminist arguments bring family power relations into public moral/political scrutiny and emphasize how traditional family divisions can constrain women's opportunities.

Some feminist streams interpret economic dependence on father/husband as structurally dangerous or oppressive and therefore prize independent income/choice.

Other feminist philosophers reject atomistic self-sufficiency and argue for **relational autonomy**.

### Strong analytical distinction

The project should distinguish:

- legitimate concern about abuse/coercion enabled by economic dependence;
- the stronger ideological thesis that asymmetrical God-ordained authority is itself oppressive;
- the still broader secular ideal that self-authored choice is the core of dignity.

These are related but not identical.

## 6. Sexual revolution amplifies autonomy of intimate choice

Twentieth-century sexual culture increasingly treats:

- dating;
- sexual relationships;
- cohabitation;
- marriage timing/partner choice

as private expressions of individual identity rather than family-governed moral decisions.

This development interacts with but is not reducible to women's civil rights.

### Project concern

For the teen/adult-child series, the practical effect is enormous:

> a young adult may believe parents have **no legitimate claim even to moral knowledge/counsel** about romance/sex because intimate life is conceived as private self-expression.

That assumption conflicts much more directly with biblical family/community ethics than the mere fact of earning a salary.

## 7. Expressive individualism

Carl Trueman / Charles Taylor category:
- https://www.crossway.org/articles/what-does-it-mean-to-be-your-true-self/

Expressive individualism treats authenticity as outwardly expressing the inner self/desire.

This can turn inherited obligations into obstacles:

- `If family rules frustrate my felt identity/desires, the rules are inauthentic oppression.`

### Relevance to double-life series

A hidden-life teenager may move from:

`I broke my parents' law / God's law`

toward

`the demand itself violates who I am`.

But do not force this ideological stage onto every sinner; earlier project P0 already says many simply live by desire without constructing a theory.

## 8. The modern autonomous adult is a compound product

Do not create monocausal history.

Likely interacting forces include:

- Enlightenment/liberal individualism;
- contract/property law;
- industrialization and wage labour;
- urbanization/mobility;
- mass education;
- consumer culture;
- contraception/sexual revolution;
- feminist political/legal movements;
- feminist/egalitarian theological arguments;
- expressive-individualist ideas of authenticity;
- digital privacy/network life.

### Strong cultural thesis

> **Modern adulthood is often imagined as emancipation from inherited authority because philosophy, law, economics and sexual culture have jointly made self-direction both materially possible and morally prestigious. Feminist currents are one important part of that history, not the whole history.**

## 9. Conservative Christian response — don't confuse dependence with virtue

A reactionary error is possible:

> `If independence is culturally idolized, therefore dependence itself is always more holy.`

Not so.

Scripture can commend maturity, work, provision, wisdom, initiative and formation of a new household.

A 30-year-old who refuses responsibility while demanding parental provision is not sanctified by dependence.

2 Thess. 3 / 1 Tim. 5 / Proverbs work against parasitic dependence.

### Better distinction

`maturity / responsibility / capacity`

≠

`autonomy from God's commands and relational duties`.

## 10. Family dependence can be holy or sinful depending on function

Healthy:

- multigenerational care;
- daughter/son contributing to family;
- temporary financial support;
- shared household with meaningful responsibility;
- adult seeking parental wisdom/blessing.

Unhealthy:

- parents preventing vocational maturity to retain control;
- adult child exploiting parents indefinitely;
- economic dependence used to coerce conscience;
- adult refusing work/discipline while claiming `family values`;
- family isolation used to conceal abuse.

## 11. Publication language guard

Avoid:

> `Modern women work because feminism taught them to rebel against fathers.`

Avoid:

> `Any young adult leaving home is autonomous individualism.`

Prefer:

> **The modern West has made legal and economic independence increasingly normal; the theological question is whether maturity is exercised under God and with continuing fidelity to real family obligations, or whether independence becomes a creed of self-rule.**

## P0 conclusion

The series can be strongly countercultural without poor history:

> **Christian maturity is not permanent childhood, and it is not sovereign self-authorship. It is increasing capacity to bear responsibility faithfully under God within the relations He actually gives.**
