# Proverbs 5–7 — Sexual Seduction, Distance, Secret Opportunity, Cost, and Marital Delight

**Status:** SCRIPTURE-FIRST SEXUAL-ETHICS EXEGESIS / RESEARCH ONLY  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** deepen the compressed Proverbs 5–7 section in module `68` for the future adolescent-double-life series, using the Bible’s own moral anatomy of sexual temptation rather than pop-neuroscience or shame-based anti-sex rhetoric.

---

# 0. Why Proverbs 5–7 deserve a dedicated pass

The project’s original questions include:

- why sexual sin is attractive;
- why secrecy makes it powerful;
- why a teenager keeps a “door” open to the same temptation;
- why consequences seem unreal until later;
- why parents need concrete sexual instruction rather than embarrassment;
- how to warn against pornography/sexting without teaching that sexuality itself is dirty.

Proverbs 5–7 address nearly all of these themes in wisdom form.

They do **not** predict smartphones.

They do give an ancient moral anatomy of:

`attractive speech / desire / proximity / secrecy / opportunity / yielding / consequence / regret`

and pair that warning with a positive vision of faithful marital sexuality.

---

# 1. Literary setting — a father teaching a son before the crisis

Proverbs 1–9 repeatedly uses the pedagogical address:

> “my son.”

The sexual warnings are therefore not emergency speeches delivered only after adultery has already happened.

They are **anticipatory formation**.

The father names:

- seduction;
- body/desire;
- flattering speech;
- places to avoid;
- consequences;
- marital delight;
- God’s omniscient observation.

### Project significance

Biblical parenting is not too embarrassed to teach about sexual temptation before the child’s hidden sexual world is fully formed.

### Strong line

> **Если родители стесняются говорить о похоти, соблазне, теле, браке и тайном грехе, Притчи не разделяют их стеснения.**

This is rhetorical synthesis.

---

# 2. Proverbs 5:3–6 — honey at the entrance, bitterness at the end

## TEXT

The lips of the forbidden/adulterous woman are described as:

- dripping honey;
- smoother than oil.

But her **end** is:

- bitter as wormwood;
- sharp as a two-edged sword;
- descending toward death/Sheol.

## The structure matters

The text does not deny the attractiveness of the beginning.

It contrasts:

`what temptation tastes/sounds like now`

with

`where the path ends`.

This is almost the same asymmetry the project identified earlier from Prov. 9 / Heb. 11 / Brooks.

## GREEN

> **Sexual temptation can be genuinely attractive at the point of invitation while concealing a bitter end.**

## RED

Do not write:

> “Sin never actually feels good.”

The wisdom warning works precisely because the bait can feel sweet.

Also do not turn “bitter end” into a deterministic list in which every individual sexual sin immediately produces every possible social/medical consequence.

Wisdom literature warns truthfully about the destructive trajectory, not a mechanical timetable.

---

# 3. Proverbs 5:7–8 — do not merely resist inside the doorway; stay far from it

## TEXT

The father commands:

> keep/remove your way far from her;
> do not go near the door of her house.

The imperatives are spatial and preventive.

The strategy is not:

> stand permanently at the threshold and prove how strong you are.

Gill, in his literal exposition, stresses keeping the greatest distance from the occasion and warns against trusting one’s own strength near temptation.

Other classic commentators likewise emphasize **flight** as the safeguard.

## Project use

This is among the strongest OT texts for radical occasion-removal.

Digital analogies may include, depending on the actual pattern:

- not keeping the hidden account;
- not retaining a sexualized private chat;
- not sleeping beside the device repeatedly used for pornography;
- not maintaining a private “friendship” whose established function is sexual temptation;
- not revisiting a site/account under the slogan “I will only look, not act.”

### Locked line

> **Библейская мудрость иногда начинается не с героического “я устою у двери”, а с более смиренного “я не пойду к двери”.**

## Guard

A modern app/account is not automatically the “door” of Prov. 5:8 for every person.

The application requires demonstrated relationship between the particular occasion and the particular sin.

---

# 4. Proverbs 5:9–14 — consequences include more than private guilt

The father warns of losses involving:

- honor/dignity;
- years/strength;
- resources going to others;
- bodily decline/groaning in some form;
- public/community disgrace;
- late regret over having hated discipline and despised reproof.

The climactic memory is especially relevant:

> **Why did I hate instruction? Why did my heart despise reproof? Why did I not listen to my teachers?**

## Project significance

The article should not treat sexual sin as a sealed private event between screen and user.

It can spread into:

- trust;
- finances;
- reputation;
- family;
- health;
- other victims;
- church relationships;
- conscience;
- future opportunities.

## Guard

Do not promise that every teenager who views pornography will literally suffer every consequence named in an ancient adultery warning.

Use the wisdom category:

> sexual sin has a cost the first pleasure does not display.

---

# 5. “I hated instruction” — why correction itself can become the enemy

This is one of the most important project links.

At first the child’s problem may seem to be:

> lust.

But preserving lust may eventually require hostility toward:

- parent warning;
- pastor warning;
- Scripture;
- device restriction;
- accountability;
- anyone who threatens access.

Thus sexual sin can begin reshaping the person’s attitude toward **instruction itself**.

This converges with:

- Heb. 3 — deception/hardening;
- Ps. 49:17 Russian Synodal numbering — hatred of correction/instruction in hypocritical worship context;
- 2 Tim. 3 — form without power;
- `109` conscience/hardening control.

### Strong synthesis

> **Иногда человек начинает защищать уже не только похоть. Он начинает защищаться от самой истины, которая мешает похоти жить спокойно.**

# 6. Proverbs 5:15–20 — the Bible’s answer is not sexual disgust

This section is indispensable.

The father moves from prohibition to positive marital delight.

He calls the son to:

- faithfulness to his own wife;
- rejoicing in the wife of his youth;
- sustained delight/sexual satisfaction within covenant marriage.

The imagery is deliberately affectionate and bodily.

Gill’s literal exposition treats the wife as God’s blessing and emphasizes loving, delighting in and remaining faithful to her.

## Project significance

A Christian article on pornography/teen lust must clearly communicate:

> **sexual desire is not evil because it is sexual.**

The Bible condemns sexual desire/actions **outside God’s order** while speaking positively about marital sexual delight.

### Important pastoral correction

Avoid teaching children implicitly:

`body = shame`

`sexual desire = dirt`

`holiness = never experiencing sexual attraction`.

Better:

> **Holiness orders desire under God; it does not require pretending the body has no sexual nature.**

## Reformed guard

Marriage itself is not regeneration and does not cure an enslaved heart automatically.

The common adolescent promise:

> “When I marry, pornography will disappear”

is not a biblical guarantee.

A person who has trained secrecy/lust needs repentance/mortification, not merely a lawful sexual outlet.

# 7. Proverbs 5:21–23 — secrecy before people is not secrecy before God

## TEXT

The reason given turns explicitly Godward:

> a man’s ways are before the eyes of the LORD; He watches/ponders all his paths.

Then:

- the wicked is captured by his own iniquities;
- held by cords of his sin;
- dies for lack of discipline;
- goes astray through great folly.

## Project importance

This directly integrates with module `110_FEAR_OF_GOD...`.

The sexual temptation chapter does not end with:

> “make sure your father does not discover it.”

It ends with God’s seeing.

### Strong line

> **У тайного чата может не быть человеческого свидетеля. У тайного греха нет места без Божьего взгляда.**

Guard: rhetorical synthesis, not claim that God is merely a surveillance system.

His presence is Creator/Judge/Lord relationship, not an electronic parent-control metaphor.

---

# 8. Proverbs 6:20–24 — parental truth travels into sexual temptation

The son is again told to keep:

- father’s command;
- mother’s teaching.

The instruction is said to:

- lead him when he walks;
- watch him when he lies down;
- speak to him when he wakes.

Then the purpose includes guarding him from the adulterous/evil woman and flattering tongue.

## Project significance

This is the exact goal of biblical sexual formation:

> parent is not physically present, but truth learned from father/mother speaks inside the decision.

This reinforces module `68`.

The smartphone therefore creates a revealing pastoral question:

> **When the child is alone with the device, whose instruction is actually speaking most loudly — parental wisdom, peer culture, Folly, or lust?**

---

# 9. Proverbs 6:25 — lust begins in the heart

The warning does not begin only at physical intercourse.

The son is warned against lusting after beauty **in the heart** and being captured by seductive presentation.

This converges naturally with Matt. 5:27–30.

## Project use

Pornography is morally serious even when no physical adulterous act occurs because Christian sexual ethics addresses:

- heart/desire;
- gaze;
- intentional sexual use of another person/image.

## Guard

Do not imply every involuntary perception of beauty is lust.

The issue is cultivated sexual coveting/desire, not mere recognition that another person is attractive.

---

# 10. Proverbs 6:27–29 — fire is not a toy

The text asks whether a man can:

- carry fire against his chest without burning clothes;
- walk on hot coals without burning feet.

The comparison exposes the fantasy of engaging adultery while remaining untouched by it.

Gill and other classic commentators read this as cause-and-effect warning: sexual sin does not remain costless/innocent.

## Project line

> **Человек, сохраняющий огонь у груди, не доказывает свободу от огня.**

Modern application:

A teenager who insists on preserving a known recurring sexual occasion because:

> “I can handle it this time”

should hear the wisdom principle.

## Guard

Do not literalize every ancient adultery consequence into every digital exposure.

Use as moral warning against presumptuous proximity.

---

# 11. Proverbs 7:6–9 — the naive youth walks toward the known zone of temptation

## Narrative

The observer sees among the inexperienced/simple a young man lacking sense.

He passes near the corner/house associated with the adulterous woman, moving toward it in twilight/evening/night.

Gill emphasizes:

- the youth’s foolishness;
- his putting himself in the way of temptation;
- darkness as chosen cover for hidden sexual action.

## Project significance

The youth is not portrayed merely as a passive victim of a surprise desire.

He has already moved himself into an **occasion-rich path**.

This makes Prov. 7 valuable for:

- nighttime unrestricted device use;
- deliberately entering sexualized chats;
- revisiting known accounts/places;
- cultivating private opportunities.

## Guard

Do not victim-blame.

If a child is groomed/coerced/assaulted, his presence in a risky place does not transfer the offender’s guilt to the victim.

The Proverbs narrative concerns voluntary foolish approach to adultery/seduction; safeguarding categories must remain distinct.

---

# 12. Proverbs 7:10–21 — temptation speaks, interprets, promises and hides risk

The seductress is not merely visually attractive.

She uses:

- calculated appearance;
- bold initiative;
- flattering/persuasive speech;
- promises of pleasure;
- an apparent safety argument: the husband is away;
- religious language/offerings in the surrounding speech (v.14), showing that religious form can coexist with sexual rebellion.

## Project parallels

A sexualized digital invitation also frequently comes wrapped in words:

- “nobody will know”;
- “delete after reading”;
- “we’re both okay with it”;
- “this is private”;
- “you’re mature enough”;
- “don’t be boring”;
- “I trust you.”

The moral power is not only image but **interpretation**.

### Brooks connection

Temptation shows bait and hides hook.

### Guard

These modern examples are applications, not quotations from Proverbs.

---

# 13. Proverbs 7:22–27 — sudden yielding and hidden endpoint

The youth follows.

The chapter piles up death imagery:

- ox to slaughter;
- animal toward snare;
- arrow;
- many slain;
- house as way to Sheol/death.

## Important point

The young man experiences the invitation as movement toward pleasure.

Wisdom narrates it as movement toward destruction.

### Core project line

> **Искушение и мудрость смотрят на один и тот же путь с разных концов: похоть показывает ближайшую комнату, мудрость — куда ведёт дорога.**

# 14. Gender/application guard — the woman is not “the cause of male sin”

This is essential for publication integrity.

Proverbs 5–7 is framed as father-to-son instruction and therefore personifies/presents the sexual danger as the adulterous/forbidden woman.

Do not infer:

> “Women are the moral source of male lust.”

The son is repeatedly held responsible for:

- listening;
- lusting;
- approaching;
- following;
- rejecting instruction.

Elsewhere Scripture equally condemns sexually immoral men and calls women as moral agents to chastity/wisdom.

### Modern application

The same wisdom categories apply to:

- boys seducing girls;
- girls seducing boys;
- mutual sexual sin;
- same-sex sexual temptation;
- manipulative digital sexual pressure;
- adults grooming minors (with abuse/safeguarding categories governing responsibility).

Do not erase the historical male-son rhetorical setting; do not universalize female blame.

---

# 15. Gill source-use guard

John Gill is useful here as a Particular Baptist commentator on the literal text.

However his Proverbs exposition sometimes moves from the literal adulteress to extended **anti-Roman-Catholic / “harlot of Rome” allegorical applications**.

Those are **not needed** for this project.

Source discipline:

- use Gill where he helps with literal sexual/moral meaning, wording, occasion, marital delight;
- do not import unrelated anti-Catholic allegory into the adolescent article;
- Scripture’s own literary context remains controlling.

---

# 16. Digital “door” taxonomy for Part II — prudential, not inspired

From Prov. 5:8 / 7 / Owen / Brooks, parents can ask:

> What is this child’s actual recurring doorway?

Possible answers:

- unrestricted browser at night;
- one particular account;
- one person;
- one group chat;
- one game voice channel;
- one route/meeting place;
- one device hidden from family;
- one pattern of being alone after a certain hour.

## Crucial guard

Do not impose the same “door” on every child.

A wise boundary targets a **demonstrated occasion**, not parental dislike disguised as Bible command.

This is `TEXT → PRINCIPLE → PRUDENCE`.

---

# 17. Positive replacement — the father offers something better than prohibition

Proverbs 5 does not end:

> “sexual desire is dangerous; suppress it forever.”

It redirects desire toward faithful covenant delight.

The project should do the same more broadly:

`stop porn`

must not be the whole sexual theology.

Children need teaching on:

- creation of male/female/body;
- dignity of persons rather than consumption;
- marriage/covenant fidelity;
- lawful sexual delight;
- patience/self-control before marriage;
- repentance without permanent sexual shame;
- Christ’s lordship over body/desire.

### Strong line

> **Библейская чистота — не ненависть к сексуальности. Это отказ позволить хорошему Божьему дару стать господином, товаром или тайным оружием против другого человека.**

# 19. Final locked synthesis

> **Притчи не предлагают юноше стоять в дверях сексуального искушения и ежедневно доказывать силу воли. Они показывают сладкую речь и горький конец, велят удалить путь от двери, предупреждают о потере чести и позднем сожалении, напоминают, что все пути человека перед глазами Господа, и одновременно рисуют законную супружескую любовь как радость, а не грязь. Для цифровой эпохи это удивительно современная мудрость без единого слова об алгоритмах: не ненавидь Божий дар — ненавидь путь, который превращает желание в тайное беззаконие; не играй у двери; слушай наставление до того, как похоть начнёт объяснять тебе, почему дверь безопасна.**
