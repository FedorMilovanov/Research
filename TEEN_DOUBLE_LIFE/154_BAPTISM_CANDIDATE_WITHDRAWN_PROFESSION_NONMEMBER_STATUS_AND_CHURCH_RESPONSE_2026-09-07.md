# Baptism Candidate, Withdrawn Profession, Nonmember Status, and Church Response

**Status:** SCRIPTURE-FIRST / REFORMED BAPTIST ECCLESIOLOGY / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Lane:** `150+` late-stage gap-closing lane.  
**Depends on:** `35`, `38`, `41`, `53`, `96`, `108`, `150–153`.

# 0. The unresolved case

A church-raised child or teenager may move significantly toward public Christian identification without ever becoming a church member.

Examples:

- asks to be baptized;
- enters baptism preparation;
- tells parents/leaders he believes;
- gives testimony privately or semi-publicly;
- participates for years in Bible study;
- is socially treated as part of the Christian youth circle;
- later abandons the process, rejects correction, or lives in deliberate serious sin.

The church then faces two opposite errors.

## Error A — inflate candidacy into membership

> “He wanted baptism, therefore he was effectively a member and can now be excommunicated.”

False in a Baptist polity if the church never baptized/received him as a member.

## Error B — erase the entire history

> “He was never a member, therefore his earlier profession, instruction, baptism request, and rejection of admonition are pastorally irrelevant; treat him exactly like a first-time visitor.”

Also false as a pastoral description.

### Governing thesis

> **Baptism candidacy is not membership, but it is not nothing. It can be evidence of substantial gospel knowledge, self-identification with Christian claims, and a pastoral relationship that makes later conscious repudiation materially different from first exposure to the gospel.**

---

# 1. 1689 chapter 29 — baptism requires actual profession

Primary confession sources:

- https://the1689.org/chapters/29
- https://www.the1689confession.com/chapter-29

1689 29.2:

> those who **actually profess repentance toward God, faith in, and obedience to Jesus Christ** are the proper subjects of baptism.

## Implication

A request for baptism normally involves more than:

> “I like church.”

It presents the church with a claim that the person understands and professes:

- repentance;
- faith;
- obedience to Christ.

The church must assess that claim fallibly.

### Guard

A request for baptism does not itself prove regeneration.

A child may:

- imitate family language;
- sincerely misunderstand himself;
- experience temporary conviction/affection;
- seek belonging;
- later reveal that the profession lacked durable root.

Therefore:

> baptism candidacy is evidence of profession/light, not an infallible certificate of conversion.

---

# 2. 1689 chapter 26 — membership is more than private intention

Primary/confessional source:

- https://rbs.org/2lbc

1689 26.6 describes church members as visible saints who manifest obedience to Christ's call in **profession and walking**, willingly consenting to walk together under Christ's ordinances.

26.7–8 then describes actual church authority, officers and members.

### Consequence

A youth who merely:

- contemplated baptism;
- entered preparation;
- made private statements;

but was never received into membership is not juridically identical to a member later removed by discipline.

### Locked rule

> **Do not invent retrospective membership.**

Church discipline must operate on real ecclesial facts.

---

# 3. Yet profession can precede formal membership

Pastoral reality is more granular than the final membership roll.

A person can publicly or semi-publicly present himself as:

- believing the gospel;
- repenting;
- desiring baptism;
- willing to obey Christ,

before the church has completed its discernment.

If later conduct flatly contradicts those claims, leaders need not say:

> “None of your former words matter because the vote never happened.”

They may truthfully say:

> “You told us you wanted to identify with Christ in baptism and professed repentance/faith. Your present defended life contradicts those claims. We are not going to pretend the contradiction does not exist.”

This is **pastoral truth-telling**, not excommunication.

---

# 4. Four statuses that must not be collapsed

## A. Curious outsider

- no meaningful prior profession;
- limited gospel knowledge;
- comes to hear/ask questions.

Posture:

- evangelistic welcome;
- patient explanation;
- no invented Christian identity.

## B. Church-raised nonprofessor

- extensive instruction/history;
- never seriously claimed faith/baptism.

Posture:

- greater light/accountability may exist;
- still no formal Christian profession to revoke;
- direct call to repentance/faith.

## C. Baptism candidate / emerging professor who later repudiates

- substantial instruction;
- explicit movement toward Christian profession;
- later serious contradiction/rejection.

Posture:

- do not call member/excommunicate if never received;
- do not erase the earlier profession;
- clarify that the church no longer treats the former claim as credible while rebellion is defended;
- reconsider visible/representative privileges;
- continue ordered admonition.

## D. Baptized/member professor later impenitent

- formal church standing exists;
- Matthew 18 / 1 Corinthians 5 / 1689 26 discipline becomes directly relevant according to polity/process.

---

# 5. Withdrawal from baptism can mean different things

Do not overread one act.

A youth may withdraw because:

- conscience says profession is not yet genuine;
- fear/anxiety;
- doctrinal uncertainty;
- conflict with parents;
- secret sin he is ashamed to reveal;
- open rejection of Christ;
- desire to preserve a sinful relationship/lifestyle.

These are not morally identical.

### Therefore

The church should ask:

- What exactly changed?
- Is the person saying “I am not sure I believe” or “I know what Christ commands and refuse him”?
- Is there hidden serious sin?
- Is he asking for help or protecting rebellion?
- Is he corrupting others?

### Guard

Do not turn every delayed baptism into apostasy.

---

# 6. A former baptism candidate may need explicit de-certification of social assumptions

A common church failure is ambiguity.

For years everyone assumes:

> “He is basically a Christian kid; baptism will happen eventually.”

Then his life materially contradicts that assumption, but nobody corrects the social narrative.

This can create false reassurance for:

- the child;
- parents;
- peers;
- potential romantic partners;
- youth leaders.

### Safe pastoral move

Without gossip or public humiliation, responsible leaders may need to make clear to the person and relevant stewards:

> **“We are not presently affirming your Christian profession as credible while you knowingly defend this pattern.”**

This is not a declaration of reprobation.

It is refusal to certify what known facts contradict.

---

# 7. Romantic/social access deserves separate stewardship

If a former baptism candidate continues in youth/church settings while knowingly living a hidden sexual life, there is a particular risk:

- believing peers may assume he is spiritually aligned;
- parents may regard him as a safe church match;
- he may leverage church identity to gain trust/intimacy.

Church leaders cannot read motives without evidence.

But if concrete deception exists, they may:

- confront him;
- protect specific vulnerable people;
- clarify that church familiarity is not spiritual endorsement;
- restrict particular close/confidential settings where risk is established.

### Guard

Do not create a public blacklist or spread intimate details unnecessarily.

---

# 8. What the church should say to the youth

A useful form:

> “Мы не считаем тебя безнадёжным и не объявляем тайный Божий приговор. Но ты сам много слышал, говорил о вере и хотел креститься. Поэтому мы не можем вести себя так, будто нынешний сознательный бунт — просто нейтральное неверие человека, впервые встретившего христианство. Ты знаешь, о чём идёт речь. Тебе нужно не сыграть ещё одну религиозную роль, а покаяться и прийти ко Христу. Пока ты защищаешь эту жизнь, мы не будем подтверждать прежний образ как будто всё осталось по-прежнему.”

---

# 9. What this does NOT authorize

Do not write or teach:

- baptism candidate = member;
- baptism request proves regeneration;
- withdrawn candidate must be publicly shamed;
- every withdrawn candidate is an apostate;
- church-raised youth can never later genuinely convert;
- leaders know election/reprobation;
- lack of baptism removes all moral responsibility.

---

# 10. Locked synthesis

> **В баптистской экклесиологии просьба о крещении не создаёт членство и не даёт основания для фиктивного “отлучения”. Но она может быть важной частью истории исповедания и света. Если человек потом сознательно живёт против того, что сам исповедовал и знал, церковь вправе перестать социально подтверждать прежний образ, прямо увещевать его и отдельно пересмотреть доверительные/представительские роли — продолжая звать его ко Христу.**
