## Church-Raised Youth, Deliberate Sin, Repudiation of Christ, and the Limits of Human Diagnosis

# 0. Why this file is necessary

The project now insists, correctly, that a church-raised youth who has received extensive gospel light and later knowingly rejects it should not be flattened into the category:

> “ordinary unbeliever, nothing special happened.”

But this creates an opposite danger:

> “He knowingly committed sexual sin after hearing the gospel; therefore Hebrews 6:4–6 / 10:26 says repentance is now impossible.”

That move is too fast and may crush a sinner whom Scripture still calls to repentance.

### Governing rule

> **The warnings of Hebrews are directly relevant to the trajectory and gravity of conscious defection after gospel privilege. They are not a license to diagnose every deliberate post-instruction sin as final, irreversible apostasy.**

---

# 1. Literary setting of Hebrews 3–6

The warning material develops cumulatively:

- Heb. 2:1–4 — do not drift / neglect such a great salvation;
- Heb. 3:7–19 — do not harden the heart; unbelief causes departure from the living God;
- Heb. 4 — hearing without faith does not profit;
- Heb. 5:11–14 — dullness of hearing;
- Heb. 6:4–8 — terrifying falling-away warning after extraordinary privileges;
- Heb. 6:9–12 — the author nevertheless expects “better things” from his readers, things accompanying salvation, and urges diligence/perseverance.

### Project implication

Hebrews does not begin with:

> “You sinned once knowingly, therefore finished.”

It warns a visible community against a developing unbelieving/hardening trajectory and distinguishes warning signs from final apostasy.

# 2. Hebrews 6:4–6 — privileges are real even where saving perseverance is absent

Primary:

- Heb. 6:4–8.

Dennis Johnson:

- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/hebrews/

John Owen:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.v.html

Owen treats enlightenment as genuine gospel instruction/knowledge and explicitly recognizes different degrees of illumination according to the means enjoyed.

Johnson reads the listed blessings as graces/privileges experienced in the visible covenant community while preserving the NT doctrine of perseverance for the truly regenerate.

### Strong use for the teen project

A child can have much more than bare exposure:

- doctrinal knowledge;
- convictions;
- religious affections;
- community participation;
- temporary moral reform;
- serious movement toward profession;

without the enduring root of saving faith.

Therefore:

> **“He was probably never regenerate” does not make the years of gospel privilege spiritually meaningless.**

---

# 3. What “falling away” in Hebrews 6 must not be reduced to

Do not equate it automatically with:

- one pornography relapse;
- masturbation;
- swearing;
- one sexual relationship;
- a season of severe depression/doubt;
- one angry statement against church;
- adolescent confusion;
- even one serious gross sin after profession.

The warning concerns repudiation so severe that it is portrayed as:

> crucifying the Son of God again / exposing him to contempt.

The passage is about apostasy, not merely the fact that Christian sinners sometimes sin deliberately.

---

# 4. Hebrews 10:19–31 — context before verse 26

The flow immediately before 10:26 includes:

- drawing near;
- holding fast confession;
- considering how to stir one another to love/good works;
- not forsaking assembly;
- encouraging one another.

Then comes the warning about deliberate sin after receiving knowledge of truth.

Dennis Johnson interprets the passage as deliberate repudiation of Christ and his sacrifice, parallel to Heb. 6.

Gill likewise rejects reading `wilfully` as:

- any single voluntary act;
- every gross immoral sin;
- every post-baptism fall.

Primary commentarial control:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/hebrews/10-26.htm

Gill instead sees a resolute, obstinate total apostasy from truth against light/evidence.

### P0 publication guard

Never quote only:

> “If we sin willfully...”

and apply it immediately to pornography without explaining the apostasy context.

---

# 5. Yet deliberate immoral rebellion can participate in an apostasy trajectory

The guard above must not become an escape hatch:

> “Hebrews only cares about doctrinal statements, so protected immorality is irrelevant.”

Hebrews 3 already joins:

- unbelief;
- sin’s deceit;
- hardening;
- falling away.

Owen’s *Nature and Causes of Apostasy* devotes major attention not only to doctrinal defection but to **apostasy from gospel holiness**.

Relevant primary pages:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.xii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.xiv.html

Owen warns about outward Christian profession combined with practical surrender to lust and pleasure, and treats such conduct as dishonoring Christ and falsely representing the gospel.

### Therefore

A youth who:

- still knows Christian doctrine;
- lives deliberately in protected sensuality;
- rejects repeated correction;
- ridicules holiness;
- increasingly renounces Christian allegiance;

may be showing **progressive defection from gospel holiness** and moving toward/manifesting apostasy.

But the pastoral conclusion is:

> **warn urgently**,

not:

> **declare omnisciently that repentance is now metaphysically impossible for this identified teenager.**

# 6. 2 Peter 2:20–22 sharpens the outward-reformation category

Primary:

- 2 Pet. 2:20–22.

Gill:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/2_peter/2.htm

Calvin:

- https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentaries_on_the_catholic_epistles/2_peter_2_20-22.htm

Both provide material relevant to gospel knowledge followed by re-entanglement.

Gill especially distinguishes:

- external reformation / notional or professed knowledge;
- from saving experimental knowledge.

He understands the later state as worse because prior light/profession aggravate the relapse.

### Context guard

2 Peter 2 is centrally about false teachers and their followers.

Use it to establish the **biblical category** of worse relapse after gospel knowledge, not to call every immoral teenager a false teacher.

# 7. 1 John 2:19 is not a universal explanation of adolescent failure

Primary:

- 1 John 2:18–19.

Calvin:

- https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentaries_on_the_catholic_epistles/1_john_2_18-19.htm

Immediate context:

- antichrists;
- doctrinal/community departure;
- people formerly among the church who manifest that they were not truly of it.

### Safe use

Visible presence and profession do not prove regeneration.

### Unsafe use

> “My son secretly watched pornography, therefore 1 John 2:19 proves he was never saved.”

Too broad.

# 8. How to describe the stages without pretending infallibility

## Stage A — heavy gospel exposure without clear profession

- knows doctrine;
- attends;
- may outwardly conform;
- no clear faith/profession.

Call:

> privileged/instructed unbelief if unbelief is clear.

Do not call final apostasy.

## Stage B — movement toward profession / baptism candidacy

- says he believes or wants baptism;
- may show initial fruit;
- not yet formally admitted.

If later he rejects Christianity, this makes the history more serious but does not retroactively create membership.

## Stage C — explicit profession followed by settled repudiation

- identified as Christian;
- later disowns Christ/gospel or persists in conduct while explicitly rejecting Christ’s authority.

`apostasy / false profession manifested` becomes much more appropriate language.

## Stage D — gross sin with conflict/return/help-seeking

Even if deliberate:

- confession;
- grief over sin itself;
- request for help;
- willingness to cut off access;
- return to truth.

Do **not** use Hebrews 6/10 to announce irreversible apostasy.

## Stage E — deepening protected rebellion

- deception planned;
- correction despised;
- gospel knowingly mocked;
- Christian profession abandoned or hollowed out;
- corrupting others;
- no desire to turn.

Use the strongest biblical warnings.

Still speak as a creature, not as the Judge who knows final election.

# 9. What does “impossible to renew again to repentance” mean pastorally?

The text is meant to terrify apostasy, not to provide parents with an infallible home diagnostic device.

A parent/pastor ordinarily does not know that a particular living person has crossed every line represented in Heb. 6.

### Therefore

If the person remains alive and willing to hear at all:

- warn;
- preach Christ;
- call to repentance;
- pray;
- do not promise cheap restoration;
- do not promise that tomorrow will always remain available;
- do not announce that God is forbidden to save him.

2 Tim. 2:24–26 provides a governing pastoral posture even toward opponents:

> gentle correction in hope God may grant repentance.

This does not contradict Hebrews.

It acknowledges our limited knowledge of whether the climactic apostasy described there has occurred in the specific case.

---

# 10. “He has heard it all already” and the function of warning

Hebrews was written to people who had heard much already.

The author does not respond:

> “You already know; I have nothing more to say.”

He repeats:

- today, hear his voice;
- do not harden;
- exhort one another;
- hold fast;
- do not throw away confidence;
- remember former days;
- endure;
- do not refuse him who speaks (12:25).

### Strong line

> **Repeated truth is not pointless merely because the hearer already knows its vocabulary. In Hebrews, warning is itself one of God’s means for keeping hearers from apostasy.**

---

# 11. Warning must not become ritual noise

At the same time, repeated admonition can become a predictable argument the hardened person has learned to ignore.

Existing modules `69` and `91_ADMONITION_MODE_CHANGE...` correctly preserve:

> truth does not change; mode/cadence may change.

Possible changes:

- shorter, clearer conversations;
- fewer circular debates;
- written boundary statement;
- elder/youth-leader involvement;
- consequences/access changes;
- no theological sparring at every family meal;
- leaving a clear open door for repentance/help.

### Guard

Changing mode is not giving up on the soul.

# 12. Practical diagnostic questions before using “apostasy” language

Ask:

1. Did he ever actually profess faith in Christ, or only participate socially?
2. Did he seek baptism merely as a possibility, or publicly confess Christ?
3. Does he now deny the gospel/doctrine, or simply refuse obedience in a specific area?
4. Does he reject Christ’s authority explicitly?
5. Does he call evil good, or merely surrender to it while knowing it is evil?
6. Does he receive any correction?
7. Is there any voluntary confession/help-seeking?
8. Is he corrupting others?
9. Has his life become a settled public contradiction of the profession?
10. What formal church standing did he actually possess?

These questions do not reveal election.

They prevent careless labels.

# 13. Final warning ladder

Use escalating biblical language proportionately:

### Level 1 — serious unbelief/disobedience under light

> You know God’s will and are refusing it. Greater light increases responsibility. (Luke 12)

### Level 2 — hardening

> Sin deceives and hardens; today is the time to hear God’s voice. (Heb. 3)

### Level 3 — worse return after knowledge/reformation

> Returning to pollution after knowing the way of righteousness is a worse state. (2 Pet. 2, context guarded)

### Level 4 — profession contradicted/abandoned

> Outward participation does not prove saving union; departure can expose false profession. (1 John 2, context guarded)

### Level 5 — explicit apostasy/repudiation

> Hebrews 6/10 gives terrifying warnings about repudiating Christ after profound gospel privilege.

### Rule

> **Do not begin at Level 5 merely because the sin is emotionally shocking.**

# 15. Locked synthesis

> **Послание к Евреям не позволяет нам успокоить воспитанного под Евангелием человека словами: “ты просто обычный неверующий”. Оно показывает особую страшную ответственность за полученный и отвергаемый свет. Но оно также не даёт родителю права назвать всякое сознательное падение окончательной апостасией. Чем ближе человек подходит к сознательному отвержению исповеданного Христа, тем сильнее должны звучать предупреждения; пока мы не знаем сердца и окончательного суда Бога, наше дело — говорить истину, увещевать, не подтверждать ложную профессию и молиться, чтобы Бог даровал покаяние.**
