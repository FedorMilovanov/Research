# Third-party reports — source lineage, independent corroboration, rumor, hearsay, and evidence weight

**Purpose:** close the evidence-lineage gap: several people may repeat the same allegation, screenshot, story or interpretation without supplying several independent sources. ## 0. Executive verdict

A parent/pastor may hear:

> `Three people told me the same thing.`

That can mean very different evidentiary structures.

### Structure A — three independent direct witnesses

Each person separately observed materially relevant facts.

### Structure B — one witness, two repeaters

A saw it; B and C heard it from A.

### Structure C — one screenshot circulated to three people

All three reports trace back to one digital artifact.

### Structure D — common group-chat narrative

Several people repeat a story after discussing it together.

### Structure E — genuinely independent mixed corroboration

One direct witness + one independent device record + one separate admission.

These are not evidentially identical.

### Locked distinctions

> **THREE REPETITIONS OF ONE SOURCE ≠ THREE INDEPENDENT SOURCES.**

> **AGREEMENT ≠ INDEPENDENCE.**

> **RUMOR ≠ VERDICT.**

> **HEARSAY / SECONDHAND REPORT ≠ NOTHING.**

> **DIRECT WITNESS ≠ INFALLIBLE WITNESS.**

> **INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION CAN STRENGTHEN A CLAIM; IT DOES NOT MAKE ERROR IMPOSSIBLE.**

> **SOURCE LINEAGE SHOULD BE KNOWN ENOUGH FOR THE DECISION BEING MADE.**

## 1. Biblical witness principle — important, but do not flatten it into a modern evidence formula

### Deuteronomy 19:15

The Mosaic judicial text requires more than one witness for a formal accusation/charge in Israel's covenantal court order.

### Matthew 18:16

Jesus invokes the witness principle in a church relational/disciplinary process.

### 2 Corinthians 13:1

Paul again uses the established-by-two-or-three-witnesses formula in an apostolic/church-correction context.

### Safe canonical synthesis

Scripture does not encourage:

> serious communal judgment based on unexamined assertion merely because one voice is confident.

But these passages are not a technical universal rule saying:

> `parents may never act on one credible source`;

or:

> `two people repeating the same rumor satisfy a magical numeric threshold`.

### P0

> **THE BIBLICAL WITNESS PRINCIPLE VALUES ESTABLISHMENT OF A MATTER, NOT ARITHMETIC DUPLICATION OF ONE REPORT.**

Evidence class: `DIRECT_SCRIPTURE → CANONICAL_SYNTHESIS`, with jurisdiction/context guard.

---

## 2. Proverbs 18:17 — first report requires examination, not cynical dismissal

The first case may appear right until another comes and examines/cross-examines.

Safe family/church use:

- hear a report;
- do not mock or suppress it;
- distinguish what the reporter knows directly from what he/she heard;
- seek relevant context where the stakes warrant;
- remain willing to update.

### Locked

`FIRST REPORT ≠ FALSE REPORT`.

`FIRST REPORT ≠ FINAL VERDICT`.

---

## 3. Source-lineage questions

For a material third-party report, useful questions can include:

1. Did you personally see/hear this?
2. If not, who told you?
3. Did you see an original message/image/account or a forwarded copy?
4. Did you discuss the story with other witnesses before giving your account?
5. Are you describing fact, inference, or what someone else concluded?
6. What part are you certain about?
7. Is there a safety reason ordinary questioning should stop and competent safeguarding process take over?

These questions are not a courtroom script.

Their function is to stop accidental source multiplication.

---

## 4. One originating screenshot can produce ten apparent witnesses

Example:

- A receives a screenshot;
- sends it to B/C/D;
- B tells a parent;
- C tells a pastor;
- D tells another family;
- all say `I saw proof`.

There may now be four genuine people who **saw the same artifact**.

That is not identical to four independent observations of the underlying event.

### Locked

> **MULTIPLE VIEWERS OF ONE ARTIFACT ≠ MULTIPLE INDEPENDENT SOURCES OF THE UNDERLYING EVENT.**

Use `290` for artifact authenticity/context.

---

## 5. Independence is not the same as lack of communication

Two witnesses may communicate and still each possess direct independent knowledge.

Conversely, two witnesses may never communicate but both derive their story from the same third source.

Therefore do not use a simplistic rule:

> `They talked, so both accounts are contaminated and worthless.`

Better distinction:

- **source independence:** did each have separate access to the underlying fact/evidence?
- **narrative independence:** did later discussion shape wording/memory/interpretation?

Both can matter.

### P0

`CONTACT BETWEEN WITNESSES ≠ AUTOMATIC COLLUSION`.

`NO CONTACT BETWEEN WITNESSES ≠ AUTOMATIC SOURCE INDEPENDENCE`.

## 6. Coordinated false alibi is a special case already owned by 287

`287` establishes:

- peers may coordinate stories;
- fabricated corroboration materially changes credibility architecture;
- `SIMILARITY ≠ COLLUSION`;
- established coordination means the confirmations were not independent.

This module does not duplicate that mechanism.

It supplies the general source-lineage control even where nobody is proven to be lying.

### Locked

> **DEPENDENCE CAN EXIST WITHOUT COLLUSION.**

Example:

three honest teenagers all repeat one mistaken original report.

---

## 7. Repetition can increase confidence in the existence of the rumor without increasing confidence equally in the underlying event

Suppose six people independently tell parents:

> `Everyone says X sent the image.`

That strongly supports:

> `a rumor/allegation is circulating`.

It does not automatically support to the same degree:

> `X actually sent the image`.

### P0

> **EVIDENCE THAT A CLAIM IS WIDELY REPEATED ≠ EVIDENCE OF EQUAL STRENGTH THAT THE CLAIM IS TRUE.**

This is especially important in church-youth social ecosystems where repetition moves fast.

---

## 8. Secondhand reports can still matter

Do not swing into:

> `If you did not personally see it, your report is useless.`

Secondhand information can legitimately:

- trigger a safety check;
- identify a possible direct source;
- reveal that another person may be at risk;
- preserve a lead before evidence disappears;
- justify asking a bounded question;
- alert competent authorities where required.

### Guard

The evidentiary description must remain accurate:

> `B reports that A told her X happened`

is not the same sentence as:

> `B witnessed X happen`.

### Locked

`LEAD ≠ FINDING`.

`SECONDHAND ≠ WORTHLESS`.

---

## 9. Direct witness can still be mistaken or overinterpret

Direct observation may be stronger than repetition, but it is not infallible.

A witness may:

- misidentify a person/account;
- misunderstand sarcasm/context;
- remember date/order incorrectly;
- infer motive;
- see only part of an interaction;
- mistake synthetic/manipulated media for an actual event.

Use `290` for digital attribution/context.

### P0

`DIRECT ≠ COMPLETE`.

`DIRECT ≠ INFALLIBLE`.

---

## 10. A witness's moral character matters differently from source independence

Parents/churches can reasonably consider credibility history.

But do not confuse:

- **independence of source**;
- **credibility of reporter**;
- **quality of underlying artifact**;
- **consistency with other evidence**.

A notoriously unreliable witness can still provide an independently verifiable true fact.

A highly respected witness can sincerely repeat a false report.

### Locked

> **REPUTATION AFFECTS WEIGHT; IT DOES NOT REPLACE VERIFICATION WHERE VERIFICATION IS MATERIAL.**

---

## 11. Group-chat consensus is particularly easy to overcount

A youth group chat can rapidly produce:

1. original allegation;
2. interpretations;
3. moral judgments;
4. cropped screenshots;
5. repeated summaries;
6. apparent consensus.

By the time an adult hears it, `many people know` may actually mean:

> many people encountered one circulating package.

### Product implication

When consequences are serious, ask for the lineage of the materially decisive claim rather than tallying names.

### Guard

Do not demand access to an entire peer group's private chats merely because a rumor exists.

Use proportionality/privacy controls `268`, `270`, `281`.

---

## 12. Parent coalition / church coalition can repeat the same error

Adults are not exempt.

Example:

- parent tells elder A;
- elder A tells elder B;
- elder B says, `several leaders have heard concerning reports`;
- the family hears this as independent ecclesial corroboration.

But all reports originate with the same parent.

### Locked

> **INSTITUTIONAL REPEATERS DO NOT BECOME INDEPENDENT WITNESSES MERELY BY HOLDING DIFFERENT OFFICES.**

This does not make the originating parental report false.

It keeps the evidence description accurate.

Use `282`.

---

## 13. Independent digital and human evidence can genuinely strengthen a case

Example:

- parent directly observes hidden account logged in on child's device;
- independent peer separately reports direct messages from same account;
- platform/email notice links the account to child's known address;
- child later admits ownership.

These are different evidence channels.

### P0

> **CORROBORATION IS STRONGEST WHEN DISTINCT SOURCES CONVERGE ON THE SAME MATERIAL FACT WITHOUT MERELY REPEATING ONE ANOTHER.**

This is `PRUDENTIAL EVIDENCE LOGIC`, not a mathematical certainty formula.

---

## 14. Contradiction between witnesses does not automatically destroy the whole case

Witnesses may differ about:

- peripheral time;
- sequence;
- exact words;
- minor detail.

Ask whether the contradiction is:

- material to the allegation;
- explainable by perspective/memory;
- evidence of fabrication;
- or simply unresolved.

### Locked

`ONE DISCREPANCY ≠ WHOLE CASE FALSE`.

And:

`GENERAL AGREEMENT ≠ EVERY DETAIL PROVED`.

Safeguarding cases require competent methods; do not improvise a family cross-examination contest.

---

## 15. Avoid witness contamination by repeated family interrogation

Parents may be tempted to separately question many peers and tell each one what others said.

This can:

- spread private material;
- alter later accounts;
- create social pressure;
- transform a bounded concern into community rumor;
- interfere with professional safeguarding/investigative processes.

### P0

> **MORE QUESTIONING ≠ AUTOMATICALLY MORE INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE.**

Where abuse/crime/safeguarding may be present, competent process should control.

---

## 16. Safeguarding reports — do not use source-lineage caution as a suppression device

This is a critical mirror guard.

A child says:

> `My friend told me another child is being abused.`

Do not answer:

> `That's hearsay, so we ignore it.`

A secondhand safeguarding report can be enough to trigger:

- a responsible safety response;
- referral/reporting according to role/jurisdiction;
- competent assessment.

### Locked

`NOT DIRECTLY WITNESSED ≠ NOT REPORTABLE / NOT ACTIONABLE FOR SAFETY`.

Exact legal duties remain jurisdiction-dependent.

---

## 17. Public accusation and formal church discipline demand stronger source discipline

The broader/more durable the verdict, the more damaging accidental source multiplication becomes.

Bad progression:

> one rumor

→ five people repeat it

→ leaders say `multiple reports`

→ public discipline language implies several independent witnesses.

### P0

> **DO NOT UPGRADE SOURCE COUNT THROUGH REPETITION.**

If the church says `multiple independent reports`, leaders should actually know why the reports are independent enough to justify that wording.

Use `282` and `290`.

---

## 18. Repair when a repeated rumor proves false/materially overstated

Use `285` / `288`.

Each person is responsible for his/her own speech.

Possible duties:

- stop repeating;
- correct recipients materially misled;
- distinguish `I heard` from `I knew`;
- apologize for certainty that outran evidence;
- preserve true parts if the case was mixed.

### Locked

`ORIGINAL SOURCE CORRECTION ≠ AUTOMATIC CORRECTION OF EVERY DOWNSTREAM REPEATER`.

## 19. Case matrix

| Pattern | Source count | Safe characterization |
|---|---:|---|
| A directly saw event; B/C repeat A | 1 originating witness | one direct source + downstream repetition |
| A/B independently saw event | 2 direct sources | independent corroboration may exist |
| A/B/C all saw same forwarded screenshot | 1 artifact, 3 viewers | multiple viewers, not 3 independent event sources |
| A says X; B reports `A told me X` | 1 underlying source | secondhand corroboration of report existence, not event |
| several youth discuss then give same story | uncertain independence | source/narrative lineage needs care; similarity alone ≠ collusion |
| parent + platform record + independent peer | multiple channels | stronger cumulative corroboration |
| six people say `everyone says X` | rumor widespread | evidence of circulation; underlying event still requires grounding |
| secondhand abuse concern | indirect safety lead | do not dismiss; route to competent safeguarding process |

## 20. Product overclaim kill-switch

Reject or rewrite:

- `Three people said it, so there are three witnesses.`
- `If several reports agree, they must be independent.`
- `Hearsay is worthless in every family/church decision.`
- `A direct witness cannot be mistaken.`
- `If witnesses talked to each other, all testimony is invalid.`
- `If witnesses never talked, their sources must be independent.`
- `A widely repeated allegation is probably true because many people know it.`
- `Secondhand safeguarding reports should be ignored until a victim personally tells the church.`
- `Two-witness biblical texts give parents a simple numerical proof rule for every household decision.`
- `Church leaders repeating a parent's allegation are multiple independent church witnesses.`

Preserve:

> **When several people report the same serious claim, ask whether they possess several sources or several copies of one source. Repetition can prove that a rumor spread without proving the underlying event. At the same time, secondhand information can be an important lead, especially for safety. Truthful judgment therefore tracks source lineage, directness, independence, corroboration and the seriousness of the action being contemplated — without turning family or church care into amateur courtroom theater.**

## 22. Final locked synthesis

> **Если трое говорят одно и то же, сначала надо понять, существуют ли три независимых основания или одна история прошла через три человека. Повторение может доказать распространённость слуха, не доказывая с той же силой само событие. Но и вторичный рассказ нельзя автоматически выбрасывать: он может быть важным следом или сигналом опасности. Поэтому родители и церковь должны считать не голоса, а источники — и описывать степень знания ровно так, как позволяют факты.**
