# Achievement idolatry, conditional approval, Christian-family reputation, and perfectionism

**Status:** RESEARCH-ONLY / CULTURAL-FAMILY MYTH AUDIT / NOT PUBLICATION APPROVAL  
**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Series:** `TEEN_DOUBLE_LIFE` / parents / church culture / fear of man  
**Purpose:** examine how a child can become a family achievement project or public Christian credential, and how this can produce shame, hypocrisy, conditional belonging, perfectionism and concealment.

---

## 1. Executive verdict

Christian parents should seek:

- diligence;
- truthfulness;
- useful skill;
- education;
- work;
- service;
- good character;
- visible obedience;
- wise reputation.

The error is **not** having standards or rejoicing in real fruit.

The error begins when the child's achievement, spirituality, social polish or public success becomes:

- the parent's identity;
- the family's justification;
- the church family's status marker;
- the condition for warmth/approval;
- the proof that parenting worked;
- a substitute for the child's actual heart before God.

### Locked distinctions

> **HIGH STANDARDS ≠ CONDITIONAL LOVE.**
>
> **DILIGENCE ≠ ACHIEVEMENT IDOLATRY.**
>
> **PUBLIC TESTIMONY ≠ REPUTATION MANAGEMENT.**
>
> **SPIRITUAL FRUIT ≠ FAMILY TROPHY.**
>
> **CHILD'S FAILURE ≠ PARENTAL IDENTITY COLLAPSE.**

The most dangerous family may not be the one that openly rejects holiness.

It may be the family in which holiness becomes a **performance asset**.

## 2. Biblical center — whose approval governs the family?

### Proverbs 29:25 — fear of man as snare

> fear of man brings a snare; trust in the Lord brings safety.

Gill explicitly understands the snare to include fear of losing:

- favor;
- friendship;
- honor;
- applause;
- protection from reproach.

Locator:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/proverbs/29-25.htm

This applies not only to children seeking peer approval.

Parents can also fear man:

- `What will the church think of us?`
- `What will relatives say?`
- `How can the pastor's child fail like this?`
- `What will people think if our daughter does not look spiritually exemplary?`
- `What will people think if our son leaves university / ministry / choir?`

### John 12:42–43 — glory from men vs glory from God

Immediate context concerns rulers unwilling to confess Christ because they loved human glory more than God's glory.

Do not turn this into a direct parenting proof-text.

But it supplies a fundamental discipleship category:

> **A religious appearance can be maintained because human approval is loved more than God's approval.**

This maps directly onto church-raised hypocrisy.

### Galatians 1:10 — pleasing men and serving Christ

Calvin draws a general lesson that those who hunt human applause cannot faithfully serve Christ.

Locator:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom41/calcom41.iii.iii.iii.html
- parallel: https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentary_on_galatians_and_ephesians/galatians_1_10-14.htm

Guard:

The Christian does not seek to offend people for its own sake. The problem is **human approval as governing lord**.

### 1 Samuel 16:7 — outward appearance and God's seeing

Immediate context is God's selection of David, not a generic anti-achievement slogan.

Allowed canonical control:

> outward impressiveness is not a reliable measure of God's judgment.

This guards families that confuse:

- attractiveness;
- charisma;
- grades;
- ministry visibility;
- musical talent;
- social confidence;

with holiness.

### Philippians 3:4–9 — credentials cannot justify

Paul's former grounds for confidence are counted loss compared with Christ.

Do not equate school achievement with Judaizing works-righteousness.

But use the deeper theological center:

> **Human credentials cannot carry the weight of righteousness, identity or acceptance before God.**

### Luke 10:20 — do not rejoice chiefly in visible ministry power

Jesus redirects the disciples from spectacular ministry success to the deeper grace of belonging to God.

Useful pastoral analogy:

- ministry usefulness is good;
- public platform is not the deepest ground of joy;
- a gifted teenager is not more secure because everybody praises his service.

### 1 Corinthians 4:7

> What do you have that you did not receive?

Achievement is received stewardship, not self-created worth.

### Colossians 3:23 / Ephesians 6:6–7

Immediate context concerns servants.

Still, the text explicitly contrasts heart-work before the Lord with eyeservice/men-pleasing.

Do not force it into a generic academic command, but preserve the theological pattern:

> **the final audience is God, not spectators.**

## 3. 1689 Confessional controls

### Chapter 16 — Good Works

Primary:

- https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-16

Critical controls:

1. good works are those God commands;
2. they are fruit/evidence of living faith;
3. ability comes from the Spirit of Christ;
4. believers never attain merit by works;
5. even sincere works contain weakness;
6. they are accepted in Christ;
7. works of the unregenerate may be materially useful but are not spiritually pleasing apart from faith/right end.

### Implication

A parent's task is not to manufacture a socially impressive portfolio called `Christian child`.

The family must not confuse:

`VISIBLE PERFORMANCE`

with

`SPIRIT-WROUGHT HOLINESS`.

### Locked gospel guard

> **A child's good works can never justify the child, and they can never justify the parent.**

This must sit beside all calls to discipline, diligence and visible fruit.

## 4. William Gouge — calling, usefulness, and a non-status end

Primary/historical source:

- William Gouge, *Of Domestical Duties*, Treatise 6.
- accessible transcription: https://christianreader.app/books/of-domestical-duties/08-treatise-6-the-duties-of-parents
- EEBO: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A68107.0001.001?view=toc

Gouge strongly supports parents:

- educating children;
- training them in useful skills;
- preparing them for a lawful calling;
- helping them exercise their gifts.

But his stated ends are not merely status:

- maintain oneself/family;
- relieve those in need;
- serve others;
- exercise God-given gifts in lawful work.

He explicitly says humans are born for others as well as themselves.

### Product significance

This gives a Reformed/Puritan alternative to both:

- anti-achievement laziness;
- status-idolatrous achievement culture.

> **TRAIN FOR USEFULNESS, NOT FOR FAMILY GLORY.**

## 5. Empirical evidence — conditional parental regard

### 5.1 2023 meta-analysis

Source:

- Haines & Schutte, `Parental conditional regard: A meta-analysis`.
- *Journal of Adolescence* 95(2), 2023.
- PMID 36345118
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36345118/

Definition:

Parental conditional regard involves giving or withdrawing affection/approval depending on whether the child meets parental expectations.

Across **31 samples**, greater conditional regard was associated with:

- introjected regulation: `r = .33`;
- contingent self-esteem: `r = .29`;
- depressive symptoms: `r = .22`;
- lower relatedness: `r = -.24`.

Important distinction:

This literature is grounded in self-determination theory.

It **does not define biblical parental love or authority**.

It provides evidence for a narrower observational claim:

> making approval/affection contingent on performance is associated with measurable maladaptive outcomes.

### 5.2 Achievement-specific conditional positive regard

Source:

- Assor et al., 2012, *Journal of Adolescence*.
- PMID 22078668
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22078668/

Study of 153 adolescents found perceived maternal conditional positive regard for academic achievement associated with:

- self-aggrandizement after success;
- self-devaluation/shame after failure;
- compulsive over-investment.

Do not overgeneralize from one sample.

But this pattern is highly relevant to the family cycle:

`SUCCESS → I AM SOMEBODY`

`FAILURE → I AM NOTHING / I MUST HIDE IT`.

## 6. Parental expectations, criticism, and perfectionism

### 6.1 2022 Psychological Bulletin meta-analyses

Source:

- Curran & Hill, 2022.
- PMID 35357848
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35357848/

Findings:

- parental expectations and parental criticism had small-to-moderate positive associations with self-oriented and other-oriented perfectionism;
- they had large positive associations with socially prescribed perfectionism;
- cross-temporal analysis suggested perceived parental expectations/criticism increased among U.S., Canadian and British college students from 1989–2019.

Authors explicitly note the broader competitiveness/individualism hypothesis remains complex and should not be oversimplified.

### Claim boundary

Do not write:

> `high expectations cause perfectionism`.

Instead:

> `excessive expectations/criticism are consistently associated with perfectionistic pressure, especially the sense that others demand perfection.`

### 6.2 2023 systematic review — academic pressure and adolescent mental health

Source:

- Steare et al., 2023, *Journal of Affective Disorders*.
- PMID 37437728
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37437728/

Use only for the evidence class it supports:

- academic pressure is investigated internationally as a potential contributor/correlate of adolescent mental-health problems;
- evidence is heterogeneous;
- it does not prove parental ambition is the sole or primary cause of depression/anxiety.

### 6.3 2026 intervention meta-analysis for youth perfectionism

Source:

- `Overcoming perfectionism during childhood and adolescence: a systematic review and meta-analysis of interventions`.
- PMID 41805454
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41805454/

20 studies / 25 intervention-control comparisons found interventions could reduce overall perfectionism and perfectionistic concerns/strivings with small effects.

Product relevance is limited:

- perfectionism is not simply a fixed personality badge;
- professional treatment may be useful where clinically significant;
- do not make therapy the theological cure for sin/fear of man.

## 7. Harvard Making Caring Common — cultural achievement pressure

This is not theological authority.

It is useful sociocultural evidence for messages young people report receiving.

### 2014 national survey report

Source:

- https://mcc.gse.harvard.edu/reports/children-mean-raise

Key finding reported by the project:

- a large majority of youth perceived adults as prioritizing achievement/happiness above caring;
- about 80% said parents were more concerned with achievement or happiness than caring;
- students were substantially more likely to believe parents were prouder of good grades than caring behavior.

Use as a U.S.-specific cultural signal, not universal anthropology.

### 2019 Turning the Tide II

- https://mcc.gse.harvard.edu/reports/turning-the-tide-2-parents-high-schools-college-admissions

The report argues that intense admissions/achievement focus can crowd out ethical character and encourages parents/schools to reduce excessive achievement pressure.

### 2026 Turning the Tide

- https://mcc.gse.harvard.edu/reports/turning-the-tide-2026

Freshness note:

Published June 2026.

The report again identifies achievement pressure as a meaningful cultural concern and argues against demanding a singular prestigious `passion` or externally impressive life narrative from teens.

### Guard

Harvard/MCC cannot define:

- sin;
- biblical calling;
- family hierarchy;
- godly ambition.

It can document a modern achievement culture worth pastorally understanding.

## 8. The specifically Christian form of achievement idolatry

Secular achievement pressure is obvious:

- grades;
- university;
- career;
- athletics;
- beauty;
- income;
- popularity.

Church families can create a more dangerous version because the prestige markers look spiritual:

- Bible knowledge;
- public prayer;
- baptism timeline;
- choir;
- youth leadership;
- theological vocabulary;
- modest dress;
- visible ministry;
- testimony nights;
- being `the pastor's good child`;
- never causing scandal.

### Dangerous equation

`RELIGIOUS COMPETENCE = REGENERATION`

This is false.

A child may become highly skilled at:

- giving the expected answer;
- praying in acceptable language;
- suppressing inconvenient questions;
- performing repentance;
- appearing morally uncomplicated.

### Central warning

> **A family can catechize a child into Christian language faster than the child learns to live before the face of God.**

The answer is not to stop catechesis.

The answer is to refuse to confuse **competence in Christian culture** with the new birth.

## 9. How achievement pressure can build double life

This is a mechanism hypothesis, not universal causation.

Possible pathway:

`PARENT IDENTITY INVESTED IN CHILD IMAGE`

→ `failure becomes relationally expensive`

→ `child learns which facts are safe to reveal`

→ `small failures hidden`

→ `successful concealment`

→ `public identity increasingly polished`

→ `private life increasingly divergent`.

### Strong project line

> **If failure threatens belonging, secrecy becomes psychologically cheap and truth becomes expensive.**

Do not write:

> `conditional parenting causes sexual sin.`

The child's lust, deception and rebellion remain his/her responsibility.

But family incentives can either make truthful disclosure easier or make reputation-preserving hypocrisy more rewarding.

## 10. Parents of ministry families / elders / visible church families

High-risk pattern:

> `My child's sin is not only his problem. It threatens my ministry credibility.`

Some ministry qualifications do concern household leadership (e.g., 1 Tim. 3; Titus 1), so this cannot be dismissed as mere irrational anxiety.

But the response must be truth, not concealment.

### Wrong responses

- suppress disclosure to protect office;
- pressure child into scripted repentance;
- minimize victim harm;
- quietly move child out of a role without truthful church governance;
- punish primarily because `you embarrassed us`;
- make other siblings preserve the family story;
- teach child that scandal is worse than sin.

### Better principle

> **If a ministry qualification is affected by the truth, the answer cannot be to make the truth disappear.**

This directly joins the project's institutional-hypocrisy lane.

## 11. The difference between shame and repentance

### Reputation shame

> `People will know what I am.`

### Parental-image shame

> `People will think we failed as parents.`

### Gospel conviction

> `I have sinned against God and people; I need truth, mercy and change.`

These can overlap emotionally.

They must not be treated as identical.

### Locked distinction

> **FEAR OF EXPOSURE CAN PRODUCE TEARS WITHOUT PRODUCING REPENTANCE.**

And:

> **PARENTAL EMBARRASSMENT CAN PRODUCE SEVERITY WITHOUT PRODUCING HOLINESS.**

## 12. Hard cases

### Case A — gifted child becomes family identity

The teenager:

- sings;
- preaches/teaches younger children;
- gets top grades;
- is praised by church adults.

Parents stop asking ordinary truth questions because `he is the good one`.

Risk:

Public excellence becomes concealment armor.

### Case B — child loses achievement

Examples:

- grades collapse;
- sports injury;
- failed exam;
- leaves prestigious school;
- loses ministry role.

Parent audit:

> `Did my warmth toward this child change because the status benefit disappeared?`

### Case C — child is spiritually unimpressive but truthful

A quiet child may:

- ask hard questions;
- confess temptation;
- struggle visibly;
- lack public gifts.

That may look worse than a polished sibling while actually containing **more truthfulness**.

Do not reward concealment by praising polish alone.

### Case D — church reputation after sexual sin

Wrong priority:

> `How do we keep anyone from finding out?`

Right first questions:

- who was harmed?
- who is at risk?
- what is true?
- what must parents/church/civil authorities do?
- what repentance/restoration process is appropriate?

Reputation is downstream of truth.

### Case E — parent fears child's ordinary non-prestigious adulthood

Child chooses lawful modest work rather than a status career.

Parents may counsel wisdom and responsibility.

They must not make prestige a divine command.

Use Gouge:

`FIT / LAWFUL / USEFUL CALLING`

not

`MAXIMUM STATUS CALLING`.

## 13. Parent audit — what do I actually celebrate?

1. What receives the longest praise in our home: character, truth, service, or status?
2. Do I become warmer after success and colder after failure?
3. Does my child know he/she can tell me an ugly truth without losing relational love?
4. Do I praise confession when it costs reputation?
5. Do I care more about a ministry role than actual holiness?
6. Do I use `what will people think?` as a primary moral argument?
7. Does my child's university/career function as my social identity?
8. Do I exaggerate or selectively present my child to church/friends?
9. Do I compare siblings by achievements?
10. Do I confuse public Christian fluency with regeneration?
11. If my child loses every impressive platform, what remains of my delight in him/her?
12. Do I secretly need my child to vindicate my parenting?

## 14. What healthy ambition looks like

The project must not react into mediocrity-as-holiness.

Biblical family formation can teach:

- diligence;
- competence;
- stewardship of gifts;
- excellence;
- finishing work;
- useful vocation;
- generosity;
- service;
- intellectual seriousness;
- bodily discipline;
- courage.

But the hierarchy is:

`GOD'S GLORY / LOVE / TRUTH / HOLINESS`

above

`STATUS / APPLAUSE / COMPETITIVE RANK`.

### Locked formulation

> **Aim high in faithfulness; hold status loosely.**

## 17. Final synthesis

> **A Christian home must not replace the world's prestige ladder with a religious prestige ladder. The aim is not to produce a child whose grades, beauty, career, Bible knowledge, choir role or polished testimony proves that the parents succeeded. Parents are stewards, not manufacturers of a public product. They should train children toward diligence, useful calling, truth and visible holiness while refusing to make affection, belonging or family dignity depend on performance. A child who knows that ugly truth is safer than beautiful deception is harder to train into hypocrisy. A family that loves the praise of God more than the praise of men can survive exposure, repentance and loss of status without needing to protect a false Christian image.**
