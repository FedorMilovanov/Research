# Household government — Eli, Pastoral Epistles, Psalm 101, family worship, and when “love” becomes connivance

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** research-only / biblical-theology + pastoral application / not legal advice  
**Purpose:** answer whether parents who keep an openly rebellious adult child at home can become morally implicated by refusing to govern their own household, while avoiding the opposite error of turning every family conflict into an eviction mandate.

---

## 0. Controlling thesis

Christian family love is not morally neutral hospitality.

A household head is not omnipotent over another adult's conscience, but Scripture and historic Reformed practice do not treat the home as a morally ownerless space in which the head may disclaim responsibility for every pattern occurring under his roof.

The core distinction:

> **Parents are not responsible to regenerate an adult child. They can still be responsible for what they knowingly authorize, finance, platform, conceal, or refuse to restrain within their actual jurisdiction.**

This file focuses on **connivance**: when affection, fear of conflict, reputation protection or false mercy becomes practical permission for ongoing evil.

---

# 1. Eli — the strongest warning against permissive affection

## 1.1. Text

1 Samuel 2–3 describes Eli's sons abusing priestly office, taking sacrificial portions unlawfully and committing sexual sin connected with the sanctuary.

God's charge against Eli is not merely:

> “Your sons sinned.”

It includes:

- **“you honor your sons above me”** (1 Sam. 2:29);
- Eli knew the iniquity;
- **he did not restrain them** (1 Sam. 3:13).

## 1.2. Gill / classical interpretation

Gill says Eli preferred his sons' honor over God's by continuing them in office when they should have been censured/punished/removed.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/1_samuel/2.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_samuel/2-29.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_samuel/3-13.htm

## 1.3. Why this matters for the series

This narrative exposes a recurring parental temptation:

> **“If I apply the consequence that my actual office requires, I will hurt my child; therefore love requires me to leave him where he is.”**

God's judgment says that sometimes refusal to remove privilege or office can itself be a way of honoring the child above God.

## 1.4. Jurisdiction guard — absolutely mandatory

Eli simultaneously occupies several roles:

- father;
- high priest;
- national judge/leader;
- superior over priestly office occupied by his sons.

Therefore this text does **not** mean:

`adult child sins => every father has Eli-like coercive power => expel immediately`.

The transferable principle is narrower:

> **When God has actually given you authority over a place, role, resource or institution, love does not permit you to pretend that known serious evil in that sphere is someone else's responsibility.**

# 2. 1 Timothy 3 — household government as a qualification for church government

Paul requires an overseer to manage his own household well and have children in subjection with dignity, reasoning:

> if a man does not know how to manage his own household, how will he care for God's church?

Classical commentators describe the family as a **lesser society** in which a man's governing character becomes visible.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_timothy/3-4.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/poole/1_timothy/3.htm

Gill explicitly contrasts good household government with Eli's failure to use authority over his sons.

## P0 transfer

The passage proves that Christian theology does not treat domestic government as inherently suspect or oppressive.

But it is an **elder qualification**, not a complete civil code for adult co-residence.

Do not derive an exact eviction threshold from it.

# 3. Titus 1 — debauchery, disorder and household credibility

Titus 1:6 requires an elder's children not to be chargeable with `asōtia` (dissolute/riotous living) or `anupotakta` (unruly/insubordinate).

Gill and Poole connect the text to household rule and to Eli's unrestrained sons.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/titus/1-6.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/poole/titus/1.htm

## 3.1. Important exegetical dispute

“Faithful children” may mean believing children or reliable/faithful children. Do not use this passage to teach that an elder has sovereign power to regenerate his children or that every unbelieving adult child automatically disqualifies him.

The less disputed point for this project is the explicit concern that an elder's household not be characterized by notorious **dissipation and insubordination**.

## 3.2. Transfer

Family order has **ecclesial credibility consequences**. The church cannot preach household faithfulness as irrelevant to leadership character.

But adult children long outside the household require a separate causal/jurisdiction assessment.

# 4. Westminster Directory for Family Worship — the household is a sphere of admonition

The 1647 Scottish Directory for Family Worship says private/family worship includes:

- prayer and praise;
- Scripture reading;
- catechizing;
- godly conference;
- **admonition and rebuke upon just reasons from those who have authority in the family**.

It also places responsibility on the head of the family to care that members perform personal/family piety.

Primary text:
https://reformedstandards.com/westminster/directory-for-family-worship.html

### Historical value

This is strong evidence that classic Reformed family theology did not imagine the household as merely shared sleeping space among unrelated autonomous consumers.

### Guard

This directory belonged to a particular Presbyterian church-order context. It does not give modern parents unlimited authority over legally competent adults, nor does it override civil law or abuse safeguards.

# 5. Psalm 101 — trusted household presence and deceit

Psalm 101:7 says the one who practices deceit shall not dwell in the king's house / the liar shall not continue before him.

The project already controls this text in `174`.

### Proper transfer

David speaks as king about his royal household/court.

Therefore:

- **strong analogy:** chronic deception can be incompatible with continued trusted residence/office in a household or institution;
- **unsafe direct law:** every private father possesses Davidic royal jurisdiction and must eject every liar.

### Application value

The verse is particularly strong where parents are being told:

> “If you love him, you must continue providing the same trusted access while he repeatedly uses deceit to exploit that access.”

No: Scripture recognizes that **trusted presence can be morally conditioned**.

# 6. Joshua 24:15 — “me and my house” without magical control

Joshua publicly declares:

> as for me and my house, we will serve Yahweh.

The statement assumes real covenantal household headship.

But Joshua cannot regenerate hearts by declaration.

### Guard

Do not use Joshua 24:15 as a proof-text that every adult family member's every inward choice is controlled by father.

Use it more modestly:

> **a household head may and should define the household's public allegiance and order under God.**

This coheres with requiring that one's home not become an officially tolerated venue for idolatry, sexual immorality, violence, theft or corruption of minors.

Source:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/joshua/24-15.htm

# 7. When tolerance becomes connivance

The series needs a sharper vocabulary than simply “strict vs loving.”

## 7.1. Connivance indicators

Parental “patience” may be becoming connivance when parents knowingly:

- provide a room specifically used for sexual immorality they are morally able to prohibit in their home;
- allow pornography/sexual material to be distributed to younger siblings;
- keep supplying devices/accounts/resources whose known primary function is ongoing defended sin without any stewardship reason;
- lie to church/family to preserve the child's image;
- refuse to disclose a genuine safeguarding risk to those who need to know;
- preserve ministry/choir/youth access merely to avoid embarrassment;
- repeatedly cancel agreed consequences after emotional repentance performances with no change in the deception system;
- permit threats/violence/theft because confronting the adult child is emotionally unbearable;
- expect spouse/other children to absorb permanent chaos for the sake of one rebel.

## 7.2. Non-connivance is not maximal punishment

Parents may refuse complicity while still:

- feeding someone hungry;
- helping obtain treatment;
- paying a directly controlled medical bill;
- meeting for meals;
- speaking kindly;
- maintaining emergency contact;
- assisting with safe temporary housing that does not facilitate the particular sin;
- forgiving personal offenses while not restoring access/trust.

Romans 12 remains a necessary positive ethic.

# 8. Household authority vs mere property ownership

Modern discussion often reduces the question to:

> “It is their house, so they can make any rule.”

That is inadequate.

A Christian household rule should satisfy more than ownership:

1. **lawful** — not requiring sin or violating legitimate civil duties;
2. **related to actual household stewardship** — safety, order, property, family morality, responsibilities;
3. **proportionate** — not arbitrary domination;
4. **truthful** — not reputation management masquerading as holiness;
5. **non-retaliatory** — consequence is not vengeance;
6. **sensitive to age/status** — minor and competent adult differ;
7. **protective of other household members**;
8. **compatible with Gospel witness** — no demand that external conformity be called regeneration.

### P0

> **“My house, my rules” is not yet a Christian argument. The stronger Christian claim is: “This household must be governed under God, and I am answerable for the sphere actually entrusted to me.”**

# 9. When asking an adult child to live elsewhere can be righteous

No single verse gives a universal algorithm.

But separate residence becomes increasingly defensible where several factors converge:

- person is legally/competently adult;
- rules are clear, lawful and connected to actual household stewardship;
- serious violations are repeated and defended;
- lying makes ordinary trust impossible;
- the home/resources are being used as infrastructure for sin;
- younger siblings/spouse/others are harmed;
- reasonable correction has failed;
- maintaining co-residence requires permanent invasive surveillance;
- there is no credible willingness to live within the household order;
- lawful alternatives exist or can be responsibly arranged.

### Not required

Parents need not wait until:

- every sin stops;
- the adult becomes converted;
- trust becomes perfect.

The standard is not sinlessness but **whether co-residence can operate under truthful, safe, governable conditions**.

### Counter-guard

Do not demand departure merely because:

- adult disagrees with parents on a prudential issue;
- adult chooses a lawful vocation parents dislike;
- daughter seeks a lawful marriage parents reject for class/status/ethnic prejudice;
- the child asks for ordinary adult privacy;
- parents cannot tolerate loss of child-level control.

---

# 10. When continued co-residence can still be wise despite serious past sin

Continue living together may be reasonable when:

- current truthfulness is improving;
- the person voluntarily discloses rather than merely being caught;
- the person accepts household rules without endless manipulation;
- safeguards can function without making everyone a detective;
- younger siblings are protected;
- employment/education/recovery plan is real;
- relapse is accompanied by warfare rather than defended peace with sin;
- parents can maintain marriage/other-child duties;
- temporary support serves restoration rather than enabling.

### Key distinction

> **Parents do not need certainty that repentance is genuine before showing mercy; they do need sufficient observable reality to make a stewardship judgment about trust and residence.**

# 11. Eli as warning against “good parent” image

A modern parent may fear:

> “If I remove support/access, people will think I am harsh.”

Eli's story reverses the question:

> **Could my desire to look loving actually be a refusal to honor God where I have a duty to act?**

The point is not severity for its own sake.

The sin may be **weakness that leaves evil structurally untouched**.

This directly parallels the church-side project guard:

> retention of attendance is not a higher good than truthful admonition.

At home:

> retention of physical proximity is not automatically a higher good than truthful household government.

---

# 12. Church / family cooperation

If an adult child is a church member and lives at home, parents and church should avoid two distortions:

### A. Parents outsource everything to church

> “He is an adult; pastors deal with morality; our house is neutral.”

False where household stewardship is implicated.

### B. Parents privatize everything

> “He is our child; church has no business knowing about public, member-disciplining sin.”

False where church membership/discipline legitimately applies.

### Better model

`FAMILY JURISDICTION` + `CHURCH JURISDICTION` remain distinct but can coordinate around shared facts without one swallowing the other.

---

# 13. Final thesis

The household should not be treated as a conversion machine or a moral free zone.

> **A Christian parent cannot save a rebel by controlling the house. A Christian parent also cannot evade responsibility for the house by saying the rebel is an adult.**

The actual question is narrower and harder:

> **What has God truly entrusted to me here, and am I governing that sphere in truth, love, justice and courage—without pretending I control the heart?**
