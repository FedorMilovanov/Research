# Puritan / Reformed / Biblical-Counseling Pass — Parental Guilt, Wayward Adult Children, and Non-Enabling Love

**Status:** SOURCE PASS / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  

---

## 1. Why this pass is needed

The project already has strong exegesis distinguishing:

- parental responsibility;
- adult-child personal guilt;
- sovereign regeneration;
- continuing family relation;
- non-enabling love.

This module tests that synthesis against:

1. Thomas Watson;
2. Richard Baxter;
3. later Reformed pastoral writers;
4. conservative biblical counseling (especially Jim Newheiser/IBCD).

None of these sources governs Scripture. They are used to test, refine, and historicize pastoral applications.

---

## 2. Thomas Watson — parents have duties; children retain real duties; parental tyranny is also sin

Primary source:

- Thomas Watson, *The Ten Commandments*, fifth commandment:
  https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/commandments.iv.v.html

Watson is valuable because he refuses a one-sided fifth-commandment theology.

### Duties of children

He includes:

- reverence;
- listening to godly counsel;
- lawful obedience;
- material care for aged parents.

He uses adult Joseph and King Solomon as examples of continuing honor.

### Duties of parents

Watson then turns the commandment around and argues parents must act in ways worthy of the office entrusted to them:

- raise children in fear/nurture of God;
- maintain appropriate authority;
- provide fittingly;
- guide them toward useful calling/work;
- act lovingly;
- act prudently;
- not provoke them to wrath.

His discussion of provocation includes:

- insulting/opprobrious speech;
- disproportionate correction;
- unjust deprivation;
- partiality;
- disgraceful parental conduct;
- commands that violate conscience.

### Project significance

Watson prevents the article from saying:

> “The whole problem is rebellious children; parents merely need to be stricter.”

A parent can **sin while correcting sin**.

A parent can also sin by indulgence.

Biblical household holiness therefore excludes both:

`cowardly permissiveness` and `tyrannical severity`.

### Historical guard

Watson’s specific seventeenth-century expectations concerning adult parental authority over marriage/calling can be much stronger than a modern article should simply reproduce.

Use his moral categories under Scripture, not every historical social application.

---

## 3. Watson: “children grow older” does not make parental concern vanish

A striking pastoral observation in the fifth-commandment exposition is that parental care can increase as children grow because the dangers become morally more serious.

That fits Proverbs 1–9:

- little-child dangers are physical and immediate;
- older-child/adult dangers increasingly include company, sexuality, greed, vocation, unbelief, and eternal matters.

### Guard

Concern does not equal unchanged jurisdiction.

The parent’s care may persist precisely while his **power to decide for the child decreases**.

This distinction is central to the adult-child lane.

## 4. Richard Baxter — mature children still owe duties, but parental government has ends/stages

Primary EEBO source:

- https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6.13?rgn=div2%3Bview%3Dfulltext

Additional source:

- https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6.3?c=eebo%3Bc%3Deebo2%3Bg%3Deebogroup%3Brgn%3Ddiv2%3Bview%3Dfulltext%3Bxc%3D1%3Bq1%3Dcirca+sacra

Baxter explicitly says precepts about filial duty still matter to those “of riper years” while parents live.

At the same time, elsewhere he treats parental government as having different acts/degrees according to its ends and recognizes that mature/married children are more at their own disposal.

### Project synthesis

Baxter supports:

> **continuing filial relation without pretending adulthood is permanent childhood.**

This is especially valuable against simplistic contemporary language in both directions.

---

## 5. Baxter on godly parents with wicked children

In his direction to children, Baxter describes the horror of children who receive:

- godly instruction;
- parental prayer;
- warning;
- holy example,

yet become profane, sexually immoral, drunken, or hostile to godliness.

His rhetoric is extremely severe by modern standards, but one theological point is important:

> he plainly recognizes the possibility of **diligent godly parents having wicked children**.

He does not infer:

> “wicked child = therefore parents could not truly have taught/prayed/lived well.”

### Guard

Do not import Baxter’s despairing rhetorical intensity as a prediction of final reprobation for any living teenager/adult.

While a person lives, the gospel call remains real.

---

## 6. Baxter’s pastoral method: tailor admonition to the person and the end

Source:

- *The Reformed Pastor*: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor.iv.v.ii.html

Baxter tells pastors to fit both **matter and manner** of admonition to the character and condition of the hearer.

He distinguishes age, situation, dominant temptations, status, etc.

For youth he particularly emphasizes sensual pleasure and mortification.

### Project relevance

This supports the project’s `MODE CHANGE` idea:

- one person needs patient reasoning;
- another needs a short clear rebuke;
- another needs privileges removed;
- another needs safety intervention;
- another needs encouragement because he is genuinely fighting sin.

“Biblical” does not mean mechanically identical counseling for every case.

## 7. Jim Newheiser / IBCD — unusually close to the project’s present synthesis

Primary counseling outline:

- *You Never Stop Being a Parent: Parenting Your Adult Children*:
  https://ibcd.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/newheiser.parenting_your_adult_children.pdf

Related materials:

- https://ibcd.org/three-reminders-parents-wayward-children/
- https://ibcd.org/cdc2-35-you-never-stop-being-a-parent-parenting-your-adult-children/
- https://ibcd.org/adult-children-and-parents/

Newheiser structures the adult-wayward-child problem around three simultaneous truths:

### A. Parents are responsible for parenting

He uses:

- Proverbs discipline texts;
- Eph. 6:4 themes;
- Eli.

### B. Children are responsible for their own choices

He uses:

- Ezek. 18;
- child-responsibility Proverbs;
- Isa. 1.

### C. Parents depend on God’s sovereign grace for conversion

He invokes:

- Cain;
- human fallenness;
- divine drawing/grace.

This three-part construction should likely become a major organizing axis in the future article.

---

## 8. Newheiser’s particularly useful line: repent of parental sin, but do not “pay penance” by enabling

His outline explicitly addresses parents who feel guilty about how they raised an adult child.

Pastoral logic:

1. If you sinned as a parent, confess the actual sin.
2. Confess appropriately to God, spouse, child.
3. Do **not** compound that guilt by financing ongoing sin.

This is extremely useful.

### Project formulation

> **Repentance for yesterday’s harshness does not require today’s permissiveness.**

> **You do not atone for past parenting failures by purchasing an adult child freedom to sin. Christ, not parental indulgence, is the atonement for parental guilt.**

Second sentence is theological synthesis, not a Newheiser quotation.

---

## 9. Fear and guilt can become engines of enabling

Newheiser notes that adult children may be persuasive/manipulative and parents may be especially vulnerable because of:

- fear;
- guilt;
- desire to maintain access/relationship;
- hope that rescue will finally produce gratitude.

### Biblical control

Prov. 29:25 warns about fear of man.

This does not mean every compassionate rescue is fear-driven.

But parents should ask:

> “Am I giving this because it is genuinely wise good—or because I am afraid he will punish me relationally if I say no?”

That is a deeply relevant post-departure diagnostic.

---

## 10. “Do not enable” needs careful definition

The project should avoid making `enable` into vague therapeutic jargon.

Use concrete biblical/moral categories.

### Participation in another’s sin

Examples:

- funding known vice;
- lying to conceal wrongdoing;
- providing a venue for sexual immorality;
- restoring a corrupting ministry role without repentance/safety change;
- allowing predatory access to siblings/youth;
- repeatedly removing consequences in ways that sustain the same sin.

### Doing good to a sinner

Rom. 12:20–21 simultaneously requires real good even toward hostile people.

Therefore:

`non-enabling` ≠ `no mercy`.

A parent can deny cash and provide food.

Deny cohabitation under immoral conditions and help find lawful work.

Refuse to lie to an employer and still drive the child to a medical appointment.

---

## 11. Newheiser on consequences — useful but must be subordinated to exegesis

His outline warns parents that rescuing may circumvent consequences that God can use to bring a sinner to repentance and invokes Luke 15 / Proverbs.

The project can use this **only with the guards already established in module 59 and 60**:

- Luke 15 is not principally a parenting manual;
- parents do not know God’s secret providential design;
- they must not manufacture suffering;
- they must not ignore acute safety danger;
- hardship is not itself repentance;
- God, not “rock bottom,” gives new life.

### Safer formulation

> **Parents are not obligated to erase every ordinary consequence of choices they did not command and cannot morally support.**

This is better than:

> “Make your child hit bottom.”

## 12. Burk Parsons / Ligonier — sovereign grace does not cancel parental means after departure

Source:

- https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/hope-for-prodigal-children

Parsons stresses that parents should not presume a prodigal is spiritually fine merely because he grew up in a Christian home.

He simultaneously emphasizes:

- God alone saves;
- God ordains means;
- parents should continue prayer;
- gospel proclamation/pleading should not be abandoned out of fear of pushing the child farther away.

### Project importance

This is a direct correction to:

> “If you keep bringing up repentance after 18, you are trying to be the Holy Spirit.”

No.

Human admonition can be an ordained means while regeneration remains God’s sovereign work.

### Guard

Persistent gospel witness must still obey the anti-quarrel/mode-change controls in module `69`.

“Never give up” on prayer/hope does not mean “repeat the same argument every day.”

## 13. Contemporary Reformed caution against deterministic parenting

Useful secondary sources:

- IBCD: https://ibcd.org/three-reminders-parents-wayward-children/
- Desiring God pastoral discussion: https://www.desiringgod.org/articles/lift-the-heavy-burden-of-shame
- Piper, *Counted Righteous in Christ* excerpt surfaced via:
  https://document.desiringgod.org/counted-righteous-in-christ-en.pdf

These sources correctly reject a hidden works-righteousness model of parenting:

> faithful parenting guarantees converted/obedient children;
> therefore wayward children necessarily prove failed parents.

The project should retain a strong Reformed corrective:

- means matter;
- parenting matters;
- consequences matter;
- but regeneration is not earned as the wage of excellent parenting.

## 14. Isaiah 1 / Cain as anti-determinism controls

Used carefully:

### Isaiah 1:2

God speaks of sons reared/brought up who rebelled.

This does not equate human parents with God.

It proves at minimum that `care/nurture → guaranteed creaturely obedience` is not a valid theological law.

### Cain

Cain receives perfect divine confrontation before murder and still sins.

This does not make warning useless.

It proves warning itself is not regenerative power.

### Strong formulation

> **The gospel does not teach parental irrelevance. It also does not teach parental omnipotence.**

## 15. A pastoral sequence after an adult child’s serious rebellion

### Step 1 — Parent self-examination

Identify **specific** sins, not atmospheric guilt.

### Step 2 — Parent repentance

Confess specifically; seek forgiveness/reconciliation where appropriate.

### Step 3 — Do not reverse poles

Harshness yesterday does not make permissiveness obedience today.

### Step 4 — Identify actual present jurisdiction

- home;
- money;
- ministry/access;
- younger siblings;
- property/resources.

### Step 5 — Identify adult child’s own jurisdiction before God

Parents cannot choose repentance, chastity, honesty, or faith for him.

### Step 6 — Use means without pretending to be sovereign

- prayer;
- Scripture;
- admonition;
- invitation;
- good example;
- wise mercy;
- church/pastoral help where welcomed/appropriate.

### Step 7 — Refuse resources that make sin easier when morally implicated

Non-enabling is stewardship, not revenge.

### Step 8 — Maintain gospel hope

Do not turn current hardening into a decree of reprobation.

## 16. Article-ready lines

> **A guilty parent needs repentance, not penance through permissiveness.**

> **A parent can confess, “I sinned against you,” without adding the false sentence, “therefore your adultery and deceit are my fault.”**

> **Past failure does not purchase the adult child a moral exemption in the present.**

> **The parent who knows he cannot regenerate his child has not become passive; he has finally learned the difference between faithful means and sovereign power.**

> **There is no contradiction between saying, “I was wrong,” and saying, “what you are doing now is also wrong.”**

---

## 17. Guards

Do not use Puritan rhetoric to:

- pronounce a living wayward child nearly beyond grace;
- reproduce every seventeenth-century household structure;
- justify coercive control of independent adults;
- justify humiliation or violence.

Do not use modern counseling to:

- replace biblical categories with therapeutic jargon;
- make `boundaries` the organizing doctrine;
- turn “consequences” into engineered suffering;
- use `enabling` as a slogan without specifying what conduct is actually being supported.

Use Scripture as authority; use these sources as subordinate witnesses and pastoral refinements.
