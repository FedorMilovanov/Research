# 1. Selected primary empirical anchor

Gemma Mestre-Bach & Marc N. Potenza (2025),
**“Adolescents, Pornography Use, and Problematic Pornography Use: A Rapid Systematic Review of Longitudinal Studies.”**

Journal:
- *Journal of the Korean Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry* 36(3):122–134.

Links:
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40631652/
- PMC full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12223668/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.5765/jkacap.250015

### Why this is selected

It is:
- recent (2025);
- specifically adolescent-focused;
- specifically longitudinal-focused;
- broad enough to survey multiple outcome domains;
- transparent about heterogeneity/limitations.

It included **44 longitudinal studies**.

---

# 2. What the review actually supports

The review reports longitudinal research examining pornography use/PPU in relation to topics including:

- sexual behaviors;
- sexual risk behaviors;
- sexual satisfaction;
- permissiveness;
- sexual aggression/dating victimization/rape myths;
- academic performance;
- religiosity;
- psychopathology/well-being;
- desensitization and contextual factors.

### Most important overall finding

The findings are **heterogeneous**:
- some studies report significant associations;
- other topics/results are inconsistent;
- more longitudinal research is needed to clarify relationships among types/patterns of use and outcomes.

### Therefore safe Part I claim

> **Longitudinal research has found associations between adolescent pornography use and several sexual, attitudinal and behavioral outcomes, but findings are heterogeneous and do not justify a simple universal causal story.**

This is the appropriate level for the article.

---

# 3. The review itself contains methodological limitations

The authors explicitly note limitations of the rapid-review method, including:

- database search restricted to PubMed/MEDLINE and PsycINFO;
- English/Spanish language restriction;
- grey literature excluded;
- no formal methodological-quality/risk-of-bias evaluation;
- initial/full-text screening primarily by one author, with consultation in uncertain cases.

### Editorial implication

Do not present this review as:

> `the definitive scientific proof that pornography causes X in every adolescent.`

### Better use

Use it to establish:

> there is a serious longitudinal research literature, but it is mixed enough that moral/theological arguments should not pretend to rest on a simplistic causal coefficient.

# 4. Secondary methodological cross-check

Pathmendra et al. (2023),
**“Exposure to Pornography and Adolescent Sexual Behavior: Systematic Review.”**

Links:
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36853749/
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015350/
- JMIR: https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e43116

Characteristics:
- adolescents 10–19;
- 19 eligible studies;
- six major databases;
- PRISMA;
- PROSPERO registration.

### Why retain as audit source rather than second main anchor

It has a stronger broad systematic-search structure than the 2025 rapid review but the underlying literature was largely cross-sectional and evidence for many sexual-behavior outcomes was conflicting/insufficient.

Its conclusion is therefore strongly compatible with our caution:

> causal inference could not generally be made from the evidence base.

### Publication role

Keep in notes/source ledger.

Do not burden main prose with two reviews unless a footnote/source panel benefits from it.

# 5. What Part I may safely say

## GREEN

- adolescent pornography use is a real and extensively studied phenomenon;
- longitudinal studies exist;
- some longitudinal associations with sexual behaviors/attitudes and other outcomes have been reported;
- findings vary by outcome/study;
- not all pornography use is identical to PPU/CSBD;
- pornography can be morally evaluated from biblical sexual ethics independently of whether clinical disorder criteria are met.

## YELLOW

Use only with specific supporting study if necessary:
- pornography use predicts a particular sexual behavior;
- high-use groups differ in a particular developmental trajectory;
- particular subgroup effects.

If retained, cite the underlying longitudinal paper as well as or instead of the review.

## RED

Do not publish:
- `pornography always escalates`;
- `pornography inevitably makes teens sexually active`;
- `pornography permanently damages the adolescent brain`;
- `every pornography user is addicted`;
- `pornography causes every measured adverse outcome`;
- `the science proves the biblical doctrine of lust`.

Biblical moral anchors:

- 1 Cor. 6:12–20;
- Eph. 5:3–12;
- 1 Thess. 4:3–8;
- Phil. 4:8 as secondary positive-thought category where contextually appropriate.

### Core methodology

The article does **not** need science to prove that pornography is sexually immoral within its stated biblical framework.

Empirical research answers narrower questions such as:
- what associations have been observed?
- how much uncertainty remains?
- which modern claims are unsupported?

### Strong project line

> **The Bible supplies the moral category; research can describe patterns and risks, but it must not be recruited to manufacture certainty it does not possess.**

# 7. Recommended Part I paragraph architecture

Do not write a mini literature review.

Possible flow:

1. define the moral issue biblically;
2. note modern private access/opportunity;
3. one sentence: longitudinal research has examined many outcomes and found a mixed/heterogeneous pattern;
4. explicitly refuse fake neuroscience/universal addiction;
5. return immediately to biblical temptation/secrecy/hardening argument.

### Example drafting logic — NOT FINAL PROSE

> Pornography does not become morally serious only when a clinician calls its use compulsive. Scripture’s sexual ethic already addresses lust and impurity. Modern research adds a different kind of information: dozens of longitudinal adolescent studies have examined pornography use alongside sexual attitudes, behaviors and other outcomes, but a 2025 rapid systematic review found a heterogeneous literature rather than one simple causal chain. That is enough reason to take the exposure seriously—and also a reason not to repeat internet mythology about universally damaged brains or inevitable escalation.

This is drafting logic, not final text.

# 8. Why not use a more sensational source

A source is not stronger because it yields a more alarming headline.

For this project, the best empirical source:
- constrains rhetoric;
- distinguishes association from causation;
- distinguishes use from problematic use;
- reveals heterogeneity;
- prevents apologetic overclaim.

This matches the user’s governing rule:

> **грехи не покрываем, но и не преувеличиваем.**
