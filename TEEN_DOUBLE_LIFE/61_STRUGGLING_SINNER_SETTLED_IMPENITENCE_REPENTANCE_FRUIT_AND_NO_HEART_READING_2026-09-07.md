# Struggling Sinner vs Settled Impenitence — Repentance Fruit Without Pretending to Read the Heart

**Status:** RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** sharpen one of the project’s most important distinctions without creating an infallible diagnostic test for regeneration.

---

## 1. The pastoral problem

Two people can be guilty of the same outward sexual sin and require very different immediate pastoral responses.

One may be:

- ashamed before God;
- bringing facts into light;
- asking for help;
- removing access;
- fighting and sometimes falling again.

Another may be:

- protecting the sin;
- demanding its acceptance;
- constructing alibis;
- recruiting others;
- refusing correction;
- using Christian appearance as cover.

It is pastorally disastrous to treat these as identical.

It is equally dangerous to infer with certainty from outward behavior exactly whom God has regenerated.

---

# 2. 1 John 1 gives the primary “light versus darkness” distinction

John does not contrast:

`perfect people` vs `people who ever sin`.

He contrasts the claim of fellowship while **walking in darkness** with walking in light, while immediately denying sinless perfection and commanding confession.

Sources:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom45/calcom45.ix.ii.i.html
- https://www.ccel.org/study/1_John_1%3A7-9

Therefore:

> A life in the light is not a life with nothing to confess. It is a life in which sin is not granted permanent protected darkness.

This is central to voluntary accountability.

---

# 3. 2 Corinthians 7 gives fruit language after painful confrontation

Paul rejoices not merely because sorrow occurred but because sorrow produced repentance. Verse 11 identifies visible consequences of godly grief: earnestness, clearing of the matter, indignation, fear, longing, zeal, readiness to see justice done.

Source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom40.xiii.iii.html

Do not turn this list into a rigid scorecard. The context concerns Corinth’s response to apostolic rebuke, not a universal six-item clinical instrument.

But it does justify expecting repentance to **do something**.

---

# 4. 1689 chapter 15 — repentance is more than feeling bad

1689.15.3 describes saving repentance as evangelical grace involving:

- Spirit-given sense of the evils of sin;
- faith in Christ;
- humility;
- godly sorrow;
- detestation/self-abhorrency;
- prayer for pardon and strength;
- purpose and endeavor to walk pleasingly before God.

1689.15.4 requires particular repentance for particular known sins.

1689.15.5 keeps hope wide open: no sin is so great that it brings damnation on those who repent; therefore repentance must continually be preached.

Sources:
- https://1689confession.org/repentance-unto-life-and-salvation/
- https://www.the1689confession.com/chapter-15

## Project consequence

After discovery, do not settle for:

> “Sorry for everything.”

Where appropriate, biblical repentance moves toward **particular truth**:

- what was done;
- whom it harmed;
- what was hidden;
- what must stop;
- what access must change;
- what restitution/protection is required.

---

# 5. 1689 chapter 17 — repeated serious sin does not automatically prove false conversion

Chapter 17.3 is crucial because it explicitly says true believers may through Satan’s temptation, the world, remaining corruption and neglect of preserving means:

- fall into grievous sins;
- continue in them for a time;
- incur God’s displeasure;
- grieve the Holy Spirit;
- have grace/comfort impaired;
- have hearts hardened;
- have consciences wounded;
- hurt/scandalize others;
- bring temporal judgments on themselves;

and yet be renewed in repentance and preserved to the end.

Source:
- https://baptistconfession.org/

This directly forbids simplistic statements such as:

> “He did it again, therefore every previous profession was certainly false.”

At the same time, perseverance doctrine cannot be used to tranquilize someone who is currently making peace with sin.

---

# 6. Watson — judgment against sin must reach will and affections

Thomas Watson is especially useful for the project’s question “he knows it is wrong but does not really fight it.”

Watson distinguishes:

- intellectual dislike of sin;
- actual hatred of sin in the will/affections.

He notes that a person may judge sin vile while retaining secret sweetness/complacency in it. True repentance involves not merely a correct proposition but a changed hostility toward sin.

Sources:
- https://www.fivesolas.com/Watson/repent2.htm
- primary EEBO: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A65293.0001.001/1%3A7.6?rgn=div2%3Bview%3Dfulltext

Watson also distinguishes godly sorrow over offense against God from mere grief because punishment has arrived.

Source:
- https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A65293.0001.001/1%3A7.2?rgn=div2&view=fulltext

# 7. A non-infallible pastoral matrix

The following is **not** a salvation test. It is a response matrix for what patterns should increase or decrease pastoral confidence that repentance is presently operating.

## Stronger signs of active repentance/warfare

- voluntarily tells truth not yet discovered;
- names sin plainly rather than euphemizing;
- does not primarily argue “everyone does it”;
- accepts that God’s law, not merely parents’ preference, is at stake;
- asks for removal of occasions/access;
- accepts proportionate consequences without making consequence-removal the condition of cooperation;
- discloses relapses instead of only admitting what can be proven;
- shows concern for people harmed/corrupted;
- stops distributing/recruiting others;
- changes company where company was feeding sin;
- seeks prayer/counsel/accountability;
- demonstrates new conflict with the sin after immediate fear has faded;
- pursues Christ and ordinary means, not merely technical abstinence.

## Stronger signs of settled impenitence / peace with sin

- insists sin be accepted as a condition of relationship;
- plans concealment;
- creates new secret accounts after correction specifically to preserve the same sin;
- lies whenever evidence permits;
- mocks holiness or those resisting the same sin;
- recruits/teaches others;
- turns church participation into camouflage or access to peers;
- refuses every reasonable means of accountability;
- treats consequences as the only problem;
- demands immediate restoration of privileges while protecting the behavior;
- weaponizes “grace,” “I’m unregenerate,” “God will save me if He wants,” or “Christians sin too” to silence admonition;
- increasingly approves the same evil in others.

Again: these are **pastoral patterns**, not access to God’s decree.

---

# 8. Recurring struggle is not the same as settled peace

Critical formula:

`recurring struggle + truth + hatred + help-seeking + renewed warfare`

is not the same as

`planned concealment + defense + social recruitment + refusal of correction + settled entitlement`.

Both can involve repeated acts.

Therefore frequency alone cannot carry the entire diagnosis.

This is particularly important with pornography, where repeated access can become deeply habituated. Habituation may explain persistence; it does not remove guilt. Conversely persistence does not automatically prove there is no repentance at all.

---

# 9. Being caught can become the beginning of repentance without being repentance itself

Do not falsely oppose discovery and repentance.

A person may initially be exposed involuntarily and then, under the Word and Spirit, genuinely repent.

Thus:

`caught` ≠ `repentant`

but also

`caught first` does not imply `repentance impossible`.

The issue becomes what follows exposure.

---

# 10. Voluntary disclosure is weighty because it reverses the concealment strategy

A particularly meaningful change is when a teenager moves from:

> “I will admit only what you can prove”

into:

> “I need to tell you what you do not know because I do not want darkness to protect this anymore.”

This resonates strongly with the 1 John light/darkness framework.

It does not mean every detail must be broadcast publicly. Confession has proper audiences:

- God;
- those sinned against where appropriate;
- parents/guardians where authority/safety applies;
- pastoral/accountability help where appropriate;
- authorities/safeguarding channels where harm/crime requires them.

“Walking in light” is not voyeuristic forced disclosure to everyone.

---

# 11. Restoration should follow fruit, not panic or image-management

When church/ministry privileges were restricted because of serious known conduct, restoration should not depend merely on:

- one emotional apology;
- a parent demanding fairness;
- passage of a fixed number of days;
- embarrassment about an empty choir position.

Nor should restoration require impossible proof of perfect sanctification.

Better prudential questions:

1. Has the specific conduct stopped or materially changed?
2. Has deception reduced or shifted into voluntary truth?
3. Is corrupting/recruiting behavior gone?
4. Is the person accepting correction?
5. Are appropriate boundaries actually being kept?
6. Has enough time passed to observe fruit proportional to the seriousness/risk?
7. Are harmed/vulnerable people protected?
8. Is return to this particular role wise, or would another form of participation be better first?

---

# 12. Unregenerate teenager: repentance is still commanded

If the teenager says:

> “I’m probably not saved, so why are you asking me to live like a Christian?”

the answer is not:

> “Fair point; keep sinning until regeneration happens.”

1689 chapter 15.4 says repentance of known particular sins is **every man’s duty**. Chapter 19.5 says the moral law binds all.

The pastoral response should distinguish:

- you cannot manufacture saving obedience apart from Christ;
- you are not therefore morally authorized to continue rebellion;
- your duty is not to perfect a Christian mask;
- your duty is to repent, believe the gospel, and obey the God who already owns you as Creator.

---

# 13. Final project formula

> **The key contrast is not “the sinner who still falls” versus “the sinner who never falls.” It is increasingly between a sinner who is bringing sin into the light to fight it and a sinner who is building a protected life in which the sin may remain, be defended, and recruit others.**

This distinction is pastorally weighty while remaining humbly short of infallible heart-reading.
