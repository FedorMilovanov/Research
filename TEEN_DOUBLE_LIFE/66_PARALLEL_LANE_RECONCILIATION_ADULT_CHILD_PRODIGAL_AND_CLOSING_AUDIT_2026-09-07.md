## 2. Reconciled adult-child position

The project must now preserve all of the following simultaneously.

### 2.1 Adulthood changes jurisdiction, not morality

Civil adulthood ends ordinary minor-child governance over a competent adult’s:

- residence;
- phone/passwords;
- daily schedule;
- employment;
- lawful independent decisions.

It does not erase:

- God’s moral law;
- family relation;
- honor principle;
- parental affection;
- prayer;
- truthful admonition;
- lawful household/property boundaries.

Core line:

> **Adulthood enlarges the sphere in which a person bears his own decisions; it does not shrink the sphere of God’s lordship.**

---

### 2.2 Release child-level control ≠ release relationship

Parents may no longer control an independent adult’s room/phone/timetable.

They may still, where contact is welcomed/lawful:

- communicate;
- ask about safety;
- meet;
- love;
- warn;
- pray;
- offer non-enabling good;
- plead for repentance;
- keep a truthful route home.

There is no biblical universal command:

> “At eighteen, after departure, initiate zero contact.”

Nor is there a command to pursue obsessively.

Contact is a wisdom question conditioned by safety, willingness, manipulation, harassment boundaries and actual relationship.

---

### 2.3 Luke 15 is not a modern contact protocol

The primary literary context is:

- sinners drawing near;
- Pharisees grumbling;
- lost sheep/coin/sons;
- joy over repentance;
- divine mercy;
- elder-brother self-righteousness.

Narrative silence does not prove:

- father never warned;
- father never inquired;
- father sent no message;
- therefore Christian parents must practice no-contact.

Nor does seeing the son afar justify modern digital tracking.

What Luke 15 strongly supplies:

- serious rebellion;
- consequences not endlessly neutralized;
- “coming to himself”;
- actual return/confession;
- extravagant reception;
- warning against self-righteous refusal to rejoice.

Important guard:

> **Do not send someone into the far country as a technique; but if a competent adult chooses it, parents may have to stop financing the far country.**

---

### 2.4 Non-enabling ≠ refusal of every good

Romans 12:9–21 provides a superior Bible-first framework to vague “boundaries” slogans:

- genuine love;
- abhor evil;
- cling to good;
- persist in prayer;
- do not curse/revenge;
- pursue peace as far as possible;
- feed/do good even to an enemy;
- overcome evil with good.

Therefore:

- cash that directly sustains vice may be refused;
- food, urgent medical help, safe transport or direct provider payment may still be real good;
- mode of help can change after deception.

Core line:

> **Love need not finance the lie it is begging the person to leave.**

But also:

> **Not enabling is not a command to become cruel or indifferent.**

---

### 2.5 James 5 permits restorative initiative

James 5:19–20 establishes a Christian category of someone acting to turn a wanderer back.

It does not provide a contact frequency or authorize harassment.

But it directly undermines the universal slogan:

> “Any initiative toward a wanderer means trying to be the Holy Spirit.”

God grants repentance; humans still admonish, teach, plead and seek restoration through appointed means.

## 3. Church discipline does not erase family relations

Module `64_OWEN_GILL_EDWARDS...` provides unusually strong primary-source convergence.

### Owen

Formal separation does not dissolve duties founded in pre-existing natural/moral relations; admonition may continue.

### John Gill

Excommunication does not break natural/civil relations such as parents/children and does not forbid hearing the external ministry of the Word.

### Jonathan Edwards

Parents retain parental affection/care; prayer, admonition and humane duties remain.

### Reconciled conclusion

> **Loss of church fellowship is not loss of biological/familial identity.**

Formal discipline can alter ecclesial fellowship without turning parents into strangers.

This also prevents two jurisdiction errors:

- parents privately impersonating church excommunication;
- church leaders ordering the dissolution of family duties God did not revoke.

## 4. Adult child at home / returning home

Distinguish:

`relationship`  
`≠ co-residence`  
`≠ money`  
`≠ trust`  
`≠ access to siblings`  
`≠ ministry privileges`.

A competent adult may be loved and welcomed relationally while residence is conditioned by lawful household rules.

Possible household categories:

- no violence/threats;
- no drugs/criminal exploitation;
- no use of home for fornication;
- no pornography distribution/sexualization of younger siblings;
- no corrupting/predatory access;
- basic truthful/respectful household order;
- work/financial responsibilities appropriate to situation;
- applicable tenancy/civil law.

Do not derive one universal eviction rule from Scripture.

For minors, the project remains absolute:

> **homelessness/abandonment is not a discipline tool.**

---

## 5. Protecting younger siblings remains a governing duty

Adult-child love cannot require sacrificing younger/vulnerable household members.

Where an adult child:

- distributes pornography;
- teaches lying;
- recruits siblings into secret chats;
- brings dangerous persons/substances;
- sexually corrupts/harasses;
- threatens others,

co-residence/access may need substantial restriction.

This is compatible with continued:

- love;
- prayer;
- meals/meetings in safer contexts;
- humane assistance;
- admonition.

## 6. Reconciled repentance/restoration position

Luke 15 + 2 Cor. 7 + 1 John 1 + 1689 ch. 15/17 + Watson must be held together.

A returning adult child may initially have mixed motives, including hardship/hunger. Do not require omniscient proof of perfectly purified motives before conversation or mercy.

At the same time:

- material need alone is not repentance;
- return to parents’ house alone is not return to God;
- apology alone is not restored trust;
- genuine repentance should produce increasingly observable truth/turning.

If repentance is credible:

- mercy should be visible;
- do not vindictively demand extra suffering merely to prove seriousness;
- practical trust/access may still rebuild over time according to risk.

The elder brother in Luke 15 protects the project against turning biblical seriousness into self-righteous resentment of mercy.
