# Private and Public Rebuke in Youth Cases
## Matthew 18, 1 Timothy 5, Public Scandal, Minor Confidentiality, and Church Correction

**Status:** P0 ECCLESIOLOGY / CORRECTION-SCOPE GUARD / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Depends on:** `35`, `41`, `50`, `151`, `157`, `159`, `165–166`.

# 0. Why this gap matters

The project insists that known hypocrisy should not be normalized.

But that can be misheard as:

> “If a church-raised teenager is living in sexual sin, expose him publicly so the whole church can rebuke him.”

The opposite mistake is:

> “Because he is a minor / because privacy matters, serious public scandal can never be addressed publicly.”

Neither is a sufficient biblical rule.

### Governing thesis

> **The scope of correction should ordinarily correspond to the scope of the offense, the person's ecclesial status, the need for restoration, and the protection of others. Biblical truthfulness does not require maximum publicity. Biblical confidentiality does not require concealment of a genuinely public scandal.**

---

# 1. Matthew 18:15–17 — private-first pattern for an offense that can be addressed privately

Primary:

- Matt. 18:15–17.

Classic Reformed control:

- Calvin, *Institutes*, church discipline section:
  https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.vi.xiii.html

Jesus' sequence moves:

1. brother-to-brother;
2. one or two additional witnesses;
3. church,

where the earlier stages fail.

### Project significance

A private sin/offense does not become spiritually more serious merely because adults tell more people.

### Guard

Matt. 18 is not a rule that every notorious/public scandal must be kept artificially private through all stages while the whole community already knows it.

Calvin explicitly distinguishes secret faults from open scandal.

---

# 2. Calvin's private/public distinction

Calvin summarizes:

- secret/private faults should ordinarily follow Christ's graduated steps;
- open sins attended by public scandal may require solemn/public correction without pretending the matter is secret.

This is highly relevant to youth cases.

### Examples

## Private pattern

Parents discover:

- hidden pornography;
- private lying;
- secret account,

with no evidence it spread to the group.

Default response should not be:

> announce details to youth group.

## Public/group scandal

Youth openly:

- distributes porn in church group;
- mocks holiness publicly;
- sexually pressures multiple youth;
- uses public Christian platform while known scandal has become common knowledge;
- publicly teaches others to lie/evade parents.

Now leaders may need a wider response because the harm/message is already wider.

# 3. 1 Timothy 5:19–20 — immediate context is elders

Primary:

- 1 Tim. 5:19–20.

Commentary controls:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_timothy/5-20.htm
- Calvin commentary:
  https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/1_timothy/5.htm

The flow begins with accusations against an **elder** and the need for witnesses.

Many commentators understand “those who persist in sin, rebuke before all” primarily in relation to proved sinning elders/presbyters, though some extend the principle more broadly.

### P0 guard

Do not cite 1 Tim. 5:20 as a simple command:

> “Every sinning teenager must be rebuked before the whole congregation so others fear.”

That ignores the immediate context.

### Valid broader principle

Public/representative wrongdoing by responsible persons may warrant public correction in order to remove scandal and warn others.

But application requires context, not proof-texting.

---

# 4. 1 Corinthians 5 — notorious insider scandal requires corporate clarity

Primary:

- 1 Cor. 5.

Calvin:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/1_corinthians/5.htm

The case is:

- notorious;
- ongoing;
- member/“called brother” context;
- congregation itself implicated by toleration/boasting.

The church must corporately act.

### Project use

This demonstrates that sometimes secrecy would itself become complicity.

### Guard

It directly applies to church-member discipline, not to publicly exposing a never-member minor merely because adults dislike his conduct.

---

# 5. Galatians 2 — public contradiction can require public correction

Paul opposes Peter publicly because Peter's conduct:

- is public;
- influences others;
- compromises gospel truth in the community.

### General prudential principle

> **Where public behavior teaches a public lie, correction may need enough publicity to correct the lie.**

### Guard

Peter is an apostolic/leader case in a unique setting.

Do not make Gal. 2 a universal youth-discipline procedure.

---

# 6. “As broad as the harm” is useful wisdom, not a mathematical law

A common pastoral maxim:

> private sin → private correction;
> public sin → public correction.

Useful, but too simple if treated mechanically.

Questions include:

- Who actually knows?
- Who was harmed/misled?
- Is the person a member?
- Was there a representative role?
- Are minors involved?
- Is sexual information at issue?
- Is safeguarding/reporting required?
- Will disclosure repair truth or merely humiliate?

### Better rule

> **Make correction as broad as truth, restoration and protection genuinely require—and no broader merely to satisfy outrage.**

---

# 7. Minors create additional confidentiality obligations

A teenager's sexual sin can become church gossip extremely quickly.

Harm can include:

- permanent stigma;
- peers spreading details;
- humiliation unrelated to restoration;
- exposing another minor's private information;
- compromising safeguarding investigation.

### Therefore

Even where leaders must act, they should minimize unnecessary detail.

Possible communication:

> “For serious reasons related to conduct and our responsibility for the group, X will not serve/participate in this role for now. Leaders are addressing the matter. Please do not speculate or spread rumors.”

rather than graphic disclosure.

### Guard

Exact communication depends on actual facts and church/safeguarding process.

---

# 8. Public silence can itself create a false narrative

Suppose everyone has seen:

- public obscene posts;
- open sexual boasting;
- public attack on Christian morality,

and the youth nevertheless appears on church platform next Sunday with no visible change.

Peers may infer:

> church considers it insignificant.

Leaders may need to correct that public message by:

- removing role;
- general teaching;
- appropriate statement;
- formal discipline if member.

### Strong line

> **Confidentiality should protect persons; it should not require leaders to publicly perform a lie.**

---

# 9. Public correction must have a restorative purpose

Calvin's discipline framework emphasizes:

- recovery of offender;
- church purity/order;
- deterrence of scandal;
- restoration of penitent.

1 Cor. 5 itself aims ultimately toward salvation/restoration, not theatrical punishment.

### Warning signs of corrupt public correction

- adult anger wants an audience;
- details added because they are shocking;
- offender is mocked;
- confession already sufficient but humiliation continues;
- disclosure exceeds the people affected;
- public shaming substitutes for pastoral work.

### Strong line

> **Publicity is a medicine only when the wound actually requires it; otherwise it can become another wound.**

---

# 10. What if the youth is not a member but public corruption is occurring?

Formal church discipline jurisdiction may not apply.

Yet leaders can still:

- remove representative role;
- enforce group rules;
- protect peers;
- correct false impressions;
- ask the person to stop disruptive/corrupting behavior;
- limit specific access.

### Public communication

Should focus on what the church is responsible to clarify, not on pretending a formal excommunication occurred.

Example:

> “He is not serving in this role. We are addressing serious conduct concerns. Attendance at preaching remains open. We ask everyone not to spread private details.”

Case-specific; not a required formula.

---

# 11. What if the youth is a baptized member?

Then `159` controls dual stewardship.

If member discipline reaches congregation-level action, parents should not hide behind:

> “He is a minor, so church cannot tell the church.”

But church should still:

- respect minor safeguarding;
- avoid unnecessary sexual detail;
- coordinate appropriately with parents/guardians unless safety dictates otherwise;
- follow constitution/process.

---

# 12. The whole church need not personally rebuke him

Corporate action can mean:

- congregation hears/affirms discipline;
- church is taught truth;
- members no longer falsely reassure;
- appropriate members speak truth when naturally related.

It does **not** require:

- every person confronting the teenager;
- coordinated social pressure;
- repeated unsolicited lectures from strangers.

### Locked formulation

> **Корпоративная правдивость не равна коллективной травле.**

---

# 13. Restoration also may need public clarification when the scandal was public

2 Cor. 2:5–11 warns against endless punitive exclusion after restorative purpose is reached.

If a public discipline/restriction occurred and credible repentance/restoration follows, the church may need to communicate restoration enough to prevent permanent stigma.

### Strong principle

> **If the church publicly marked the fall, it should not hide the restoration so completely that the person remains publicly branded forever.**

Safeguarding restrictions can remain distinct.

---

# 14. P0 guards

Never write:

- 1 Tim. 5:20 commands public rebuke of every sinful child;
- Matt. 18 requires every public scandal to stay private until three exact procedural rounds finish;
- private sexual sin should automatically be announced to youth group;
- confidentiality means known public scandal can never be addressed publicly;
- public correction is justified because humiliation helps repentance;
- nonmember administrative restriction equals public church discipline.

---

# 15. Final locked synthesis

> **Библейское обличение не стремится к максимальной огласке. Тайный грех обычно не нужно превращать в церковный спектакль; открытый скандал нельзя прятать так, чтобы церковь сама начала подтверждать ложь. Поэтому круг обличения должен быть настолько широким, насколько требуют истина, восстановление и защита людей — и не шире ради стыда. Для несовершеннолетних это особенно важно: церковь обязана хранить святость, не превращая сексуальный грех ребёнка в пищу для церковных слухов.**
