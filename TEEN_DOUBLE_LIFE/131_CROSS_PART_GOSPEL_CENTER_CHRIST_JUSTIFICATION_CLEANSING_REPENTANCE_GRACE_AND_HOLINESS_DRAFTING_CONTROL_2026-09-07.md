# 0. Why this control is needed

The Research corpus is now unusually strong on:

- sinful desire;
- deception and secrecy;
- pornography / sexualized speech / corrupt company;
- conscience and hardening;
- parental boundaries;
- church holiness and differentiated access;
- false vs credible repentance;
- trust rebuilding;
- safeguarding;
- adult-child non-enabling.

That strength creates a publication risk of its own.

A drafter could accurately reproduce the diagnosis and still produce an article whose practical movement feels like:

`you sinned`  
`→ you were exposed`  
`→ restrictions follow`  
`→ prove enough fruit`  
`→ perhaps trust / privileges return`.

That is not an adequate Christian gospel architecture.

The articles must instead preserve this order:

`God names and judges sin`  
`→ exposure does not atone for sin`  
`→ consequences do not pay for sin`  
`→ Christ alone is the ground of pardon and righteousness`  
`→ saving faith receives and rests on Christ`  
`→ repentance is evangelical grace, not self-payment`  
`→ grace trains the redeemed person to renounce ungodliness`  
`→ changed life bears fruit`  
`→ human trust/access may still require prudent rebuilding`.

The central distinction:

> **Fruit is evidence of repentance; fruit is not the price of justification.**

And:

> **Consequences can serve restraint, protection, restitution, and trust rebuilding; they cannot serve as atonement.**

# 1. Romans 3:21–26 — the answer to guilt is not a successful reform program

## Textual center

Paul moves from universal guilt to a righteousness of God manifested apart from works of law, received through faith in Jesus Christ, with justification freely/by grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

The passage grounds justification in Christ's redemptive work and God's justifying action, not in the sinner's subsequent record of improved behavior.

## Publication use

When the article has exposed:

- lust;
- lying;
- obscene speech;
- hypocrisy;
- hardened patterns;
- injury to others,

it must not leave the reader with the impression:

> `Now build a sufficiently clean record so God can receive you.`

The gospel answer is Christ.

### Safe synthesis

> **The sinner does not repair his standing before God by producing a cleaner month. He needs a righteousness he does not possess and a redemption he cannot buy.**

### Guard

Do not use justification to erase repentance, restitution, discipline, or temporal consequences.

Paul's doctrine of free justification is not antinomian permission.

# 2. 1689 LBCF 11 — justification must stay logically prior to trust restoration

Primary source:

- https://the1689.org/chapters/11
- https://baptistconfession.org/

## 11.1

The confession says God freely justifies the effectually called:

- by pardoning sins;
- by accounting/accepting them righteous;
- for Christ's sake alone;
- not for anything wrought in them or done by them;
- not by imputing faith itself or other evangelical obedience as righteousness;
- through faith receiving/resting on Christ and his righteousness.

## 11.2

Faith is the alone instrument of justification, yet saving faith is not alone in the justified person; it accompanies the other saving graces and works by love.

## 11.3

Christ's obedience and death fully satisfy divine justice for those justified; justification is free grace.

## Project consequence

Never allow this sequence:

`apology`  
`+ 90 clean days`  
`+ phone compliance`  
`+ restored parental trust`  
`= God now accepts you`.

That is false.

Human trust may legitimately be rebuilt through visible faithfulness.

Divine justification is not a parental trust ladder.

### Strong line

> **Parents may need time before entrusting the phone again. Christ does not justify by a probation calendar.**

### Equal-and-opposite guard

Do not infer:

> `Because justification is free, the parent should immediately restore every privilege.`

That confuses salvation with stewardship.

# 3. 1 John 1:5–2:2 — light, confession, cleansing, and the Advocate belong together

## Literary sequence

1 John deliberately refuses two opposite errors.

### Error A — claiming fellowship while walking in darkness

A religious claim is contradicted by a settled dark walk.

This is directly relevant to the double-life theme.

### Error B — claiming sinlessness

John also says that claiming to have no sin is self-deception.

Therefore `walking in the light` cannot mean sinless perfection.

### Confession and cleansing

John connects confession with God's faithfulness/justice in forgiveness and cleansing.

### 2:1–2

Immediately after warning against sin, John points believers who sin to Jesus Christ the righteous as Advocate and to his propitiatory work.

Calvin on 1 John 2:1–2 emphasizes both sides:

- the gospel calls us away from sin;
- assurance rests in Christ's intercession/sacrifice rather than in pretending we never fall.

Primary commentary:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom45.v.iii.i.html

## Publication use

This should govern the emotional/theological exit from Part I.

The article should not end:

> `Sin deceives you, hardens you, and exposes your hypocrisy.`

It should move:

> `Come into the light; stop defending the darkness; do not claim sinlessness; confess; look to Christ the righteous Advocate.`

### Strong synthesis

> **The opposite of double life is not a performance of sinlessness. It is life in the light, where sin is no longer protected from truth and Christ—not secrecy—is the sinner's refuge.**

### Critical guard

1 John is written to a Christian readership and cannot be used as an automatic assurance formula for every unrepentant church child:

> `Say the words “I confess” and you are certainly regenerate.`

The text must be preached evangelistically and pastorally without turning verbal confession into a sacramental mechanism.

# 4. 1 Corinthians 6:9–11 — sexual sin can be real history without becoming permanent identity

## Context

Paul lists grave sins, including sexual sins, and warns that the unrighteous will not inherit God's kingdom.

Then comes the decisive gospel contrast:

> such were some of you.

Paul speaks of washing, sanctification, and justification in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of God.

## Project use

This text protects the series from two symmetrical lies.

### Lie A — minimization

> `Sexual sin is just normal experimentation, so the gospel need not name it seriously.`

Paul names grave sexual sin seriously.

### Lie B — permanent identity sentence

> `You did filthy things, therefore “filthy” is all you can ever be.`

Paul can speak of terrible former patterns with a real past tense:

> `such were some of you`.

### Strong line

> **The gospel is severe enough to name sexual sin as kingdom-threatening and powerful enough to say, “such were some of you.”**

### Guard

Do not use the verse to promise every exposed teenager immediate observable transformation.

The text describes God's saving action, not a timetable parents can manufacture.

# 5. Titus 2:11–14 — grace is neither indulgence nor mere pardon detached from holiness

Primary text:

- Titus 2:11–14.

Calvin:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43.v.iv.iii.html

## Direct structure

The grace of God appears bringing salvation and **teaching/training** God's people to:

- deny ungodliness;
- deny worldly desires;
- live soberly/righteously/godly;
- wait for Christ.

Christ gave himself:

- to redeem from lawlessness;
- to purify for himself a people;
- zealous for good works.

Calvin explicitly guards against using divine mercy as an occasion for licentiousness and connects manifested grace with holy life.

## Project consequence

A gospel-centered article cannot say:

> `Grace means God forgives, therefore the pornography / lies / corrupting speech are secondary.`

Nor can it say:

> `Holiness is what earns access to grace.`

The Pauline movement is:

> **saving grace itself trains renunciation and new life.**

### Strong line

> **Grace is not God's way of making peace with the protected double life. Grace is God's saving favor in Christ that teaches the redeemed person to say no to the life that once ruled him.**

# 6. 1689 LBCF 14–15 — faith and repentance both terminate in Christ, not self-reform

## Chapter 14.2 — Saving Faith

The principal acts of saving faith have immediate relation to Christ:

- accepting;
- receiving;
- resting on him alone

for justification, sanctification, and eternal life.

Primary:

- https://the1689.org/chapters/14

## Chapter 15.3 — Repentance unto Life and Salvation

Saving repentance is evangelical grace.

The sinner, made sensible of sin's evil by the Holy Spirit, **by faith in Christ** humbles himself with godly sorrow/detestation, seeks pardon and strength of grace, and purposes/endeavors by the Spirit's supply to walk before God.

Primary:

- https://the1689.org/chapters/15

## 15.5 — hope must remain genuinely open

The confession states that no sin is so small that it does not deserve damnation, yet no sin is so great that it shall bring damnation on those who repent; therefore repentance must be constantly preached.

### Project consequence

Do not preach repentance as:

> `Become morally presentable and then come back to Christ.`

The confession itself locates saving repentance **in faith in Christ**.

### Strong line

> **Christian repentance is not the journey from being too dirty for Christ to becoming clean enough for Christ. It is the Spirit-worked turning of a sinner who comes to Christ precisely because he cannot cleanse or justify himself.**

### Guard

Do not use 15.5 to pronounce a specific person's repentance genuine without fruit/discernment.

The promise of mercy does not remove pastoral evaluation of a profession.

# 7. Ezekiel 36:25–27 — parents cannot manufacture the new heart the article demands

## Redemptive-historical context

Ezekiel promises covenantal cleansing, a new heart, a new spirit, and God's Spirit causing obedience in the restoration/new-covenant trajectory.

## Project use

The series repeatedly says:

> external restraint is not regeneration.

Ezekiel gives positive theological content to that statement.

Parents can:

- teach;
- command;
- remove occasions;
- discipline;
- model;
- pray;
- bring the child under the Word;
- seek pastoral help.

They cannot:

- remove the heart of stone by technique;
- install the Spirit by surveillance;
- produce saving faith through sufficient strictness.

### Strong line

> **A parent can take the phone from the hand. Only God can give the new heart that will hate the darkness when no parent is watching.**

### Major guard

Do not use Ezekiel 36 as a mechanical promise that every child of Christian parents will necessarily be regenerated.

Respect the covenant/redemptive-historical setting.

# 8. The gospel does not cancel consequences

This distinction must appear explicitly in Part II and Part III.

Suppose the child truly repents and believes.

There may still be:

- lost device privileges;
- restitution;
- repaired relationships;
- time before broader trust;
- removal from choir/platform;
- church process if a professing member;
- safeguarding restrictions;
- civil/legal consequences where applicable.

Those consequences are not necessarily contradiction of grace.

They may serve:

- protection;
- truth;
- stewardship;
- repair;
- training;
- church order.

### But never say

> `You must suffer this long before God will forgive you.`

That converts a prudential consequence into penance.

### Strong distinction

> **Forgiveness answers moral debt before God and personal vengeance; trust answers present reliability; access answers stewardship and safety. Do not make one category do the work of the others.**

`130` deepens the trust/time/re-entrustment lane.

# 9. The gospel does not cancel the call to costly fruit

Matthew 3:8, Acts 26:20, Proverbs 28:13, Luke 19, and Titus 2 all prevent cheap verbalism.

A teenager who says:

> `Jesus forgives me`

while intentionally preserving:

- the hidden account;
- the porn source;
- the corrupting relationship;
- the lie;
- the victim's unrepaired harm;
- the doctrinal defense of sin,

must not be reassured as though invocation of grace itself proves repentance.

### Correct pastoral order

1. Christ alone justifies.
2. Repentance does not merit justification.
3. Saving faith is not dead.
4. Repentance bears fruit.
5. Fruit can be observed fallibly over time.
6. Human beings cannot infer election infallibly from a checklist.

### Strong line

> **Do not make fruit the root of acceptance with God; do not preach an acceptance with God that is content to remain fruitless.**

# 11. Part II mandatory gospel center

Part II deals with parents, consequences, discovery, boundaries, and repentance.

## Required order after discovery

1. establish truth/safety;
2. name the sin accurately;
3. explain guilt before God;
4. **preach Christ rather than merely negotiate compliance**;
5. distinguish sorrow from repentance;
6. remove known occasions proportionately;
7. require/encourage truthful fruit and restitution;
8. rebuild stewardship/trust prudently.

### P0 prohibition

Do not allow the parent's practical plan to become the functional gospel:

> filter + punishment + counseling + accountability = salvation.

All may be useful means/stewardship.

None is the new birth or justification.

### Parent-facing synthesis

> **Your job is not to become the Holy Spirit. Your job is also not to become passive. Teach, command, guard, discipline, pray, bring the Word, preach Christ, and remember that only God gives life.**

# 12. Part III mandatory gospel center

Part III deals with:

- church holiness;
- choir/platform;
- youth access;
- insiders/outsiders;
- discipline;
- restoration.

This part has special risk of sounding like ecclesial gatekeeping without gospel purpose.

## Required distinction

`PUBLIC WORD / GOSPEL HEARING`  
≠ `MEMBERSHIP / TABLE / REPRESENTATIVE ROLE / TRUSTED ACCESS`.

A child may lose:

- choir place;
- youth leadership;
- private access;
- platform;

without the church communicating:

> `Christ is closed to you until you improve.`

### Strong line

> **The church may close a platform while keeping the pulpit's gospel summons wide open.**

### Discipline purpose

For professing members, correction/discipline aims at:

- holiness;
- truth;
- protection;
- repentance;
- restoration,

not humiliation as an end in itself.

### Restoration guard

When repentance is credible, 2 Corinthians 2 / Galatians 6 prevent endless punitive identity.

But `restored fellowship` does not automatically mean `restored sensitive access`.

# 13. Adult-child companion mandatory gospel center

The adult-child lane correctly emphasizes changed jurisdiction, non-enabling, contact, and no manufactured suffering.

The gospel center adds:

- parents cannot engineer regeneration by controlling the far country;
- parents do not save by financing the far country either;
- hope rests in God's ability to save, not in the parent's perfect strategy;
- truthful admonition should point to Christ, not merely to coming home;
- physical return home is not identical to repentance;
- estrangement from parents and reconciliation to God are related pastoral realities but not synonyms.

### Strong line

> **The deepest hope is not merely that the child comes back to the house, but that the sinner comes to Christ.**

### Guard

Do not weaponize this to dismiss ordinary relational reconciliation as unimportant.

Family repair matters; it is simply not the gospel itself.

# 14. Shame control — conviction must not become an anti-gospel identity sentence

Existing modules `09`, `28`, `47`, `109`, and `130` already distinguish:

- guilt;
- shame;
- terror;
- repentance;
- fruit;
- trust.

This module adds the Christological control.

### Forbidden parent/pastor language

- `You are disgusting beyond hope.`
- `After what you did, Christ cannot receive you.`
- `You ruined yourself forever.`
- `You can never be clean.`
- `You must earn your way back to God.`

Those formulations deny or obscure the gospel.

### Equally forbidden reassurance

- `It was nothing.`
- `God forgives, so stop feeling bad.`
- `Everybody does it.`
- `If you prayed once, we know everything is spiritually fine.`

### Better theological structure

> `What you did is real sin.`  
> `You cannot erase it by excuses or punishment.`  
> `Christ is sufficient for sinners who come to him.`  
> `Repentance does not mean pretending the consequences disappear.`  
> `Do not hide; come into the light.`

# 15. Regenerate struggler vs unregenerate child — Christ is necessary in both cases, but pastoral claims differ

## Regenerate struggler / credible believer in grievous fall

Do not preach:

> `You fell, therefore Christ has certainly rejected you.`

1689 chs. 11, 15, 17 preserve:

- continuing forgiveness;
- renewed repentance;
- perseverance;
- real seriousness of grievous falls.

Use 1 John 2's Advocate text carefully and strongly.

## Unregenerate / no credible profession

Do not give covenant assurance he has not professed/possessed.

Say:

- you need Christ;
- you need the new birth;
- your Christian family cannot save you;
- your baptism/choir/church familiarity cannot substitute for faith;
- come to Christ rather than waiting to become morally presentable.

## Uncertain case

Do not force certainty from one crisis.

Preach both warning and promise.

### Strong line

> **Where the heart is uncertain to us, the gospel is not uncertain: the command is to repent and believe in Christ.**

# 16. Grace and means — reject fatalistic Calvinism

Sovereign grace does not imply:

> `If God wants to save him, nothing we do matters.`

1689 ch. 14 expressly connects saving faith ordinarily with the ministry of the Word and appointed means.

Parents/church should therefore:

- teach Scripture;
- pray;
- preach;
- admonish;
- model repentance;
- bring wise believers close;
- remove corrupting access;
- preserve gospel hearing;
- call for faith and repentance.

### Strong line

> **The God who ordains the end also ordains means; trusting sovereign grace is not an excuse to abandon the child to silence.**

### Guard

Means are not levers that force regeneration.

---

# 17. Grace and discipline — reject behavioristic moralism

The opposite error says:

> `If we build a tight enough system, we can make a holy child.`

No.

A system can:

- restrain;
- protect;
- expose patterns;
- reduce occasions;
- teach consequences;
- protect siblings/peers.

It cannot:

- justify;
- regenerate;
- create love for God;
- create the fear of God;
- unite a sinner to Christ.

### Strong line

> **A locked-down phone can be wise stewardship. It is a terrible substitute savior.**

# 19. Mandatory cross-part acceptance test

Before any manuscript leaves `draft: true / noindex: true`, answer all ten questions in the prose itself, not merely in Research notes:

1. **What does God call sin here?**
2. **Why is the child/person morally responsible?**
3. **What role do world, flesh, Satan, peers, and digital opportunity play without becoming excuses?**
4. **What can parents/church/means genuinely do?**
5. **What can only God do?**
6. **What has Christ done for sinners?**
7. **How is Christ received—what is saving faith?**
8. **What is repentance, and why is it not payment?**
9. **What fruit/consequences/trust rebuilding may follow without becoming a second gospel?**
10. **What real hope does the article leave both the sinner and the parent/church?**

If questions 6–8 are absent, the manuscript is **not theologically ready**, even if every behavioral recommendation is correct.

# 21. Publication-safe gospel spine

The entire series can be held together by the following theological movement:

> **The hidden life is serious because sin is first against God, not merely against family reputation. Exposure does not cleanse it. Punishment does not atone for it. Better habits cannot justify the sinner. God calls the sinner out of darkness to Christ, whose righteousness and redeeming death—not the sinner's reform record—are the ground of pardon. Saving grace does not leave the old life protected: it teaches renunciation, truth, holiness, restitution where possible, and visible fruit. Parents and churches therefore neither minimize sin nor pretend they can regenerate. They use God's appointed means, impose wise boundaries, protect others, distinguish forgiveness from trust, and keep pointing the sinner beyond restored privileges to Christ Himself.**

This is project synthesis, not a direct quotation.

# 22. Final verdict

**P0 finding:** the existing source corpus contains the necessary gospel texts and confessional doctrines, but the compression architecture needed one explicit cross-part control preventing diagnosis/discipline/trust-restoration material from functionally displacing Christ and justification.

**Locked corrections:**

- Christ, not behavior reform, is the ground of acceptance with God.
- repentance is evangelical grace exercised by faith in Christ, not penance.
- fruit is necessary evidence/category, not meritorious ground.
- saving grace trains holiness; it does not ratify a protected double life.
- consequences/trust/access remain distinct from forgiveness/justification.
- Part I must end with gospel light/Advocate hope, not hardening alone.
- Part II must preach Christ before turning repentance into a discernment grid.
- Part III may restrict platform/access while keeping gospel hearing open.
- adult-child hope must aim beyond return-home mechanics to reconciliation with God in Christ.

**No broad new research pass is warranted on this axis.**
