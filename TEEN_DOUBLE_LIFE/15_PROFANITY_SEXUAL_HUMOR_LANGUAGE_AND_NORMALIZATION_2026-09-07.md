## 1. Evidence status: здесь нужно быть осторожнее, чем в теме peer norms/sexting

По profanity specifically подростковая литература заметно слабее, чем по:

- peer norms;
- sexual behavior;
- social-media use;
- pornography;
- disclosure/secrecy.

Поэтому future article может уверенно обсуждать **язык как социальный и нравственный маркер**, но не должна придумывать сильные количественные causal claims о мате.

Главная исследовательская дисциплина:

> **не делать из мата магическую первопричину, но и не считать язык морально нейтральным.**

## 2. Media profanity correlates with profanity attitudes/use in adolescents

Pediatrics study N=223 middle-school adolescents found media profanity exposure positively associated with:

- attitudes/beliefs more accepting of profanity;
- self-reported profanity use;
- physical/relational aggression.

Attitudes toward profanity statistically mediated part of the association between media exposure and profanity/aggression outcomes.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22007012/

### Limitation

This is correlational/cross-sectional evidence and cannot prove that hearing profanity causes later moral deterioration.

Safe wording:

> **media profanity exposure, acceptance of profanity and adolescent profanity use cluster together, with attitudes appearing to be an important link.**

---

## 3. Content matters more than merely “screen time”

Longitudinal music study N=548 adolescents found:

- aggressive music content predicted higher aggression/lower prosocial behavior over one year;
- sexual content predicted earlier sexual intercourse initiation;
- effects were content-specific rather than simply amount-of-music effects.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25828552/

This supports a broader principle:

> **what enters repeatedly matters, not merely how many minutes a device is used.**

Again, this is not evidence that one explicit song determines conduct.

---

## 4. Humor can reduce perceived seriousness inside digital peer groups

The 2026 WhatsApp-group study already logged in module `05` found adolescents encountered violent, sexual-violence and hate content in group chats and qualitative analysis identified **humor** as a mechanism by which disturbing material could be treated as less serious/ordinary in peer interaction.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41825908/

### Relevance

A sexualized meme or obscene joke can perform several functions at once:

- entertainment;
- testing boundaries;
- signaling group membership;
- reducing embarrassment;
- making objection socially costly;
- converting moral concern into “ты просто душнила/ребёнок”.

Not every joke performs all these functions.

## 5. Sexual name-calling can be normalized as “just joking”

2023 focus-group study among Norwegian 13–15-year-olds examined peer sexual harassment.

Among boys, sexual name-calling could be framed as a joke, while girls experienced aspects of the behavior as intimidating; trivialization contributed to perceiving the behavior as normal.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37154062/

### Guard

Small qualitative sample; not prevalence evidence.

But it directly supports one important mechanism:

> **“это шутка” can function as moral downgrading of sexualized behavior.**

This is highly relevant to church/peer cultures where sexual speech is hidden under humor.

## 6. Sexual harassment rises across early adolescence in longitudinal data

Longitudinal study followed 242 youth at grades 5, 7 and 9 and found sexual-harassment victimization increased across this developmental period, with patterns related to puberty, peer network and power.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19250663/

Earlier developmental study N=1,213 grades 6–8 likewise found peer-to-peer sexual harassment during early adolescence and links with puberty/mixed-gender peer networks.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11893096/

### Why include this in language module

Sexual culture does not begin only when intercourse begins.

It can develop through:

- sexual labeling;
- jokes;
- comments about bodies;
- humiliation;
- rumors;
- harassment;
- status talk.

A family that asks only «занимался ли ты сексом?» may miss much of the formative sexual environment.

## 7. Language can be a boundary marker between two identities

Pastoral/research synthesis:

### Public/family register

- respectful speech;
- church vocabulary;
- controlled affect;
- no explicit sexual references.

### Hidden peer register

- obscenity;
- sexual slang;
- ironic blasphemy;
- sexual objectification;
- ridicule;
- cynical humor.

The linguistic switch itself may become a marker:

> **“Now I am with my real people; adult rules no longer apply.”**

Direct psycholinguistic studies specifically on Christian adolescent double-register are lacking.

Therefore this should be presented as a qualitative/pastoral construct, supported indirectly by code-switching/group-identity theory and peer-norm evidence, not as a measured universal phenomenon.

## 8. Why obscenity can feel “adult”

Possible social functions:

- breaking childhood taboos;
- signaling independence from parents;
- displaying familiarity with adult/sexual content;
- showing toughness;
- proving lack of religious naivety;
- making oneself less vulnerable to ridicule.

These motives align with peer-status/masculinity material in module `11`, but targeted profanity-status studies in adolescents remain a gap.

Do not overstate.

## 9. Biblical speech ethics is much broader than a banned-word list

Relevant texts:

- Ephesians 4:29 — corrupting speech vs edification;
- Ephesians 5:3–4 — sexual immorality/impurity alongside filthiness, foolish talk and crude joking;
- Colossians 3:8 — anger, malice, slander, obscene talk;
- James 3:1–12 — tongue reveals moral inconsistency;
- Matthew 12:34–37 — speech and heart;
- Proverbs 10:19; 12:18; 15:1–4.

### Especially Ephesians 5:4

This text is unusually relevant because it places **crude joking/filthy speech** beside a larger sexual-moral context.

Future exegesis must determine exact lexical range and avoid equating every earthy joke with the verse.

But the passage clearly prevents the Christian article from reducing purity to physical sexual acts while treating speech as irrelevant.

## 10. “Words do not matter” is not a Christian option

The article should avoid two errors.

### Error A — lexical legalism

> holiness = never uttering a list of forbidden syllables.

This ignores context, meaning, intent and broader heart/speech ethics.

### Error B — linguistic neutrality

> words are arbitrary sounds, therefore obscene speech has no formative/moral significance.

This conflicts with Scripture's repeated treatment of speech as morally revealing and socially powerful.

The better focus is:

- what does this speech express?
- what does it celebrate?
- whom does it degrade?
- what behavior does it normalize?
- what group identity does it signal?

## 11. Crude humor can become moral camouflage

A recurring pattern worth exploring:

`sexual idea → joke format → laughter → objection becomes socially awkward → repeated joke → seriousness fades → more explicit material becomes possible`.

This sequence is not universally experimentally established.

But components are supported by:

- peer reinforcement;
- WhatsApp humor-normalization qualitative evidence;
- sexual harassment “joking” evidence;
- social norm literature.

Future article can present it explicitly as **a mechanism**, not a quantified law.

---

## 12. Parents can make a mistake by policing vocabulary but missing worldview

Example:

A teenager stops swearing around parents but continues to:

- objectify girls/boys;
- celebrate promiscuity;
- exchange explicit content;
- mock chastity;
- lie;
- maintain sexualized peer identity.

External vocabulary improved; moral structure did not.

Conversely, an isolated ugly word should not automatically be treated as proof of a whole secret sexual life.

### Practical research principle

> **Speech is evidence, not an infallible diagnosis.**

---

## 13. The hidden-language question parents should ask

Not merely:

> «Ты материшься?»

but:

- how do your friends talk about girls/boys?
- what jokes get the biggest reaction?
- what would make someone look childish?
- what words do you use in chat that you would never use here?
- what kind of sexual content gets forwarded as humor?
- who gets humiliated?
- would you be embarrassed if the whole chat were read aloud — and why?

These are pastoral prompts, not validated questionnaire items.

## 14. Active media conversation may be more formative than restriction alone

Three-wave study N=681 adolescents found patterns suggesting active parental media monitoring was more adaptively associated with prosocial/self-regulatory pathways than restrictive monitoring alone, though findings varied by reporter.

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26641307/

This supports the broader dossier principle:

> parents need to interpret content with children, not merely count prohibited words or minutes.

1. Adolescent media profanity exposure has been associated with more accepting profanity attitudes and profanity use, though causal evidence is limited.
2. Media content — including aggressive/sexual music content — has shown longitudinal associations with specific adolescent outcomes.
3. Qualitative digital-peer research indicates humor can lower perceived seriousness of harmful sexual/violent content.
4. Peer sexual harassment may be trivialized as joking, contributing to normalization.
5. Sexualized peer culture includes speech, labeling and humor, not only sexual acts.

## 18. Research conclusion

The strongest future claim is not:

> «мат развращает подростка».

It is more precise:

> **Language is one of the places where a group's moral world becomes audible. Obscenity and sexual humor can signal belonging, normalize sexualized categories and turn serious things into socially protected jokes — but speech must be interpreted as part of the larger peer, media and heart system, not as an isolated diagnostic test.**
