# Церковь, упорный грех и границы общения: attendance ≠ membership ≠ Table

**Статус:** RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Дата:** 2026-09-07

---

## 1. Главная коррекция

Фраза:
> «Осознанным грешникам не место в церкви»

слишком неточна и может стать небиблейской.

Нужно различать как минимум пять категорий:

1. **public attendance / hearing the Word**;
2. **recognized membership**;
3. **fellowship as a professing brother/sister in good standing**;
4. **Lord’s Supper**;
5. **teaching/leadership/service roles with qualification requirements**.

New Testament treats these differently.

---

## 2. 1 Corinthians 5 — primary disciplinary case

Paul addresses a man whose sexual immorality is scandalous and tolerated in the church.

1 Cor 5:9–13 clarifies:

- church is **not** commanded to withdraw from immoral outsiders in general;
- discipline targets one who **bears the name of brother** while persisting in listed scandalous sins;
- church must judge those inside;
- offender is to be removed from recognized community standing.

Text:
https://www.esv.org/verses/1%2BCorinthians%2B5%3A9%E2%80%9313/

Academic/theological study of Paul’s Deuteronomic exclusion language:
https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/exclusion-from-the-people-of-god-an-examination-of-pauls-use-of-the-old-testament-in-1-corinthians-5/

## 3. Excommunication is not “you may never enter the room”

1 Cor 14:23–25 explicitly imagines unbelievers entering the assembled church and being confronted/convicted by intelligible speech.

Commentary orientation:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_corinthians/14-23.htm

### Therefore
A disciplined person treated as outsider is not automatically barred from ever attending public preaching.

The church can say:

> «Мы no longer affirm your profession as credible membership while you persist without repentance. You are not in good standing and should not approach the Table as though nothing is wrong. But we still want you under the preached Word and call you to repent.»

This is category clarity, not leniency.

---

## 4. The Lord’s Supper is the sharpest point of contradiction

1 Cor 11:27–32 warns of eating/drinking in an unworthy manner and divine discipline/judgment.

Calvin explicitly discusses open fornicator/drunkard/etc. approaching without repentance as a grave profanation.

Source:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/1_corinthians/11.htm

### Guard
The table is not for morally flawless people.

There is a difference between:

- believer fighting/repenting of sin;
- believer who fell grievously and seeks restoration;
- person openly defending/continuing grave sin while demanding recognition as in good standing.

Do not weaponize “unworthy” against tender consciences.

---

## 5. Matthew 18 — goal is gaining/restoration

Matt 18:15–20 gives escalating confrontation when brother refuses to hear.

Reformed orientation:
https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/discipline-of-the-church

The endpoint is serious, but the stated goal begins with:
> gain your brother.

Discipline is not revenge, theatrical purity or social execution.

Restoration after repentance matters (2 Cor 2:5–11).

---

## 6. 2 Thessalonians 3 — boundaries without enemy-hatred

Paul commands withdrawal from persistently disorderly professing brother, but then:
> do not regard him as an enemy; warn him as a brother.

Commentary:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_thessalonians/3.htm

This is extremely valuable for our series:

> **Church discipline can become relationally colder without becoming hateful; it can remove false reassurance while preserving the desire for restoration.**

---

## 7. What counts as “living in sin” for discipline purposes?

Need avoid two opposite errors.

### Error A — any recurrence = unrepentant life
A believer may fall repeatedly, struggle, confess, seek help and still need severe pastoral intervention. Repetition alone does not automatically prove settled impenitence.

### Error B — verbal apology = repentance regardless of conduct
A person may say “sorry” only to avoid consequences while:

- continuing same behavior;
- hiding channels;
- defending the sin;
- refusing reasonable boundaries;
- recruiting others;
- blaming church for naming it sin.

### Indicators needing assessment
- doctrinal defense of sin;
- concealment/deception;
- refusal to cease known behavior;
- refusal of correction;
- absence/presence of concrete fruit;
- willingness to make restitution/protect harmed persons;
- willingness to accept consequences;
- trajectory over time.

No single checklist infallibly reads regeneration.

## 8. Minor child who attends church with parents

Church discipline categories become complicated if child is not a baptized/recognized member.

If an unbelieving minor is not a professing member, 1 Cor 5 membership discipline may not map directly. Yet church/parents may still:

- teach;
- restrict youth activities for safety;
- prevent predatory/harassing behavior;
- involve safeguarding;
- require supervision;
- refuse ministry/public roles;
- call to repentance.

### Guard
Do not pretend an unbaptized unbelieving teen can be “excommunicated” in exactly the same sense as a covenanted member.

---

## 9. Professed Christian teen/member

If baptized/professing member persists in grave unrepentant sexual immorality, deception or abuse, church discipline can become directly relevant depending on local polity.

The aim:

- protect church holiness;
- protect victims/others;
- remove false assurance;
- call sinner to repentance;
- preserve integrity of Table/membership.

---

## 10. Safeguarding is not replaced by Matthew 18

If disclosure involves:

- sexual abuse;
- exploitation;
- illegal images;
- coercion;
- extortion;
- violence;
- danger to minors,

church must not hide matter behind “private church discipline.” Reporting/protection duties may apply and must be verified by jurisdiction.

Spiritual discipline and safeguarding can run simultaneously.

---

## 11. OT “purge the evil” and NT discipline

Paul’s use of Deuteronomic purge language in 1 Cor 5 shows covenant continuity in **community holiness**, but transformation in sanction:

Old Covenant Israel:
- civil/covenantal judicial sanctions in national polity.

New Covenant church:
- ecclesial exclusion / loss of recognized fellowship, not execution.

1689 19.4 confirms Israel judicial law expired with that polity; general equity remains.

This is the correct place for the “stoning parallel”: **seriousness and covenant exclusion**, not imitation of penalty.

## 12. Future article formulations

### Strong
> Церковь — место для грешников, которые нуждаются в Христе. Но она не имеет права превращать упорное нераскаянное зло в ещё один допустимый стиль христианской жизни.

### Stronger distinction
> Отлучение не говорит: «тебе больше нельзя слышать Евангелие». Оно говорит: «пока ты упорно защищаешь эту жизнь, церковь не будет подтверждать твоё заявление, будто между тобой и Христом всё в порядке».

### Table warning
> Самая опасная пастырская мягкость — помочь человеку продолжать тяжёлый грех и одновременно дать ему церковными знаками почувствовать, будто Бог этот мир уже одобрил.

All are theological/pastoral formulations requiring local polity nuance.
