## 2. Module 46 — Owen, *Of Temptation*

Key gains:

- temptation includes state/condition/company/opportunity, not only final sinful solicitation;
- distinguish being tempted from **entering into temptation**;
- Christ’s `watch and pray` is prevention-oriented;
- believer must know peculiar heart/temper/lust/weakness;
- avoid occasions fitted to that particular corruption;
- young people may first love a bad **company** and later grow familiar with its sins;
- privacy/opportunity can overthrow strong public resolutions;
- different children can therefore reasonably need different boundaries.

Key project line:

> **Equal parental love does not require identical exposure.**

Modern application is inference; never claim Owen taught smartphone policy.

## 3. Module 47 — Watson, true/counterfeit repentance

Counterfeits emphasized:

- tears/natural softness;
- legal terror/fear of punishment;
- short-lived sorrow under crisis;
- good motions/intentions;
- vows/resolutions;
- leaving one gross sin while preserving another.

True-repentance direction:

- changed judgment of sin;
- changed affection toward sin;
- changed life;
- turning from sin **and toward God**;
- free/open confession rather than only forced admission;
- continuing fruit.

Key project distinction:

> **Being sorry that sin became expensive is not yet the same as being sorry that sin is sin.**

Guard:

- relapse does not automatically prove no repentance;
- parents/pastors cannot infallibly read regeneration through a checklist.

## 4. Module 48 — Baxter, *Christian Directory*

This became a major primary source for the child/peer/parent lane.

Baxter explicitly:

- acknowledges childish incapacity/passions/weak reason;
- nevertheless speaks directly to children/youth as responsible before God;
- calls duty to God their greatest duty;
- tells parents to exercise government/education, not wait passively;
- tells parents to watch tongues especially against lying, railing, ribald/filthy talk and profanity;
- tells parents not to make light of such speech;
- treats corrupt playfellows/company as a major danger;
- describes peer boasting in filthy/profane speech and mocking children who refuse to conform;
- tells youth themselves to avoid companions who entice them into filthy speech/sin;
- tells fallen people not to cover serious sin with excuses but confess/repent;
- treats refusal to abstain from bait/occasion/temptation as spiritually serious.

Historically crucial:

> profanity, filthy peer speech, boasting and peer mockery long predate digital media.

Modern difference:

> digital technology can keep that peer company continuously present.

Do not import Baxter’s paedobaptist covenant claims as controlling Baptist ecclesiology.

## 6. Module 50 — children’s choir/youth ministry policy matrix

Key ladder:

`public preaching`  
`→ congregational singing`  
`→ educational/catechetical singing`  
`→ youth/small-group access`  
`→ formal choir/public platform`  
`→ helper/leadership`  
`→ unsupervised access to younger/vulnerable children`.

Threshold may rise as:

- representation increases;
- trust increases;
- intimacy increases;
- safeguarding risk increases.

Major principle:

> **Do not close the door to the Word; do not open every door to influence.**

No explicit NT children’s choir admission code exists.

Therefore:

- formal choir standards are strong pastoral/ecclesial inference;
- not divine law verbatim;
- serious known corruption can justify temporary ministry restriction;
- ordinary childish sin must not create perfectionistic automatic suspension;
- restoration must remain possible;
- forgiveness ≠ automatic restored sensitive access.

3. sin minimized;

5. God portrayed as mercy detached from holiness;

and

## 8. Module 52 — Charnock + Flavel

Charnock’s crucial distinction:

> outward reformation may be **of excellent use** while remaining insufficient for regeneration.

This is one of the project’s most valuable anti-false-dichotomy controls.

Therefore:

`restraint ≠ regeneration`  
**but**  
`restraint is not useless`.

A parent can truthfully say:

> “Taking the device will not purify your heart; I will still not knowingly supply the avenue through which you are choosing impurity.”

Charnock also insists regeneration is inward but necessarily tends to outward holiness.

Flavel reinforces:

- parental delegated authority/accountability;
- child reverence/obedience;
- personal guilt not erased by parental failure.

## 9. Module 53 — Spurgeon, children / membership / conversion

Spurgeon provides especially useful Baptist controls:

- no hereditary profession/religion;
- children of godly parents need Holy Spirit/new birth personally;
- church membership of child should not receive a lower saving standard than adult membership;
- same real repentance and faith **in kind**;
- do not mistake child terror/emotion for conversion;
- do not require adult maturity/demeanor;
- do not postpone seeking child conversion until adulthood.

Core synthesis:

> **No inherited regeneration; no delayed gospel; no childish substitute for repentance; no demand for adult maturity.**

Choir application remains inference because Spurgeon’s direct statement is membership, not choir policy.

> **Childhood changes capacity and pastoral method; it does not suspend God’s moral claim.**

A child may simultaneously be:

- immature;
- unregenerate;
- dependent;
- emotionally volatile;

and still genuinely obligated:

- to obey lawful parental authority;
- speak truth;
- reject sexual corruption;
- receive correction;
- fear God;
- repent and believe.

## 11. Strongest newly consolidated thesis on choir/ministry

> **Church ministry is not a prize for perfect children, but neither should it become camouflage for known rebellion.**

Future article must carefully distinguish:

- a weak/struggling child who receives correction;
- an unconverted but teachable child;
- a child in serious hidden sin who has been exposed and is being shepherded;
- a settled rebel who refuses correction;
- a corrupting child who recruits others;
- a victimized child whose behavior requires safety/help rather than moral blame for abuse.

Do not use one policy for all six.
