# 1. Richard Baxter — parents must actively restrain, instruct and catechize children

## Source A — *The Saints’ Everlasting Rest*

Baxter presses those entrusted with children/servants to teach diligently, command households after God, and bring children up in the Lord’s nurture/admonition.

He explicitly condemns parental neglect where children:

- do not know God/law;
- take God’s name in vain;
- slight worship;
- remain uncorrected and uninstructed.

He treats excessive parental tenderness that refuses correction as spiritually dangerous, not loving.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/baxter/saints_rest.iii.IX.html

### Safe use

> Parents are not faithful merely because they pray for conversion. They are commanded to teach, restrain, correct and order the household under God while seeking conversion.

### Guard

Baxter sometimes uses very severe rhetorical warnings characteristic of his era. Quote/paraphrase proportionally; do not import every seventeenth-century disciplinary assumption into modern practice.

---

# 2. Baxter — church discipline displays Christ’s holiness and protects the congregation

## Source B — *The Reformed Pastor*

Baxter argues that discipline:

- shames sin;
- humbles the sinner;
- manifests the holiness of Christ, doctrine and church;
- deters others;
- helps keep congregation/worship pure.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor/pastor.iii.i.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor.all.html

This is highly relevant to the user’s concern:

> when everyone knows serious rebellion exists, but leaders continue giving normal public/church privileges with no admonition, the community may learn that holiness preached is not holiness enforced.

### Important ecclesiological guard

Baxter’s parish model is not identical to a 1689 Baptist regenerate-membership model.

Do not use Baxter to erase:

- outsider/member distinction;
- baptism/membership distinction;
- church censure vs parental discipline distinction.

Use the narrower principle:

> **pastoral silence toward known scandal undermines the visible seriousness of holiness.**

---

# 3. Baxter — personal dealing is part of pastoral ministry

Baxter’s emphasis on personal catechizing/instruction is a direct corrective to a passive church strategy:

> “Keep him in activities and maybe someday a sermon will work.”

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor/pastor.iv.ii.html

He insists on:

- personal instruction;
- examination;
- reproof;
- patient teaching;
- discipline when necessary.

### Application

When a youth is known for pornography distribution, sexual corruption, profane speech, manipulative dating behavior or systematic deception, merely leaving him in youth programs without direct pastoral contact is not a serious shepherding model.

---

# 4. Baxter — children must be treated as children, not miniature mature adults

In *The Reformed Pastor*, Baxter also warns that children should not be dealt with as people of full maturity; foundational instruction comes before advanced expectations.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor/pastor.iii.ii.html

This is useful against an overreaction.

### Correct balance

`same moral Lord`  
`same need for repentance/faith`  
`different maturity/capacity`.

Therefore:

> equal accountability before God does not mean identical expectations of prudence, knowledge, emotional steadiness or leadership maturity.

---

# 5. John Owen — mortification is not mere temporary suppression after exposure

Owen’s *Mortification of Sin* is highly relevant after discovery.

He warns that a serious eruption can awaken:

- terror;
- shame;
- fear of scandal;
- temporary hatred of sin;
- intense resolutions.

Yet sin may simply hide until pressure passes.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/mort/mort.i.viii.html

### Future-article implication

> **The week after discovery is a poor time to declare victory merely because the child cries, deletes apps and behaves perfectly.**

The pastoral question is whether there is continuing war against sin when the immediate shock is gone.

---

# 6. Owen — flee occasions, not merely the final act

Owen explicitly instructs believers to identify:

- ways;
- companies;
- opportunities;
- conditions

that give advantage to a particular lust and set themselves against them.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/mort/mort.i.xiv.html

His famous practical point:

> one who dares to dally with occasions of sin will dare to sin.

### Application to modern adolescent temptation

Modern “occasion” may include:

- private late-night phone;
- hidden browser;
- explicit-content feed;
- secret account;
- sexually corrupt peer chat;
- specific relationship;
- unsupervised high-risk context.

This provides a stronger biblical-counseling foundation for radical boundaries than generic neuroscience.

---

# 7. Owen — real mortification is Spirit-enabled

Owen’s *Dominion of Sin and Grace* emphasizes Romans 8:13:

> mortification is through the Spirit.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/sin_grace.i.x.html

### Guard

Do not reduce biblical victory to:

- willpower;
- friction design;
- parental filters;
- accountability software;
- “better dopamine.”

Those can be means/conditions, but mortification in a believer is a work of the Spirit through faith and ordained means.

# 8. Thomas Watson — outward obedience can look impressive while lacking faith

Watson’s *Body of Divinity* distinguishes true evangelical obedience by:

- right rule;
- right principle (faith);
- right end (God’s glory);
- breadth/uniformity;
- constancy.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity.ix.iv.html

He specifically notes that external obedience from an unregenerate person may look impressive but lacks the faith-principle by which Christian obedience is acceptable.

### Relevance to children’s choir

Visible church participation cannot itself certify spiritual life.

The child can:

- sing correctly;
- memorize accurately;
- behave during rehearsal;
- appear devout publicly;

while remaining unregenerate or hypocritical.

This does **not** mean the act should never be required/taught; it means adults must not confuse outward religious competence with grace.

---

# 9. Watson — godly parentage does not produce adoption

Watson explicitly gives biblical examples:

- Abraham / Ishmael;
- Isaac / Esau.

He says God’s children are not such merely by natural birth from godly parents, but by adoption/grace.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity/divinity.ix.iv.html

### Use

This directly guards ДВР material from covenant-family presumption.

---

# 10. Watson — acceptable worship requires spiritual principle

In *The Lord’s Prayer*, Watson argues that acceptable duty is spiritual and comes from a renewed principle; unregenerate people may pray/hear externally while lacking the inward root.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/prayer.vi.html

Again the correct conclusion is not:

> “unregenerate people should never pray/hear.”

Use 1689 16.7/22.3 to prevent that error.

The conclusion is:

> **outward worship is not a substitute for regeneration and repentance.**

---

# 11. John Flavel — children owe real obedience/reverence and remain personally accountable

Flavel discusses duties of children to parents in strong moral terms:

- reverence;
- obedience;
- faithfulness;
- accountability before God.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/fountain.v.xxxi.html

He also warns children not to use their parents’ bad example as a sanctuary from God’s judgment; they possess reason/conscience and personal responsibility.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/pneum/pneum.x.html

### Useful thesis

> **Neither godly parents save a child by inheritance nor ungodly parents remove the child’s personal responsibility before God.**

This reinforces the project’s sovereignty/responsibility balance.

---

# 12. Stephen Charnock — outward reformation is useful but not regeneration

Charnock makes a highly useful distinction:

- outward reformation can be of excellent use;
- it changes appearance/conduct;
- it does not create the new creature;
- restraint can remove opportunity without changing inward inclination.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nec_regen.ii.html

This may be one of the best historical formulations for parenting boundaries.

### Strong synthesis

> **Cutting off opportunity may restrain a lion; it does not change the lion’s nature. Yet Scripture never concludes from this that restraints are useless. Restraint and regeneration answer different questions.**

Use cautiously because Charnock’s analogy is rhetorical, not a direct parenting command.

---

# 13. Charnock — regeneration necessarily tends toward holiness

Charnock’s discourse on regeneration emphasizes that new birth produces a principle of holiness and escape from worldly corruption through lust.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nat_regen/nat_regen.i.html

### Use

This helps distinguish:

`profession + settled peace with sin` from `credible evidence of new life`.

### Guard

Do not demand sinless perfection. Regeneration creates warfare/new direction, not immediate maturity.

---

# 14. Spurgeon — same repentance and faith in child and adult, not same maturity

Spurgeon’s “Do Not Sin Against the Child” is a primary control for the child-conversion issue.

Source:

- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child

He explicitly warns against:

- delaying concern for child conversion;
- mistaking transient emotion for repentance;
- lowering the essence of repentance/faith for children;
- demanding unnecessary adult solemnity/maturity.

### Key distinction

> **same gospel realities, age-appropriate evidences.**

# 15. Spurgeon — no inherited church Christianity

Sources:

- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/a-pastoral-visit
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/sermons10.xviii.html

Spurgeon rejects automatic church identity by birth and insists children need the Spirit/new birth personally.

### Relevance

A children’s ministry that quietly communicates “you belong spiritually because your parents belong” risks cultivating false assurance.

# 16. Calvin on Psalm 50 — religious speech cannot shelter impure life

Calvin identifies Psalm 50:16ff as an attack on those whose religion lies in outward ceremonies while they seek to cover impurity of life.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom09/calcom09.xvi.iv.html

Textual sequence:

`God’s statutes/covenant in mouth`  
`+ hatred of correction`  
`+ participation in theft/adultery`  
`+ evil/deceitful speech`.

This is exceptionally relevant to deliberate double life.

---

# 17. Calvin on Amos 5 — songs do not pacify God when life/worship are corrupted

Calvin emphasizes hypocrisy and corrupt worship, especially the attempt to satisfy God by ceremonies while refusing true obedience/godliness.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/study/Amos_5%3A18-24

### Guard

Amos’s historical context is covenant Israel, with specific worship corruption and social injustice. Do not turn Amos 5 into a one-verse direct rule:

> “A sinful teenager must not physically sing.”

Use the covenant-worship principle:

> **ritual/song does not compensate for unrepentant rebellion.**

---

# 18. Calvin on Ephesians 6 — children are under God-grounded parental authority

Calvin emphasizes:

- parental authority derives from God;
- children owe real obedience;
- obedience is limited by superior obedience to God.

Sources:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom41/calcom41.vi.vi.html
- https://www.ccel.org/c/calvin/comment2/eph.htm

This is directly relevant to claims that parental requirements are illegitimate unless the child is regenerate.

# 19. 1689 worship + good works + law + church = crucial four-chapter system

## Chapter 16 — Good Works

- only God-commanded works are good;
- regenerate works are accepted in Christ;
- unregenerate works lack faith/right end and cannot please God;
- neglect is nevertheless more sinful.

## Chapter 19 — Law

- moral law binds all;
- law remains useful to believers and others;
- gospel strengthens rather than dissolves moral obligation.

## Chapter 22 — Worship

- God is to be feared/loved/praised/served with whole being;
- acceptable worship is prescribed by God;
- prayer required of all;
- singing is worship and is to be offered with understanding, faith, reverence and godly fear.

## Chapter 26 — Church

- visible churches constituted of visible saints/professors whose life does not destroy profession;
- church privileges are joined to government/censure;
- hearing/attendance must remain distinct from formal membership/privileges.

Sources:

- https://the1689.org/chapters/16
- https://the1689.org/chapters/19
- https://the1689.org/chapters/22
- https://the1689.org/chapters/26

### Central synthesis

> **The confession does not create a moral waiting room for the unregenerate. It distinguishes duty from saving acceptability, restraint from regeneration, public access to means from church privileges, and profession from credible holy walking.**

# 21. Modern conservative warning: preaching holiness without enforcing it creates hypocrisy

MacArthur/GTY on church discipline argues that churches lose integrity when holiness is denounced in preaching but known sin is never addressed in congregational life.

Source:

- https://www.gty.org/sermons/2330/the-discipline-of-gods-children-part-1

This is not Puritan primary authority, but it directly echoes Baxter’s pastoral principle.

### Use

Helpful modern bridge:

> **if a church says sin matters from the pulpit but behaves as though it does not matter in participation/discipline, young people learn the practical message, not merely the verbal one.**

# 22. Children’s worship/ministry — source synthesis

The sources collectively support:

### Directly

- children are morally commanded;
- parents must instruct/discipline;
- unregenerate people remain under God’s law;
- outward worship does not cover rebellion;
- true church membership/profession should not be destroyed by unholiness of life;
- pastors must address known sin rather than simply wait passively.

### Strong pastoral inference

- a church can require behavioral/spiritual standards for visible children’s ministry roles;
- knowingly retaining a persistently corrupting child in choir/platform without admonition can teach religious hypocrisy;
- temporary removal from visible ministry can function as truthful correction without closing access to preaching/catechesis.

### Not directly established

- a universal biblical rule that every children’s choir member must already be regenerate;
- a universal age for ministry eligibility;
- a rule that every serious child sin requires removal from every church activity;
- an inspired “choir discipline process.”

# 24. Publication voice recommendation

Puritan material should not be used merely to make prose archaic or severe.

Use it because the older writers often retain categories modern literature blurs:

- guilt vs circumstance;
- desire vs act;
- conviction vs repentance;
- suppression vs mortification;
- restraint vs regeneration;
- worship vs ceremony;
- membership vs mere presence;
- discipline vs abandonment;
- parental tenderness vs indulgence.

The article should sound modern Russian but reason in these categories.
