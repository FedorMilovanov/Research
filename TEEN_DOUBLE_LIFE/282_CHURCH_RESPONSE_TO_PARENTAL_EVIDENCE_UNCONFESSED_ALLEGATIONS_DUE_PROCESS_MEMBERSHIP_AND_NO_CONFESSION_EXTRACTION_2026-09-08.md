## 0. Executive verdict

The project must reject two opposite procedural errors.

### Error A — confession-only epistemology

> `Unless the teenager personally admits it, the church cannot know or act on anything.`

That is too strong.

Scripture contains church action based on:

- credible report;
- witnesses;
- public/notorious conduct;
- examination of matters,

not only voluntary self-confession.

### Error B — parent-accusation infallibility

> `The parents say it happened, therefore the church should treat every allegation as established and pressure the child until he confesses.`

That is also too strong.

Parents can be:

- truthful and well-informed;
- mistaken;
- drawing an inference beyond evidence;
- emotionally injured and overconfident;
- themselves unsafe/abusive in exceptional cases.

### Locked center

> **SELF-CONFESSION ≠ ONLY POSSIBLE EVIDENCE.**

> **PARENTAL REPORT ≠ AUTOMATIC ECCLESIAL VERDICT.**

> **LOW CREDIBILITY ≠ PROOF OF EVERY ALLEGATION.**

> **CHURCH DISCIPLINE REQUIRES ACTUAL CHURCH JURISDICTION AND SUFFICIENTLY ESTABLISHED FACTS.**

> **PASTORAL ADMONITION ≠ CONFESSION EXTRACTION.**

> **SAFEGUARDING ACTION MAY BE REQUIRED BEFORE OR WITHOUT AN OFFENDER'S CONFESSION.**

# 1. 1 Corinthians 5 shows the church can act on established report, not only self-confession

Paul begins:

> `It is actually reported...`

The Corinthian sexual scandal is treated as notorious and church-cognizable.

Gill explicitly reads the offense as:

- publicly known;
- commonly talked of;
- committed by one inside the church;
- therefore within the church's cognizance.

Source:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/1_corinthians/5.htm

### P0

The offender's personal confession is not presented as the epistemic prerequisite for Paul's/church's recognition of the scandal.

### Guard

This is a notorious member case with apostolic involvement. Do not transfer it into:

> `one rumor about one teenager is enough for excommunication`.

### Safe conclusion

> **Church knowledge can arise from credible evidence/report apart from voluntary confession.**

Evidence class: `DIRECT_SCRIPTURE + CLASSICAL_EXEGESIS_STRONG`.

---

# 2. Matthew 18 values witnesses and graduated process

Matthew 18:15–17 does not reduce truth to:

> `the accused admits it`.

The sequence introduces one or two others so that a matter may be established in a serious communal process.

The wording draws on the scriptural witness principle.

### Safe transfer

Where a private interpersonal offense is disputed, church procedure should care about:

- what is actually alleged;
- what can be established;
- who has direct knowledge;
- whether the dispute is private/public;
- whether further church action is warranted.

### Guard

Matthew 18 is not a universal forensic manual for abuse/crime.

Use `24` and `242`.

Evidence class: `DIRECT_SCRIPTURE → CANONICAL_SYNTHESIS`.

---

# 3. 1689 chapter 26 — real censures require real church membership/jurisdiction

1689 26.12 states that those admitted to church privileges are under church censures and government according to Christ's rule.

Sources:
- https://baptistconfession.org/
- https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-26

### Consequence

For a **baptized minor member**:

- church discipline is a real category;
- parental punishment does not replace it;
- minority does not make membership fictitious.

For a **nonmember youth**:

- church can still govern its meetings, roles, youth access, safety and public witness;
- but it should not theatrically imitate excommunication of someone never admitted as a member.

### Locked

`MEMBER CENSURE ≠ NONMEMBER ADMINISTRATIVE RESTRICTION`.

`ROLE REMOVAL ≠ EXCOMMUNICATION`.

Use `154`, `159`.

# 4. Four different church information states

## State A — teenager voluntarily confesses material facts

This may substantially simplify pastoral assessment.

Still ask:

- is anyone at risk?
- is there another harmed person?
- is the youth a member?
- is the confession materially sufficient?
- is safeguarding/civil jurisdiction triggered?

`CONFESSION ≠ AUTOMATIC CASE CLOSURE`.

## State B — parents provide direct documentary evidence; child denies

Examples:

- messages;
- account ownership evidence;
- public posts;
- transaction/property evidence;
- reliable logs.

Church may consider the evidence without requiring a confession to make the evidence real.

But leaders should verify provenance/context enough for the decision being contemplated.

## State C — parents report what they personally observed/heard; child denies

This is evidence, but the church must still distinguish:

- direct observation;
- inference;
- hearsay;
- interpretation of tone/motive.

## State D — parents strongly suspect but lack concrete support

The church may:

- pastorally listen;
- encourage truth;
- support prudent family boundaries;
- watch actual church conduct.

It should not convert suspicion into an ecclesial factual finding merely because the teenager lied before.

---

# 5. Evidence should be proportional to the action contemplated

A single standard for every action is unwise.

### Low-level pastoral conversation

Can begin from:

- concern;
- credible report;
- obvious relational rupture.

### Temporary role pause / youth access restriction

May sometimes be justified by:

- meaningful unresolved risk;
- known deception;
- pending safeguarding review,

without a final ecclesial guilt adjudication.

### Formal membership censure / public discipline

Requires much stronger factual and procedural grounding.

### Safeguarding emergency action

May need to happen quickly on credible risk before the church possesses a complete moral adjudication.

### Locked

> **THE MORE SERIOUS AND PUBLIC THE ECCLESIAL VERDICT, THE STRONGER THE FACTUAL AND JURISDICTIONAL GROUND SHOULD BE.**

Evidence class: `PRUDENTIAL APPLICATION`, with direct church-discipline controls.

---

# 6. A pastor should separate three conversations that families often collapse

## A. “What happened?”

Fact question.

## B. “Was it sin / what kind of sin?”

Moral/exegetical question.

## C. “Is the person repentant / what should church do?”

Pastoral/ecclesial judgment.

These are related but not identical.

### Failure mode

If the pastor begins with:

> `Confess your rebellion right now`

before establishing the disputed factual claim, the moral verdict has been embedded in the question.

### Better principle

> **ESTABLISH FACTS BEFORE DEMANDING REPENTANCE FOR THE DISPUTED FACT.**

Where facts are already established, admonition can be direct without pretending uncertainty.

---

# 7. No confession extraction through spiritual pressure

Dangerous techniques include:

- `God told me you did more.`
- `If you were saved you would admit what your parents suspect.`
- `Your denial proves hardness.`
- `We know there is another account` when leaders do not know it.
- prolonged group confrontation until the youth produces the desired answer;
- promising restoration/relief only if a specific unproved allegation is admitted.

### Locked

> **DO NOT LIE ABOUT GOD, EVIDENCE OR ECCLESIAL KNOWLEDGE TO PRODUCE A CONFESSION.**

`AUTHORITY ≠ LICENSE TO BLUFF`.

`PASTORAL AUTHORITY ≠ LICENSE TO CLAIM REVELATION ABOUT DISPUTED FACTS`.

Use `259`, `274`.

---

# 8. A denial can be morally significant without proving innocence

If evidence is strong and the teenager continues denying, the denial itself may become additional deception.

But that conclusion depends on the underlying fact being sufficiently established.

### Example

Verified direct evidence shows continued contact after the youth said it stopped.

Now the church can address:

- the conduct;
- the false statement;
- ongoing impenitence if the youth continues defending/denying what is established.

### Mirror

If evidence is genuinely ambiguous, denial remains a disputed claim, not automatic added sin.

### Locked

`DENIAL OF ESTABLISHED FACT = POTENTIAL NEW FALSEHOOD`.

`DENIAL OF UNPROVED ALLEGATION ≠ PROOF OF FALSEHOOD`.

---

# 9. Parent evidence must be described accurately

Church leaders should ask:

- Did you personally see this?
- Is this a screenshot/log/file or your inference from behavior?
- Is the account identity verified?
- Could there be another plausible context?
- What exactly did the child previously say?
- Are dates materially relevant?
- Is anyone else a direct witness?
- Is there a safeguarding reason not to continue ordinary questioning?

### P0

Do not let language silently upgrade:

`we suspect`

→ `we know`

→ `he admitted`

when only the first is true.

`EVIDENCE DESCRIPTION MUST NOT OUTRUN EVIDENCE`.

---

# 10. Documentary sexual material requires exposure minimization

Church leaders do **not** need to view every sexual image/message to prove seriousness.

Existing `165` and `266` govern:

- enough truth for real duty;
- no pornography-by-description;
- no unnecessary circulation;
- safeguarding/legal evidence handling.

APSAC's current practice-guideline catalogue includes a 2025 guideline opposing showing CSAM/sexted images to parents, guardians and other adults.

Source:
- https://apsac.org/practice-guidelines/

### P0

`EVIDENTIARY SERIOUSNESS ≠ MAXIMUM SEXUAL EXPOSURE TO LEADERS`.

Exact evidence handling is jurisdiction-dependent.

---

# 11. Church should not make parents permanent investigators for church discipline

Parents can supply evidence and daily observations.

But once the church is considering formal membership discipline, church leaders must own their actual ecclesial judgment.

Bad pattern:

> `Keep searching his phone until you find enough for us to discipline him.`

This can turn home life into outsourced ecclesial surveillance.

### Locked

`PARENTS ≠ CHURCH PROSECUTION UNIT`.

`ELDERS ≠ SUBSTITUTE PARENTS`.

Church and family cooperate without collapsing stewardships.

---

# 12. Church should not demand proof that exceeds what it requires in ordinary serious cases merely because the offender is a minor

The opposite distortion is:

> `We will believe nothing unless he admits it because this is a family matter.`

If the youth is actually a member, serious established hypocrisy cannot be made jurisdictionally invisible merely by calling it parental conflict.

### P0

The church should distinguish:

- household preference dispute;
- established biblical sin;
- public scandal;
- membership contradiction;
- safeguarding risk.

Use `159`.

---

# 13. What if the parents are themselves accused of abuse/coercion?

Then normal information-sharing assumptions change.

Church must not automatically:

- give disclosure back to the alleged abuser;
- treat parental authority as proof of parental innocence;
- demand private confrontation with alleged offender;
- suppress statutory/safeguarding routes.

Use `24`, `242`, `249`.

### Locked

`PARENTAL AUTHORITY ≠ IMMUNITY FROM EVIDENTIARY SCRUTINY`.

`CHILD REPORT ≠ AUTOMATIC PROOF`.

Again, truth and protection require competent process.

---

# 14. Administrative safety action is not the same as a final moral verdict

Church may temporarily:

- pause a youth role;
- prevent unsupervised access to younger children;
- change transportation/DM arrangements;
- assign supervision;

while a serious concern is being assessed.

### Guard

Leaders should communicate internally/externally with accurate status language:

> `temporary safety measure / pending assessment`

rather than:

> `proved offender`

unless the facts are actually established.

### Locked

`PRECAUTION ≠ CONVICTION`.

`PRECAUTION ≠ DECLARATION OF INNOCENCE`.

This is especially important where minors' reputations can be permanently damaged by careless church gossip.

---

# 15. Public correction needs proportionate factual confidence

Use `167`.

The wider the correction circle, the greater the risk of:

- unnecessary stigma;
- false accusation if facts are wrong;
- exposure of other minors;
- gossip.

Therefore:

> **publicity should never be used to compensate for evidentiary weakness.**

If anything, wider/public action demands greater care.

### P0

`UNCERTAIN FACT + BIGGER AUDIENCE ≠ STRONGER TRUTH`.

---

# 16. Repentance can be judged only after the object of repentance is sufficiently clear

Parents/pastors sometimes ask:

> `Do you repent?`

before clarifying:

- what conduct is admitted/established;
- what the child says about it;
- what ongoing arrangement remains defended.

### Better sequence

1. establish material facts enough for the decision;
2. name the moral issue from Scripture;
3. hear the person's response;
4. distinguish confusion, disagreement, denial, confession and defended impenitence;
5. assess fruit over time where appropriate.

### Locked

> **REPENTANCE IS NOT A GENERIC WORD TO BE EXTRACTED; IT IS A TURN FROM ACTUAL SIN TOWARD GOD.**

---

# 17. A nonmember can still be morally admonished without formal adjudication theater

A nonmember church-raised youth may be clearly living in serious sin.

Church can:

- preach/admonish truth;
- tell him plainly what Scripture says;
- limit roles/access;
- protect peers;
- encourage attendance under the Word;
- refuse false assurance.

But it should not force a mock trial merely to simulate member discipline.

### Locked

`RETENTION OF ATTENDANCE ≠ MORAL SILENCE`.

`NONMEMBER ≠ MORALLY UNACCOUNTABLE`.

`NONMEMBER ≠ MEMBER UNDER CHURCH KEYS`.

# 18. Product-ready case matrix

| Case | Facts | Status | Church action emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| private admitted sin, nonmember | established/admitted | no formal member censure | admonition, parents, role/safety boundaries |
| private established sin, member denies | strong evidence | member jurisdiction | fact review, direct admonition, discipline process if impenitence established |
| parental suspicion only | unresolved | any status | no factual verdict; prudent observation/support |
| public notorious sin, member | established/public | member jurisdiction | proportionate public/corporate clarity |
| serious safeguarding allegation | credible risk, facts incomplete | any status | immediate protection/competent reporting; no confession prerequisite |
| role risk with investigation pending | unresolved but meaningful risk | any status | temporary precaution accurately labeled |
| parents alleged unsafe | disputed/safety-sensitive | minor | independent safeguarding route; do not default disclosure to alleged abuser |

This matrix is `PRUDENTIAL APPLICATION`, not canon law.

# 19. Product overclaim kill-switch

Reject or rewrite:

- `The church can act only if the teenager confesses.`
- `If parents say it happened, elders should assume guilt.`
- `A previous liar's denial proves the new accusation.`
- `Safeguarding requires the offender's admission before protective action.`
- `A temporary safety restriction proves guilt.`
- `Parents must keep searching until elders have enough evidence.`
- `Church leaders should see every explicit message/image.`
- `Public accusation is justified when private evidence is weak.`
- `A minor member is exempt from real church discipline.`
- `A nonmember youth can be excommunicated in substance if not in name.`
- `Pastors may claim God revealed disputed facts to compel confession.`

Preserve:

> **The church must be neither naïve nor inquisitorial: it may act on sufficiently established truth without waiting for a scripted confession, but it must not convert parental suspicion, prior lying or pastoral authority into proof that does not exist.**

# 21. Final locked synthesis

> **Если родители приносят церкви реальные доказательства серьёзной двойной жизни подростка, пастор не обязан делать вид, будто истины не существует до тех пор, пока ребёнок сам не произнесёт “я признаюсь”. Но церковь также не получает права объявлять каждое родительское подозрение установленным фактом. Нужно различить доказанное, вероятное и неизвестное; членство и не-членство; пастырское обличение, административную защитную меру и формальную церковную дисциплину. Покаяние нельзя добывать духовным давлением. Церковь должна действовать на основании правды — и сама обязана говорить правду о том, что она действительно знает.**
