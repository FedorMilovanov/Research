## 0. The pastoral problem

A parent may eventually reach a situation in which there is no immediate decision left to make.

The adult son or daughter:

- has left or lives separately;
- remains in contact;
- does not want to return;
- continues to defend sexual sin, deception, intoxication, profane company, relational rebellion or unbelief;
- knows the family's biblical convictions already;
- may periodically express regret without durable fruit;
- may still write, visit, ask for ordinary help or speak warmly;
- may also accuse the parents of being the problem whenever sin is named.

The parents then live inside a long unresolved interval.

The danger is that every day becomes organized around the unanswered question:

> `When will he/she repent?`

This can produce opposite distortions:

1. **normalization** — preserve pleasant contact by refusing to mention reality;
2. **obsessive pursuit** — make every contact an interrogation, sermon or investigation;
3. **manufactured hope** — treat every warm message, tear or short improvement as proof that repentance has occurred;
4. **despair** — interpret the absence of visible change as proof that God will never save;
5. **child-centered family idolatry** — spouse, younger children, church, work and worship all become secondary to the wayward adult;
6. **punitive freezing** — keep the relation permanently cold even if later fruit becomes clear.

### Locked center

> **Long-term waiting is not passive normalization and not permanent crisis management. It is continuing faithfulness before God while the outcome remains outside parental control.**

# 1. Scripture treats parental grief over folly as real, not as spiritual failure

## Proverbs 17:25

A foolish son is described as grief to his father and bitterness to the mother who bore him.

Gill explicitly connects the grief to the son's folly/wickedness and the ruin he is bringing upon himself.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/proverbs/17-25.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/proverbs/17.htm

### P0

Do not counsel parents:

> `If you trusted God, this would not hurt so much.`

The wisdom literature expects the moral ruin of a child to wound faithful parents.

### Equal guard

Pain itself does not prove the parent's response is righteous.

A parent can grieve rightly and still:

- speak sinfully;
- manipulate;
- neglect spouse/other children;
- obsessively monitor;
- make the child the emotional center of the family.

# 2. Proverbs connects sexual folly with parental grief and material ruin

## Proverbs 29:3

The son who loves wisdom gladdens his father; the companion of prostitutes wastes substance.

Poole and Gill connect the sexual/financial folly with grief and dishonor to the father.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/proverbs/29-3.htm
- https://biblehub.com/proverbs/29-3.htm

### Relevance to the present project

This verse is particularly useful because the project repeatedly deals with:

- sexual appetite;
- waste;
- money;
- foolish company;
- parental grief.

### Guard

Do not reduce every modern sexual problem to prostitution, and do not turn a wisdom proverb into an exact household policy.

The safe transfer is moral structure:

> **sexual folly can simultaneously damage the sinner, consume resources and wound the family.**

# 3. Grief over another person's rebellion can coexist with doctrinal clarity

## Romans 9:1–3

Paul speaks of great sorrow and continual anguish over unbelieving Israel.

Gill emphasizes that this is deep, internal and continuing grief, not theatrical emotion.

Source:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/romans/9-2.htm

## Romans 10:1

Paul's heart's desire and prayer remains that Israel might be saved.

Yet Romans 9–11 does not soften:

- unbelief;
- self-righteousness;
- divine sovereignty;
- responsibility;
- judgment.

Gill explicitly notes that Paul's affection does not prevent him from identifying Israel's errors.

Calvin likewise treats Paul's prayer as genuine love while Paul continues to tell the truth about their condition.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/romans/10-1.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/romans/10.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/romans/10.htm

### P0

> **LOVE CAN PRAY FOR SALVATION WITHOUT LYING ABOUT PRESENT CONDITION.**

### Parent application — analogy, not direct exegesis

Romans 9–10 is not a parenting manual.

But its moral pattern strongly defeats the false choice:

`either love the rebel`

or

`tell the truth about rebellion`.

Paul does both.

# 4. Grief at sin itself can be godly

## Psalm 119:136

The psalmist weeps because people do not keep God's law.

Gill understands the grief as distress that God's law is despised, His authority trampled and His glory denied.

Source:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/psalms/119-136.htm

### P0

The deepest parental sorrow should not be merely:

> `He embarrassed us.`

> `She ruined our plans.`

> `People will think we failed.`

Biblically ordered grief increasingly becomes:

> `God is dishonored; the child is destroying himself/herself; truth is hated; Christ is rejected.`

### Anti-idolatry implication

Reputation-centered sorrow is different from holiness-centered sorrow.

A parent may need to repent not of grief, but of **what the grief is mainly about**.

---

# 5. Matthew 10:37 establishes the highest relational priority

Christ says that whoever loves son or daughter more than Him is not worthy of Him.

Gill explicitly says Christ does not destroy natural family affection; He subordinates it to Christ.

Source:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/matthew/10-37.htm

### P0

> **THE CHILD MAY BE DEARER THAN ALMOST ANY CREATURE; THE CHILD MAY NOT BECOME DEARER THAN CHRIST.**

This matters when parents are tempted to:

- call sin normal to preserve relationship;
- abandon church/family duties to monitor the child continuously;
- attack faithful believers who confront the child;
- finance rebellion because the alternative feels emotionally unbearable;
- treat the child's happiness as the family's highest good.

### Equal guard

Matthew 10:37 does not authorize coldness.

Gill specifically preserves natural duties and affection.

The issue is priority, not emotional annihilation.

# 6. David and Absalom — grief is real, but parental feeling can overwhelm another vocation

## 2 Samuel 18:33–19:8

David's grief over Absalom is among Scripture's most intense parental laments.

The text is complicated:

- Absalom died in active rebellion;
- David loved him deeply;
- David's own earlier sins formed part of the background of the family catastrophe;
- David was simultaneously father **and king**.

Classic commentators note that David's grief became so consuming that Joab had to confront him because his public conduct dishonored/undermined the loyal servants who had risked their lives.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/2_samuel/18-33.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/cambridge/2_samuel/18.htm

### P0

> **PARENTAL GRIEF DOES NOT CANCEL OTHER GOD-GIVEN STEWARDSHIPS.**

Modern analogical applications may include:

- spouse;
- younger children;
- church membership/service;
- work;
- bodily health;
- ordinary worship;
- existing obligations to others.

### Guard

David's kingship is not equivalent to modern family life.

Do not use Joab's confrontation as a license to tell grieving parents to “get over it.”

The transferable point is narrower:

> grief can become disordered when it absorbs duties God has not cancelled.

# 7. Hope in God is not a private prophecy about the child

Existing control remains:

`HOPE IN GOD ≠ CONFIDENCE IN THE CURRENT REPENTANCE CLAIM`.

A further distinction is needed:

`HOPE IN GOD ≠ CERTAINTY THAT GOD WILL SAVE THIS SPECIFIC PERSON IN THE WAY/TIMING I EXPECT`.

### P0

Parents may pray boldly and persistently for salvation.

They may not claim a promise Scripture has not given:

> `God has guaranteed my adult child will return because I prayed enough / raised him in church / claimed a verse.`

### Pastoral result

Biblical hope says:

- God is able to save;
- God's arm is not shortened;
- repentance is His gift;
- means matter;
- prayer matters;
- no present hardness is stronger than sovereign grace.

Biblical hope does **not** require invented revelation about the final outcome of one particular adult child.

---

# 8. Prayer is an ongoing duty; prayer is not a substitute for omniscience

Romans 10:1 gives a strong general pattern of prayer for the salvation of unbelieving people.

Existing adult-child controls already preserve prayer after relational distance or church discipline.

### P0

> **PRAYER ≠ SURVEILLANCE.**

Parents do not need exhaustive information in order to pray faithfully.

The inability to know:

- every friend;
- every night;
- every private account;
- every sexual act;
- every hidden conversation;

is painful, but it does not create a divine duty to obtain omniscience.

### Application

A useful discernment question remains:

> `Do I need this information for a real present duty, safety issue, legal responsibility or concrete act of love — or because uncertainty itself has become intolerable to me?`

### Guard

This does not prohibit reasonable safety clarification after sudden disappearance, credible danger, exploitation or acute risk.

Use earlier safety/contact modules.

# 9. Long-term contact must not become “everything is normal now”

Existing relational controls remain authoritative:

`RELATION ≠ VOLUNTARY CLOSENESS ≠ TRUST ≠ FELLOWSHIP ≠ ACCESS ≠ APPROVAL ≠ ROLE`.

A parent can maintain ordinary natural contact while refusing moral fiction.

### Examples of ordinary contact that may remain

- answer a message;
- ask about work/health;
- meet for a meal;
- help with legitimate illness/emergency;
- send a birthday greeting;
- listen without endorsing;
- offer practical good that does not fund defended sin.

### What ordinary contact does not require

- refer to unmarried sexual cohabitation as a marriage;
- affirm a sinful relationship;
- stop identifying lying as lying;
- pretend there has been repentance;
- restore financial support;
- return keys/access;
- bring a dangerous influence around younger children;
- restore church privileges.

### P0

> **ORDINARY KINDNESS ≠ MORAL NORMALIZATION.**

---

# 10. Every conversation need not become an altar call

Existing `MODE CHANGE, NOT TRUTH CHANGE` remains controlling.

If the adult child already knows:

- the parents' biblical judgment;
- the gospel;
- the call to repent;
- the consequences of continuing;

then repeating the identical argument at every contact may become circular strife rather than faithful instruction.

### P0

> **NOT EVERY CONVERSATION MUST REPEAT THE WHOLE CASE; NO CONVERSATION REQUIRES PRETENDING THE CASE DISAPPEARED.**

This allows:

- normal human conversation;
- genuine affection;
- listening;
- strategic silence;
- concise truth when directly relevant;
- fuller conversation when the child actually opens the question.

### Guard

Do not turn `MODE CHANGE` into cowardice:

> `We never mention sin because we are afraid the child will stop speaking to us.`

Retention of contact is not a higher good than truth.

---

# 11. The parent's whole life must not become a waiting room

This is a stewardship inference from multiple biblical obligations, not a single proof text.

Parents remain responsible before God for whatever relations/vocations actually remain:

- marriage;
- younger/dependent children;
- church;
- work;
- care of aging relatives where applicable;
- hospitality/service;
- worship;
- bodily stewardship.

### P0

> **A WAYWARD ADULT CHILD DOES NOT RECEIVE A NEW VOCATION CALLED “THE FAMILY'S ENTIRE LIFE.”**

The household should not communicate to younger children:

> `Your sibling's rebellion is now the organizing center of our family.`

Nor should a spouse be indefinitely abandoned emotionally because all attention is devoted to monitoring the absent child.

# 12. Waiting does not require preserving the same practical arrangement

A parent may continue praying and hoping while:

- declining money requests;
- refusing to fund cohabitation/addiction/sexual sin;
- maintaining separate residences;
- limiting access to younger children;
- changing holiday arrangements;
- declining dishonest explanations to extended family/church;
- requiring restitution for specific damage before specific trust is restored.

### P0

`WAITING FOR REPENTANCE ≠ FREEZING EVERY BOUNDARY UNTIL REPENTANCE`.

Different domains can change at different speeds.

---

# 13. Waiting also does not require permanent suspicion after real fruit appears

Existing return/restoration controls remain:

`MERCY MAY BE IMMEDIATE; RE-ENTRUSTMENT MAY BE GRADUAL`.

But the reverse error must remain visible:

> if evidence of truth, restitution, changed company, changed sexual conduct, help-seeking and durable submission to correction accumulates, parents may not preserve maximum suspicion forever merely because the previous deception was severe.

### P0

> **LONG WAITING MUST NOT HARDEN THE PARENT INTO A PERSON WHO NO LONGER KNOWS HOW TO RECEIVE GOOD NEWS.**

Luke 15 / 2 Cor. 2 controls remain relevant here, with all prior exegesis guards.

# 14. What counts as “news” versus “evidence of repentance”

Long waiting often produces interpretive inflation.

Examples:

- `he wrote kindly`;
- `she came to dinner`;
- `he listened without yelling`;
- `she said she misses church music`;
- `he cried`;
- `she broke up for two days`;
- `he asked for a Bible verse`.

These may be encouraging signs.

They are not self-authenticating conversion/repentance proof.

### P0

Use categories:

1. **contact signal**;
2. **softening signal**;
3. **truth signal**;
4. **repentance claim**;
5. **observable repentance fruit**;
6. **durable re-entrustment evidence**.

Do not collapse 1–2 into 5–6.

### Equal guard

Do not despise small beginnings or require dramatic emotional performance.

This taxonomy is epistemic/pastoral, not a six-stage biblical sacrament.

# 15. Parents may need their own church/pastoral support

Long-term grief creates temptations:

- bitterness;
- marital division;
- shame;
- secrecy;
- obsession;
- retaliation;
- despair;
- gossip disguised as prayer requests;
- isolation from church because the family feels exposed.

### P0

The parents themselves remain sheep who may need:

- admonition;
- comfort;
- correction;
- help preserving marriage/other children;
- protection from self-condemnation where they have repented of real parental sin;
- challenge where they continue to sin.

Do not make the pastor merely the child's investigator.

---

# 16. David is a warning against both coldness and absorption

The Absalom narrative should never be flattened into:

> `David loved too much.`

Nor:

> `David's grief proves parents should ignore every other duty.`

The narrative presents extraordinary affection inside a morally and politically catastrophic family history.

### Safe synthesis

> **Do not become emotionally inhuman toward the rebel. Do not let the rebel become functionally sovereign over every remaining duty.**

---

# 17. A long-term pastoral rhythm

This is prudential synthesis, not a biblical command sequence.

A potentially faithful long-term rhythm can look like:

1. **pray** without inventing guaranteed timing;
2. **grieve** before God without making family reputation the center;
3. **tell the truth** when relevant;
4. **avoid circular strife** where nothing new is being heard;
5. **do ordinary good** that does not enable defended sin;
6. **preserve boundaries** proportionate to actual risk/trust;
7. **continue other vocations** before God;
8. **receive real fruit** without demanding theatrical suffering;
9. **reassess domains separately** rather than switching every privilege at once;
10. **leave regeneration/outcome to God** while using lawful means.

### Locked formula

> **PRAY WITHOUT OMNISCIENCE. LOVE WITHOUT LYING. GRIEVE WITHOUT MAKING THE CHILD GOD. WAIT WITHOUT PUTTING THE WHOLE HOUSEHOLD IN SUSPENDED ANIMATION.**

# 18. Anti-overclaim controls

Do not write:

- `If parents pray enough, the child will certainly return.`
- `If the child has not returned, the parents lacked faith.`
- `Real faith means parents stop hurting.`
- `Every warm contact means repentance has begun.`
- `Every continuing contact normalizes sin.`
- `Every reduced-contact period is abandonment.`
- `Every boundary is manipulation.`
- `Every act of financial help is enabling.`
- `The parents must keep monitoring until they know everything.`
- `The parents should simply move on as though the child no longer matters.`

## Final synthesis

> **The parent of a long-term prodigal is not called to choose between truth and love, nor between hope and realism. Scripture permits deep grief, commands higher loyalty to Christ, models prayer for the unbelieving, and refuses to make another person's rebellion the sovereign organizer of every remaining duty.**

> **The long wait therefore remains active Christian faithfulness: prayer without omniscience, grief without idolatry, kindness without normalization, boundaries without revenge, and hope in God without inventing a promise He has not given.**
