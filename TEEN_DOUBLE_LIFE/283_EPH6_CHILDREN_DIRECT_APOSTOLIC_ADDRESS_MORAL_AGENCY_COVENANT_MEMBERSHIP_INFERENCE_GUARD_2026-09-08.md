# Ephesians 6:1–4 — Children as Direct Apostolic Hearers
## Moral Agency and the Covenant-Membership Inference Guard

**Status:** RESEARCH / TARGETED EXEGESIS / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `34`, `43`, `160`, `273–282`

## 1. Direct data

Paul directly addresses `children` and commands:

- obedience to parents `in the Lord`;
- honoring father and mother.

He directly addresses fathers and commands:

- no provocation to wrath;
- nurture in the Lord’s discipline/instruction.

Colossians 3:20–21 supplies a parallel household command.

## 2. What this decisively establishes

### Children are present moral hearers

Paul does not postpone moral address until:

- a later personal testimony;
- legal adulthood;
- a church membership interview.

The text itself does **not** state the baptismal status of the addressed children. Therefore do not turn Eph 6 into an argument from silence that their moral duty specifically precedes baptism. The project’s stronger claim — that moral/filial duty exists before baptism — is independently grounded in Creator-creature obligation, the moral law and the parent-child relation.

### Christian parental formation is presently mandated

The father’s duty is explicitly religious, not merely secular care until conversion occurs.

### Child obedience is grounded above family preference

Gill emphasizes that obedience is due because it is right under God’s law and command. Calvin likewise treats parental authority as divinely appointed but subordinate to God.

This is one of the strongest direct biblical controls against the project’s target error:

> “He is unsaved, so what Christian conduct can we demand?”

The force here comes from the direct moral address and the parent’s religious duty, not from an inferred baptismal status.

## 3. Paedobaptist inference

Presbyterian/Reformed interpreters reasonably point out that the children are addressed inside letters to churches and in explicitly Christian terms. This fits naturally with their doctrine that believers’ children belong to the visible covenant community.

The argument is cumulative:

`CHILDREN ADDRESSED AS PART OF CHURCH ASSEMBLY + ABRAHAMIC CONTINUITY + HOLY CHILDREN + COVENANT SIGN -> CHILD MEMBERSHIP/BAPTISM`.

## 4. Baptist limit

The direct address itself does not say:

- these children are baptized;
- these children are church members;
- their parental Christian status gives them the covenant sign.

Children can be physically/pastorally included in congregational hearing without formal membership.

Older exegete H. A. W. Meyer explicitly argues that infant baptism does not follow from Eph 6:1 and that children may stand in a holy Christian household relation without baptism.

## 5. Important inference for Baptist ecclesiology

The Baptist must not use nonmembership to make the apostolic address disappear.

Even if an unbaptized child is not a member under church keys, the congregation still reads a letter in which Christ’s apostle directly speaks to children.

Therefore a Baptist church needs a theological/pastoral category for:

> **nonmember children who are nevertheless direct hearers of the Word in the assembled Christian community.**

This conclusion is a **strong pastoral inference**, not a claim that Eph 6 identifies the children’s formal membership status.

This is precisely where weak Baptist practice can become incoherent:

- it brings children every week;
- Paul speaks directly to children;
- parents are commanded to form them in the Lord;
- yet church practice may treat church-raised nonmembers as spiritually undefined until they seek baptism.

## 6. Age and capacity

The command presupposes children able to receive meaningful instruction. It does not supply one universal chronological threshold.

Thus:

`CAPACITY MODULATES MODE OF ADDRESS`;

it does not mean:

`NO DUTY EXISTS BEFORE A MODERN LEGAL AGE`.

This converges interestingly with paedobaptist `years/age of discretion` language while remaining distinct from their membership doctrine.

## 7. Integration into Part II

Eph 6 should carry more weight than abstract denominational comparison for the public parent section.

Possible structure:

1. Paul addresses the child now.
2. Paul commands the parent now.
3. regeneration remains God’s sovereign work.
4. therefore parents neither abdicate authority nor manufacture conversion.

## 8. Integration into Part III

The church should ask:

> If the apostolic letter directly addresses children in our hearing, how can our pastoral practice treat church-raised nonmembers as nobody’s responsibility until a membership application?

This does not create formal membership, but together with the church’s teaching vocation and the parents’ member relation it strongly supports direct teaching and exhortation.
