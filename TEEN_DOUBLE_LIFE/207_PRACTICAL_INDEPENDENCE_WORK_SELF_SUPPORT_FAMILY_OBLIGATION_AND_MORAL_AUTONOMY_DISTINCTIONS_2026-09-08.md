# Practical independence — work, self-support, family obligation, and moral autonomy

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** research-only / biblical maturity and family-government control / not publication approval

---

## 0. Governing thesis

Modern language often treats `самостоятельность` as one indivisible good.

Biblically it must be separated into at least two things:

1. **practical maturity / competence** — work, responsibility, financial stewardship, ability to make judgments, bear consequences and care for others;
2. **moral autonomy / self-authorship** — the claim that maturity frees the person from inherited duties, God-given authority and covenant/natural relations unless voluntarily chosen.

The first can be a mark of maturity.

The second is incompatible with creaturely submission to God and with continuing duties such as the fifth commandment.

### Central formula

> **Biblical adulthood is not independence from obligations; it is increasing capacity to carry obligations.**

---

# 1. Scripture expects movement from dependence toward responsibility

## 1.1. Work and quiet responsibility

1 Thess. 4:11–12 commends quiet life, attending to one's own affairs and working with one's hands so believers may walk properly and not be dependent in a disordered way.

2 Thess. 3:6–12 condemns chosen idleness and says that one unwilling to work should not expect to be maintained by others.

### Guard

These texts do not condemn:

- disability;
- temporary unemployment;
- full-time caregiving;
- study/training;
- crisis dependence;
- ordinary household interdependence.

They condemn irresponsible unwillingness and disorder.

### P0

> **Economic competence can be part of Christian maturity; permanent unnecessary dependency is not automatically a more biblical family model.**

---

# 2. 1 Timothy 5 reverses the direction of care as children mature

1 Tim. 5:4 tells children/grandchildren to learn piety toward their own household and make return to parents.

Gill interprets this as adult children caring materially for parents in old age, repaying in measure the care, expense, education and settlement they received while young.

Source:
https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/1_timothy/5.htm

Calvin's commentary preserves the same apostolic context: family has first responsibility before the church is burdened.

Source:
https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43/calcom43.vii.i.v.html

### Theological consequence

The biblical trajectory is not:

`CHILD RECEIVES → ADULT ESCAPES FAMILY CLAIMS`.

It can become:

`CHILD RECEIVES → ADULT BECOMES CAPABLE OF GIVING BACK`.

### P0

> **Growing capacity increases some family duties instead of erasing them.**

---

# 3. Fifth-commandment honour becomes materially concrete in adulthood

Mark 7:9–13 condemns religious reasoning that allows a person to withhold material support from father/mother while claiming the resources are devoted to God.

1 Tim. 5 supplies the positive counterpart.

Thus lifelong honour is not merely:

- speaking politely;
- sending birthday messages;
- avoiding insults.

It may include real care when parents age or suffer need.

### Implication

A person cannot coherently argue:

> "I am independent now, therefore my parents have no morally significant claim on my resources or time."

The exact extent is prudential and depends on competing duties, but the category of obligation remains.

---

# 4. Parents should actually prepare children for competence

Baxter's graded-government discussion assumes parents teach children progressively toward useful life:

- knowledge;
- labour/calling;
- manners;
- godliness;
- a settled course of living where close oversight is no longer necessary.

Primary source:
https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43800/43800-h/43800-h.htm

This is important against a distorted anti-modern reaction.

A father who deliberately keeps an able adult daughter or son:

- financially helpless;
- unable to transact ordinary life;
- incapable of work;
- unable to make any judgment without him

cannot automatically call that "biblical submission."

### P0

> **Faithful parental government should ordinarily aim at mature stewardship, not manufactured helplessness.**

---

# 5. Competence is not sovereignty

A young woman can:

- hold a job;
- earn salary;
- own/use property lawfully;
- manage money;
- study;
- travel lawfully;
- exercise substantial gifts;

without thereby establishing:

> "No father, mother, church, future husband, civil authority or natural duty can make a morally weighty claim on me."

Likewise a son can support himself while remaining arrogant, sexually immoral and contemptuous of parents.

### P0

`SELF-SUPPORT ≠ SELF-SOVEREIGNTY`.

---

# 6. Proverbs 31 is a strong competence text but not an autonomy text

The worthy wife:

- considers and buys a field;
- plants a vineyard;
- conducts production/trade;
- manages household resources;
- acts with strength and wisdom;
- extends care to the poor.

The passage therefore blocks a caricature of biblical womanhood as economic incompetence.

But she acts as a wife within an ordered household, not as an argument against marriage structure.

### P0

> **Economic agency and household order are not opposites in Scripture.**

---

# 7. The unmarried and widow texts also prevent dependence from becoming a universal feminine virtue

Earlier `188` controls show:

- 1 Cor. 7 can describe an unmarried woman as directly concerned with the things of the Lord;
- 1 Tim. 5 knows widows with distinct responsibilities and support structures;
- Lydia appears as a commercially active household figure;
- Anna serves God in widowhood.

### Result

A woman does not become spiritually incomplete merely because she lacks a husband.

This does not erase the special role of husband headship **where marriage exists**.

---

# 8. Financial support by parents creates stewardship questions but not purchase of conscience

Parents may lawfully decide:

- what they will fund;
- whether to provide a car;
- whether to pay rent/tuition;
- whether a competent adult should contribute to household costs.

The child may therefore face natural choices:

> accept support with reasonable conditions,

or

> assume more costs personally.

### But

Money cannot make an otherwise sinful parental demand righteous.

Parents may not say:

> "Because we pay tuition, you must sin / marry this person / deny Christ / tolerate abuse."

### P0

`FINANCIAL DEPENDENCE` can increase legitimate stewardship/accountability but does not create unlimited moral jurisdiction.

---

# 9. An adult child cannot demand the benefits of dependence while claiming the rhetoric of total autonomy

A frequent incoherent pattern is:

> "I am an adult; you have no say."

combined with:

> "Continue paying housing, food, phone, car, education and consequences of my decisions."

This does not mean parents gain ownership of the adult.

It means the moral structure of shared resources cannot honestly be erased.

### Product-safe principle

> **Кто требует полной практической независимости, должен быть готов нести гораздо большую долю практических последствий своих решений. Кто принимает существенное содержание семьи, не может честно считать семейный порядок совершенно посторонним для себя.**

This is prudential reasoning, not a direct biblical quotation.

---

# 10. Parents can also sin by using dependence to prevent maturation

Possible patterns:

- refusing reasonable employment because work reduces parental control;
- sabotaging every attempt at responsible budgeting;
- withholding basic documents/property unlawfully;
- making adult competence feel like betrayal;
- financially rescuing every irresponsible choice and then complaining the child never matures;
- demanding that an adult remain dependent so the parent remains indispensable.

### Biblical control

Eph. 6:4 / Col. 3:21 forbid provoking/discouraging children.

Baxter's teleological/graded government gives historical theological support to the idea that parental government should achieve ends and then change form.

### P0

> **Parents must not confuse being needed with being obeyed, or child dependence with successful discipleship.**

---

# 11. Adult formation should aim at reciprocal family piety

A mature Christian household ideally moves through stages:

`parent provides / teaches / protects`

→

`child practices responsibility under oversight`

→

`adult increasingly bears own load`

→

`adult may establish new household`

→

`adult remains capable of honouring/helping parents and siblings`.

This is not a fixed age chart.

It is a **direction of responsibility**.

---

# 12. Relationship to modern Russian ECB language of `полная самостоятельность`

Module `206` establishes that a Union children's-ministry Q&A uses this phrase as the endpoint of progressive responsibility transfer.

A biblically safer interpretation is:

> **full practical adulthood / responsibility**, not absolute moral independence from family obligations.

The official social concept's simultaneous emphasis on family primacy, father responsibility and preparation for constructive adult development supports this more relational reading.

Sources:
- https://baptist.su/about/ministries/kids/questions-answers-kids
- https://baptist.su/faith

### P0

Future Product should not attack the mere word `самостоятельность`; define the kind being criticized.

---

# 13. Feminism / autonomy precision

Some feminist or egalitarian systems may contribute to a cultural ideal in which inherited authority is presumptively suspect.

But practical female competence is not itself feminism.

Likewise Western male adulthood has long been shaped by individualism, wage labour, mobility and contract ideology.

### Therefore

Do not write:

> "A daughter who wants a job and her own income has absorbed feminism."

Better question:

> **What does she believe her competence means about God's authority, her parents, the church, marriage and obligations to others?**

---

# 14. Product-safe synthesis

> **Самостоятельность бывает зрелой и бунтарской. Зрелая самостоятельность умеет трудиться, платить, отвечать за решения и помогать другим. Бунтарская самостоятельность превращает эти способности в лозунг: «теперь никто ничего не вправе от меня требовать». Первая может быть плодом хорошего воспитания. Вторая — не взросление, а автономия.**

And:

> **Чем взрослее ребёнок, тем меньше родители должны делать за него то, что он способен делать сам; но тем больше сам взрослый ребёнок способен исполнять реальные обязанности перед Богом, семьёй и ближними.**
