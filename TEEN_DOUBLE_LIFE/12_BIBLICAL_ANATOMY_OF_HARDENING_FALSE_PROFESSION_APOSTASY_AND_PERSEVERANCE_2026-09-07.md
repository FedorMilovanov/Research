## 1. Центральный вопрос

Что именно можно сказать богословски о подростке из верующей семьи, который:

- знает Евангелие;
- умеет говорить правильными словами;
- внешне участвует в церковной жизни;
- тайно любит сексуальный грех/пошлость/ложь;
- всё лучше скрывает это;
- всё меньше испытывает внутреннее сопротивление;
- иногда начинает защищать то, чего раньше стыдился;
- в крайнем случае открыто отказывается от веры?

Нельзя автоматически выбрать одну из двух схем:

### Ошибка A

> «Он крещён/вырос в церкви, значит точно возрождён; это просто временный подростковый этап».

### Ошибка B

> «Он тяжело и сознательно грешит, значит Бог уже окончательно его оставил и покаяние невозможно».

Библейская картина сложнее.

## 2. Начальная точка — не нейтральное детское сердце

1689, chapter 6:

- человечество находится в падшем состоянии;
- corrupted nature передана потомству;
- человек по природе находится в состоянии греха;
- из внутренней corruption исходят actual transgressions.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/chapter-6

Confession directly connects this to James 1:14–15 and Matthew 15/Mark 7-type anthropology.

### Implication for ДВР

Being born into a believing family changes the child's **providential environment**, not the child's natural Adamic condition.

The child may receive extraordinary privileges:

- Scripture from infancy;
- prayer;
- Christian moral vocabulary;
- church community;
- examples of faith;
- warnings;
- gospel preaching.

But those privileges are not identical to regeneration.

This is precisely why upbringing matters greatly **without being regeneration itself**.

## 3. Sovereign grace is not random grace

1689 chapter 10 describes effectual calling as God's free, powerful work by Word and Spirit:

- enlightening the mind;
- taking away the heart of stone;
- renewing the will;
- drawing the sinner to Christ;
- making the sinner willing by grace.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-10

1689 chapter 14 says saving faith is the work of the Spirit and is **ordinarily wrought by the ministry of the Word**.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/chapter-14

Chapter 5 says God's ordinary providence makes use of **means** and genuine second causes.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-5/

### Synthesis

Therefore:

> **God alone regenerates, but God ordinarily works in a world of means rather than random religious outcomes.**

Parents cannot produce the new birth.

Yet:

- teaching Scripture matters;
- church matters;
- companions matter;
- discipline matters;
- examples matter;
- warnings matter;
- prayer matters;
- access/boundaries matter.

This rejects both Pelagian parenting and practical fatalism.

## 4. Hebrews 3: the danger is progressive hardening through deceit

Hebrews 3:12–14 is one of the most central texts for the future series.

The movement is:

`evil/unbelieving heart → danger of falling away → daily exhortation → deceitfulness of sin → hardening → need to hold confidence firm to the end`.

TGC Hebrews commentary emphasizes that the wilderness generation's rebellion/disobedience was rooted in unbelief and hearts hardened through sin's deceitfulness, and that the prescribed response is daily mutual exhortation/perseverance within the believing community.

Source:

https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/hebrews/

### Crucial implication

The text does not present hardening merely as:

> «one day the conscience suddenly disappears».

It explicitly connects hardening with **deceitfulness**.

That maps powerfully onto the research corpus:

- minimization;
- normalization;
- selective disclosure;
- peer validation;
- rationalization;
- redefining the forbidden as ordinary;
- practicing two identities.

Psychology does not prove Hebrews 3, but it helps describe some human processes through which self-deception can be socially reinforced.

## 5. Sin deceives before it openly dominates

A useful future-article sequence:

### Stage 1 — “This is wrong, but only once.”

Conscience protests; desire seeks exception.

### Stage 2 — “It is not really that serious.”

Moral minimization begins.

### Stage 3 — “Everyone does it.”

Peer/descriptive norm becomes justification.

### Stage 4 — “My parents just do not understand.”

Authority is morally reinterpreted as ignorance/control.

### Stage 5 — “This is actually normal.”

The old prohibition now seems excessive.

### Stage 6 — “People who condemn it are hypocrites/repressed.”

The moral evaluator becomes the problem.

### Stage 7 — “I do not believe this part of Christianity.”

Behavioral conflict may become doctrinal revision.

### Stage 8 — open abandonment or hardened double life.

This sequence is **pastoral synthesis**, not a claim that every adolescent follows eight stages.

Its biblical warrant is the concept of deceit → hardening, while empirical modules provide mechanisms of normalization and moral disengagement.

## 6. Ephesians 4: callousness is morally serious

Ephesians 4:17–19 describes Gentile life in terms of:

- futility of mind;
- darkened understanding;
- alienation from God's life;
- hardness of heart;
- becoming callous;
- giving oneself over to sensuality;
- greediness for impurity.

The passage is highly relevant because it places:

**hardness → loss of sensitivity/callousness → self-giving to sensuality**

within one moral portrait.

TGC commentary/teaching literature likewise highlights the callous imagery and the danger of persisting until moral sensation is reduced.

Source orientation:

https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/podcasts/tgc-podcast/transformed-together-ryan-kwon/

### Guard

Do not convert this into a medical/neuroscience statement.

Paul is describing **moral-spiritual callousness**, not measuring dopamine receptors or neurological injury.

---

## 7. 1 Timothy 4:2 — “seared conscience” must be kept in context

1 Timothy 4:1–5 concerns departure from the faith, deceitful spirits/demonic teaching and hypocritical liars whose consciences are seared.

TGC commentary notes the contrast between these consciences and the “good conscience” emphasized elsewhere in the letter; the context concerns false teachers/apostasy and doctrinal lies.

Source:

https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/1-timothy/

### Therefore future article must NOT say

> «подросток смотрел порно X лет — у него теперь буквально сожжённая совесть из 1 Тим 4:2».

That is exegetically careless.

### What can be said

Scripture undeniably recognizes grave states of:

- defiled conscience;
- wounded conscience;
- rejected conscience;
- callousness;
- hardening;
- hypocritical lying;
- apostasy.

So the moral danger is real.

But 1 Tim 4:2 is not a diagnostic checklist for deciding that a particular child is beyond repentance.

---

## 8. Romans 1: repeated sin can become judicial abandonment

Romans 1:18–32 contributes an even more frightening category:

- suppression of truth;
- exchange of God's glory/truth;
- dishonorable passions;
- God “gave them up”;
- approval of evil as well as practice.

1689 chapter 5 paragraph 6 explicitly uses Romans 1 in its doctrine of judicial hardening: God may, as righteous judge, give ungodly people over to their own lusts and temptations, while the sinfulness remains entirely from the creature.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-5/

### Theological balance

Two truths must remain together:

1. **human beings harden themselves**;
2. Scripture also knows **God's judicial hardening/giving over** as judgment.

This makes habitual deliberate sin terrifying without making God the author of sin.

---

## 9. But the parent cannot infer the secret decree of God

Even if judicial hardening is biblical, a parent has no warrant to say:

> «God has now finally hardened my son/daughter; there is no point calling him/her to repentance.»

The parent's revealed duty remains:

- proclaim truth;
- exhort today;
- call to repentance;
- pray;
- discipline wisely;
- use appointed means;
- refuse despair as a substitute for obedience.

Deuteronomy 29:29 is relevant as a governing principle: secret things belong to God; revealed things govern our duty.

### Key future line

> **The doctrine of election tells the parent whom to trust — God. It does not tell the parent which child to stop evangelizing.**

This is theological synthesis, not quotation from the confession.

---

## 10. Temporary faith / false assurance is a real confessional category

1689 chapter 18 explicitly distinguishes:

- temporary believers / other unregenerate persons who may deceive themselves with false hopes;
- true believers who can attain assurance based on Christ and evidences of grace.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-18

Chapter 14 likewise distinguishes saving faith in kind from the faith/common grace of temporary believers.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/chapter-14

### Importance for ДВР

A church-raised adolescent may display:

- correct doctrine;
- prayer language;
- emotional religious experiences;
- participation;
- fear of hell;
- respect for parents;
- periods of moral seriousness.

None of these, singly, proves regeneration.

Future article must therefore reject **presumptive regeneration by upbringing**.

But it must equally reject cynical presumption that every struggling church child is false.

---

## 11. Perseverance means a true believer can fall grievously without finally falling away

1689 chapter 17 is essential to avoid a second major theological mistake.

It teaches true saints cannot totally/finally fall from grace, yet can through:

- Satan's temptation;
- the world;
- remaining corruption;
- neglect of means

fall into **grievous sins**, continue in them for a time, incur God's fatherly displeasure, grieve the Spirit, have graces/comfort impaired, hearts hardened and consciences wounded, scandalize others and suffer temporal judgments — yet they will renew repentance and be preserved to the end.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-17

### This is vital for our article

Severe hidden sin does not automatically prove:

> «never regenerate».

But durable impenitence should not be reassured as if profession alone guarantees salvation.

The pastoral task is warning + calling + examining fruit, not reading the decree.

## 12. Therefore there are at least three distinct theological trajectories

### A. Unregenerate religious adolescent increasingly revealed as such

Possible pattern:

- external religion;
- no saving love for Christ;
- hidden sin becomes dominant;
- eventually doctrine/profession adjusts to desires;
- perhaps open departure.

This is compatible with temporary faith/false assurance categories.

### B. True believer falling grievously for a season

Possible pattern:

- genuine faith;
- serious temptation/neglect;
- grievous hidden sin;
- wounded conscience/hardening for a time;
- severe discipline/consequences;
- eventual repentance/restoration.

1689 ch.17 explicitly leaves room for grievous falls without final apostasy.

### C. Unregenerate adolescent later genuinely converted

Possible pattern:

- years of hypocrisy or indifference;
- later effectual calling;
- real repentance/new faith;
- former religious knowledge becomes understood spiritually rather than merely socially.

1689 ch.10/15 allows exactly the category of persons converted at riper years after serving various pleasures.

### Why this taxonomy matters

Parents cannot reliably distinguish A/B/C at the first crisis.

Therefore the response must remain:

- serious enough for A;
- hopeful enough for B;
- evangelistic enough for C.

---

## 13. “He left the faith” can mean several things descriptively

Modern sociology may measure:

- disaffiliation;
- deconversion;
- nonattendance;
- identity change;
- loss of belief.

Confessional theology asks another question:

> Was there ever saving faith/regeneration?

1 John 2:19 is relevant to final departure from apostolic fellowship, while Hebrews warnings require the visible covenant community to take apostasy seriously.

Future article should not flatten this tension.

### Publication wording

Better:

> «Он отказался от христианского исповедания / ушёл из церкви / перестал считать себя верующим»

than automatically:

> «Он был возрождён и потерял спасение».

That latter claim conflicts with the adopted 1689 framework.

## 14. Saving repentance is not merely regret

1689 chapter 15 defines saving repentance as evangelical grace involving:

- Spirit-produced awareness of the evils of sin;
- faith in Christ;
- godly sorrow;
- detestation of sin;
- seeking pardon and grace;
- purpose/endeavor to walk pleasing to God;
- particular repentance for known particular sins.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/chapter-15

And it concludes with a crucial anti-despair principle:

> no sin is so great that it brings damnation to those who repent.

This provides the correct counterweight to the hardening material.

### Future article should be frightening but never hopeless

Not:

> «Keep doing this and you will certainly become irredeemable after some threshold».

But:

> **Do not presume upon tomorrow. Sin deceives and hardens. “Today” is the biblical word. And the command and promise of the gospel is still: repent and come to Christ.**

---

## 15. Assurance should not be built on childhood profession alone

1689 chapter 18 locates assurance in:

- Christ's blood/righteousness;
- promises of the gospel;
- inward evidences/graces;
- Spirit's witness;
- a life tending toward humble holiness.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-18

It also teaches assurance can be shaken by special sin and negligence.

### Implication for parents/church

Dangerous reassurance:

> «Ты помолился молитвой в 8 лет, поэтому что бы сейчас ни происходило, ты точно спасён».

Dangerous opposite:

> «Ты упал тяжело, поэтому твоя прежняя faith was certainly fake».

Better:

- examine present relation to Christ;
- call to repentance;
- look for fruit over time;
- rest salvation in Christ, not childhood ceremony or parental optimism.

---

## 16. The warnings are themselves means of perseverance

Hebrews 3 does not create a contradiction between divine preservation and human exhortation.

The command is:

> exhort one another **every day** so that none is hardened by sin's deceitfulness.

Within Reformed theology, warnings/exhortations can be among the means God uses to preserve His people.

This is consistent with:

- chapter 5 — providential means;
- chapter 14 — Word and other appointed means strengthen faith;
- chapter 17 — neglect of preservation means can accompany grievous falls.

### Major relevance to parenting

A parent should neither think:

> «if my child is elect, warnings are unnecessary»

nor:

> «my warning itself regenerates him».

The warning is an obedient means; efficacy belongs to God.

## 17. Satan: neither excluded nor made a shortcut explanation

A future article should explicitly retain the biblical triad:

- **the flesh / corrupt desires**;
- **the world / social order opposed to God**;
- **the devil / personal spiritual adversary**.

Relevant texts:

- Ephesians 2:1–3;
- Ephesians 6:10–18;
- 1 Peter 5:8–9;
- James 4:7–8;
- 1 John 2:15–17.

1689 chapter 5 paragraphs 5–6 also explicitly includes temptations of Satan and the world in its doctrine of providence/hardening.

Source:

https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-5/

### Guard

Do not say:

> «Satan directly caused this specific TikTok recommendation»

unless there is biblical warrant for that specific claim — which there is not.

Better:

> **A digital system can objectively furnish images, relationships and rewards that align with fleshly desire and worldly norms; Scripture additionally teaches that the Christian struggle takes place within real spiritual warfare.**

This preserves spiritual reality without speculative demonology.

---

## 18. “The world” is more than bad content

Biblically, the world is not merely a collection of explicit videos.

It is a rival value-system:

- lust of flesh;
- lust of eyes;
- pride of life (1 John 2:16);
- glory from one another rather than God;
- normalization of passions;
- identity independent of Creator.

Therefore a phone filter may block pornography while leaving intact:

- vanity;
- status hunger;
- envy;
- fear of man;
- sexual self-display;
- ridicule of purity;
- consumerist self-definition.

This is why technical filtering is necessary in some contexts but cannot equal discipleship.

## 19. Hardening and formation move in both directions

Hebrews 3 gives **daily exhortation** as response to daily hardening danger.

This is extremely important for the research project.

Formation is cumulative:

### Corruptive formation

`small compromise → repetition → group reinforcement → concealment → justification → harder heart`.

### Holy formation

`Word → truthful conversation → warning → repentance → prayer → good companions → practiced self-control → repeated obedience`.

Neither side should be reduced to habit psychology alone.

But the daily structure of Hebrews helps explain why ordinary family/church rhythms matter.

---

## 20. Why mass double life among ДВР deserves institutional examination

If serious double life becomes common in a church/community, theology does not permit leaders to respond only:

> «each child has a sinful heart».

That is true but incomplete.

Hebrews 3 addresses a **community** with communal responsibility to exhort one another.

Biblical leadership texts also require shepherding, teaching and example.

Therefore widespread patterns should trigger examination of:

- household discipleship;
- parental presence;
- youth-group norms;
- preaching/application to adolescents;
- whether sexual topics can be named biblically;
- whether church adults know teenagers personally;
- whether confession is met with truth and restoration or gossip/panic;
- whether peer leadership within youth culture is understood;
- whether families outsource formation to weekly programs.

This does not transfer children's guilt to institutions.

It recognizes that institutions also have duties.

---

## 21. A four-axis diagnostic frame for the future article

Instead of asking only «is this child saved?», analyze four axes.

### Axis 1 — Moral behavior

What is being done, how often, with whom, with what harm?

### Axis 2 — Truth relationship

Is there lying, concealment, voluntary disclosure, confession, rationalization?

### Axis 3 — Spiritual orientation

How does the adolescent respond to Christ, Scripture, warning, sin and repentance?

### Axis 4 — Environment/formation

What peers, platforms, family dynamics and church norms reinforce the behavior?

This prevents a one-dimensional diagnosis.

## 22. Statements safe for publication in the adopted theological framework

1. Children of believers are not regenerated by birth or Christian culture.
2. God alone effectually calls and gives saving faith/repentance.
3. God ordinarily uses means, especially the Word; therefore upbringing and church ministry are not irrelevant.
4. Sin can deceive and harden progressively.
5. Scripture knows grave moral callousness, defiled/seared conscience and judicial hardening.
6. No parent can infer from behavior alone that God has finally abandoned a particular teenager.
7. A true believer can fall into grievous sin for a time without finally falling away.
8. Final apostasy from profession is compatible with the category of temporary/false faith rather than loss of true salvation in the 1689 framework.
9. Genuine repentance bears fruit and is more than being caught.
10. No sin is too great to damn the person who truly repents and rests in Christ.
11. Warnings, exhortation, family discipleship and church fellowship are meaningful means, not rivals to divine sovereignty.

## 25. Research conclusion

The future article should hold together a tension that is easy to lose:

> **Repeated hidden sin is genuinely terrifying because Scripture says sin deceives, hardens and can culminate in apostasy and divine judgment. Yet the parent is never authorized to replace the gospel call with a verdict from the secret counsel of God.**

The response to hardening is not fatalism.

Hebrews says: **today** — exhort.

The response to hypocrisy is not automatic reassurance.

Scripture says: examine, confess, forsake, come into the light.

The response to severe sin is not despair.

The gospel says that repentance itself is grace and that no sin is too great to condemn the one who truly repents and rests in Christ.

Thus the future article can be severe without being cruel, Reformed without being fatalistic, and hopeful without minimizing the possibility of real apostasy from a merely external profession.
