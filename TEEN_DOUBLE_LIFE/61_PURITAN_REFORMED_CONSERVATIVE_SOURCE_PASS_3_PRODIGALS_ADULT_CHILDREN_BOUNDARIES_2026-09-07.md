**Status:** SOURCE AUDIT / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION

**Purpose:** build a Scripture-first source bank for the new adult-child departure lane; rank sources; preserve tensions; avoid one-slogan counseling.

---

# 1. Method

Source order remains:

1. Scripture in historical-grammatical context.
2. 1689 LBCF as confessional synthesis and guard.
3. Primary Reformed/Puritan/classical Protestant sources.
4. Conservative biblical counseling / pastoral literature.
5. Historical examples.
6. Modern secondary material only as subordinate wisdom.

This pass is intentionally **not** psychology-led.

---

# 2. Central thesis being stress-tested

> A parent of a wayward adult child must relinquish child-level control when jurisdiction changes, but Scripture does not command the parent to relinquish love, prayer, truthful admonition, all contact, or all purposeful pursuit. Conversely, love does not require financing, housing, concealing, or facilitating a settled sinful pattern. The morally faithful response is case-dependent and must distinguish relationship, authority, aid, enabling, household stewardship, church discipline, and safeguarding.

This thesis survives the source pass better than either extreme:

- perpetual parental control;
- immediate total disengagement.

# 20. Source synthesis: what the corpus collectively DOES support

## Strong

1. Adult-child relation changes materially as the child becomes self-governing.
2. Lifelong honor/family relation is not identical with perpetual minor-child obedience.
3. Parents may continue prayer, concern, admonition, and relationship after a child leaves.
4. Scripture gives no universal no-contact command for prodigals.
5. Love does not require moral affirmation.
6. Love can include refusing resources that sustain sin.
7. Natural/lawful consequences may be providentially useful.
8. Parents must not pretend they can regenerate by technique.
9. Household rules for an adult resident are a legitimate prudential category.
10. Protecting younger siblings can warrant stronger access/residence restrictions.
11. Formal church discipline does not automatically dissolve family duty.
12. Genuine return should be met with mercy, not vindictive probation.

# 22. Important source tensions, not to be harmonized dishonestly

## Tension A — Gouge/Baxter historical household authority vs modern adulthood

The Puritans often assume stronger parental government than modern Western law/culture.

Yet Baxter/Gouge themselves distinguish grown/self-governing children from minors.

Use the distinction; do not import every seventeenth-century civil assumption.

## Tension B — maintaining relationship vs redemptive release

BCC/Ligonier emphasize continuing prayer/pleading/relationship.

Rugged Love emphasizes consequences and releasing control/resources.

These are not necessarily contradictory.

A faithful synthesis is often:

> relationship remains; enabling stops.

## Tension C — mercy vs restored trust

Luke 15 depicts astonishing reception.

Modern safeguarding/household prudence may still require staged restoration of practical privileges.

Do not make the parable say more than it says.

## Tension D — family relation vs church discipline

1 Cor. 5 has real separation language.

9Marks' Reformed source survey shows family duties remain.

Therefore one institution's discipline does not annihilate another institution's divinely created relation.

# 23. New publication-grade thesis candidates

> **A grown child's freedom from parental management is not freedom from parental love, and neither is it freedom from God's law.**

> **The Christian parent must learn two relinquishments at once: relinquish the fantasy of controlling the adult heart, and relinquish the fantasy that love requires financing everything the heart chooses.**

> **Sometimes love follows with a message; sometimes love stands still; sometimes love closes the wallet; sometimes love opens the dining-room door. Wisdom asks what tells the truth and does actual good in this concrete case.**

> **The prodigal's father is not a proof-text for parental disappearance. The chapter begins with a shepherd searching and a woman sweeping the house. Jesus is teaching the joy of recovering the lost, not issuing a universal communication policy.**

> **When the adult child leaves, the parent's jurisdiction may shrink overnight. The parent's calling to holiness, prayer, truth, repentance for his own sins, and love does not.**

> **Do not keep a prodigal comfortable in the far country with Christian money; do not make the far country artificially cruel in order to imitate providence.**

All are synthesis, not quotations.
