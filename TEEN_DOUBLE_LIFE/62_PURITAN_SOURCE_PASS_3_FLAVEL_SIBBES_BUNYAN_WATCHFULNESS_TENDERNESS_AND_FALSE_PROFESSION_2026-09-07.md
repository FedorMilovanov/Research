# Puritan Source Pass 3 — Flavel, Sibbes, Bunyan

**Status:** RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** deepen the Scripture-first pastoral framework with additional Reformed/Puritan voices that correct opposite errors: carelessness around temptation, crushing the weak, and tolerating fruitless profession.

---

# 1. John Flavel — keeping the heart before temptation reaches full strength

Flavel’s *Saint Indeed / Keeping the Heart* is especially useful because it refuses to treat sin merely as the final outward act.

He describes a progression in temptation:

1. object presents/provokes;
2. appetite is stirred by perceived pleasure/profit;
3. mind consults about means;
4. will chooses;
5. desire becomes fully engaged.

His point is pastoral, not clinical: the temptation is easier to resist earlier, before the heart has yielded and the process has gained strength.

Sources:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed/saintindeed.v.ii.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed.v.iii.ix.html

## Project application

This strongly supports a practical principle already drawn from Matthew 5/Owen:

> **Do not make the final click the first line of defense.**

The real battle may need to occur at:

- device location;
- time of night;
- private browsing permission;
- group-chat membership;
- choice of company;
- initial entertainment feed;
- first tolerated sexual joke;
- first rationalization.

This should not become a mechanistic “five stages of every sin.” Flavel’s use is pastoral wisdom: early watchfulness is easier than late rescue.

# 2. Flavel — the pleasure argument must be answered with the whole end of sin

Flavel directly addresses Satan’s argument from pleasure: sin comes with an attractive face and real promise of delight. His answer is not to deny the delight but to set it against conscience, judgment and the whole consequence of sin.

Source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed.v.iii.ix.html

This fits Hebrews 11:25 / Proverbs 9:17–18 and the project’s central formulation:

> **Sin often gives enough immediate sweetness to make the lie persuasive while hiding the end of the road.**

This is far superior to saying pornography or vulgarity is attractive only because teenagers “do not understand consequences.”

---

# 3. Flavel — keeping the heart presupposes regeneration

Flavel explicitly says true “keeping of the heart” presupposes a prior work of sanctifying grace that has given the heart a new spiritual bent; external means cannot make an unsanctified heart right with God.

Source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed/saintindeed.v.i.html

## Crucial guard

This does **not** mean external restraints are useless for the unregenerate. It means they cannot produce the new governing principle of grace.

Thus:

`boundary` can restrain conduct;

`instruction` can teach truth;

`church` can place someone under means;

`parental discipline` can govern a household;

but

`regeneration` must come from God.

This reinforces the Charnock/Owen distinction already locked in the corpus.

# 4. Richard Sibbes — do not crush the bruised reed

Sibbes’ *The Bruised Reed* supplies an essential counterweight to severe pastoral rhetoric.

His governing text is Isaiah 42:3 as applied to Christ in Matthew 12:20: Christ does not break the bruised reed or quench the smoking flax.

Sibbes repeatedly emphasizes:

- grace can be very small at first;
- weak believers can be genuinely weak;
- ministers/governors must be tender with weak beginnings;
- weakness is not identical to hypocrisy;
- bruising after sin can drive a believer more deeply to Christ rather than prove rejection.

Sources:
- https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Bruised_Reed_and_Smoking_Flax.html?id=45W7Se5darAC
- https://www.apuritansmind.com/puritan-favorites/puritan-favorites-richard-sibbes-1577-1635/the-bruised-reed-by-richard-sibbes/

## Project importance

Without Sibbes, a strict article could accidentally teach:

> “Any adolescent who falls badly should be treated as a hardened hypocrite.”

Sibbes prevents this.

A bruised, ashamed, weak believer may require:

- real correction;
- real consequences;
- real boundaries;

while also requiring extraordinary tenderness because Christ Himself deals gently with weak grace.

---

# 5. Sibbes — “weakness” is not “peace with sin”

Sibbes should not be weaponized in the opposite direction.

His comfort is for bruised reeds/smoking flax, not for a person demanding permission to cherish rebellion.

He distinguishes the work of grace from full-strength perfection and notes that God’s children can sin seriously, yet there is contrary spiritual life and subsequent self-condemnation/return to mercy.

Source:
- https://www.apuritansmind.com/puritan-favorites/puritan-favorites-richard-sibbes-1577-1635/the-bruised-reed-by-richard-sibbes/

Thus the project can hold both:

> **Do not break the weak struggler.**

and

> **Do not rename settled rebellion “weakness.”**

# 6. Bunyan — fruitless profession inside the visible church

John Bunyan’s *The Barren Fig-Tree* is valuable for the project’s concern about religious profession used as cover.

Bunyan treats the fig tree planted in the vineyard as an image of a person occupying a place of visible religious privilege/profession while remaining fruitless. His rhetoric is severe about the presumption of carrying God’s name while lacking grace.

Source:
- https://johnbunyan.org/en/the-barren-fig-tree/

## Use with strong contextual guard

Do not use Bunyan to say:

- every choir child is a church member;
- every immature child with poor fruit is a “barren fig tree” condemned by Christ;
- church presence itself is hypocrisy.

Use him to sharpen the general warning:

> **Religious proximity and religious naming can become dangerous self-deception when they are treated as substitutes for grace and fruit.**

This is especially relevant when a known deliberate double life is allowed to continue while visible church roles signal “everything is fine.”

# 7. Brooks + Flavel + Owen form a coherent temptation triad

### Brooks — the lie

- show bait, hide hook;
- minimize sin;
- promise easy later repentance;
- use wicked company.

Source orientation:
- https://www.eternallifeministries.org/tb_remedies.htm

### Flavel — the heart process

- object provokes;
- pleasure attracts;
- mind consults;
- will chooses;
- desire gains strength.

### Owen — the occasion / particular weakness

- know your frame;
- know peculiar lust;
- identify occasions matched to it;
- watch and pray before entering temptation.

Sources:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.viii.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.x.html

## Modern mapping

A smartphone can compress all three:

`bait` + `occasion` + `private access` + `repeat availability`.

This is the right way to speak of digital amplification without inventing new anthropology.

# 8. Sibbes + Watson form a coherent post-discovery balance

### Watson

Repentance must include more than fear of punishment; it includes sight, sorrow, confession, hatred and turning.

### Sibbes

Weak grace must not be crushed, and Christ’s tenderness toward a bruised believer should shape ministers/governors.

Together:

> **Do not confuse tears with repentance; do not despise genuine weak repentance because it is still weak.**

This is precisely the balance needed after a child is caught in pornography/sexual sin.

# 9. Puritan pastoral architecture now available to the project

The research corpus can now use an integrated classical framework:

- **Owen:** indwelling sin, mortification, particular lusts, entering temptation;
- **Brooks:** devices of Satan, bait/hook, easy-repentance deception, company;
- **Watson:** anatomy of repentance and counterfeit sorrow;
- **Baxter:** parental government, children’s responsibility, bad company, tender love + correction;
- **Flavel:** keeping heart, early-stage temptation, pleasure/cost;
- **Sibbes:** Christ’s tenderness toward weak grace;
- **Bunyan:** danger of fruitless religious profession;
- **Spurgeon:** child conversion now, no hereditary religion, careful but real admission of converted children.

This is broad enough that the final article need not over-rely on any one Puritan as if he were inspired.

---

# 10. Publication red-lines

1. Puritans are witnesses/counselors, not the canon.
2. Do not quote severe rhetoric without context/qualification.
3. Do not convert Bunyan’s church-profession imagery into automatic condemnation of nonmember children.
4. Do not use Sibbes to excuse settled rebellion.
5. Do not use Owen/Flavel to imply believers save themselves by vigilance.
6. Do not use Baxter’s historical household specifics as direct modern civil law.
7. Always return to the biblical text that governs the doctrine/application.
