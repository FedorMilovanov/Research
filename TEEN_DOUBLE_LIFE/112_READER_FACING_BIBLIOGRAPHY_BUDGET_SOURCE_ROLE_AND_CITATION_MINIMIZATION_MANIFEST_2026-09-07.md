# 0. Problem

Research now contains well over one hundred files and a very large source bank.

That is good for verification and bad for publication if transferred mechanically.

A reader-facing article should not communicate rigor by dumping every source the researchers opened. It should communicate rigor by making every important claim traceable to the **best source actually used**.

Therefore distinguish:

## Verification corpus

Everything needed to test, challenge, corroborate, prune and qualify claims.

## Reader bibliography

Only sources that materially support claims visible in the published article.

> **A source may be essential to Research and unnecessary in the public bibliography.**

This is source compression, not evidence deletion.

---

# 1. Product-policy constraints already verified

Current editorial policy requires:

- primary texts before secondary summaries where available;
- official/archival sources for attributed historical/theological positions;
- direct links for strong attributions, quotations, dates and disputed claims;
- Russian reader-facing prose and Russian renderings of English quotations;
- English titles/DOIs/URLs may remain as bibliographic identifiers;
- no Wikipedia as evidentiary foundation;
- `validate:all` and `audit-pro` gates for content changes.

The policy does **not** require showing every source consulted.

Therefore this manifest adds an editorial budget, not a competing policy.

---

# 2. Source-role taxonomy

Every source entering a public article should have one clear role.

## S1 — Primary biblical text

Scripture is the governing moral/theological authority.

Reader citation pattern:

- verse references in prose;
- full quotation only when the wording itself is being interpreted;
- avoid reproducing six parallel passages where two establish the point.

## S2 — Confessional synthesis

1689 LBCF is a subordinate doctrinal synthesis/guard.

Use when it clarifies:

- regeneration;
- repentance;
- perseverance;
- moral law;
- worship;
- visible church.

Do not cite the confession merely to repeat a point Scripture just made if no confessional distinction is added.

## S3 — Primary historical pastoral witness

Examples:

- Brooks;
- Owen;
- Watson;
- Charnock;
- Calvin;
- Gill;
- Spurgeon;
- Sibbes.

Role:

- sharpen a pastoral mechanism;
- demonstrate historical continuity;
- supply one memorable formulation.

They do not create an inspired command.

## S4 — Modern official descriptive source

Examples:

- Pew;
- WHO/HBSC;
- UNICEF;
- NCMEC;
- Children’s Commissioner.

Role:

- quantify contemporary access/contact/exposure/reporting;
- describe modern digital pathways;
- document victimization/safeguarding conditions.

Do not use official status to inflate what the dataset proves.

## S5 — Peer-reviewed empirical synthesis

Prefer systematic reviews/meta-analyses/longitudinal reviews when the claim is broad.

Role:

- association/heterogeneity;
- prevalence ranges;
- causality limitations;
- clinical distinctions.

## S6 — Modern pastoral secondary source

Use sparingly.

Role:

- practical illustration;
- contemporary application;
- not the foundation for exegesis or historical attribution when better sources exist.

# 3. Global public-source budget

These are **editorial ceilings/targets**, not divine rules and not product-policy limits.

The purpose is to prevent citation inflation.

## Per core article

Target:

- **6–10 visible primary Scripture anchors**;
- **1–3 confessional paragraph clusters** where materially useful;
- **2–4 historical writers** total;
- **2–5 modern factual/empirical sources** total;
- **0–2 modern pastoral secondary sources** only if they contribute something unique.

Expected public bibliography:

> **roughly 10–18 non-Bible source entries per article**, not 40–70.

A long article may exceed this only because distinct visible claims genuinely require distinct sources.

## Adult companion

Target:

- 6–10 Scripture anchors;
- 1–3 confessional clusters;
- 3–5 historical sources because family/church-discipline continuity matters;
- little or no modern empirical material;
- local law/safeguarding source only if the article gives jurisdiction-specific procedural advice.

Expected non-Bible bibliography:

> **roughly 8–16 sources.**

# 9. Citation stacking rule

Avoid paragraphs ending with six links that all say roughly the same thing.

Use multiple sources together only when they perform different evidentiary functions.

Good stack:

- one primary historical text + one confession paragraph because one shows historical pastoral reasoning and the other states the project’s confessional synthesis.

Good empirical stack:

- one recent systematic review + one direct institutional dataset when one establishes association literature and the other establishes modern exposure scale/pathway.

Bad stack:

- four pastoral websites repeating “parents should talk to teens.”

Bad stack:

- three pornography reviews merely to make the paragraph look stronger.

# 10. Sources that should generally remain backstage

Unless a final sentence specifically needs them, do not expose:

- generic depression/anxiety social-media correlations;
- boredom-proneness literature;
- reward-circuit fMRI studies;
- moral disengagement meta-analyses;
- broad identity-development literature;
- every secrecy/disclosure study;
- every peer-norm study;
- duplicate sexting prevalence meta-analyses;
- adult moral-incongruence literature applied indirectly to teens;
- secondary summaries of Puritans when primary text exists;
- historical sources consulted only to reject an overclaim.

These sources did valuable **audit work** even if the reader never sees them.

---

# 11. Negative-evidence principle

Sometimes a source’s job is to stop us saying something.

Examples:

- pornography reviews stop universal causal language;
- moral-incongruence literature stops equating religious guilt with clinical disorder;
- social-media reviews stop equating high use with problematic use;
- 1 Timothy 4 context stops generic `seared conscience forever` diagnosis.

Such a source does not automatically need a public bibliography entry if the rejected claim never appears in the published article.

Add it publicly only when the article explicitly corrects the myth and cites the correction.

---

# 12. Bibliography ordering recommendation

If current product rendering permits meaningful grouping, prefer conceptual groups rather than a single undifferentiated wall:

1. **Писание и исповедание** — if confession is listed separately from verse references;
2. **Классические источники**;
3. **Современные исследования и официальные данные**;
4. **Дополнительные пастырские материалы** — only if any were actually used.

If product component expects one flat source list, preserve source strength by ordering:

`primary/official → academic synthesis → pastoral secondary`.

Do not invent a new source UI solely for this series without product-design justification.

---

# 13. Reader-facing source annotations

Short annotations can help when source role is otherwise unclear.

Useful:

- `систематический обзор 44 продольных исследований`;
- `опрос подростков 11, 13 и 15 лет в 44 странах/регионах`;
- `первичный текст трактата`;
- `текст Второго Лондонского исповедания 1689 года`.

Avoid:

- internal grades (`A1`, `B1`, `GREEN`);
- Research module numbers;
- audit jargon;
- long defensive explanations after every link.

The reader should see why a source matters without seeing the whole internal control plane.

---

# 14. Final bibliography test per article

Before release, inspect every non-Bible source and ask:

1. Does the published prose actually use a claim from this source?
2. Is this the strongest available source for that claim?
3. Is there a primary/official source we can link instead?
4. Does the wording preserve the source’s scope/sample/context?
5. Is this source duplicated by another source doing the same job?
6. If removed, would any reader-facing claim become unsupported?
7. If the source only constrained an overclaim that was removed, can it remain backstage?
8. Is a direct quotation translated into Russian and verified in the original?
9. Is the bibliography entry an identifier/source, not an English quotation embedded in prose?
10. Would the article still look rigorous if the source list were half this length? If yes, prune again.

---

# 15. Final source-minimization verdict

The Research corpus should remain large.

The public bibliography should remain selective.

Recommended visible non-Bible source targets:

- **Part I:** 8–10
- **Part II:** 5–7
- **Part III:** 5–7
- **Adult companion:** 4–7

These are editorial targets, not hard policy caps.

The controlling principle is:

> **Not “how many sources did we find?” but “what is the strongest direct source required to support what we actually published?”**
