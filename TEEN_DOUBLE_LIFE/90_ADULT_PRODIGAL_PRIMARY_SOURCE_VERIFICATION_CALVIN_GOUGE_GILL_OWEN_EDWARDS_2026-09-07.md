## 1. Luke 15 must not be turned into a mechanical parenting protocol

### Primary source: John Calvin, Commentary on Luke 15:11–24

Source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom32/calcom32.ii.lxi.html
- alternate legacy HTML: https://www.ccel.org/c/calvin/comment3/comm_vol32/htm/lxii.htm

Calvin explicitly states that the parable confirms the preceding teaching about God receiving repentant sinners. The younger son represents sinners returning to grace; the father represents God’s readiness to forgive.

He also explicitly warns against over-comparing the earthly father and God: a mortal father cannot renew a stubborn heart, whereas God can change the heart.

### VERIFIED publication claims

**Strongly supported:**

> Luke 15 is primarily a revelation of divine mercy toward repentant sinners and a rebuke of those who resent such mercy, not a detailed parenting manual for adult children.

**Strongly supported:**

> The narrative cannot legitimately be used to command universal `no contact`, universal pursuit, universal financial rescue, or a fixed number of warnings after an adult child leaves.

Reason: none of those procedural details are supplied by the text.

### Calvin on repentance

Calvin links the son’s `came to himself` and confession with:
- recognition of sin;
- grief/shame over sin;
- confession;
- return.

This supports the project distinction:

> **physical return home ≠ automatic repentance.**

The Lukan son does not merely relocate. He returns in an explicitly penitential posture.

### Important guard

Do not infer:

> `The father saw him while far off` = `the father stood at the road every day watching the horizon`.

The latter is devotional imagination, not stated narrative data.

# 2. William Gouge — grown children remain under the fifth commandment, but subjection changes

Primary EEBO source:
- https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A68107.0001.001/1%3A5.96?rgn=div2&view=fulltext

Accessible modernization:
- https://christianreader.app/books/of-domestical-duties/07-treatise-5-duties-of-children

Gouge explicitly asks what happens when children are grown and no longer under parental government.

His answer is highly useful for this project:

- there may be a difference in parts and manner of subjection;
- being grown/freed from household government changes the form of relation;
- yet as long as the parent-child relation remains, honour remains due.

### VERIFIED project synthesis

> **Maturity can alter the form and extent of parental government without abolishing filial honour.**

### Forbidden overclaim

Do not write:

> `Gouge says an adult 18-year-old must obey every parental command just like a minor.`

He does not say that. He explicitly distinguishes those still under government from those freed from it.

# 3. John Gill — parental power lessens when children are grown, but filial reverence does not cease

Primary source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html

Gill writes in his chapter on the respective duties of parents and children that:

- children are under real duties to parents;
- parental power over children is **less when grown up**;
- observance, gratitude, and filial reverence do not therefore cease;
- duties arise from a natural relation.

Gill also connects filial duty with 1 Tim. 5:4 and repayment/care toward parents.

### VERIFIED project synthesis

> **Adult independence does not mean moral independence from all family duty.**

### Useful distinction

`less parental power`  
≠ `no relationship`  
≠ `no right to counsel`  
≠ `no duty of honour`.

# 4. 1 Timothy 5 and Mark 7 strongly disprove “adulthood ends family obligation”

Primary text/commentary:
- Calvin on 1 Tim. 5:1–4: https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43/calcom43.iii.vii.i.html
- 1 Tim. 5 text/context: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/c/calvin/comment2/1tim.htm
- Calvin, Fifth Commandment: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom05.ii.i.ii.html
- Mark 7 text: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/bible/asv.Mark.7.html
- Matthew Henry on Mark 7: https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc5.Mark.viii.html

1 Tim. 5:4 places on children/grandchildren a duty to show piety toward their own household and repay parents.

Mark 7:9–13 applies `honour father and mother` to material care for needy parents; Jesus condemns a religious mechanism used to escape this obligation.

### VERIFIED project thesis

> **The New Testament itself applies the fifth-commandment principle beyond childhood dependence.**

Therefore the project may firmly reject:

> `Once you turn 18, the fifth commandment no longer has any practical claim on you.`

### Guard

This does **not** prove that parents retain minor-child governance indefinitely.

It proves continuing honour/duty, not perpetual infantilization.

# 5. Owen — excommunication does not dissolve natural/moral family duties

Primary source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/evangelicalchurches/evangelicalchurches.iii.xiv.html

Owen explicitly distinguishes ordinary voluntary social converse from obligations arising from antecedent natural/moral relationships.

He states that excommunication does not release duties belonging to:
- husband/wife;
- parents/children;
- magistrate/subject;
- master/servant;
- blood relations.

He also says an excommunicated person may/ought still to be admonished while willing to hear admonition.

### VERIFIED project thesis

> **Even the strongest formal church censure does not erase the parent-child relation or every continuing duty of care and admonition.**

Therefore ordinary adult-child rebellion cannot automatically justify invented `family excommunication` that is stricter than historic Reformed ecclesiology itself.

### Guard

Owen does restrict ordinary chosen fellowship with an excommunicated church member. Do not flatten his view into:

> `excommunication changes nothing relationally`.

It changes church fellowship. It does not dissolve prior natural duties.

# 6. John Gill — same conclusion from Baptist ecclesiology

Primary source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.iii.vi.html

Gill says excommunication:
- does not invade civil affairs;
- does not break natural/civil relations of parents and children;
- does not forbid attendance on external ministry of the Word.

### VERIFIED project thesis

This strongly supports the project distinction:

`church discipline` ≠ `erasure of family relation` ≠ `ban from hearing preaching`.

It also strengthens the earlier youth/church-access matrix:

`public hearing of Word`  
≠ `membership`  
≠ `Table`  
≠ `trusted ministry access`.

# 7. Jonathan Edwards — parental affection/care remain even under excommunication

Primary source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/edwards/works2.v.html

Edwards explicitly preserves:
- prayer for the censured person;
- admonition;
- ordinary humane help in distress;
- natural/civil relationship duties;
- parental affection and care toward children.

### VERIFIED project thesis

> **Biblical separation from scandalous sin is not equivalent to emotional abandonment, refusal of humane aid, or cancellation of parenthood.**

This gives strong historical support for the current formula:

> `do not finance the far country` does not mean `stop doing all good to the person in the far country`.

# 8. Calvin on 2 Timothy 2:25 — sovereign repentance increases, not decreases, patient admonition

Primary source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43/calcom43.iv.iii.v.html

Calvin says even when a case appears nearly hopeless, the servant of Christ should exercise meekness and make the attempt to restore, because repentance is God’s gift.

### VERIFIED project synthesis

> **Belief in sovereign regeneration is an argument against manipulative control, but it is not an argument for pastoral passivity.**

Our duty:
- sow;
- water;
- teach;
- admonish in meekness.

God gives increase/repentance.

This aligns extremely well with the project’s Reformed synthesis of sovereign grace + ordained means.

# 9. Calvin on Titus 3:10 — important category-error correction

Primary source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43.v.v.iv.html

Calvin reads Titus 3:10 as solemn ministerial/church correction of a heretical/factious person.

### REJECTED overread

Do **not** write:

> `The Bible says after two conversations with a rebellious adult son, stop speaking to him.`

That is not the contextual claim of Titus 3:10.

### Allowed use

Titus 3 contributes to a broader biblical recognition that:
- admonition can reach a point of recognized futility;
- endless quarrelling is not always faithfulness;
- formal ecclesial cases can require withdrawal.

But its `first and second admonition` is **not a universal family-contact counter**.

# 10. 2 Samuel 13:21 — textual-critical verification

Source:
- NET Bible textual note: https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=2Sa&chapter=13&tab=commentaries&verse=21

NET correctly notes that the LXX and part of the Old Latin tradition contain an expansion stating that David would not grieve/punish Amnon because he loved him as firstborn.

The Masoretic Text lacks that clause.

### REQUIRED publication guard

If the final article uses the detail:

> `David did not punish Amnon because he loved him as firstborn`,

it must be labelled as a **textual variant / ancient textual witness**, not simply quoted as uncontested Hebrew narrative.

### Safe canonical claim without variant

The MT securely states:
- David heard;
- David was very angry.

The subsequent narrative does not report David judicially resolving the crime before Absalom’s revenge.

Do not claim more than the text supplies.

# 11. Source-verification verdict

## GREEN / strong

- Luke 15 primary meaning = divine mercy toward repentant sinners, not procedural adult-child protocol.
- Luke 15 return includes repentance-confession, not mere geography.
- Gouge: grown children still owe honour though subjection changes.
- Gill: parental power lessens with maturity while filial reverence remains.
- 1 Tim. 5 / Mark 7: adult family obligations remain real.
- Owen/Gill/Edwards: church censure does not erase natural family duties.
- Calvin 2 Tim. 2:25: sovereign repentance supports patient, meek instruction.
- Calvin Titus 3:10: ecclesial context blocks simplistic `two warnings then no contact` use.

## YELLOW / use with qualification

- David’s failure to punish Amnon: narratively plausible/strong, but the explicit motive `because he loved his firstborn` is textually variant.
- `letting consequences teach` from Luke 15: acceptable as providential/pastoral analogy, not a command to engineer suffering.
- adult-child house rules: prudent application, not a detailed NT statute.

## RED / do not publish

- `Luke 15 commands parents to stop contacting a child who leaves.`
- `Luke 15 commands parents to chase the child continuously.`
- `The father waited at the road every day.`
- `Titus 3:10 gives parents a two-warning cutoff rule.`
- `Turning 18 abolishes fifth-commandment duty.`
- `Excommunication dissolves parent-child duties.`
- `David’s motive in 2 Sam. 13:21 is textually uncontested.`

## Publication-ready synthesis

> **Scripture does not give parents a technique for regenerating a prodigal. It gives them duties: truth without manipulation, love without complicity, prayer without presumption, honour for changed adult jurisdiction, refusal to finance protected rebellion, willingness to admonish while admonition can be heard, and readiness to receive real repentance without pretending that trust and access are identical to forgiveness. The heart remains God’s domain.**
