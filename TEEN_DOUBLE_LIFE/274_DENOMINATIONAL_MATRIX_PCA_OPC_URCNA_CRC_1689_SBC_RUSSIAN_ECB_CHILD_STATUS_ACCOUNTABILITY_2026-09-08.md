## 2. PCA — strongest Presbyterian formalization found

Current 2026 PCA BCO is unusually useful for the project because it makes the child’s ecclesial relation concrete rather than merely sacramental.

### Core controls

- Visible church: professing believers **together with their children**.
- Children of believers: non-communing members through covenant/right of birth.
- Entitlements/duties include Baptism, pastoral oversight, instruction, and government.
- All baptized persons are under watchful care/instruction/government even if adult and not yet professing.
- All baptized persons, as church members, are subject to discipline and entitled to its benefits.
- Parents carry primary spiritual nurture/training but are responsible to the church for discharging it.
- Church is to keep constant relations with children, encourage profession at discretion, and reclaim the wayward.
- Baptismal directory: at age of discretion the child is subject to covenant obligations — faith, repentance, obedience — and must either confess Christ or stand as covenant-breaker subject to discipline.

### Project significance

This is the clearest answer to the user’s intuition that paedobaptist polity can produce a different *formal* accountability structure before an adolescent makes a profession. The child cannot be classified as ecclesially unrelated simply because he has not become a communicant.

### Guard

`SUBJECT_TO_DISCIPLINE` must not be flattened into `IDENTICAL_JUDICIAL_CENSURE_AT_EVERY_AGE`.

PCA discipline uses a broad sense including governance/training/guardianship as well as judicial process. Future research should distinguish formative discipline of non-communing children from judicial censure/excommunication procedures for professing members.

## 3. URCNA — strongest mature-baptized-member disciplinary case

The current 2026 Church Order retains a dedicated Article 59 category:

> mature baptized members delinquent in doctrine or life are admonished and, if persistent, excluded from the church; classis advice is sought before exclusion.

Article 60 then requires an excluded baptized member who returns to be readmitted only upon public profession of faith.

Synod Calgary 2026 material explicitly explains this as a process distinct from excommunication of professing/communicant members and traces the category to Dutch Reformed polity.

### Project significance

This is a concrete example where covenant-child membership has institutional consequences before public profession. It directly prevents the Baptist-style practical inference:

`no personal profession yet -> therefore no formal ecclesial accountability`.

### Guard against caricature

URCNA synodical material has also rejected the presumption that baptized children should simply be held regenerate until they prove otherwise. Therefore the strongest Dutch Reformed model is:

`OBJECTIVE BAPTIZED MEMBERSHIP + PERSONAL CALL TO FAITH/REPENTANCE + REAL DISCIPLINE`

not:

`BAPTIZED -> CERTAINLY REGENERATE`.

## 4. CRCNA — do not merge into generic Dutch Reformed practice

CRCNA is important as a separate comparator because its present Table policy is more baptism-to-Table continuous than PCA/OPC/URCNA.

Current denominational material allows baptized members who demonstrate age- and ability-appropriate faith and obedience to participate under elder supervision, without making one formal profession mechanism an absolute scriptural prerequisite.

### Project consequence

Never write:

> “Reformed paedobaptists all treat baptism, profession, membership and Communion identically.”

They do not.

## 5. 1689 — the Baptist model is not low-accountability by confession

### 1689 16.7

Works of unregenerate persons may materially be things God commands and may be useful to themselves/others. They do not spiritually please God as saving obedience, yet neglect of them is more sinful and displeasing.

### 1689 19.5–6

The moral law forever binds all — justified and others — because of God’s authority as Creator; it is useful to believers and also to others to reveal duty, guilt, and need.

### 1689 22.3, 22.6

Prayer is required of all; private-family worship and public assembly remain normal Christian duties/order.

### 1689 26 + 29

Visible congregations are constituted by visible saints/professors; baptism belongs to those who actually profess repentance, faith, and obedience.

### Synthesis

1689 deliberately separates:

`MORAL/FILIAL/RELIGIOUS DUTY NOW`

from

`VISIBLE-CHURCH MEMBERSHIP/BAPTISM AFTER CREDIBLE PROFESSION`.

Therefore a Baptist who says “we cannot demand obedience because he is unregenerate” is not following the logic of 1689 anthropology or law.

## 6. SBC — contemporary broad Baptist comparator

BF&M 2000 holds together:

- believer’s baptism;
- baptism as prerequisite to membership privileges / Table;
- parental duty to teach spiritual and moral values;
- consistent example and loving discipline;
- child duty to honor and obey parents.

### Significance

The weak-child-accountability problem cannot be attributed to credobaptism as such. It is a pastoral/formation failure that can occur inside credobaptist churches despite their official doctrine.

## 7. Russian Union ECB — locally relevant comparator

Official doctrinal/social-concept materials preserve two principles simultaneously:

1. the church should consist of the regenerate and baptism follows personal faith/new birth;
2. parents bear primary responsibility for children’s spiritual upbringing, while church assists rather than replaces the family.

### Project use

This allows the final Russian article to criticize permissive Baptist practice without falsely telling Russian Baptists that their official regenerate-church ecclesiology itself requires permissiveness.

## 8. Comparative diagnosis

### Paedobaptist structural strength

`CHILD HAS AN ECCLESIAL ADDRESS`.

Someone can point to the membership roll, session/consistory, baptismal vows, catechesis obligation and disciplinary process. There is less institutional room for the church to classify the child as merely a parent’s private evangelistic concern.

### Paedobaptist structural danger

`ECCLESIAL STATUS MAY BE MISHEARD AS SOTERIOLOGICAL CERTIFICATE`.

Confessions can guard against this, but culture/practice may still slide into baptismal or covenantal presumption.

### Credobaptist structural strength

`SIGN + MEMBERSHIP FOLLOW CREDIBLE PERSONAL PROFESSION`.

This protects the New Testament connection among discipleship, profession, baptism and visible-church admission.

### Credobaptist structural danger

`THE PRE-MEMBERSHIP CHILD MAY BECOME ECCLESIALLY UNDERDEFINED`.

If parents/pastors infer `not formal member -> ordinary outsider`, a church-raised child can receive enormous gospel privilege but little named pastoral accountability. This is a practice defect, not a necessary consequence of 1689.

## 9. Publication-safe conclusion

> The confessional contrast is not “Presbyterian children have moral duties, Baptist children do not.” Both traditions have strong resources for present child obedience and urgent repentance. The decisive institutional contrast is that Presbyterian/Dutch Reformed churches ordinarily assign children of believers formal visible-church status before personal profession, while Baptist churches reserve that status for credible professors. This difference can materially affect how responsibility is organized and enforced — but neither model may identify ecclesial status with regeneration, and neither permits unbelief to become a moral exemption.
