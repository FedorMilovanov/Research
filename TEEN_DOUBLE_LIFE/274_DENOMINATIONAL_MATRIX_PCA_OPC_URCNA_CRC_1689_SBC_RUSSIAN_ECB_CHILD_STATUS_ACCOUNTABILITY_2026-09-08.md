# 2. PCA — current polity is stronger than “pastoral oversight only”

Primary current source:

- PCA Administrative Committee, **Book of Church Order 2026**:
  https://www.pcaac.org/bco/
  https://www.pcaac.org/book-of-church-order/

### Current controls

- visible church includes professing believers together with their children;
- children of believers are non-communing members through covenant/right of birth;
- baptism, pastoral oversight, instruction and government belong to their formal ecclesial status;
- BCO 27.1 defines discipline in a **broad** sense (government, inspection, training, guardianship, control) and a **restricted technical** sense (judicial process);
- BCO 27.2: **all baptized persons, being members of the Church, are subject to its discipline**;
- current Part II contains a dedicated chapter 28, `Disciplining of Non-communing Members`;
- BCO 29.1 defines an offense as a proper object of judicial process in terms of a church member **professing faith in Christ**;
- parents carry primary spiritual nurture/training and are accountable to the church for that duty;
- church should maintain relation with children, encourage profession at discretion, and reclaim the wayward.

### Correction to an earlier provisional reading

Two opposite summaries are unsafe.

First, do **not** say:

> `non-communing children receive only soft pastoral care and are outside formal church discipline`.

That is too weak. Current BCO 27.2 expressly places **all baptized members** under church discipline, and chapter 28 gives non-communing members a formal nurture/government/reclamation framework.

Second, do **not** infer from BCO 27.1–27.2 that an ordinary technical judicial prosecution necessarily applies to a non-professing child in the same way it applies to a professing communicant. BCO 29.1 is a real limiting control: the proper object of judicial process is framed as the conduct of a church member **professing faith in Christ**.

Historical Southern Presbyterian discussions about limits on judicial prosecution may illuminate development, but the current conclusion should rest on the current PCA text itself rather than substituting older debates for it.

### Required guard

The safe current statement is:

> **PCA non-communing children are real members under formal church discipline, instruction, government and reclamation; technical judicial process must be stated only where the applicable BCO provisions actually establish it, and BCO 29.1 prevents treating 27.2 as a blanket proof of ordinary judicial prosecution before profession.**

Therefore do not write either:

> `PCA has no formal discipline before profession`,

or:

> `a baptized toddler receives the same technical judicial process and censures as an adult communicant`.

### Project significance

The PCA model materially strengthens the user’s intuition: before personal profession, a church child is already formally on the church’s pastoral/governmental map. Yet baptism still does not create basic moral responsibility or guarantee regeneration.

# 3. OPC — exact noncommunicant discipline/removal pathway verified

Primary current source:

- OPC **Book of Discipline**:
  https://www.opc.org/BCO/BD.html
- OPC **Form of Government / Directory for Worship**:
  https://www.opc.org/BCO/

### Current controls

OPC distinguishes:

- communicant members;
- noncommunicant members, ordinarily baptized covenant children.

Both are real church members under sessional pastoral oversight, instruction and government.

The decisive current locator is **Book of Discipline II.B.3.d(6)**:

- the session continually exhorts the noncommunicant to profess faith in Christ;
- if the person neglects that exhortation **and rejects covenantal responsibility of submission to home or church**;
- the session may, after prior notification, **erase the name from the roll**.

The Book of Discipline separately states that **erasure is an act of discipline without full process**.

The Directory also preserves the transition rule:

`NONCOMMUNICANT -> COMMUNICANT = CONFESSION OF FAITH`.

### Project significance

This closes an important evidentiary gap. OPC child membership is not merely ceremonial inclusion. It produces a real ecclesial obligation to profession, home/church submission, and a real disciplinary/removal consequence if mature covenant youth persistently refuse that relation.

### Guard

`ERASURE ≠ EXCOMMUNICATION WITH FULL PROCESS`.

Do not collapse distinct censures/procedures just because both are forms of church discipline.

# 4. URCNA — clearest mature-baptized-member exclusion rule

Current official source identity:

- URCNA Publications, **Church Order Tenth Edition [Ratified 2024 w/ 2026 Amendments]**, posted 2026-08-26:
  https://www.urcna.org/pubsarts/

Current Article 59 core language was already operative before the 2026 cleanup proposal:

- mature baptized member delinquent in doctrine/life;
- admonition;
- persistence;
- classis advice;
- exclusion from the church of Christ.

Article 60 then governs return/readmission through public profession.

### 2026 ratification nuance

Synod Calgary 2026 considered a minor amendment to Article 59 removing the misleading parenthetical reference to Appendix 8. The **substantive exclusion rule itself was already current**; the proposed change did not create the category.

As of 2026-09-08, URCNA’s official publication page warns that some Calgary 2026 amendments shown in the most up-to-date edition remain highlighted as **not yet ratified**. Therefore Product must not say generically:

> `all Calgary 2026 Church Order amendments are final`.

For this project the safe claim is narrower:

> **Article 59’s mature-baptized-member exclusion mechanism is established current polity; the 2026 cleanup of its Appendix-8 reference has a separate ratification state.**

### Project significance

URCNA gives the most concrete current example in this comparison of pre-profession covenant membership producing formal exclusion consequences.

# 5. CRCNA — do not merge it into generic Dutch Reformed practice

CRCNA is a separate comparator because its present Table policy is more baptism-to-Table continuous than PCA/OPC/URCNA.

Current denominational material permits baptized members who demonstrate age- and ability-appropriate faith and obedience to participate under elder supervision, without making one formal profession mechanism an absolute scriptural prerequisite.

### Project consequence

Never write:

> `Reformed paedobaptists all treat baptism, profession, membership, discipline and Communion identically.`

They do not.

# 6. Continental confessional basis — status is theological, not merely administrative

The institutional model rests on substantive covenant theology.

### Heidelberg Catechism Q74

Children as well as adults are included in God’s covenant and people; baptism incorporates them into the Christian church and distinguishes them from the children of unbelievers.

### Belgic Confession Article 34

Believers’ children are to receive baptism as the covenant sign; the confession explicitly draws a continuity line from circumcision to baptism.

### Canons of Dort I.17

Children of believers are described as holy, not by nature but by the gracious covenant in which they are included with their parents; godly parents are comforted concerning children whom God takes in infancy.

### Guard

A Baptist may learn from the institutional seriousness of this model, but may not silently borrow `covenant child` as a confessionally neutral label. In the Three Forms it carries substantive sacramental/covenantal content.

# 7. 1689 — the Baptist model is not low-accountability by confession

### 1689 16.7

Works of unregenerate persons may materially be things God commands and may be useful to themselves/others. They do not spiritually please God as saving obedience, yet neglect of them is more sinful and displeasing.

### 1689 19.5–6

The moral law forever binds all — justified and others — because of God’s authority as Creator; it is useful to believers and also to others to reveal duty, guilt and need.

### 1689 22.3, 22.6

Prayer is required of all; private-family worship and public assembly remain normal Christian duties/order.

### 1689 26 + 29

Visible congregations are constituted by visible saints/professors; baptism belongs to those who actually profess repentance, faith and obedience.

### Synthesis

1689 deliberately separates:

`MORAL / FILIAL / RELIGIOUS DUTY NOW`

from

`VISIBLE-CHURCH MEMBERSHIP / BAPTISM AFTER CREDIBLE PROFESSION`.

Therefore a Baptist who says `we cannot demand obedience because he is unregenerate` is not following the logic of 1689 anthropology or law.

# 8. SBC — contemporary broad Baptist comparator

BF&M 2000 holds together:

- believer’s baptism;
- baptism as prerequisite to membership privileges / Table;
- parental duty to teach spiritual and moral values;
- consistent example and loving discipline;
- child duty to honor and obey parents.

### Significance

The weak-child-accountability problem cannot be attributed to credobaptism as such. It is a pastoral/formation failure that can occur inside credobaptist churches despite their official doctrine.

# 9. Russian Union ECB — locally relevant comparator

Official doctrinal/social-concept materials preserve two principles simultaneously:

1. the church should consist of the regenerate and baptism follows personal faith/new birth;
2. parents bear primary responsibility for children’s spiritual upbringing, while church assists rather than replaces the family.

### Project use

The final Russian article may criticize permissive Baptist practice without falsely claiming that Russian Baptist regenerate-church ecclesiology itself requires permissiveness.

# 10. Comparative diagnosis

### Paedobaptist structural strength

`CHILD HAS AN ECCLESIAL ADDRESS`.

The membership roll, baptismal vows, session/consistory, catechesis duty and formal discipline give the child a named institutional relation before personal profession. The exact process attached to that discipline remains polity- and status-specific.

### Paedobaptist structural danger

`ECCLESIAL STATUS MAY BE MISHEARD AS SOTERIOLOGICAL CERTIFICATE`.

The confessions themselves contain guards, but culture/practice may still drift into covenantal or baptismal presumption.

### Credobaptist structural strength

`SIGN + MEMBERSHIP FOLLOW CREDIBLE PERSONAL PROFESSION`.

This protects the connection among discipleship, profession, baptism and visible-church admission.

### Credobaptist structural danger

`THE PRE-MEMBERSHIP CHILD MAY BECOME ECCLESIALLY UNDERDEFINED`.

If `not formal member` becomes `ordinary outsider`, a church-raised child can receive enormous gospel privilege while no one assumes named pastoral ownership. This is a practice defect, not a necessary consequence of 1689.

# 12. Publication-safe conclusion

> **The confessional contrast is not “Presbyterian children have moral duties, Baptist children do not.” Both traditions have strong resources for present child obedience and urgent repentance. The decisive institutional contrast is that Presbyterian/Dutch Reformed churches ordinarily assign children of believers formal visible-church status before personal profession, while Baptist churches reserve that status for credible professors. This difference materially affects how pastoral ownership and discipline are organized. Yet neither model may identify ecclesial status with regeneration, and neither permits unbelief to become a moral exemption.**
