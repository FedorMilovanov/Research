# Unregenerate Children, Law, Gospel, Discipline, and the Myth of a Moral Neutral Zone

**Status:** RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Control:** Scripture → 1689 LBCF → Reformed/Puritan pastoral application.

---

## 0. Problem statement

A common practical error in Christian parenting/church ministry sounds like:

> “He is probably unregenerate, so what do you expect? We cannot demand Christian behavior from him. Let him live; pray for salvation.”

This can contain one true premise and several false conclusions.

True:

- regeneration is sovereign;
- an unregenerate child cannot produce the spiritual fruit of regeneration by parental force;
- external obedience is not saving faith.

False conclusions:

- therefore the child is not presently obligated by God’s moral law;
- therefore parents should cease requiring truthfulness, chastity, obedience, restraint, reverence, or correction;
- therefore church leaders should overlook scandalous conduct until conversion;
- therefore moral expectations necessarily create hypocrisy.

The biblical category is not “neutral child awaiting a spiritual event.”

The child is a fallen image-bearer, accountable to God, living under providential authority and in urgent need of Christ.

---

# 1. Total depravity explains sin; it does not excuse sin

Key texts:

- Gen. 6:5 / 8:21 — evil inclination of the human heart;
- Ps. 51:5 — sinfulness from conception;
- Mark 7:20–23 — evil proceeds from within;
- Eph. 2:1–3 — dead in sins, following flesh/world/devil;
- Rom. 8:7–8 — fleshly mind hostile to God and unable to submit to God’s law;
- James 1:14–15 — desire entices, conceives, gives birth to sin.

The Reformed doctrine of inability means **moral inability arising from corrupt nature**, not innocence.

A debtor who hates payment is not released from debt because he is unwilling to pay.

### Publication line

> **Inability to regenerate oneself is not inability to be guilty. The very bondage that makes grace necessary is itself part of man’s fallen condition, not an excuse that suspends God’s claims.**

---

# 2. 1689 LBCF 19.5 explicitly destroys the “no responsibility until saved” idea

The moral law binds:

> “all, as well justified persons as others.”

Ground:

> the authority of God the Creator.

Sources:

- https://the1689.org/chapters/19
- https://1689confession.org/the-law-of-god/

This means the unregenerate child is obligated to:

- worship God;
- honor parents;
- speak truth;
- preserve chastity;
- reject theft/violence;
- reject covetousness;
- repent of sin.

He cannot say:

> “Because I am not saved, the commandments are irrelevant to me.”

Nor can the parent say it for him.

---

# 3. The law has a use for the unregenerate

1689 19.6 says the law is of great use not only to believers but also to others.

It:

- informs of God’s will and duty;
- discovers sinful pollution;
- exposes guilt;
- shows need of Christ;
- forbids sin;
- warns by threatenings.

Therefore biblical parenting of an unregenerate child should not remove the law.

The law is not the power that regenerates, but it is part of the truth by which sin is exposed and the need for Christ becomes intelligible.

### Error to reject

> “Talking about commandments before conversion only produces legalism.”

That would make large portions of biblical evangelism impossible.

Correct pattern:

`law reveals sin/duty → gospel reveals Christ/grace → parent refuses to confuse external compliance with new birth`.

---

# 4. 1689 16.7: even unregenerate obedience is not simply “worthless, so why require it?”

1689 says works of unregenerate people:

- may materially be things God commands;
- may be useful to themselves and others;
- do not please God spiritually because they do not proceed from faith/right manner/right end;
- do not merit grace;
- **yet neglecting them is more sinful and displeasing to God**.

Source:

- https://the1689.org/chapters/16
- https://1689confession.org/good-works/

This is nearly a direct answer to permissive parenting theology.

Example:

An unregenerate teenage son’s truthful confession does not become saving righteousness merely because it is truthful.

But:

> lying is still worse than telling the truth.

An unregenerate daughter’s sexual restraint does not regenerate her.

But:

> fornication is not therefore morally equivalent to restraint.

A boy who obeys a lawful parental boundary is not justified by that obedience.

But:

> defiant disobedience is still sin.

### Strong line

> **External restraint is not regeneration; nevertheless, God never commands parents to prefer visible lawlessness until regeneration arrives.**

---

# 5. Ephesians 6 assumes children can be commanded now

Paul’s address to children is itself decisive against the neutral-zone concept.

Children are commanded to obey and honor.

Parents are commanded to bring them up in:

- discipline (`παιδεία`);
- admonition/instruction (`νουθεσία`) of the Lord.

This is not merely information transfer.

The household is to be ordered under Christ.

Calvin notes that the child’s obedience is grounded in God’s authority and that parental authority is real though subordinate to God.

Sources:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom41/calcom41.vi.vi.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom41/calcom41.iv.vii.i.html?scrBook=Eph&scrCh=6&scrV=2

---

# 6. “Demanding obedience creates hypocrisy” is only partly true

A parent can indeed produce hypocrisy if he teaches:

> “Behave correctly and I will treat you as regenerate.”

or

> “Our family reputation matters more than whether your heart loves God.”

But the answer is not to cease commanding obedience.

Better distinction:

## External conformity falsely interpreted as salvation

Bad.

## External restraint required as creaturely/filial duty while openly teaching need for regeneration

Biblical.

A parent may say:

> “You must not lie because God forbids lying and because I am responsible to govern this home. Obeying me will not save you. Christ alone saves sinners. But your need of salvation does not make lying lawful.”

That is neither legalism nor Pelagianism.

---

# 7. Spurgeon: do not postpone conversion until adulthood

Spurgeon explicitly attacks the idea:

> children can be left unconverted now and dealt with later.

In “Do Not Sin Against the Child,” he calls this neglect and argues that Scripture gives no warrant to delay seeking children’s conversion.

He also insists:

- do not mistake first emotion for conversion;
- do not demand more from children than adults in kind;
- do demand the same **real repentance and faith** in kind;
- do not educate children in self-deception.

Source:

- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child

This is particularly important for ДВР.

### Two opposite forms of child neglect

1. **Presumption:** “He grew up Christian, therefore he is Christian.”
2. **Deferral:** “He is probably not Christian, therefore holiness can wait.”

Spurgeon rejects both.

---

# 8. Spurgeon: inherited profession is dangerous

Spurgeon strongly warns against “hereditary profession” / inherited religion.

Children of believers still require:

- new birth;
- personal repentance;
- personal faith;
- work of the Holy Spirit.

Sources:

- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/a-pastoral-visit
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/sermons10.xviii.html

### Relevance to children’s church roles

A child who sings, attends, memorizes Scripture, serves in a choir or knows Christian vocabulary must not be quietly taught:

> “Those activities themselves make your spiritual condition safe.”

The more visible the church activity, the more carefully adults should avoid confusing ecclesial familiarity with regeneration.

---

# 9. Watson: obedience must be from faith to be evangelical obedience, yet God’s command remains the rule

Thomas Watson distinguishes true evangelical obedience by:

- right rule (Word of God);
- right principle (faith);
- right end (God’s glory);
- universality;
- constancy.

He also explicitly observes that godly parents can have wicked children; birth from believers does not make one a child of God by grace.

Sources:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity.ix.iv.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/divinity/divinity.ix.iv.html

### Use

This helps keep two categories separate:

`the duty commanded` and `the spiritual quality by which obedience is acceptable in Christ`.

Children are not exempt from the first merely because they lack the second.

---

# 10. Baxter: personal instruction, not passive waiting

Richard Baxter’s *Reformed Pastor* presses pastors toward personal catechizing/instruction rather than merely public preaching and passive expectation.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor/pastor.iv.ii.html

His historical parish model is not identical to modern Baptist ecclesiology, but the pastoral principle is useful:

> **known souls under pastoral care should be personally instructed and admonished, not merely allowed to orbit church activities indefinitely while no one addresses their actual life.**

This directly challenges:

> “He attends youth group, so hopefully the environment will eventually fix him.”

If serious rebellion is known, **personal admonition is required**.

---

# 11. Parenting sequence for an unregenerate child

Not a mechanistic formula, but a Scripture-shaped model:

1. **Name God’s command clearly.**
2. **Name the child’s concrete sin clearly.**
3. **Do not call external conformity regeneration.**
4. **Require appropriate obedience in the home.**
5. **Apply consequences proportionate to conduct/risk.**
6. **Explain that inability to love God reveals need for grace rather than permission to rebel.**
7. **Present Christ, forgiveness and new heart urgently.**
8. **Pray for conversion now, not merely someday.**
9. **Do not create church roles that falsely certify the child’s spiritual state.**
10. **Do not withdraw gospel access merely because the child is rebellious.**

---

# 12. Why permissiveness can deepen false assurance

If adults consistently say:

> “He is unconverted, so we cannot expect anything,”

while the child:

- sings in choir;
- goes on youth trips;
- participates in church projects;
- receives no direct rebuke for known sexual profanity/rebellion;
- remains socially recognized as a normal church child,

then the child may learn a practical theology:

> **God’s holiness is serious in sermons but optional in church life.**

This is especially dangerous when he learns he can retain all the **social benefits of Christian belonging** while refusing the moral claims of Christ.

This is a pastoral inference, not an empirical universal law.

---

# 13. Why strictness can also create hypocrisy if gospel truth is absent

The opposite danger:

- family image obsession;
- punishment for being discovered, without heart instruction;
- pressure to make a false profession;
- forcing baptism/membership/service for reputation;
- rewarding concealment more than confession;
- demanding adult-level polish rather than child-appropriate obedience.

This can create a trained religious actor.

Therefore:

> **The solution to permissive hypocrisy is not coercive hypocrisy.**

The aim is truthful categories:

- “You are loved as our child.”
- “You are under God’s law.”
- “You are not saved by obeying us.”
- “You are not free to rebel because you are unsaved.”
- “You need Christ.”
- “We will govern the home while praying for your heart.”

---

# 14. Adult child transition

Civil adulthood changes parental jurisdiction but not God’s moral authority.

For an adult child:

- parents cannot treat him as a minor indefinitely;
- independent life means broader self-government;
- God’s law still binds;
- church admonition still applies according to the person’s profession/status;
- a parent may set lawful conditions for residence in the parent’s home.

Do not say:

> “At 18, God’s authority ends.”

Nor:

> “At 18, parents retain every childhood power forever.”

Different authorities must be distinguished.

---

# 15. Key thesis for future article

> **A child does not become accountable when he is converted; conversion changes his heart and relation to God, not the fact that God was already his Creator and Judge. Therefore Christian parents must neither counterfeit regeneration through external conformity nor suspend moral formation while waiting for regeneration. They are to teach the law, restrain evil, proclaim Christ, discipline wisely, pray, and seek the child’s present repentance and faith.**

Shorter:

> **No inherited salvation. No moral neutral zone. No salvation by discipline. No permission to rebel while waiting for grace.**

## Audit guardrails

**DIRECT:** moral law binds all (1689 19.5 reflecting Scripture).  
**DIRECT:** children are commanded to obey (Eph. 6; Col. 3).  
**CONFESSIONAL:** unregenerate works do not please God spiritually, yet neglect of commanded duties is more sinful (1689 16.7).  
**PURITAN/BAPTIST SUPPORT:** Spurgeon rejects both inherited profession and delayed concern for conversion.  
**FORBIDDEN:** “Parents can regenerate through discipline.”  
**FORBIDDEN:** “External obedience proves salvation.”  
**FORBIDDEN:** “Unregenerate children have no moral duties.”  
**FORBIDDEN:** “If a child is unconverted, church leaders should ignore known serious sin until conversion.”
