# 0. The question

A child may grow up in a believing family and for years:

- hear the gospel repeatedly;
- read Scripture;
- know Christian doctrine;
- attend worship and Bible study;
- learn the language of repentance/faith;
- live outwardly without open rebellion;
- seek baptism or speak as though moving toward a Christian profession;
- receive personal admonition from parents/pastors;

and later deliberately move into sexual immorality, profanity, blasphemy, deception, contempt for correction, or explicit rejection of Christian obedience.

A common response is:

> “He is simply unregenerate. What did you expect? Treat him like any other unbeliever.”

That statement contains one possible truth and one serious category error.

Possible truth:

> The child may indeed never have been regenerate.

Category error:

> `unregenerate` does not mean `same history of light, privilege, profession, responsibility, and pastoral relation as every other unregenerate person`.

### Governing thesis

> **Unregenerate is a soteriological description. It does not erase the history of received gospel light. Scripture repeatedly distinguishes ignorance from knowing rejection, and greater privilege from lesser privilege.**

Therefore a church-raised youth who knowingly defects from truth may be as truly unsaved as a coworker who has barely heard the gospel, while nevertheless standing under **greater light, aggravated responsibility, and a different pastoral problem**.

---

# 1. Three axes must never be collapsed

## Axis A — soteriological condition

Questions:

- regenerate or unregenerate?
- true faith or false/temporary profession?
- grievously fallen believer or never-converted professor?

Humans cannot read God’s decree infallibly.

1689 ch. 17 protects the reality of grievous falls in true believers; ch. 18 guards assurance and false assurance; earlier Research already established that one serious relapse cannot by itself prove reprobation.

## Axis B — degree of received light and moral privilege

Questions:

- how much Scripture has the person heard?
- did he understand the gospel substantially?
- has he been personally admonished?
- did he once speak approvingly of Christian truth?
- did he seek baptism or publicly identify with Christ?
- is present sin committed against known truth rather than simple ignorance?

Scripture clearly recognizes degrees of accountability according to knowledge/privilege.

## Axis C — ecclesial jurisdiction / visible status

Questions:

- visitor?
- child of members?
- baptism candidate?
- publicly professing but never admitted member?
- baptized member in good standing?
- member under discipline?
- excommunicated former member?

Formal church censure belongs to this axis and cannot be inferred merely from Axis B.

### Critical consequence

A person can be:

> **outside formal membership jurisdiction**

while still being:

> **far from an ordinary first-time outsider in knowledge, history, influence, and accountability.**

This is the category the project previously needed but had not named sharply enough.

# 2. Luke 12:47–48 — known will aggravates accountability

Primary text:

- Luke 12:47–48.

Jesus distinguishes between the servant who **knew** his master’s will and did not act accordingly, and one who did not know. Both remain accountable, but the known-will case receives the stronger judgment.

Commentary control:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/luke/12-47.htm

The standard commentarial reading recognizes a general principle:

> exceptional privilege and clearer knowledge, when rejected, aggravate guilt.

### Project use

This is one of the cleanest answers to the phrase:

> “He is just an unbeliever like anyone else.”

Not every unbeliever has received equal light.

The Bible does not teach:

`unbelief = one flat moral category with no difference in privilege or culpability`.

### Publication-safe formulation

> **The child who knows his Lord’s revealed will and deliberately rejects it is not excused because he was never regenerated. Lack of regeneration explains his need for grace; it does not reduce known disobedience to innocent ignorance.**

# 3. John 15:22–24 — Christ’s words remove an excuse

Primary:

- John 15:22–24.

Calvin:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom35/calcom35.v.v.html

Calvin stresses that Christ is speaking of his coming **with his doctrine**: contempt of that doctrine renders opponents more inexcusable.

### Guard

Jesus is speaking in a unique redemptive-historical setting to people confronted by Christ himself. Do not flatten the text into an arithmetic formula for every adolescent.

### Valid principle

More revealed light can increase responsibility when it is knowingly despised.

# 4. 2 Peter 2:20–22 — a later state worse than the beginning

Primary:

- 2 Pet. 2:20–22.

Calvin:

- https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentaries_on_the_catholic_epistles/2_peter_2_20-22.htm

Gill:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/2_peter/2.htm

The immediate chapter concerns false teachers and corruption. Therefore it is **not** a direct diagnostic label for every church-raised teenager.

But its theological structure is highly relevant:

- knowledge of Christ/the gospel;
- real outward escape/reformation;
- later entanglement and conquest by pollution;
- the latter condition described as worse than the first;
- knowing the way of righteousness and then turning from it aggravates the case.

Gill explicitly allows outward moral reformation and substantial notional/professional gospel knowledge without saving regeneration.

Calvin emphasizes the peculiar inexcusable character of turning from a holy way already known.

### Strong project synthesis

> **External reformation plus gospel knowledge can exist without regeneration; when such a person later returns deliberately to corruption, Scripture does not describe the history as morally equivalent to never having known the way of righteousness.**

### Guard

Do not identify every relapse with the false teachers of 2 Peter 2.

Context controls category strength.

# 5. Hebrews 3 — the visible community can contain an unbelieving heart that falls away

Primary:

- Heb. 3:7–19.

Gill:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/hebrews/3.htm

The warning is addressed within a community hearing God’s voice.

The danger is:

- evil unbelieving heart;
- departing/falling away from the living God;
- hardening through sin’s deceit;
- failure to enter because of unbelief.

The prescribed response is not passive labeling:

> “Well, he is unregenerate.”

It is:

> **exhort one another every day** lest hardening progress.

### Project implication

A church that knows a youth has lived for years under the Word and is now moving into hardened, defended rebellion should not make his unregenerate status an argument **against** admonition.

Hebrews gives almost the opposite instinct:

> **known danger of unbelieving departure intensifies the need for truthful exhortation.**

# 6. Hebrews 6:4–8 — external participation in visible-community blessings is a real category

Primary:

- Heb. 6:4–12.

Dennis Johnson, TGC Biblical Commentary:

- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/hebrews/

John Owen, *The Nature and Causes of Apostasy from the Gospel*:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.v.html

Owen treats “enlightened” as real instruction in gospel doctrine and explicitly recognizes **degrees of gospel knowledge according to means, capacity, and diligence**. He distinguishes substantial illumination/common operations from saving perseverance.

Johnson similarly places the warnings among blessings operative in the visible covenant community without making them proof of final regeneration.

### Major project use

This gives a category broader than:

> “never saved, therefore nothing spiritually significant happened before.”

A person may receive:

- extensive instruction;
- real conviction;
- real external reform;
- real participation in church life;
- real understanding of gospel truth;

without the root of saving faith.

Those privileges are still privileges, and their rejection is still serious.

### P0 guard

**Do not declare every backsliding/church-raised sexual sinner to be the irreversible apostate of Heb. 6:6.**

The passage describes a terrifying, climactic repudiation. It is a warning category, not a shortcut to reading the secret heart.

# 7. Hebrews 10:26–31 — not every voluntary sexual sin equals final apostasy

Primary:

- Heb. 10:19–31.

Gill:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/hebrews/10-26.htm

Dennis Johnson:

- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/hebrews/

This text is especially easy to abuse because the phrase “sin wilfully” can be detached from context.

Gill explicitly argues that the passage does **not** mean:

- any single voluntary sin;
- every gross immoral act;
- every post-profession fall.

He interprets the target as resolute, obstinate apostasy from truth against light/evidence.

Johnson likewise reads 10:26–31 as deliberate repudiation of Christ, parallel to the apostasy warnings of Heb. 6.

### Therefore

A teenager who knowingly watches pornography, lies, blasphemes, or commits sexual sin is guilty of deliberate sin.

But one must **not automatically conclude**:

> “Hebrews 10:26 says there is no sacrifice left for him; therefore repentance is impossible.”

That would exceed the text.

### Yet the warning remains frighteningly relevant

A pattern of:

- received knowledge;
- growing contempt;
- rejection of admonition;
- settled abandonment of Christian profession;
- deliberate repudiation of Christ/truth;

moves toward precisely the kind of apostasy Hebrews warns against.

The church should warn before it presumes it can diagnose the endpoint.

# 8. Owen — “wide difference” between apostates and others

Primary:

- John Owen, *The Nature and Causes of Apostasy from the Gospel*.
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.x.html

Owen explicitly refuses to judge the eternal state of every outward professor, but then insists there is nevertheless a **wide difference between apostates and others**. He invokes 2 Pet. 2:21: better not to have known the way of righteousness than, after knowing, to turn from it.

This is almost exactly the project’s disputed question.

### Article-ready synthesis

> **We do not know the decree of God from outward history. But Scripture does require us to distinguish the person who has scarcely heard Christ from the person who has lived under gospel light, professed or approached profession, and now knowingly turns against what he knows.**

Owen also treats apostasy from gospel holiness—not only doctrinal heresy—as a grave category when outward Christianity remains while life is surrendered to lust and pleasure.

Primary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy/apostasy.i.xii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.xiv.html

This is particularly important for a youth who may not articulate a formal anti-Christian creed but practically renounces Christ’s rule through defended, habitual immorality while retaining Christian forms.

### Guard

Owen’s broad category of “apostasy from holiness” should not be converted into formal church excommunication jurisdiction where no membership/profession status exists.

Moral diagnosis and ecclesial jurisdiction remain distinct axes.

# 9. Baxter — sin against greater profession/light carries aggravated hypocrisy

Primary:

- Richard Baxter, *The Reformed Pastor*:
  https://ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor/pastor.ii.ii.html
- *The Causes and Danger of Slighting Christ and His Gospel*:
  https://ccel.org/ccel/baxter/causes/causes.iii.i.html

Baxter is especially strong on the principle that sin carries more hypocrisy/perfidiousness where a person has spoken against it, engaged against it, or professed Christian seriousness while secretly cherishing it.

His *Slighting Christ* also identifies the pattern:

- informed of gospel truth;
- verbally promising willingness to receive Christ on his terms;
- nevertheless clinging to sinful courses and refusing to let practice be changed.

### Project use

This supports the claim that **religious formation/profession can aggravate hypocrisy rather than neutralize guilt when knowingly contradicted**.

### Guard

Baxter’s rhetoric is severe. The article should use him to sharpen biblical categories, not to replace careful exegesis or declare a child finally lost.

# 10. 1 John 2:19 — useful but narrower than the project case

Primary:

- 1 John 2:18–19.

Calvin:

- https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentaries_on_the_catholic_epistles/1_john_2_18-19.htm

The immediate context concerns antichrists/false teachers leaving the Christian community.

Calvin emphasizes that their departure manifested that outward presence/profession had never been saving union with Christ.

### Valid use

The text strongly confirms that:

> visible proximity to church and outward profession do not prove regeneration.

### Invalid overuse

Do not say:

> “A teenager stopped obeying his parents / fell into pornography, therefore 1 John 2:19 proves he was an antichrist.”

The passage’s immediate category is narrower.

# 11. Desire for baptism is spiritually meaningful but not identical to church membership

A youth may say:

> “I want to be baptized.”

Possible realities include:

- genuine emerging faith;
- sincere but immature conviction;
- desire for belonging;
- imitation of peers/family;
- temporary religious affection;
- a profession later contradicted by life.

### Baptist guard

The church should not retroactively pretend:

> “he was formally a member because he once wanted baptism.”

Nor should it say:

> “wanting baptism and years of explicit Christian identification mean nothing at all.”

They matter to the history of **light/profession**, even if they do not create formal membership jurisdiction.

### Key distinction

> **Baptism candidacy can increase the seriousness of contradiction without creating excommunication jurisdiction that the church never actually possessed.**

# 12. Proposed terminology for publication

Avoid a single label too early.

## Prefer descriptive categories

Depending on facts:

- `невозрождённый, выросший под Евангелием`;
- `получивший много света, но отвергающий его`;
- `бывший/ложный исповедующий` where an actual profession existed;
- `человек, отходящий от известной ему истины`;
- `ожесточённый слушатель`;
- `церковно воспитанный, но нераскаянный`.

## Use `apostate / отступник` carefully

Stronger and safer when there was:

- identifiable Christian profession/allegiance;
- substantial gospel knowledge;
- later conscious repudiation of that profession/faith.

Do not use merely because:

- parents are believers;
- child attended church;
- child committed serious sin.

### Useful phrase

> **apostate-like pastoral problem**

may describe the analogy during Research, but final Russian prose should usually explain the facts rather than rely on a technical label.

# 13. What “ordinary unbeliever” gets wrong

The phrase can wrongly erase at least six realities:

1. **greater light**;
2. **former Christian identification/profession**;
3. **known personal admonitions already rejected**;
4. **hardening through repeated hearing without obedience**;
5. **existing relational obligations of parents/church leaders who already know him**;
6. **his influence on children/youth who may still regard him as one of “us.”**

### Strong line

> **The issue is not whether he is “saved enough” to be treated seriously. The questions are: how much light has he received, what did he profess, what is he now rejecting, and what kind of fellowship or influence can the church responsibly entrust to him?**

# 14. “There is nothing new to tell him” — correct and incorrect senses

## Correct

There is no second gospel reserved for hardened church kids.

Parents/pastors may indeed discover that the child already knows:

- creation/fall;
- sin;
- judgment;
- cross;
- resurrection;
- repentance;
- faith;
- sexual ethics;
- warnings against hypocrisy.

More information alone may not be the missing element.

## Incorrect

Therefore:

> “stop speaking truth; there is nothing to say.”

Heb. 3:13 commands continued exhortation in the face of hardening.

2 Tim. 2:24–26 instructs the Lord’s servant to correct opponents with gentleness in hope that **God may grant repentance**.

Source:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/2_timothy/2.htm

### Better formulation

> **He may need no new gospel, but he still needs the old gospel addressed to his actual rebellion, with warnings he can no longer hide behind as unfamiliar information.**

Cadence/mode may change; endless argument is not required. See modules `69` and `91_ADMONITION_MODE_CHANGE...`.

# 15. Final theological verdict

### GREEN

- Two unregenerate people can differ radically in received gospel light and therefore in aggravated responsibility.
- A church-raised youth who knowingly rejects truth should not be described pastorally as though his history were identical to an uninstructed outsider.
- Extensive gospel privilege can exist without regeneration.
- External reformation can exist without regeneration.
- Conscious defection from known truth is a distinct and dangerous biblical category.
- Repeated admonition remains appropriate; God alone grants repentance.

### YELLOW

- `apostate` — use only when actual prior profession/allegiance and later repudiation justify the term.
- Heb. 6 / Heb. 10 — use as warning framework with narrow apostasy guards.
- 2 Pet. 2 — immediate context is false teachers; use structurally, not as automatic label.

### RED

Do not write:

- every church kid who sins sexually is an apostate;
- every child who wanted baptism had saving faith;
- Heb. 10:26 means every deliberate sexual sin is unforgivable;
- Heb. 6 proves we know a specific teenager can no longer repent;
- unregenerate = morally exempt;
- unregenerate = identical pastoral history to every outsider;
- “nothing new to tell him” = stop admonishing him.

# 16. Locked formulation

> **Невозрождённость не обнуляет свет. Человек, который почти не знал Евангелия, и человек, который годами жил под Словом, знал путь правды, стремился к христианскому исповеданию и затем сознательно отвергает известную ему истину, могут оба оказаться невозрождёнными — но Писание не делает их историю и ответственность одинаковыми. Больший свет не спасает без благодати; отвергнутый больший свет увеличивает ответственность.**
