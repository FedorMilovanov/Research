# 1. Why Owen is central to the user’s correction

The project originally risked describing hardening too cognitively:

> “Eventually the teen decides the sexual behavior is normal.”

The user corrected this:

> he may still know it is wrong, but no longer meaningfully fights or reasons about it; he simply lives according to passions/desires, with remaining moral boundaries depending on how far corruption has proceeded.

Owen offers a much better pre-modern theological anatomy.

His sequence is roughly:

`sin draws mind away from duty/sense of sin`  
`→ entangles affections with bait/pleasure`  
`→ obtains consent of will`  
`→ actual sin`  
`→ repeated deceit can contribute to hardening/declension`.

This is not a neuroscience model. It is moral/spiritual analysis grounded in Rom. 7, James 1, Heb. 3 and Eph. 4.

---

# 2. Owen: “heart” includes mind, will, affections and conscience

Owen notes that Scripture uses `heart` broadly for the inner moral person:

- mind/understanding judges;
- will chooses/refuses;
- affections love/dislike/cleave;
- conscience warns/judges;
- together they operate as one principle of moral action.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.vii.html

### Project importance

This is a better framework than saying:

> “his brain turned off.”

A child can retain propositions in the intellect while the whole moral operation of the heart is increasingly governed by lust.

---

# 3. Owen: sin’s deceit first attacks the mind

Owen treats deceit as especially dangerous because the mind normally:

- guides;
- directs;
- chooses what is presented to will/affections.

If the mind is drawn away from:

- due sense of sin;
- attentive obedience;
- serious consideration of God/grace,

then the affections and will become easier to capture.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xii.html

### This directly corrects an oversimplification

The person need not first formulate:

> “Pornography is morally good.”

Sin can prevail more practically:

> **the known truth ceases to receive sustained attention at the point where desire is active.**

The doctrine remains in memory; practical moral attention is diverted.

---

# 4. Owen: experience of sin can produce LESS fear when conviction decays

One of the most striking passages for the project:

Owen describes people who at first have strong convictions:

- sorrow;
- fear;
- resolutions against sin.

But if the sense of sin remains merely at the level of temporary affection and is not deeply fixed in mind/judgment, it decays.

He observes that some people, with more experience of sin, come to be **less afraid of it**, eventually even contemptuous toward the very convictions they once felt.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xii.html

### Relevance to adolescent double life

This is extremely close to the user’s observation:

First exposure:

> shock / fear / conscience.

Later repeated secret practice:

> less resistance / less reflection / more automatic following of desire.

### Guard

Owen’s work is primarily about **indwelling sin in believers**, though he also compares unregenerate conviction patterns.

Do not automatically identify every adolescent case with Owen’s believer category.

---

# 5. Hebrews 3:13 is one of Owen’s governing texts

Owen repeatedly invokes:

> “hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

His logic:

- sin deceives;
- therefore watch against deceit;
- otherwise hardening can follow.

This gives the project a very strong theological sequence:

> **Hardening is not merely repeated rule-breaking; it is repeated rule-breaking under the deceptive work by which sin makes itself less alarming, more reasonable and more desirable.**

Pastoral synthesis, not direct quotation.

---

# 6. Owen: affections are entangled by the bait

Chapter XI describes James 1’s “enticed” imagery as bait/hook-like entanglement.

Sin presents itself as suitable for satisfying corrupt desire.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xv.html

Examples in Owen:

- wine;
- adulterous beauty;
- riches;
- objects suited to lust/covetousness.

### Modern application

Digital sexual content is particularly capable of supplying:

- immediately suitable visual objects;
- novelty;
- secrecy;
- repeated imaginative material.

But do not say the technology creates the affection.

The corrupt affection finds a fitting object/opportunity.

---

# 7. Owen: the soul becomes willing to listen to extenuations

A particularly useful diagnostic:

Owen says entanglement is evident when the soul becomes ready to listen to thoughts like:

- “Is it not a little one?”
- “There is mercy.”
- “I will give it up later.”

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xv.html

This converges almost perfectly with Brooks:

- minimize sin;
- presume on mercy;
- postpone repentance.

### Modern child language

- “It’s not that serious.”
- “God forgives.”
- “I’ll stop later.”
- “At least I’m not doing what they do.”

The concern is not a single defensive sentence but a pattern by which the soul protects the lust.

---

# 8. Owen: after mind + affections, the will consents

Chapter XII argues actual sin is conceived through consent of the will.

The will chooses under some appearance of present good/suitability.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xvi.html

### Project relevance

This preserves **human agency**.

The person is not a passive neurological victim of:

- algorithm;
- hormones;
- peers;
- Satan.

Those may tempt/facilitate/deceive.

Sin reaches action through the person’s own willing consent.

### Important line

> **Temptation may narrow the field of vision; it does not turn the sinner into a morally innocent machine.**

Pastoral synthesis.

---

# 9. Owen: law of sin operates easily because it is inward

Owen emphasizes indwelling sin’s facility:

it does not need an external door opened because corruption is already present in the faculties of the soul.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.vi.html

### Reformed anthropology correction

Therefore:

> internet is not the root disease.

Even if every device disappears, the sinful heart remains.

Yet external infrastructure matters because it supplies occasions/objects/companions to an inward law already ready to act.

---

# 10. Owen: religious duties themselves can become outward shell

Owen notes sin’s opposition to spiritual duty and the danger of duties stripped of inward spiritual affection/end.

He warns that outward considerations can give someone satisfaction in a duty while it lacks what renders it acceptable to God.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xiv.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.ix.html

### Children’s choir relevance

This does **not** directly regulate choirs.

But it strengthens the theological warning:

> religious performance can continue while the heart’s real moral/spiritual engagement is deeply disordered.

This is exactly why a church should not use visible participation as proof everything is spiritually fine.

---

# 11. Owen: grace and indwelling sin can truly conflict in a believer

Owen’s entire treatise assumes real grace can coexist with remaining sin and severe warfare.

This is essential for avoiding the error:

> “If lust remains powerful, the person cannot be regenerate.”

He distinguishes:

- reigning/dominion of sin;
- remaining indwelling sin;
- grace’s rule contested by corruption.

### Project requirement

Do not use strong descriptions of habitual lust to erase 1689 ch. 17 perseverance/grievous-fall categories.

A true believer can be in severe danger/declension and need urgent mortification.

---

# 12. But “remaining sin” must not become reassurance for peace with sin

Owen is equally incompatible with:

> “Christians sin, so settled pornography/deception is normal.”

The whole work is a summons to:

- watch;
- know deceit;
- resist;
- mortify;
- attend to Word/duty.

### Core distinction

> **Remaining sin explains warfare. It does not sanctify surrender.**

---

# 13. Refined model for the project’s “inner debate disappears” theme

Instead of:

`sin → he decides it is normal → sin more`.

Use a richer possible progression:

1. truth is known and conscience initially reacts;
2. desire repeatedly seeks satisfaction;
3. mind is drawn away from sustained sense of sin;
4. excuses/minimizations receive easier hearing;
5. affections become increasingly entangled with pleasure/object;
6. will repeatedly consents;
7. secrecy protects the pattern from correction;
8. conviction becomes less vivid;
9. behavior becomes increasingly ready/ordinary;
10. remaining moral boundaries may persist in other areas;
11. doctrinal justification may appear later—or may never be fully articulated;
12. hardening may deepen if deceit is not interrupted.

### Classification

This is **Owen-informed pastoral synthesis**, not a universal twelve-stage law.

---

# 14. Relation to conscience

Owen notes conscience may make an “uproar” when affections/will move into sin, unless conscience becomes bribed/debauched or insufficiently stirred.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xiv.html

### Safe language

- conscience can become less practically sensitive;
- repeated self-deception can make warning less effective;
- Scripture warns of defiled/hardened/seared conditions in distinct contexts.

### Forbidden

- “conscience mechanically dies after N repetitions”;
- “brain damage explains moral callousness”;
- “this teenager is permanently beyond repentance.”

---

# 15. Parent/pastor diagnostic questions from Owen

Not clinical screening; pastoral examination.

- What truth do you already know but cease considering when temptation arrives?
- Which excuse do you most quickly accept?
- What pleasure is your imagination placing before you?
- What consequence does your mind stop considering?
- What occasion makes the affection especially strong?
- What do you refuse to surrender despite saying you want freedom?
- When did this sin stop frightening you as it once did?
- What correction do you now find irritating that once pierced you?
- Are you fighting the lust or mainly fighting the people interrupting it?

---

# 16. Key future article lines

> **A hardened sinner does not always become a philosopher of his sin. Sometimes he simply becomes less willing to think while the lust is speaking.**

> **The doctrine may remain in the head while the desire receives the practical vote.**

> **Repeated sin can make yesterday’s alarm become today’s routine without ever requiring a formal declaration that evil is good.**

> **The danger is not merely forgetting a rule, but becoming easier to deceive about the rule precisely when obedience costs something.**

All are rhetorical/pastoral synthesis, not Owen quotations.
