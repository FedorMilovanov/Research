# 0. Part I promise

Part I must answer:

> **Why can a teenager raised around Christian truth build a second life of pornography, profanity, sexualized communication, secret accounts, and deception; why is that life attractive; what has the digital age changed; and how can repeated sin become increasingly easy to inhabit without pretending that every such child is finally apostate or clinically “addicted”?**

# 1. Biblical source packet

## A. Mark 7:20–23 — source of moral evil

### Direct claim

Jesus locates sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery, coveting, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, and foolishness as proceeding **from within**, from the human heart.

### Publication use

Use near the beginning to prevent technology determinism.

### Allowed synthesis

> The phone can deliver the object of lust; it does not manufacture the fallen heart that lusts.

### Forbidden overreach

Do not use Mark 7 to say environment/companionship/opportunity therefore do not matter. Scripture elsewhere treats corrupt company and occasions seriously.

---

## B. James 1:14–15 — desire → enticement → sin → death

### Direct claim

Temptation operates through a person being drawn/enticed by his own desire; desire conceives and gives birth to sin, and sin grows toward death.

### Publication use

Main anthropology of temptation.

### Important guard

James does not absolve Satan/world as irrelevant categories elsewhere in Scripture; here he specifically destroys the excuse that God is the author of temptation and locates culpable desire within the person.

---

## C. Proverbs 9:13–18 — stolen waters / hidden end

### Direct claim

Folly advertises stolen water and secret bread as sweet/pleasant while her guests do not know the dead are there.

### Publication use

One of the strongest literary images for:

- real short-term attraction;
- secrecy;
- hidden cost/end.

### Strong synthesis

> Sin often does not need to lie that the first taste will be pleasant; it lies by hiding where the table is located and where the meal ends.

Synthesis only.

---

## D. Hebrews 11:24–26 — fleeting pleasure of sin

### Direct claim

Moses chooses mistreatment with God’s people rather than temporary enjoyment of sin, valuing reproach for Christ above Egypt’s treasures.

### Publication use

Supports the proposition that Scripture does not deny real temporary pleasure.

### Guard

Do not turn this into a general neuropsychological theory of reward.

---

## E. 1 Corinthians 15:33 — corrupt company

### Direct claim

Bad company corrupts good morals/character.

### Publication use

Bridge from internal desire to social formation.

### Guard

Immediate chapter context concerns denial of resurrection and the social effect of such error. The proverb-like warning legitimately establishes corrupting companionship, but do not pretend Paul is directly discussing teenage group chats or AI companions.

---

## F. Hebrews 3:12–13 — deceit of sin / hardening / daily exhortation

### Direct claim

Believers are warned against an evil unbelieving heart departing from God and commanded to exhort one another daily so none is hardened by sin’s deceitfulness.

### Publication use

Primary hardening text.

### Key theological observations

- hardening is connected to deception;
- community exhortation is a divinely appointed countermeasure;
- warning is severe and covenantal, not a clinical diagnosis;
- the text does not authorize parents to declare a specific teenager irreversibly hardened.

### Publication line candidate

> **Sin hardens not only by being repeated but by deceiving the sinner while it is repeated.**

Synthesis.

---

## G. Ephesians 4:17–19 — darkened understanding / callousness / sensuality

### Direct claim

Paul describes Gentile futility, darkened understanding, alienation from God, hardness of heart, loss of sensitivity/callousness, and self-giving to sensuality/greed for impurity.

### Publication use

Supports progressive moral insensitivity and “giving oneself over.”

### Guard

Do not translate `ἀπηλγηκότες` into modern neurological damage.

Do not diagnose every repeated sexual fall as the full Eph. 4 state.

---

## H. Titus 3:3 — enslaved to passions and pleasures

### Direct claim

Paul includes believers’ former condition as foolish/disobedient/deceived and enslaved to various passions and pleasures.

### Publication use

Short text for the specific correction:

> the sinner may not be conducting a philosophical debate about whether his lust is “normal”; he can simply be living in slavery to passions/pleasures.

---

## I. Romans 1:18–32 — retained knowledge / suppression / judicial giving-over

### Direct claims relevant to Part I

- truth can be known and suppressed;
- desire and moral rebellion coexist with retained knowledge;
- divine giving-over is judicial;
- v.32 shows knowledge of God’s judgment can coexist with doing and approving evil.

### Publication use

Use sparingly because the passage is theologically heavy.

### Critical guard

Do not use God’s `gave them over` as a parental technique.

Do not collapse every individual teen sin into the full corporate/idolatrous argument of Romans 1.

---

## J. 1 John 1:5–2:2 — darkness / light / confession / Christ the Advocate

### Direct claim

Claiming fellowship while walking in darkness is contradicted by conduct; walking in light involves truthful relation to sin, confession is joined to forgiveness/cleansing, and the movement continues to Jesus Christ the righteous Advocate.

### Publication use

End Part I by moving from hidden life toward light **and Christ**, setting up Part II without making exposure/accountability the functional gospel.

### Guard

Do not make walking in light mean sinlessness; 1 John 1:8–10 explicitly rejects that.

Do not imply that merely saying “I confess” mechanically proves regeneration.

# 2. Classical source packet — keep deliberately small

## 2.1 Thomas Brooks — *Precious Remedies Against Satan’s Devices*

### Primary use

Brooks supplies the memorable pastoral mechanics:

- bait shown, hook concealed;
- sin painted with virtuous names/colors;
- lesser sins minimized;
- saints’ sins shown while their sorrow/repentance is hidden;
- future repentance presented as easy;
- occasions of sin treated with false confidence;
- wicked company used as a device.

### Why he belongs in Part I

Brooks converts “why is sin attractive?” into a richer biblical-pastoral account than modern reward jargon.

### Publication method

Use **one short direct quotation at most** plus paraphrase.

### Source

Historical/public-domain scan/transcription already verified in Research source ledger; reopen exact edition before final quotation.

Candidate verification URL:
- https://www.apuritansmind.com/wp-content/uploads/FREEEBOOKS/PreciousRemediesAgainstSatansDevices-ThomasBrooks.pdf

### Guard

Satan’s devices do not erase James 1 personal desire/responsibility.

## 2.2 John Owen — *Of Temptation*

### Primary verified source

CCEL:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.x.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.vii.html

### High-value verified claims

Owen urges the believer to:

- know his own heart;
- identify peculiar lusts/corruptions and weaknesses;
- keep distance from occasions suited to those weaknesses;
- watch specific companies, retirements, employments, and opportunities that provoke the corruption.

He argues that when a particular lust meets a fitting occasion/opportunity, the person is already in a dangerous state of temptation.

### Publication use

- individual susceptibility;
- why access matters without being root cause;
- why “I can handle it” is not spiritual maturity.

### Guard

Do not present Owen’s seventeenth-century examples as if he were discussing smartphones or AI directly.

## 2.3 John Owen — *Indwelling Sin*

### Primary verified source

CCEL:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xv.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/indwellingsin/indwellingsin.i.xvi.html

### High-value verified structure

Owen describes sin’s deceit as operating progressively through mind, affections and will and connects Heb. 3:13 with hardening through sin’s deceit.

### Publication use

Strong classical support for:

> not necessarily “he has concluded the sin is normal”; he may retain correct doctrine while truth exercises less practical government over desire/action.

### Guard

Owen’s treatise concerns indwelling sin in believers in substantial sections. Do not automatically transfer every statement as a diagnosis of an unregenerate teenager.

---

## 2.4 John Flavel — *Keeping the Heart*

### Primary verified source

- https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed/saintindeed.v.ii.html

### High-value verified claim

Flavel traces temptation through early motions toward consultation, choice, and fuller engagement, stressing that temptation is easier checked early than after it gathers force.

### Publication use

A short supporting reference for:

> fight sin early; do not wait for the moment of maximum heat and call that the only point of self-control.

### Guard

Do not convert Flavel into a deterministic psychological stage model.

## 3.1 Pew Research Center — U.S. teens, 2024 internet baseline

- most teens have AI romantic partners;

- evil desire arises from the fallen human heart;
- sin can offer temporary pleasure;
- secrecy and bad company are morally dangerous;
- sin deceives and hardens;
- repeated moral knowledge does not guarantee practical obedience;
