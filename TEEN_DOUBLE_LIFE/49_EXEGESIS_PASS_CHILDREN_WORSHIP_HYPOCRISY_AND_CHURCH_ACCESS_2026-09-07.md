# 1. Ephesians 6:1–4 — children under command, fathers under command

## Historical-literary context

Ephesians 5:22–6:9 addresses household relations under the lordship of Christ.

Paul addresses:

- wives/husbands;
- children/fathers;
- servants/masters.

Children are directly addressed as moral hearers.

## Direct teaching

Eph. 6:1:

> children are commanded to obey parents “in the Lord,” because this is right.

Eph. 6:2–3 appeals to the fifth commandment.

Eph. 6:4 simultaneously commands fathers:

- not to provoke children to anger;
- to bring them up in discipline/instruction of the Lord.

Calvin emphasizes that obedience is grounded in God’s authority and is limited by superior obedience to God.

Sources:

- https://biblehub.com/library/calvin/commentary_on_galatians_and_ephesians/ephesians_6_1-4.htm
- https://www.ccel.org/c/calvin/comment2/eph.htm

## Allowed application

- children are not morally neutral;
- parental authority is real and God-derived;
- discipline/instruction are parental duties;
- authority is limited by “in the Lord” and cannot demand sin;
- parental provocation/harshness is also sin.

## Forbidden overreach

- “Eph. 6 authorizes any parental rule whatsoever.”
- “A disobedient child may be abandoned.”
- “Parental discipline regenerates.”

# 2. Psalm 50:16–21 — religious lips plus hated correction

## Literary/covenantal context

Psalm 50 is a divine covenant-lawsuit scene. God addresses his covenant people, first correcting false views of sacrifice and then confronting the wicked person who speaks covenant words while rejecting God’s instruction.

The sequence is especially important:

- recites statutes;
- covenant on lips;
- hates discipline/correction;
- casts God’s words behind;
- joins theft/adultery;
- gives mouth to evil/deceit;
- assumes divine silence means approval.

Calvin identifies the target as people trying to shelter impurity of life beneath outward religion/ceremony.

Primary commentary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom09/calcom09.xvi.iv.html

## Direct teaching

God condemns the attempt to combine religious profession with rejection of his instruction and wicked conduct.

## Allowed application

Highly relevant to a church child/teen who:

- knows worship language;
- visibly sings/participates;
- consciously rejects correction;
- lives a sustained hidden immoral life;
- interprets continued religious participation as evidence everything is spiritually tolerable.

Safe line:

> **Religious lips cannot make peace between God and a life that hates his correction.**

## Forbidden overreach

- “Psalm 50 is a direct children’s-choir statute.”
- “Every child who sins while singing is the wicked person of Psalm 50.”

The focus is settled religious hypocrisy/rejection of correction, not ordinary imperfection.

# 3. Isaiah 1:10–17 — commanded worship made hateful by rebellious life

## Historical context

Isaiah addresses Judah/Jerusalem under covenant judgment.

The people continue:

- sacrifices;
- assemblies;
- Sabbaths/festivals;
- prayer.

Yet their hands are morally polluted and they fail in justice/righteousness.

Calvin emphasizes that outward religious actions are worthless where fear of God/good conscience are absent and outward worship serves as a mask.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom13/calcom13.viii.i.html

## Direct teaching

God does not accept outward covenant worship as a substitute for repentance and righteous life.

## Allowed application

- church activity cannot compensate for deliberate rebellion;
- parents/leaders should not teach children a ceremonial view of holiness;
- visible worship participation must never function as false spiritual certification.

## Forbidden overreach

- “God commands every unregenerate child not to enter worship.”
- “Any known sin makes all worship impossible until perfect behavior.”

Isaiah condemns settled covenant hypocrisy, not the repentant sinner who comes needing mercy.

# 4. Amos 5:21–24 — songs severed from covenant righteousness

## Historical context

Amos speaks to the northern kingdom in a broader indictment including:

- covenant apostasy;
- distorted worship;
- exploitation/injustice;
- confidence in religious observance.

God rejects festivals, offerings and songs.

Calvin stresses that hypocrites imagine God can be pacified by religious forms while they continue rebellion.

Sources:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/amos/5.htm
- https://www.ccel.org/study/Amos_5%3A21-24

## Direct teaching

Religious music/ritual cannot function as compensation for covenant unfaithfulness.

## Allowed application

> **Song does not bribe God into ignoring the life of the singer.**

Use in a theological section on hypocrisy/worship.

## Forbidden overreach

- reducing Amos 5 merely to private sexual purity; social injustice and covenant apostasy are central to context;
- claiming Amos directly legislates children’s choir admissions;
- saying every sinful singer is individually the object of Amos’s national judgment oracle.

# 5. Matthew 15:7–9 — lips near, heart far

## Context

Jesus confronts Pharisaic/traditional religion and quotes Isaiah 29.

The issue is not merely emotional “sincerity.”

It includes:

- honoring God with lips;
- heart far from God;
- worship rendered empty by human teaching that displaces God’s command.

Calvin emphasizes outward profession without inward sincerity/obedience.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/comment3/comm_vol32/htm/xlvi.htm

## Allowed application

Double-life article may use this text to expose the danger of religious speech/song that becomes disconnected from the heart’s allegiance.

## Forbidden overreach

- treating every inconsistency as Pharisaic hypocrisy;
- assuming leaders can infallibly see heart-distance from one incident.

Observable sustained conduct + refusal of correction gives stronger pastoral grounds than isolated failure.

# 6. 1 Corinthians 5:1–13 — scandal tolerated inside the professing church

## Historical-literary context

Paul confronts a notorious sexual relationship tolerated by the Corinthian church.

His rebuke falls not only on the offender but on the church for arrogant/remiss tolerance.

Calvin emphasizes:

- the church’s failure to mourn/correct;
- ecclesial discipline over insiders;
- the distinction between “one called a brother” and outsiders;
- close familiarity with the scandalous professor may communicate approval.

Sources:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom39.xii.iii.html
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/1_corinthians/5.htm

## Direct teaching

- scandalous unrepentant professing insiders must not simply remain affirmed in church fellowship;
- church jurisdiction/censure is distinct from judgment of outsiders;
- tolerated sin has community implications (“leaven”).

## Allowed application

- a professing/baptized/member teenager is not exempt from discipline merely because young;
- youth culture should not normalize known corruption by insiders;
- ministry privileges and intimate fellowship can rightly be restricted.

## Forbidden overreach

- formally excommunicating an unbaptized outsider under 1 Cor. 5 categories as though he were a church member;
- using “do not associate” to forbid every evangelistic/public interaction with unbelievers;
- treating a struggling repentant sinner as the same as the defiant scandalous case.

# 7. 1 Corinthians 14:23–25 — unbelievers can enter the gathered assembly

## Context

Paul regulates intelligibility/order in corporate worship, especially tongues/prophecy.

His hypothetical explicitly includes:

> an unbeliever or uninstructed person entering the assembled church.

Calvin understands the scene as an outsider entering and being exposed to understandable speech/conviction.

Sources:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom39/calcom39.xxi.iii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/comment3/comm_vol39/htm/xxi.iii.htm

## Direct teaching

The gathered church’s worship is not conceived as a sealed meeting into which no unbeliever can physically enter/hear.

## Allowed application

This strongly guards against the slogan:

> “Known sinner? Never let him attend preaching.”

Public gospel hearing is a different category from ministry privilege or trusted youth access.

## Forbidden overreach

- “Because outsiders may enter public worship, every outsider has a right to every small group, choir, youth trip or private relationship.”

The text establishes presence in assembly, not unrestricted ecclesial/social privilege.

# 8. 2 Timothy 3:1–5 — form of godliness, life denying its power

## Context

Paul describes perilous conditions among people marked by a broad cluster:

- love of self/money;
- boasting/pride;
- abuse/profanity;
- disobedience to parents;
- unholiness;
- lack of self-control;
- brutality;
- love of pleasure rather than love of God;
- form/appearance of godliness while denying its power.

The following verses connect some of these people with corrupt teachers/infiltrators who enter households and oppose truth.

Calvin treats the passage as a serious church danger requiring pastoral vigilance.

Don Carson makes an especially important qualification: “denying its power” is not necessarily verbal denial but life-conduct that contradicts religious form; yet “avoid such people” cannot mean separating from anyone who ever exhibits one listed sin, otherwise everyone would be excluded.

Sources:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43/calcom43.iv.iv.i.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43/calcom43.vii.ii.iii.html
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/sermon/how-to-think-about-the-last-days-2-timothy-3-1-17/

## Allowed application

Very useful for a **settled pattern** where:

- pleasure outranks God;
- parental authority is despised;
- religious appearance remains;
- life contradicts profession;
- person influences others corruptly.

## Forbidden overreach

- label every teenager who swears or falls sexually as a 2 Tim. 3 false professor;
- use “avoid” as a universal command never to evangelize/contact a sinner;
- infer reprobation from one episode.

# 9. 1 Corinthians 15:33 — corrupt companionship

## Immediate context

Paul’s warning about bad company occurs in his resurrection argument, related to people/teaching undermining resurrection hope and resulting ethical corruption.

Thus it is broader than a generic proverb about “bad friends,” though the moral principle is valid.

## Allowed application

- companionship shapes morals;
- church youth leadership should take active corrupting influence seriously;
- a child repeatedly recruited into sexual/profane conduct by a specific group may need separation.

## Forbidden overreach

- “all non-Christian friendships are forbidden.”
- “a child must never interact with bad people.”

Biblical wisdom distinguishes mission/contact from intimate formative fellowship.

# 10. 2 Timothy 2:22 — flee youthful passions WITH pure-hearted companions

## Context

Paul instructs Timothy as a minister to flee youthful passions and pursue righteousness, faith, love and peace **along with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart**.

## Direct theological significance

Mortification is both:

- negative: flee;
- positive: pursue;
- communal: with godly people.

## Application

The parental/church answer to corrupt youth culture is not only exclusion:

> **remove corrupting access + construct holy companionship.**

This supports godly peers, mentoring and church counter-formation.

# 11. Historical-grammatical synthesis for child choir/ministry question

No major text above directly gives:

> “children’s choir eligibility requirements.”

Therefore future article must distinguish:

## Direct biblical propositions

- children have real moral duties;
- parents must discipline/instruct without provoking;
- outward worship does not cover settled rebellion;
- church must discipline scandalous professing insiders;
- unbelievers can hear public proclamation;
- corrupt companionship matters;
- youthful lusts should be fled and holy companionship pursued.

## Confessional synthesis

1689:

- moral law binds all (19.5);
- unregenerate neglect of commanded duty remains sinful (16.7);
- singing is worship (22.5);
- visible church profession should not be destroyed by unholy life and privileges exist under church government (26).

## Prudential pastoral inference

- formal choir/worship ministry can have conduct requirements;
- a known corrupting child can be temporarily removed from visible platform/access;
- public hearing/catechesis can remain open unless safety/disruption requires restriction;
- restoration should follow credible change rather than automatic time served.

# 12. Central exegesis guard

Future article must never use prophetic/discipline texts merely to sound severe.

For every major text ask:

1. Who is addressed?
2. Covenant/church status?
3. What conduct is actually named?
4. Individual or corporate indictment?
5. What is the commanded sanction?
6. What transfers directly under the new covenant?
7. What is general-equity/wisdom application only?

This will keep the article both **hard on sin and hard to refute exegetically**.
