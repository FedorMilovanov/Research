# Particular Baptist / 1689 control — marriage consent, parental authority, Collins, Keach, and confessional limits

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** research-only / Particular Baptist confessional-historical audit / not publication approval  
**Purpose:** prevent the family-government lane from importing Presbyterian/Puritan marriage procedure into a 1689 Baptist article without Baptist verification.

---

## 0. Executive finding

The Particular Baptist evidence supports a **strong doctrine of parental authority, child obedience, household formation, real marital consent, and marriage under God's moral restrictions**.

What has **not yet been established** from the 1689 Confession itself is a Westminster-Directory-style rule requiring parental consent from every adult at first marriage.

Therefore:

> **Strong parental involvement is fully compatible with the Particular Baptist tradition; a specific adult-parental-consent procedure must be argued from Scripture/history and labeled correctly rather than called “the 1689 position.”**

---

# 1. Second London Baptist Confession 1689, chapter 25

## 1.1. Marriage is a God-ordained male/female union

25.1–2 affirms one man/one woman and the purposes of mutual help, legitimate offspring, and prevention of sexual immorality.

Primary texts:
- https://baptistconfession.org/
- https://the1689.org/chapters/25/paragraphs/1/

## 1.2. Paragraph 3 — capacity and consent are explicit

The confession says marriage is lawful for people who are **able with judgment to give their consent**, and Christians must marry “in the Lord,” not entering spiritually destructive unions.

This is important for the daughter/father lane.

The 1689 itself does not define marriage as a paternal transfer in which the woman's own consent is juridically irrelevant.

### P0

> **Any father-led marriage model claimed as 1689-compatible must preserve actual rational consent of the parties.**

## 1.3. What ch. 25 does NOT say

Chapter 25 does not contain an explicit clause:

- requiring parental consent for adult first marriage;
- granting a father a universal adult-daughter marriage veto;
- commanding adult unmarried daughters to remain resident with parents;
- assigning generic male “covering” to unmarried women.

### Evidence boundary

Absence is not proof that Particular Baptists rejected parental consent.

It means only:

> **do not attribute an unstated detailed rule to the confession itself.**

---

# 2. Comparison with Westminster — an important textual distinction

The Westminster **Confession** itself, like the Second London Confession, does not place the detailed parental-consent procedure in its marriage chapter.

The stronger rule appears in the Westminster **Directory for Public Worship**, which says the parental-consent procedure is also to be observed for adults with living parents in first marriage, while forbidding parents to force marriage or refuse without just cause.

Source:
https://reformedstandards.com/westminster/directory-for-publick-worship.html

### Why this matters

The proper distinction is not:

`Presbyterians had parental consent / Baptists rejected it`.

The real research question is:

> **Did Particular Baptist church practice, catechesis, associational documents or pastoral theology preserve a similar expectation outside the confession text?**

That requires historical evidence.

# 3. Hercules Collins — Orthodox Catechism, fifth commandment

Hercules Collins' *An Orthodox Catechism* (1680) is a Particular Baptist adaptation of Heidelberg.

Its fifth-commandment answer requires that the Christian:

- honor;
- love;
- be loyal to father/mother and authorities;
- obey/submit appropriately in correction;
- bear patiently with their failings;
- recognize that God uses authorities to govern us.

Primary/near-primary sources:
- https://anorthodoxcatechism.org/
- Oxford Angus Library bibliographic/original scan landing: https://theangus.rpc.ox.ac.uk/treasures/an-orthodox-catechism-being-the-sum-of-christian-religion-contained-in-the-law-and-gospel/

### Research significance

Collins did not baptize a modern anti-authority adulthood into Baptist identity.

A Particular Baptist catechism can speak very strongly about God governing through parents/authorities.

### Guard

The catechism does not by this answer specify the exact age at which one form of parental government changes or give a detailed adult-daughter/courtship polity.

Use it for **authority theology**, not to fabricate unstated application details.

# 4. Benjamin Keach — children are morally obligated whether regenerate or not

Keach repeatedly exhorts children to honor/obey parents “in all things in the Lord.”

In *A Golden Mine Opened*, he explicitly lists relational duties before Christ:

- husbands toward wives;
- wives toward husbands;
- parents toward children;
- children toward parents.

Parents must bring children up in the fear/nurture of the Lord, pray for them, avoid harsh/bitter provocation and give good example. Children are to honor and obey parents “in all things in the Lord.”

Primary text:
https://llds.ling-phil.ox.ac.uk/llds/xmlui/bitstream/handle/20.500.14106/A47542/A47542.html?isAllowed=y&sequence=5

Keach's child material also insists that unconverted status does not excuse children from moral obligation or reform of life.

Useful source/retrieval control:
https://www.pristinegrace.org/articles/62136/dear-children

Secondary locator guide collecting primary Keach references:
https://proclaimhisname.net/2021/03/01/should-parents-require-obedience-from-their-children-evaluating-the-requirement-for-our-children-to-obey-through-the-works-of-benjamin-keach/

### P0

This strongly confirms the project's earlier rule:

> `UNREGENERATE ≠ MORALLY EXEMPT`.

And it demonstrates that demanding outward obedience while preaching the necessity of regeneration is not foreign to early Particular Baptist pastoral theology.

---

# 5. Keach also guards against parental harshness

The same *Golden Mine* passage that requires child obedience says parents must not provoke children through harshness/bitterness.

This is important methodologically.

The Particular Baptist tradition does not force a choice between:

- serious child obedience;
- serious parental accountability.

Both are under Christ.

### P0

> **A parent cannot use a Baptist doctrine of obedience to exempt his own anger, bitterness, hypocrisy or provocation from judgment.**

---

# 6. Particular Baptist marriage practice was ecclesially/publicly significant

Historical Baptist material reports that Particular Baptist pastors—including figures such as Hanserd Knollys and Benjamin Keach—solemnized marriages and that early Baptist churches debated how marriage should be solemnized outside parish-Church-of-England practice.

Historical source:
https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/tbhs/volumes/01-2.pdf

### Research value

This supports a broader point:

> early Particular Baptists did not necessarily imagine marriage as a purely private romantic contract with no church/community dimension.

### Guard

This evidence does **not** by itself establish a specific parental-consent rule.

Do not fill that gap by inference.

# 7. Particular Baptist use of marital consent as a theological analogy

A modern Founders Ministries exposition of 1689 ch. 25 notes that Hercules Collins used marital consent as an analogy in baptismal polemics: a spiritual-marriage/covenant relation requires a subject capable of such consent.

Source:
https://founders.org/articles/of-marriage-the-1689-baptist-confession/

The page also points to Collins' *An Orthodox Catechism* and early Baptist material.

### Value

This reinforces that **consent/capacity is not a modern feminist intrusion into Baptist marriage theology**.

The 1689 text itself explicitly requires competent consent.

# 9. Confessional hierarchy for future Product prose

When writing from a 1689/Reformed Baptist stance, distinguish four levels.

## LEVEL A — direct biblical/confessional

Examples:

- male/female marriage;
- rational consent;
- marriage in the Lord;
- fifth-commandment honour;
- child obedience in the Lord;
- husband/wife duties;
- parental nurture/non-provocation.

## LEVEL B — strong Particular Baptist pastoral/catechetical tradition

Examples:

- Collins' strong fifth-commandment authority language;
- Keach's strong child-obedience and parental-formation teaching;
- ecclesial/public seriousness of marriage.

## LEVEL C — broader historic Reformed practice

Examples:

- Second Helvetic parental-consent clause;
- Westminster Directory adult-first-marriage parental consent;
- Gouge/Baxter strong parental consent/courtship controls.

## LEVEL D — prudential stronger modern father-led application

Examples:

- adult unmarried daughter ordinarily remains in paternal household until marriage;
- all courtship access routed through father;
- father's approval operates as practical veto in most cases;
- major adult decisions routed through paternal governance.

### P0

A future article may defend Level D, but it must **argue upward from Scripture** and label inference/prudence. It may not simply call Level D “the 1689 doctrine.”

# 10. Stronger conclusion than either modern extreme

The Baptist evidence permits a forceful criticism of autonomous adulthood:

- Collins gives parents/authorities God-derived moral weight;
- Keach demands real obedience and rejects indulgence of children's sinful desires;
- the 1689 makes marriage morally regulated by God rather than individual desire;
- marriage is not open to unions with ungodliness simply because two adults choose them.

Yet it simultaneously blocks paternal absolutism:

- consent of competent parties is explicit;
- obedience is “in the Lord”;
- parents themselves answer to Christ;
- harsh/bitter provocation is condemned;
- no explicit 1689 text grants fathers generic husband-like jurisdiction over all mature unmarried daughters.

### Preferred synthesis

> **Particular Baptist family theology is anti-autonomy without being anti-agency: the child is under real God-given authority, the adult remains a morally situated son or daughter, marriage requires actual consent, and every human relation remains under Christ rather than becoming sovereign.**

# 12. Final guard

Never write:

> “1689 Baptists required parental consent for every adult first marriage.”

Not established by current exact evidence.

Never write:

> “Because 1689 chapter 25 mentions the parties' consent, parents have no special claim once the child is adult.”

Also not established and contradicted by broader Particular Baptist fifth-commandment theology.

Safe:

> **The 1689 Baptist tradition joins competent marital consent to a strong doctrine of parental authority and relational duty. Historic Reformed practice often went further by formally requiring parental consent even after adulthood; whether a modern 1689 Baptist should reproduce that exact procedure requires fresh biblical argument rather than confessional name-dropping.**
