## 2. Mainstream media и sexual scripts

Meta-analysis mainstream sexual media exposure:

- 59 studies;
- 394 effect sizes;
- small but significant associations with sexual attitudes and behaviors;
- effects stronger among adolescents than emerging adults.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30691937/

Longitudinal pornography/sexually explicit material literature similarly links exposure with sexual norms, gender attitudes, sexual preoccupancy and some behaviors, though causal certainty is limited.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28433892/

### Key distinction

Pornography is not the only place that teaches a sexual script.

A sexual script may be taught repeatedly through:

- lyrics;
- jokes;
- memes;
- influencer self-presentation;
- relationship plots;
- comments under videos;
- peer reactions;
- fashion/body-content;
- dating stories.

The script is not always:
> «do this now».

Often it is:
> **«this is what normal, attractive, mature, funny, desirable people are like.»**

## 3. Музыка как part of socialization

Longitudinal study N=548:

- sexual content in music associated with earlier sexual initiation;
- aggressive content associated with more aggression and less prosocial behavior over time.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25828552/

Longitudinal national youth study N=1 461 found degrading sexual lyrics associated with later sexual behavior.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16882784/

AAP policy review historically emphasized that music is deeply embedded in adolescent socialization and increasingly private via personal devices/headphones.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19841124/

### Future article guard

Нельзя создать «индекс нечестивости жанров» из этих studies.

Нужно анализировать:

- content;
- repetition;
- identity significance;
- peer use;
- emotional meaning;
- what the teenager celebrates/imitates.

### Stronger pastoral question

Не только:
> «Есть ли мат в песне?»

Но:
> **«Какой человек в этой музыке представлен как достойный зависти? Что здесь считается силой, любовью, сексуальностью, успехом и свободой?»**

## 4. Vulgarity может становиться маркером принадлежности

Language acts as social identity.

В подростковой группе profanity/sexual humor может выполнять функции:

- signal in-group membership;
- demonstrate non-child status;
- show fearlessness;
- mock adult norms;
- lower shame around sexual topics;
- test boundaries;
- reward transgression with laughter.

Прямой evidence по profanity → sin слабее, чем по peer norms broadly, поэтому нельзя преувеличивать.

Но literature по peer influence показывает, что **norm display and reinforcement** имеют measurable effects.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42383466/

### Theological layer

Еф. 5:3–4 связывает sexual immorality/uncleanness/covetousness с filthy/foolish/crude joking в одном moral speech field.

Кол. 3:8 также включает obscene talk в контекст снятия old self.

Будущая статья может показать, что речь не просто «грязный звук», а часть moral imagination.

## 5. Appearance economy

Social media усиливает:

- quantified approval;
- peer comparison;
- body surveillance;
- sexualized self-presentation;
- pressure to optimize appearance.

Systematic review/meta-analysis 2025 found association between social-media social comparison and body-image concerns/eating-disorder symptoms.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39721448/

Three-level meta-analysis 2025 examined social media and self-objectification.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40311165/

Conceptual review 2025 highlights **incidental appearance exposure**: adolescents do not always search for body/appearance material; it can enter ordinary feeds.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39667292/

### Relevance

Sexualization can begin before explicit sexual behavior.

A teenager may gradually learn to interpret body as:

- instrument of status;
- commodity of attention;
- public project;
- object to rank against others;
- currency for romantic/sexual validation.

This can interact with both vanity and insecurity.

## 6. Boys are not outside body/status pressure

Body/social-media literature often focuses on girls because effects can be particularly visible around thinness/appearance, but boys face their own ideals:

- muscularity;
- dominance;
- toughness;
- sexual success;
- status objects;
- ridicule avoidance.

The future article should not create gender caricatures.

Better:

> girls and boys may be exposed to overlapping but differently configured status economies.

## 7. Consumerism and status

Study N=703 adolescents found materialism positively associated with SNS addiction; associations involved fatalism/self-concept clarity pathways.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37067503/

This does not prove capitalism/social media causes materialism.

But it gives a useful lane:

> **the modern adolescent is not only tempted to lust; he/she is constantly invited to build identity from consumption, appearance and public response.**

This matters biblically because 1 John 2:16 joins:

- desire of flesh;
- desire of eyes;
- pride of life.

The research corpus should not isolate sexuality from broader worship/desire structures.

## 8. Substance/party normalization is another template

Review of media/marketing influences on adolescent substance use finds consistent evidence that youth are highly exposed to substance portrayals and that exposure is associated with later use; social norms/cognitions partly mediate effects.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30393590/

10-year longitudinal study N=4 840 found media substance exposure linked with alcohol use partly through perceived peer norms during adolescence.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31025156/

### Why relevant to our article

A hidden adolescent world often clusters behaviors/norms:

- profanity;
- sexual joking;
- drinking/vaping/drugs;
- sexual status;
- mocking adults;
- music/content identity.

We must avoid assuming all cluster in every child, but peer subculture can bundle them as one package called **«normal cool life»**.

## 9. Risk culture and online challenges

Review of online extreme challenges describes youth vulnerability through peer pressure and access to dangerous content.

Источник:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35912791/

Ofcom qualitative research on violent online content found children described exposure as sometimes unavoidable; teenage boys reported sharing violent material to fit in, and recommender/group messaging were identified as pathways.

Источник:
- https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/encountering-violent-online-content-starts-at-primary-school

### Broad principle

Sex is not the only area where:

`shock → repetition → group humor → normalization → status`.

The same general social mechanism can operate around violence, humiliation, substance use and dangerous challenges.

## 10. AI changes the production side of sexual harm

Common Sense Media 2026 representative US survey of 1 314 teens 13–17:

- nearly half had seen AI-generated explicit sexual material;
- almost a quarter of those exposed had seen such material depicting themselves or someone they knew.

Источник:
- https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/teens-and-explicit-deepfakes-in-the-age-of-ai

### Why this matters structurally

The barrier between fantasy and shareable sexual image has collapsed.

A peer no longer necessarily needs:

- a real intimate photo;
- sophisticated editing;
- adult pornography production.

Synthetic image generation can create sexual humiliation from ordinary photographs.

### Taxonomy needed

- voluntary creation/sharing;
- malicious fabrication;
- coercion;
- bullying;
- extortion;
- victimization.

Never call a victim morally equivalent to the person who fabricated/distributed content.

## 10A. AI companions create a new private conversational environment

AI-generated sexual imagery is only one side of the new AI environment. A distinct 2025–2026 development is the rapid normalization of **AI assistants and AI companions as private, persistent conversational partners**.

### Pew 2026 — U.S. teens

Pew Research Center surveyed U.S. teens ages 13–17 in Sept.–Oct. 2025 and reported in February 2026:

- 64% said they had used AI chatbots;
- 16% said they had used chatbots for casual conversations;
- 12% said they had used them for emotional support or advice.

Source:
- https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/

### eSafety 2026 — Australian children

The Australian eSafety Commissioner reports a demographically representative 2026 survey of 1,950 children ages 10–17:

- 79% had ever used an AI assistant or AI companion;
- 8% had ever used an AI companion specifically;
- 54% of users reported at least one companion-type purpose;
- 22% reported chatting about feelings/challenges in life;
- 20% sought mental-health/wellbeing advice;
- 20% of children who had used an AI assistant/companion reported at least one potentially inappropriate or harmful interaction;
- 32% had shared personal or potentially sensitive information with such tools.

Sources:
- https://www.esafety.gov.au/research/talking-to-machines-childrens-experiences-with-ai-assistants-and-companions
- https://www.esafety.gov.au/industry/basic-online-safety-expectations/ai-services/findings-october-2025

The same eSafety research reports that among teens ages 13–17 who had ever used an AI assistant/companion:

- 4% said it had chatted/messaged with them about kissing or sex;
- 3% said it had shared a sexual image or video with them.

### Why this is not merely another “screen-time” claim

A group chat is a persistent peer environment. An AI companion can be a **persistent conversational environment without another human peer being present at all**.

Potentially relevant features include:

- privacy;
- immediate response at any hour;
- remembered conversational context;
- simulated warmth/intimacy;
- romantic or sexual roleplay on some services;
- disclosure of personal information;
- the possibility that a child uses the system for companionship, advice, emotional regulation or fantasy while parents see only “AI use.”

This suggests a new structural category for the project:

> **peerless hidden companionship** — a private relationship-like conversational space whose social force does not depend on a real peer being online.

This is a research synthesis, not a clinical diagnosis and not a claim that an AI system is literally a friend, person, spiritual being or moral agent equivalent to a human companion.

### Theological guard

Do not create a fourth spiritual enemy called “AI.”

The existing biblical frame remains:

`world + flesh + devil`.

AI systems are part of the opportunity/media environment through which human desires, cultural scripts and sometimes harmful outputs may be encountered. They do not remove personal responsibility and must not be mystified as intrinsically demonic.

### Publication guard

Do not write:

- “most teenagers have AI girlfriends/boyfriends”;
- “AI companions inevitably make children sexually immoral”;
- “12% of teens are emotionally dependent on AI”;
- “talking to an AI assistant is itself sinful.”

The strong factual claim is narrower:

> **A measurable minority of teenagers already use chatbots for ordinary conversation or emotional support, and official child-safety research documents that some AI-companion interactions include sexualized or otherwise harmful content.**

## 11. Mainstream platform ≠ safe by category

Children's Commissioner found pornography exposure on mainstream social platforms as well as dedicated sites.

Sources:
- https://www.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/resource/a-lot-of-it-is-actually-just-abuse-young-people-and-pornography/
- https://www.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/resource/pornography-and-harmful-sexual-behaviour/

Ofcom 2026 continues regulatory research because children’s harmful exposure pathways include feeds, recommendations and user-to-user services.

Sources:
- https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/childrens-online-experiences-research-report
- https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/transparency-notice-research-on-ai-chatbot-social-media-and-video-sharing-platforms-to-explore-online-safety

### Parent implication

A simplistic whitelist:

> «это не porn site, значит безопасно»

is obsolete.

Risk now depends on:

- account age settings;
- recommender behavior;
- peer messages;
- search;
- hashtags;
- shared links;
- platform moderation;
- child behavior;
- chatbot/companion safety controls where conversational AI is used.

Ephesians 2:1–3 integrates:

## 15. Strong working line

> **Современный мир редко говорит подростку: «стань развращённым». Он чаще говорит: «будь настоящим, будь желанным, будь свободным, не будь скучным, получай опыт, не отставай, покажи себя» — а затем наполняет эти слова своими образами того, что такое свобода, зрелость, любовь и успех.**

This is rhetorical synthesis; each component needs claim mapping, not presented as a measured quotation.

A second guarded working line for the AI delta:

> **Раньше тайный цифровой мир почти всегда требовал другого человека или найденного контента. Теперь у подростка может быть ещё и собеседник, который отвечает в любое время, помнит контекст и имитирует близость — даже когда ни один реальный друг не онлайн.**

This is structural synthesis, not a prevalence claim and not a statement that AI is literally a human relationship.

## 16. Conclusion

The article should not become anti-technology nostalgia.

The deeper concern is **formation**.

Who teaches the teenager:

- what body means;
- what sex means;
- what courage means;
- what success means;
- what freedom means;
- what humor means;
- what love means;
- what a man/woman worth admiring looks like;
- and what kind of “voice” deserves trust when a machine can now simulate attention, advice and intimacy?

If family and church leave those questions largely unanswered, modern commercial/peer/media systems will not leave them unanswered.
