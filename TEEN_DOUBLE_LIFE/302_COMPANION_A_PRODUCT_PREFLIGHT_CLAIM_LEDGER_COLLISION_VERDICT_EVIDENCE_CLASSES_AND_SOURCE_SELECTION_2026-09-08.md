# 3. Reader promise / narrative spine

The article should not begin as a generic doctrine of adult children.

Begin with the concrete owner scenario:

> A church-raised son or daughter reaches adulthood after years of hidden life, exposure, conversations and broken promises, then suddenly leaves. The parents are told opposite slogans: “chase him” or “let go immediately.” Scripture gives neither mechanical formula.

Narrative spine:

1. **He left — what changes now?** adult jurisdiction changes; God's lordship and natural relation do not disappear.
2. **First hours/days: clarify safety before winning the argument.**
3. **Luke 15: what the parable actually says — and what it does not say.**
4. **Contact without control; truth without a daily courtroom.**
5. **When the mode must change.**
6. **Love without false normalcy; relational distance without family excommunication.**
7. **Hope in God without credulity toward repentance claims.**
8. **The long middle: prayer, grief, ordinary kindness, other family duties.**
9. **If the child turns back: receive the person, do not manufacture more exile.**
10. **Mercy, forgiveness, trust and re-entrustment are not one switch.**
11. **Do not become the elder brother.**
12. **Christ and sovereign grace remain the final hope.**

House/money questions should receive only a short signpost to Companion B.

General fifth-commandment adulthood taxonomy should receive only what is necessary to prevent `18 = moral emancipation`; detailed doctrine belongs to Companion C.

# 6. Claim ledger — Luke 15 exegesis

## Claim C1 — primary meaning

> Luke 15 is centrally about lostness, repentance, divine mercy/joy in receiving sinners, and the elder-brother/Pharisaic refusal to rejoice.

### Evidence class

`DIRECT_SCRIPTURE + CLASSICAL_EXEGESIS_STRONG`.

Primary controls:

- Luke 15:1–32;
- Calvin on Luke 15;
- `59`, `90`, `99`, `106`, `216`.

## Claim C2 — procedural silence

> The parable does not establish a universal parental contact schedule during the son's absence.

### Evidence class

`DIRECT_EXEGETICAL_NEGATIVE_GUARD` / safest expressed as limitation of what the text states.

The narrative does **not** tell us:

- warning cadence before departure;
- letters/messages;
- reports during absence;
- duration;
- tracking;
- no-contact request.

### Locked

`NARRATIVE SILENCE ≠ COMMAND`.

Therefore neither is warranted:

- `Luke 15 commands zero contact`;
- `Luke 15 commands continuous pursuit`.

## Claim C3 — consequences

> The story permits saying that the father is not shown financing the far country and that the son's misery becomes part of the providential road by which he comes to himself.

### Evidence class

`DIRECT_NARRATIVE_OBSERVATION + CLASSICAL_EXEGESIS_STRONG`, parental transfer = `PRUDENTIAL_APPLICATION`.

### Guard

`ALLOW CONSEQUENCES ≠ ENGINEER MISERY`.

Parents do not know what dose of suffering would produce repentance and may not manufacture danger, homelessness, humiliation, or sin as conversion technique.

## Claim C4 — return

> The son's return is penitential, not merely geographical.

### Evidence class

`DIRECT_SCRIPTURE + CLASSICAL_EXEGESIS_STRONG`.

He comes to himself, rises, returns, names sin against heaven and father, and confesses.

### Guard

Material distress can be an occasion of awakening; do not require perfectly purified introspective motives before hearing a returning child.

# 15. Gospel / sovereignty closure

The article must not end with a contact algorithm.

### Final theological center

- parents cannot regenerate an adult child;
- consequences cannot guarantee repentance;
- the perfect wording cannot create a new heart;
- God gives repentance;
- God uses means: truth, prayer, admonition, providence, church, ordinary love;
- Christ receives sinners who truly come to Him;
- justification is not earned by a successful family probation period;
- therefore parents can act faithfully without pretending to control the outcome.

Use:

- 2 Tim. 2:25;
- Luke 15 in its Christ/Pharisee context;
- cross-series Gospel control `131_CROSS_PART_GOSPEL_CENTER...` where needed;
- Calvin on sovereign repentance;
- 1689 only where exact confessional paragraphs genuinely support the statement.

### Desired ending

Not:

> “Here is the correct sequence that will bring your child back.”

But:

> **Speak truth without trying to become the Spirit. Love without financing the lie. Pray without demanding omniscience. Wait without suspending every other duty. And if God grants repentance, do not stand at the door as the elder brother when the Father teaches you to rejoice.**

This is a pastoral synthesis, not a claim that the parable makes the modern parent identical to God the Father.
