# Parent Accountability to Church for Child Nurture
## Paedobaptist Insight and 1689 Analogy

**Status:** RESEARCH / ECCLESIOLOGICAL SYNTHESIS / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `58`, `72–73`, `159–160`, `273–286`

## 1. The PCA insight

PCA BCO 28 places primary spiritual nurture/training responsibility on parents while also saying parents are responsible to the church for the faithful discharge of that duty.

This produces a powerful two-level structure:

- church does not replace parents;
- parents do not become spiritually autonomous from church accountability merely because the subject is their child.

## 2. Can a 1689 Baptist borrow this principle?

Yes, in a controlled form.

A Baptist need not make the child a church member in order to say that **member parents themselves are accountable to Christ’s church for serious unrepentant failures in their own discipleship**, including grave parenting sins.

Examples can include:

- knowingly enabling serious immorality;
- covering abuse to preserve family reputation;
- refusing basic biblical instruction while claiming Christian faithfulness;
- provoking/cruelty/violence;
- persistent refusal to protect siblings from known danger;
- using church activity as a substitute for actual parenting;
- directly teaching children to violate God’s commands.

### Guard

Not every imperfect parenting judgment becomes a discipline case.

## 3. Jurisdiction model

### Child nonmember

No formal Baptist member censure.

### Parent member

Real church accountability for the **parent’s own conduct**.

### Child pastoral care

Teaching, admonition, catechesis, safeguarding and relational care according to appropriate access/parental authority.

This triangulation can close part of the institutional gap:

`CHILD NOT MEMBER` does not imply `FAMILY SPIRITUAL ORDER OUTSIDE CHURCH CONCERN`.

## 4. Why this is important for permissive families

Suppose parents say:

> “Our sixteen-year-old is unregenerate, so we let him choose whether pornography, sexual relationships, profanity and church attendance matter; he has to decide his own faith.”

A church should not merely debate the teenager’s membership status.

It can ask whether the **Christian parents** have abandoned duties Scripture directly assigns to them.

This changes the frame:

- parents cannot regenerate him;
- parents cannot force saving profession;
- parents are still commanded to bring him up under the Lord’s discipline/instruction;
- church leaders may teach/counsel the parents accordingly.

## 5. Why this is not Presbyterian membership through the back door

The child still does not receive:

- baptism;
- formal membership;
- Table privileges;
- member censure

without the Baptist profession/admission process.

The accountability lies first with the parents as members and with elders as teachers/shepherds of members, while pastoral ministry reaches the child as a known nonmember.

## 6. Opposite danger — church micromanagement

A church can misuse this principle by turning every parenting disagreement into ecclesial enforcement.

Must distinguish:

- explicit biblical command;
- prudent parenting judgment;
- house rule;
- educational choice;
- conscience issue;
- preference.

Church discipline cannot be used to enforce one family culture where Scripture permits multiple faithful options.

## 7. Safeguarding exception

Where abuse, sexual harm, criminal conduct or serious danger is alleged, ordinary family/church internal processes do not displace lawful civil/safeguarding obligations.
