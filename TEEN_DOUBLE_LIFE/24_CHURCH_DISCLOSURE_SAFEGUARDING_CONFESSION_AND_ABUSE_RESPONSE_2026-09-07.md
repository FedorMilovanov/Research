# Церковь после признания: исповедание греха, safeguarding и реакция на возможное насилие

**Статус:** RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Дата:** 2026-09-07

---

## 1. Почему этот модуль обязателен

Будущая статья может побудить родителей, подростков и служителей вскрывать скрытую сексуальную жизнь. Это создаёт ответственность.

За словами:

- «сексуальная переписка»;
- «отправлял фото»;
- «мне присылали»;
- «старший человек писал»;
- «меня заставили»;
- «я соглашался, но боялся»

могут скрываться **совершенно разные моральные и safeguarding ситуации**.

Церковь не имеет права свести всё к одной категории «сексуальный грех подростка».

## 2. Fundamental taxonomy

### A. Voluntary sin

Examples:

- deliberate pornography seeking;
- consensual sexualized chat with peers;
- voluntary image requests/sharing;
- deception to preserve access.

Requires:

- moral truth;
- repentance;
- consequences/boundaries;
- restoration/accountability.

### B. Victimization/coercion

Examples:

- grooming;
- adult sexual solicitation;
- threats;
- sextortion;
- unwanted explicit content;
- image-based abuse;
- fabricated sexual deepfake;
- pressure by older/more powerful peer.

Requires first:

- safety;
- reporting according to law/policy;
- evidence preservation where appropriate;
- trauma-informed support.

### C. Mixed case

A teenager may have made sinful choices **and** subsequently become exploited.

Example:

> voluntarily sent an image once → later blackmailed.

His initial action may require repentance; **the blackmail is not his guilt**.

This distinction is essential.

## 3. Good safeguarding response begins with calm listening, not interrogation

Current Church of England safeguarding guidance for disclosures involving children/young people instructs church officers to:

- remain calm;
- listen carefully;
- let young person tell story in own time;
- ask only clarifying questions;
- reassure that disclosure was right;
- not promise secrecy;
- explain what will happen next;
- pass concern to safeguarding responsibility/statutory pathway.

It explicitly warns against probing/leading questions and says ordinary church officers are not to conduct the investigative interview.

Sources:
- https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/safeguarding-e-manual/responding-well-victims-and-survivors-abuse/section-1-responding-well-all-victims-and-survivors
- https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/safeguarding-e-manual/reporting-safeguarding-concerns-and-allegations/section-1-what-do-receipt-safeguarding-concern-or-allegation

### Why useful beyond Anglican polity

Legal/reporting structures differ by jurisdiction/denomination, but basic disclosure principles are broadly transferable:

> listen, do not contaminate account through amateur investigation, do not promise impossible confidentiality, escalate safeguarding concern properly.

## 4. Evangelical ministries need actual child-safety systems, not just trusted leaders

Evangelical Council for Abuse Prevention (ECAP) Child Safety Standards organize ministry protection around:

- governance;
- child safety program;
- policies/procedures;
- access control/supervision;
- screening;
- background checks;
- training;
- incident response plan;
- receiving/making reports;
- notifications;
- organizational investigation boundaries.

Source:
- https://ecap.net/standards/

### Strong implication

> **“Мы все братья и доверяем друг другу” — не safeguarding system.**

Christian trust does not abolish prudence, documentation, screening or reporting structures.

## 5. A healthy church culture must make disclosure possible

Church of England current guidance emphasizes that healthy culture should:

- facilitate disclosure;
- make reporting routes clear;
- explain what happens after report;
- clarify support and limits of confidentiality;
- avoid critical/closed/fear-generating organizational culture.

Source:
- https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/safeguarding-e-manual/responding-well-victims-and-survivors-abuse/section-1-responding-well-all-victims-and-survivors

### Relevance to our article

A church can preach strongly against sexual sin yet still create a culture where:

- victims fear being blamed;
- teenagers fear family humiliation;
- leaders protect reputation;
- disclosures are suppressed.

That is not biblical holiness.

Truth must be safe **to tell**, even when what is told is ugly.

## 6. Do not promise absolute pastoral confidentiality to a minor disclosure

Safeguarding guidance explicitly warns against promises like:

> «I won't tell anyone.»

Where a child may be at risk, information may have to be shared through proper safeguarding/statutory channels.

Source:
- https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/safeguarding-e-manual/reporting-safeguarding-concerns-and-allegations/section-1-what-do-receipt-safeguarding-concern-or-allegation

### Future pastoral language

Better:

> «Я не буду распространять это людям, которым не нужно знать. Но если речь о твоей безопасности или насилии, мне может понадобиться подключить тех, кто обязан тебя защищать. Я объясню тебе, что произойдёт дальше.»

This is sample principle, not jurisdiction-specific legal script.

## 7. The church must not conduct amateur forensic interviews

Why not press for every detail?

- it may retraumatize child;
- leading questions can distort account;
- explicit sexual detail may be unnecessary pastorally;
- trained authorities may need to interview.

Church safeguarding guidance says disclosure should unfold at survivor’s pace and questions should be limited to clarification.

Source:
- https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/safeguarding-e-manual/responding-well-victims-and-survivors-abuse/section-1-responding-well-all-victims-and-survivors

### Application to pornography confession

For voluntary sin, accountability needs enough truth to understand pattern/risk.

It usually does **not** need a vivid catalogue of pornographic scenes.

Truthfulness ≠ rehearsing imagery.

## 8. Matthew 18 must not be misused in abuse cases

Church of England spiritual-abuse guidance explicitly warns against telling a victim to use Matthew 18 by confronting the alleged abuser directly; that can be harmful.

Source:
- https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/safeguarding-e-manual/safeguarding-children-young-people-and-vulnerable-adults/section-42-spiritual-abuse

### Theological significance

Church discipline texts cannot be used to bypass:

- protection of minors;
- criminal reporting duties;
- power imbalance;
- evidence preservation.

Forgiveness and reconciliation are not substitutes for justice/safety.

## 9. Repentance is not a reason to stop safeguarding

Suppose an older youth/adult offender says:

> «I repented.»

Christian theology should rejoice at genuine repentance.

But repentance does not automatically establish:

- immediate trustworthiness;
- eligibility for child access;
- absence of legal consequences;
- restored ministry role.

### Key principle

> **Forgiveness, trust, access and office are distinct categories.**

A repentant person may still require strong permanent/long-term boundaries to protect children.

This is pastoral/theological synthesis; exact legal/organizational actions depend on jurisdiction and facts.

---

## 10. The teenager who sinned may also need safeguarding from himself/others

A child caught in pornography/sexting may face:

- adult contacts;
- blackmail;
- redistribution of images;
- suicidal shame;
- retaliatory peer bullying;
- coercive relationship.

Therefore discovery should include a **risk assessment**, not only discipline.

Questions at high level:

- Is anyone older involved?
- Has anyone threatened you?
- Has any image been distributed?
- Is someone demanding more?
- Are you afraid of anyone?
- Are you in immediate danger?

Avoid investigative detail beyond role competence.

---

## 11. Parents and pastors must avoid making family/church reputation the highest good

A dangerous reaction:

> «Nobody must find out; this will shame the church/family.»

This can protect abusers, deepen secrecy and teach child that public image matters more than truth.

### Biblical correction

Eph. 5:11–13 emphasizes exposure of works of darkness.

Prov. 28:13 contrasts concealment with confession/forsaking.

Church safeguarding is not a secular intrusion into gospel ministry; properly ordered, it is one concrete way of refusing to cover darkness.

---

## 12. Strong moral language and survivor-centred response can coexist

Non-judgmental listening during disclosure does not mean moral relativism.

It means **do not decide guilt/credibility while child is trying to tell what happened**.

Later, voluntary actions can be addressed morally with precision.

### Distinction

- `receiving disclosure` = calm, safe, clarifying, protective;
- `moral/pastoral assessment` = later, with facts and appropriate roles;
- `investigation/legal response` = competent authorities/processes where required.

Confusing these moments can damage truth-seeking.

---

## 13. A church needs a path for confessions that are not abuse allegations too

Not every adolescent pornography confession triggers statutory safeguarding.

Church should still have wise pastoral architecture:

- same-sex mature mentor where appropriate;
- parent involvement appropriate to minor status and safety;
- clear privacy boundaries;
- concrete accountability;
- no voyeuristic questioning;
- digital safety plan;
- theological counsel;
- restoration review;
- professional mental-health support when indicated.

This is proposed pastoral architecture, not universal clinical protocol.

## 14. Church youth group itself can be a hidden-risk environment

Earlier peer-contagion module showed religious label does not cancel peer dynamics.

Church safeguarding standards should account for:

- youth group chats;
- leader-to-minor DMs;
- rides/offsite contact;
- overnight events;
- private counseling spaces;
- image sharing;
- volunteer screening;
- adult/child ratios;
- reporting pathways.

ECAP standards provide an evangelical framework for governance, supervision, screening and response.

Source:
- https://ecap.net/standards/

## 15. Theological category error to avoid: victim's shame ≠ guilt

Sexual victimization often produces shame even where victim did not sin.

Future article must never tell abused child:

> «You are sexually dirty now.»

If voluntary sinful choices occurred, name those precisely.

If violence/coercion occurred, guilt belongs to perpetrator for that act.

This precision is essential to a biblical doctrine of justice.

## 16. Support should continue after initial disclosure

Church safeguarding guidance emphasizes ongoing survivor support rather than treating report as one administrative event.

Source:
- https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/safeguarding-e-manual/responding-well-victims-and-survivors-abuse/section-2-co-creating-support-victims-and-survivors-church-based-abuse

### Relevance

After a digital sexual crisis, family/church may focus on:

- device;
- offender;
- rule violation.

The adolescent may still be carrying:

- fear;
- humiliation;
- social fallout;
- distrust;
- spiritual confusion.

Restoration is longer than incident closure.

## 17. Publication-ready claims

**Claim A:** churches need clear safeguarding/reporting structures and trained responses; trust alone is insufficient.  
**Strength:** current institutional standards.

**Claim B:** receiving child disclosure should emphasize calm listening, limited clarifying questions, no false confidentiality promises and proper escalation.  
**Strength:** current formal safeguarding guidance.

**Claim C:** voluntary sin and victimization can coexist in same case and must be morally separated.  
**Strength:** analytical/biblical-safeguarding synthesis.

**Claim D:** repentance/forgiveness do not automatically restore trust, access or ministry fitness.  
**Strength:** theological/safeguarding synthesis.

## 18. Strong conclusion

> **Когда тайная сексуальная жизнь подростка выходит на свет, церковь должна быть одновременно святой и безопасной: не называть грех нормой, не называть жертву виновной, не прикрывать репутацией зло, не обещать невозможную тайну и не превращать пастора в следователя. Истина, покаяние, защита и справедливость не конкурируют друг с другом.**
