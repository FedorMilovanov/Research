# Church Children — Membership vs Pastoral Ownership
## Keys, Jurisdiction, and Care

**Status:** RESEARCH / ECCLESIOLOGY-PASTORAL CONTROL / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `35`, `41`, `50`, `95–96`, `150–160`, `273–285`

> Central problem: Baptist ecclesiology often answers “who is a member?” carefully but can leave “which nonmembers are nevertheless our sustained pastoral responsibility?” underdefined.

## 1. Membership and shepherding are not coextensive concepts

A local church may have pastoral responsibilities toward people who are not formal members:

- regular children of members;
- baptism candidates;
- long-term attenders;
- unbelieving spouses;
- seekers;
- vulnerable youth present in ministry;
- visitors requiring safeguarding response.

This does not mean the church possesses identical keys/jurisdiction over every person.

### Locked distinction

`PASTORAL CARE / ADMONITION / TEACHING / ACCESS STEWARDSHIP`

≠

`FORMAL MEMBER CENSURE / EXCOMMUNICATION`.

## 2. The paedobaptist solution

PCA/OPC/URCNA substantially collapse this particular gap for believers’ children by assigning them formal baptized/noncommuning membership.

Advantages:

- membership records name them;
- elders/session/consistory owns catechesis and oversight;
- discipline categories exist;
- waywardness cannot be dismissed solely for nonprofession;
- the child’s relation to church is institutionally durable.

Disadvantage from Baptist perspective:

- formal membership is granted absent credible personal profession.

## 3. The 1689 Baptist solution must be different, not absent

The Baptist answer cannot simply be:

> “They are not members.”

That answers the keys question but not the shepherding question.

A coherent model:

### Formal church

Members are visible saints/professors admitted according to Baptist polity.

### Covenant/Christian household

Parents possess direct God-given formation/discipline duties toward children.

### Congregational pastoral field

Children regularly living under the church’s Word are known objects of:

- teaching;
- catechesis;
- evangelism;
- admonition;
- safeguarding;
- relationship;
- role/access decisions;
- prayer.

The church does not call these things excommunication when no membership exists.

## 4. A practical “pastoral ownership” category

Possible Research terminology:

`KNOWN NONMEMBER UNDER SUSTAINED PASTORAL CARE`.

This is descriptive, not a new sacramental status.

Examples:

- twelve-year-old child of two members attending weekly;
- sixteen-year-old raised in church who openly rejects faith;
- seventeen-year-old baptism candidate whose profession becomes doubtful;
- dependent child in serious sexual/digital sin affecting youth peers.

## 5. What pastoral ownership can include

Without claiming member keys:

- assigning a pastor/elder who knows the family;
- direct conversation with child according to age/safety policy;
- coordinated parent support;
- catechesis and gospel explanation;
- specific admonition of known sin;
- prayer/follow-up;
- restrictions on choir/platform/trusted peer access where warranted;
- safeguarding measures;
- distinguishing struggling/help-seeking from corrupting/defiant conduct;
- re-evaluating baptism candidacy;
- maintaining public worship access where safe.

## 6. What it cannot automatically include

- suspension from Lord’s Table if never admitted;
- excommunication from membership never possessed;
- public censure as though member process had occurred;
- compelled profession;
- unlimited elder access overriding parents/safeguarding;
- replacing parental government over ordinary home life.

## 7. Why this matters for Part III

The church article needs to answer a question more basic than choir policy:

> **Does our congregation believe it has a duty to know and directly address the spiritual lives of the children growing up under its preaching, even before they become members?**

If the answer is functionally no, the church has created a gap that paedobaptist polity fills by membership.

The Baptist response should not be to deny that the gap exists; it should be to fill it without changing the membership boundary.

## 8. Baptist accountability to learn from PCA

PCA explicitly says parents are responsible to the church for spiritual nurture/training of children.

A Baptist church should investigate whether an analogous, non-paedobaptist principle is warranted:

> member parents are themselves accountable as members for fulfilling biblical parenting duties.

This does **not** make the child a member. It makes the parent’s neglect potentially a member-accountability issue where serious and unrepentant.

This is a potentially powerful structural answer:

- direct parental authority over child;
- church keys over member parent;
- pastoral care toward child;
- no inherited membership needed.

## 9. Hard case — parents refuse any church involvement

Distinguish:

- ordinary parental discretion;
- church counsel;
- a parent’s own sinful neglect/cover-up;
- safety/abuse reporting obligations;
- child membership status.

Church leaders must not claim ownership of a nonmember minor in a way that overrides lawful parental authority. But member parents also cannot invoke “my child, none of your business” to shield their own serious sin or prevent all ordinary pastoral concern while participating in church life.

## 10. Hard case — youth corrupts other youth

Even without membership, the church has stewardship over:

- its gatherings;
- its leaders;
- vulnerable persons;
- ministry roles;
- access to supervised/unsupervised settings.

Therefore restricting a nonmember youth from certain access is not counterfeit excommunication. It is ordinary stewardship/protection.

## 11. Final synthesis

> **Баптистское “ты ещё не член церкви” должно отвечать на вопрос о ключах, а не становиться ответом на вопрос “должна ли церковь тебя пасти вообще?”. Пресвитерианская система решает этот вопрос через детское членство. Реформированный баптист может сохранить более узкую границу членства, но обязан построить столь же серьёзную систему катехизации, личного увещевания, поддержки родителей и защиты церковной среды.**
