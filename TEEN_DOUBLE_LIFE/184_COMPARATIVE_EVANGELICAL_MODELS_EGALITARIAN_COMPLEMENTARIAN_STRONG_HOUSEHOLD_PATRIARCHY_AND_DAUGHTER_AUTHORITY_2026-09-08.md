# Comparative Evangelical Models
## Egalitarian / Mainstream Complementarian / Strong Household-Patriarchal Approaches

**Status:** RESEARCH / COMPARATIVE THEOLOGY / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08

## Purpose

The public debate is often caricatured as:

`feminism` vs `Bible`.

That is too crude for a serious article. Among professing conservative/evangelical Christians there are at least three distinguishable models, each appealing to Scripture differently.

The project is confessionally conservative and complementarian, but should understand competing readings accurately before criticizing them.

---

## Model A — Evangelical egalitarian / mutualist

Representative source:
- CBE International: https://www.cbeinternational.org/resource/head-house-home/
- https://www.cbeinternational.org/resource/case-mutual-submission-ephesians-5-0/
- https://www.cbeinternational.org/resource/biblical-submission-within-marriage/

### Typical claims

- men and women are equal in dignity and also equal in governing authority within marriage/church, depending on version;
- Eph. 5:21 controls the household code as mutual submission;
- `kephale` in Eph. 5/1 Cor. 11 may mean source/origin rather than ruler;
- NT household texts transform ancient hierarchy toward mutuality;
- Gal. 3:28 is frequently used as part of a larger equality argument;
- coercive patriarchal practice is viewed as a major historical distortion.

### Strongest cautions this stream raises

These should not be dismissed merely because the project rejects egalitarian conclusions:

1. abuse can be theologized as submission;
2. husband headship must not be equated with selfish domination;
3. not every male possesses authority over every female;
4. women's gifts/agency are real biblical data;
5. descriptive ancient patriarchy is not automatically normative simply because it existed.

### Project disagreement

The project does **not** accept that Eph. 5 wife-submission/husband-headship can simply be dissolved into symmetrical marital authority.

It also rejects using Gal. 3:28 to erase role distinctions established elsewhere.

### Guard

Criticize the actual exegesis, not a stereotype such as `egalitarians hate family`.

## Model B — Mainstream evangelical complementarian

Representative sources:
- CBMW Danvers Statement: https://cbmw.org/about/the-danvers-statement/
- John Piper on parental blessing: https://www.desiringgod.org/interviews/is-it-important-to-have-your-parents-blessing-in-marriage
- Sinclair Ferguson/Ligonier on lifelong honour: https://learn.ligonier.org/podcasts/things-unseen-with-sinclair-ferguson/honoring-our-parents-for-life

### Typical claims

- man and woman are equal as image-bearers;
- husband bears a unique leadership/headship responsibility toward wife;
- wife has a distinctive submission responsibility toward her own husband;
- qualified male eldership is normative;
- adult children continue to honor parents;
- adulthood/new household changes the form of parental authority;
- parental blessing in marriage is highly important but not normally absolute for a competent adult.

Piper's formulation is particularly useful:

> parental blessing is important, not absolute; adult Christians should take parents very seriously, use time/prayer/other mature people, and may still marry against parental judgment if after serious process they conclude the parents are wrong.

### Strength

This model strongly resists egalitarian flattening while distinguishing:

`father/daughter` from `husband/wife`.

### Risk

In some churches it can become practically individualist:

- `ask parents for advice, then do whatever you want`;
- courtship becomes couple-only emotionally before families know what is happening;
- the fifth commandment becomes ceremonial courtesy rather than meaningful deference.

The project may critique that practical weakness without claiming mainstream complementarian doctrine itself commands atomism.

## Model C — Strong household / biblical-patriarchal / father-guardianship stream

Representative examples:
- Chalcedon article: https://chalcedon.edu/resources/articles/daddys-girl-courtship-and-a-fathers-rights
- strong courtship literature summarized in `177–181`.

### Typical claims

- father has covenantal government over household;
- unmarried daughter remains under father until marriage;
- courtship should be explicitly father-governed;
- father may veto suitors;
- daughters should ordinarily remain under paternal protection/provision until marriage;
- marriage marks transfer into husband's headship.

### What this model sees clearly

- modern autonomy is historically unusual and biblically suspect;
- family is more than an emotional support network;
- parents have real protective duties;
- private romance can bypass wisdom and authority;
- fatherless courtship can leave young women vulnerable to manipulation;
- marriage is familial/community-forming, not merely a private romantic contract.

### Serious exegetical risks

Some representatives make claims stronger than the texts securely support, for example:

- generic `male covering` as if every adult woman must have a male human governor;
- treating daughter as a form of paternal property;
- transferring all Mosaic household/civil structures directly into modern church life;
- using 1 Cor. 11:3 husband/man language to create universal father-like authority over adult women;
- ignoring 1 Cor. 7:34/39, Numbers 30 age questions, Rebekah's consent, or historical distinction between minor and grown children;
- treating civil/economic independence itself as rebellion.

### P0 red line

The project must explicitly reject any claim that a father owns a daughter in a creaturely analogue of God's ownership.

God alone has absolute lordship.

## Focus on the Family / Boundless — useful intermediate conservative approach

Source:
- https://www.boundless.org/advice/why-should-a-woman-be-under-someones-protection-in-issues-regarding-marriage/

This article is especially valuable because it addresses the exact question.

It argues:

- marriage is communal, not merely private;
- family/older believers should be involved;
- a father asking a suitor about intentions/character is not inherently meddling;
- individualistic romantic privacy is historically recent;
- father-submission differs from husband-submission;
- parental blessing should normally matter;
- where faithful parents are absent, church/community wisdom may fill a protective role.

### Guard

Some Trinitarian-submission argumentation used in this older article belongs to a debated theological discussion and should not become a foundation for family authority claims.

Use the family/counsel argument; do not build on contested eternal-subordination formulations.

## Westminster/Puritan model — not identical to any modern label

Westminster Larger Catechism 124–132 + Gouge/Baxter/Calvin/Gill produce a distinct historical picture:

- hierarchy is real;
- authority is relation-specific;
- adult status changes power;
- honour persists;
- lawful parental counsels deserve real obedience/deference;
- superiors have reciprocal duties and can sin by tyranny;
- family participation in marriage is substantial;
- adult children's welfare/will cannot simply be erased.

This is arguably a better historical control for the project than importing one twentieth/twenty-first-century movement label wholesale.

## Cultural feminism vs theological egalitarianism — distinguish

Do not use `feminism` as a single undifferentiated category.

At minimum distinguish:

1. **political/legal feminism** — civil rights, property, education, voting, protection from violence;
2. **cultural individualism** — self-definition, independence as a virtue, suspicion of inherited obligations;
3. **sexual-revolution feminism** — liberation from traditional sexual/marital norms;
4. **evangelical egalitarianism** — theological claim of nonhierarchical male/female roles;
5. **radical/ideological feminism** — may explicitly interpret patriarchy itself as oppressive structure.

A conservative Christian may reject (3)/(4)/(5) in important respects while recognizing that not every gain associated historically with women's civil capacity is rebellion against Scripture.

### Example

A woman owning property, receiving education, earning income, or exercising a genuine spiritual gift does not itself establish egalitarian marriage theology.

Prov. 31 itself prevents lazy equations of female economic competence with feminist rebellion.

## Publication comparison table

| Question | Egalitarian | Mainstream complementarian | Strong household/patriarchal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Husband headship | re-read as mutual/nonhierarchical | direct marital leadership | direct and often broader household government |
| Wife submission | mutualized | distinctive wife-to-husband duty | strong wife obedience/submission |
| Adult daughter | autonomous adult with family counsel | adult responsibility + lifelong honour | generally under father until marriage |
| Parental marriage consent | advice/blessing | highly important, not usually absolute | often required/decisive |
| Courtship | couple-led with counsel | couple + family/church involvement | father/family-led |
| Female work | unrestricted by sex as principle | ordinarily permissible, family duties control | some versions highly home-centered |
| Main danger seen | patriarchy/domination | both egalitarianism and domination | autonomy/feminism |

This table describes tendencies, not every adherent.

## Project recommendation

Build from:

`Scripture → 1689/WLC relational authority grammar → Puritan/Reformed exegesis → modern comparison`.

Do not make a modern movement identity the interpretive starting point.

### Strong synthesis

> **The antidote to feminist individualism is not paternal absolutism. It is ordered creaturely dependence under God: real fathers, real mothers, real adult responsibility, real marriage headship, real church authority, real limits, and real accountability for every authority-holder.**
