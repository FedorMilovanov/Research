# High-Accountability 1689 Pastoral Model Without Infant Baptism

**Status:** RESEARCH / PASTORAL SYNTHESIS / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `34`, `43`, `150`, `159–161`, `273–276`

> Purpose: answer the user’s practical concern without using a sacramental conclusion as a shortcut. This is a Baptist synthesis that deliberately learns from the strongest paedobaptist accountability structures while preserving credobaptist ecclesiology.

## 1. Governing proposition

> **A Baptist church can reject infant baptism and inherited membership while still refusing to treat its children as ecclesially anonymous, morally neutral, or pastorally optional.**

The church does not possess the keys over a nonmember in the same formal sense as over a member. But lack of member-censure jurisdiction does not erase:

- parental stewardship;
- pastoral responsibility toward known children;
- repeated gospel-light accountability;
- duty to hear the Word;
- moral law;
- the need for present repentance and faith;
- protection of the congregation from a corrupting influence.

## 2. The five-axis model

Every child/youth case should be classified on five independent axes.

### Axis 1 — creaturely moral responsibility

Already present.

Questions:

- Does the child know the conduct is forbidden by God?
- What moral command is implicated?
- Is sin accidental/immature, struggling, concealed, defended, or recruiting?

Regeneration does not create this axis.

### Axis 2 — filial / household responsibility

Already present while lawful parental authority applies.

Questions:

- minor/dependent/adult-at-home/independent?
- what lawful household rule was communicated?
- what protective duty do parents owe siblings/child?
- what consequence is proportionate?

Baptism does not create this axis.

### Axis 3 — gospel-light responsibility

Varies by history.

Questions:

- years under preaching/catechesis?
- understands gospel substantially?
- prior profession or baptism candidacy?
- repeated personal admonition?
- deliberate rejection of known truth?

This explains why a church-raised unregenerate youth may be `not an ordinary unbeliever` without being a formal church member.

### Axis 4 — ecclesial status / jurisdiction

Must be factually precise.

Possible Baptist statuses:

- child-attendee / nonmember;
- regular youth participant;
- baptism candidate;
- credible professor awaiting baptism/admission;
- baptized member;
- member under discipline;
- former/excommunicated member.

Only this axis controls formal member censures/keys.

### Axis 5 — soteriological credibility

Questions:

- credible present profession?
- warfare/repentance/confession?
- defended impenitence?
- temporary/false profession possible?

Do not claim infallible regeneration or reprobation.

## 3. What to learn from Presbyterian/Dutch Reformed polity

### Learn: every child should have an ecclesial address

A Baptist church can maintain a pastoral child/youth register without calling it a membership roll.

Possible fields:

- family/guardians;
- ordinary pastoral contact;
- catechesis stage;
- baptism/profession status;
- safeguarding constraints;
- known serious pastoral concerns;
- assigned elder/pastoral owner where appropriate.

**Guard:** do not create quasi-membership with sacramental/disciplinary rights that contradict the church constitution.

### Learn: parents are not spiritually private actors

Even where parents carry primary authority, church leaders may appropriately ask whether parents are faithfully instructing, disciplining, protecting, and bringing children under the Word.

This does not make elders substitute parents.

### Learn: wayward children require a reclamation plan

Not simply:

> “He is not saved; keep praying.”

Possible responses:

- named pastoral conversation;
- direct Scripture admonition;
- parent-elder coordination;
- restriction from trusted/representative youth roles where needed;
- protective peer boundaries;
- continued access to public Word and gospel invitation;
- periodic re-engagement rather than institutional forgetting.

### Learn: age of discretion is more useful than a magic civil birthday

Moral address and gospel summons are calibrated to understanding but are not suspended until 18.

## 4. What must not be borrowed

If the church remains 1689/credobaptist, do not silently import:

- membership by parental faith/birth;
- baptism before credible personal profession;
- covenant-breaking language that presupposes formal paedobaptist membership unless clearly marked analogically;
- member censure/excommunication for someone never admitted as a member;
- sacramental assurance from infancy.

## 5. The anti-permissive Baptist household sentence

A parent may truthfully communicate:

> “Ты не обязан изображать, будто ты уже верующий. Мы не будем покупать послушание ложным исповеданием. Но то, что ты ещё не веришь, не делает ложь, блуд, жестокость, наркотики, порнографию, богохульство или сознательный бунт законными. Бог уже твой Творец, мы уже твои родители, Его Слово уже говорит к тебе, и Христос сегодня повелевает тебе покаяться и веровать.”

**Status:** pastoral synthesis, not a required script.

## 6. Church response categories for an unbaptized church-raised adolescent

### Public worship

Ordinarily preserve access; do not excommunicate a nonmember from hearing the Word.

### Family worship / parental instruction

Parents may require ordinary household participation without forcing false testimony.

### Youth social access

Not an unconditional right. A persistently corrupting youth may require supervised/restricted access depending on risk and role.

### Choir / representative platform

Not equivalent to public worship. Where known defended scandal contradicts what the role communicates, restriction may be appropriate without pretending the youth was formally excommunicated.

### Small group / discipleship

Can remain a place of admonition/help, but leaders must distinguish help-seeking struggle from active recruitment/corruption/safety risk.

### Lord’s Table / baptism

Do not use as behavioral leverage or conversion manufacture. Credible personal faith/profession controls admission according to Baptist polity.

### Formal church discipline

Do not counterfeit membership jurisdiction. If not a member, use pastoral admonition, parental authority, role/access stewardship, safeguarding, and gospel warning rather than calling it excommunication.

## 7. Baptized Baptist minor

Once a credible professor has been baptized and admitted as a member, the gap closes formally:

`parental stewardship + church membership stewardship`.

Module `159` already governs this case.

At that point the church must not say:

> “He is under 18, therefore church discipline does not apply.”

Nor parents:

> “He is our minor child, therefore elders have no ecclesial authority.”

## 8. The difficult case: child seeks baptism partly to belong

The paedobaptist model resolves belonging first and profession later. A Baptist church must therefore be especially careful that the human need for belonging does not push an immature child into premature profession.

Questions:

- Is the child able to articulate personal sin and need of Christ?
- Is repentance personal rather than scripted?
- Is faith resting on Christ rather than family identity?
- Is there a credible pattern appropriate to age, not adult-level polish?
- Does the child believe baptism is a reward/entry ticket to peer belonging?
- Are parents anxious for a visible certificate?

### Rule

> **Do not solve the Baptist pre-membership pastoral gap by baptizing children before a credible profession. Solve the pastoral gap by actually pastoring them.**

## 9. The difficult case: openly unbelieving adolescent

Do not force:

> “Say you believe.”

Do not concede:

> “Then Christian moral order does not apply.”

Model:

`HONEST UNBELIEF + REQUIRED TRUTH/ORDER + CONTINUED WORD/GOSPEL + NO FALSE PRIVILEGES + REAL CONSEQUENCES`.

## 10. The difficult case: church-raised youth rejects known gospel

Use `150` controls.

Do not flatten him into:

> “same as every unbeliever.”

Assess accumulated light, former identification, rejected admonition, influence on peers, and hardening.

But do not call him formally apostate/excommunicated unless the historical profession/member status warrants those terms.

## 11. High-accountability Baptist church checklist

A church that rejects infant membership should still be able to answer:

1. Who is responsible for knowing and catechizing the children of members?
2. How do elders know when a church-raised teenager is entering defended serious sin?
3. What pastoral process exists before baptism/member jurisdiction?
4. How are parents supported and, where appropriate, corrected?
5. How are worship access, youth fellowship, trusted roles, safeguarding, and membership distinguished?
6. How do leaders avoid treating choir/youth participation as implicit salvation certification?
7. How do they avoid using baptism as a belonging solution?
8. How is a resisting adolescent repeatedly called to Christ without endless nagging or institutional abandonment?
9. What changes at credible profession/baptism?
10. What changes at adulthood, residence change, or independence?

If a credobaptist church cannot answer these, the problem is not necessarily its baptism doctrine; it may have failed to construct the pastoral institutions needed by its own theology.

## 12. Final synthesis

> **The strongest lesson from paedobaptist polity for a Baptist is not “therefore baptize the infant.” It is “do not allow withholding baptism to become withholding pastoral ownership.” A child can remain outside formal church membership until credible profession while still being fully under God’s moral claim, real parental government, extraordinary gospel privilege, organized church care, and an urgent call to repent and believe today.**
