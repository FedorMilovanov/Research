# 1. Why these two authors matter

The project repeatedly needs to hold together propositions that modern discussion often tears apart:

1. external restraint is **not** regeneration;
2. external restraint can still be morally/socially useful;
3. children remain directly accountable;
4. parents exercise delegated authority;
5. inherited family religion does not save;
6. personal guilt cannot be transferred entirely to parents/peers/environment.

Stephen Charnock and John Flavel provide unusually clear language for this balance.

---

# 2. Charnock: regeneration is a real inward change, not a new religious appearance

In *The Necessity of Regeneration*, Charnock argues from John 3 that regeneration is:

- a real change;
- a change of inclination/nature;
- necessary for true gospel life;
- not merely a new relative title or external practice.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nec_regen/nec_regen.ii.html

### Application to ДВР

A child can acquire:

- Christian vocabulary;
- choir discipline;
- church habits;
- Bible knowledge;
- externally clean conduct

without the new birth.

Therefore church/parents must never interpret:

> `well-trained church child` = `regenerate child`.

---

# 3. Charnock: outward reformation is insufficient — but “of excellent use”

This is perhaps Charnock’s most important contribution to the current project.

He explicitly says outward reformation can exist without regeneration and does not change the inward nature.

Yet he calls it **of excellent use**.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nec_regen/nec_regen.ii.html

### Critical synthesis

This destroys two opposite errors.

## Error A — behaviorism

> “If I can force good behavior, I have made a Christian.”

False.

## Error B — permissive fatalism

> “If restraint does not regenerate, restraint is useless.”

Also false.

### Project line

> **A locked door cannot give a new heart. That does not make a locked door useless when a child is trying to walk through it toward destruction.**

Rhetorical application, not Charnock quote.

---

# 4. Charnock: fear can restrain action without changing desire

Charnock distinguishes reformation proceeding from:

- force;
- fear;
- outward interest/reputation

from inner renewal.

His analogies include clipped wings/removed claws: the ability/opportunity can be restrained while inward propensity remains.

### Application after discovery

When parents remove:

- phone;
- account;
- private access;
- dangerous friendship opportunity,

they may indeed only be restraining conduct.

That fact should not be hidden.

Pastoral honesty:

> “This restriction cannot make you pure. It is here because I will not knowingly provide the occasion through which you are presently choosing impurity.”

This preserves both anthropology and responsibility.

---

# 5. Charnock: outward restraint can hide inward lust

He also warns that concern for reputation/self-interest can produce outward abstinence while inward lust remains active.

### This is directly relevant to church children

A child may stop public profanity because:

- choir status is threatened;
- parents are watching;
- device privileges depend on it.

Yet privately desire and secret behavior may continue.

### Correct conclusion

Not:

> “Consequences are bad because they can produce mere external obedience.”

Rather:

> **Consequences can restrain evil; parents/pastors must simultaneously refuse to confuse restraint with repentance.**

---

# 6. Charnock: true new birth necessarily shows itself outwardly

The other side is equally important.

Charnock says regeneration is inward but manifests outwardly.

He rejects the idea of:

> inward Christianity with no outward holiness.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nec_regen/nec_regen.ii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nat_regen/nat_regen.i.html

### Application

A profession of regeneration cannot be made immune from evaluation by life.

Do not say:

> “We cannot see the heart, therefore conduct tells us nothing at all.”

Correct:

> we cannot infallibly see regeneration, but Scripture expects fruit/direction consistent with new life.

---

# 7. Charnock: convictions are not regeneration

Charnock explicitly distinguishes:

- terror/conviction;
- awareness of wrath/foulness of sin;
- external religious acts

from the new birth itself.

### Application after discovery

A teenager can experience:

- intense fear;
- conscience pain;
- embarrassment;
- religious resolutions

without regeneration or true repentance.

This converges with Watson/Owen.

---

# 8. Charnock: Christian parents should seek children’s regeneration, not merely worldly success

Charnock explicitly applies the necessity of regeneration to Christian parents:

> parents should endeavor all they can toward their children’s regeneration/new-creature life; great estates/worldly prosperity are not the primary necessity.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/nec_regen/nec_regen.ii.html

### Guard

Charnock is not teaching that parents causally produce regeneration.

His whole theology says regeneration is God’s supernatural work.

Therefore:

> **parents seek the end through God-appointed means; God gives life.**

---

# 9. Charnock on inability: perversity does not excuse

In *Efficient of Regeneration*, Charnock argues that gospel commands are not unjust merely because fallen man cannot fulfill them spiritually; inability lies in corruption/perversity, not in the intrinsic absurdity of God’s command.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/charnock/efficient_regeneration/efficient_regeneration.ii.html

### Relevance

This directly supports:

> **“He is unregenerate” cannot be converted into “God therefore has no right to command him.”**

This is the same moral-inability vs innocence distinction already controlled by 1689 ch. 9/19.

---

# 10. Flavel: parents bear delegated authority and accountability

In *Fountain of Life*, Flavel describes children’s duties toward parents, grounding reverence in God’s authority.

Parents are entrusted by God with care over their children and are accountable to God.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/fountain/fountain.v.xxxi.html

### Implication

Parental government is not mere private preference.

It is delegated stewardship.

That both strengthens and limits it:

- parents must govern;
- parents must answer to God for how they govern.

---

# 11. Flavel: children owe reverence/obedience even when socially above parents

Flavel uses biblical examples to show filial duty is not erased by:

- status;
- age/achievement;
- worldly superiority.

### Relevance to older adolescents/adult children

This supports continued honor/respect.

### Guard

Honor is not identical to lifelong minor-child jurisdiction.

Civil adulthood/independent household authority remains distinct.

---

# 12. Flavel: parental failure does not become a sanctuary from child responsibility

Flavel elsewhere argues that children cannot simply excuse themselves by parental bad example; they possess personal conscience/reason and stand before God.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/pneum/pneum.x.html

### Important project principle

> **Bad parenting can be real sin and a real influence without becoming a transfer of the child’s guilt to the parent.**

Likewise:

- church hypocrisy can tempt;
- peers can corrupt;
- technology can facilitate;

but the child remains responsible for his own sin.

# 13. Combined model: restraint and regeneration answer different questions

## Restraint asks

> What evil may be prevented/limited now?

Tools:

- law;
- authority;
- consequences;
- barriers;
- supervision;
- separation from corrupt occasions.

## Regeneration asks

> Who changes the sinner’s heart/nature so he loves God and holiness?

Answer:

> God by the Spirit through the Word, according to sovereign grace.

### Therefore

`restraint ≠ regeneration`  
**and**  
`restraint is not therefore useless`.

This should become one of the project’s permanent theological guardrails.

# 14. Application to children’s choir

Suppose a child is removed from choir because of serious known sexual corruption.

The church should understand:

### What removal can do

- stop false representation;
- protect others;
- make consequences real;
- interrupt access/opportunity;
- communicate seriousness.

### What removal cannot do

- regenerate;
- create love for holiness;
- prove repentance;
- prove reprobation.

### Strong line

> **Ministry restriction is an act of church order and restraint, not a sacrament of regeneration.**

Rhetorical synthesis.

---

# 15. Application to parents

A parent can say simultaneously:

> “I know taking the phone does not change your heart.”

and:

> “I would be irresponsible to give you unrestricted access to the very avenue you are presently using to feed this sin.”

That is not theological inconsistency.

It is precisely the distinction Charnock helps recover.

---

# 16. Application to false assurance

Charnock strongly warns against judging spiritual state by external acts alone.

Therefore:

- choir participation;
- Bible memorization;
- baptism/profession;
- outward sexual restraint;
- polite speech

cannot by themselves establish regeneration.

But persistent contrary fruit also cannot simply be declared irrelevant.

### Balanced line

> **Conduct cannot create the new birth, but the new birth does not remain permanently indifferent to conduct.**
