## 0. Why this is a distinct digital module

The existing corpus already treats pornography, sexting, sextortion, platform affordances, and AI companions. Three newer mechanisms require separate treatment:

1. synthetic sexual imagery can victimize a child who never created an intimate image;
2. repentance does not automatically erase distributed digital artifacts;
3. sexualized digital consumption can be relational/transactional rather than merely passive pornography.

These are not arguments that technology creates a new category of sin beyond biblical anthropology. They are new *mechanisms and objects* through which lust, deceit, exploitation, greed, domination, false witness, and counterfeit intimacy can operate.

# GAP J — AI NUDIFY / SEXUAL DEEPFAKES OF PEERS

## J1. Governing thesis

AI-generated sexual imagery breaks a common pastoral assumption: that image-based sexual harm begins when a victim voluntarily creates or sends an intimate image. A normal photograph can now be transformed into a realistic fabricated nude/sexual image without the depicted child’s consent.

The moral analysis therefore includes more than lust:
- appropriation of another person’s likeness;
- fabrication/falsehood;
- sexual objectification;
- humiliation and reputational harm;
- distribution and coercion;
- potential child sexual exploitation material implications depending on jurisdiction/content.

## J2. Scripture map

- **Genesis 1:26–27** — the person represented is an image-bearer, not raw material for sexual consumption. Guard: image-of-God doctrine does not itself answer every legal/image-right question.
- **Exodus 20:16; Ephesians 4:25** — false witness/falsehood: a deepfake materially represents another person as doing/being exposed in a way that did not occur.
- **Matthew 5:27–30** — heart-level sexual coveting; do not collapse the text into the entire deepfake analysis, because deception and harm to another are additional dimensions.
- **1 Thessalonians 4:3–6** — sexual holiness includes not wronging/exploiting another.
- **Ephesians 5:3–12** — sexual impurity and works of darkness; use with contextual care.
- **James 3:5–10** — destructive use of communicative power; analogous to distribution/humiliation, not a technical AI text.

## J3. Contemporary evidence and official response

- **Thorn, Deepfake Nudes and Young People (2025)**: youth survey evidence that synthetic nude/deepfake phenomena are known among adolescents. Treat as NGO survey evidence, not national prevalence unless sampling/weighting explicitly supports it.
- **eSafety Commissioner (Australia), 2025–2026**: multiple official interventions/warnings concerning nudify services, school incidents, and access to services used to create sexualized deepfakes. High-value A-class regulatory evidence for the existence and regulatory treatment of the mechanism; not a prevalence measure for all teens.
- **Children’s Commissioner for England**: public work on nudification/deepfake sexual harms to children.
- **UK Government / DfE 2025**: policy/education response to AI-generated sexual imagery and misogyny; legal details must be date- and jurisdiction-verified at publication.
- **Internet Watch Foundation 2025/2026**: documented growth in realistic AI-generated child sexual abuse imagery in its detection environment. Scope guard: dark-web/CSAM detection counts do not estimate ordinary peer-school prevalence.

## J4. Hard cases

1. A 14-year-old generates a nude deepfake but never sends it to anyone.
2. A group chat requests “nudify” versions of girls at school.
3. A student forwards an image believing it is real.
4. A victim originally posted a normal swimsuit photo publicly.
5. The generated image is obviously fake/cartoon-like versus photorealistic.
6. The victim is an adult teacher rather than a minor peer.
7. The generator says, “No one was actually naked, so no sexual boundary was crossed.”
8. A church teenager uses AI to place a peer’s face onto explicit adult pornography.

Pastoral response must address intent, fabrication, sexualization, distribution, harm, and safeguarding separately.

# GAP K — DIGITAL PERMANENCE AFTER REPENTANCE

## K1. Governing thesis

A teenager may genuinely repent while copies, screenshots, mirrors, caches, forwarded files, or synthetic derivatives remain outside their control. The gospel can fully address guilt before God without pretending temporal consequences have vanished.

Required distinction:

**forgiveness / justification ≠ deletion of consequences ≠ restored trust ≠ complete data erasure ≠ restored access or role.**

Restitution is fruit of repentance and love of neighbor; it is not atonement.

## K2. Scripture map

- **Luke 19:1–10** — Zacchaeus’s restitution flows from salvation/repentance. Guard: his specific fourfold repayment is not a universal numeric Christian restitution law.
- **Ephesians 4:25–28** — putting off falsehood/theft and putting on truthful/productive conduct that benefits others: useful `put off + put on` pattern.
- **Proverbs 28:13** — confession plus forsaking.
- **Acts 19:18–20** — costly destruction of practices/material tied to former occult life; analogical support for decisive relinquishment, not a direct digital-deletion command.
- **Philemon** — transformed relationships and responsibility; avoid using Onesimus to bypass questions of power or legal status.
- **Psalm 51** — forgiveness/restoration without denial of grave consequences.

## K3. Operational mitigation

Possible actions depend on platform and law:
- stop further distribution;
- delete material from devices/accounts under the offender’s control;
- preserve evidence first where law enforcement/safeguarding requires it;
- use platform reporting/takedown mechanisms;
- for minors’ explicit images, use services such as NCMEC **Take It Down** where applicable;
- inform affected persons/guardians through an appropriate safeguarding process where needed;
- do not contact a victim directly to “apologize” if no-contact or safety considerations make contact harmful;
- do not promise total erasure.

Important tension: immediate deletion can conflict with evidence preservation. Product must not give universal technical instructions in an active abuse/crime case.

## K4. Restitution questions

- Must the repentant teenager identify every recipient?
- What if disclosure creates fresh humiliation for the victim?
- What if the teen cannot access an old account?
- What if images remain on another country’s platform?
- Should a future spouse be told about past image-based harm? That is a separate pastoral-disclosure question, not automatically answered by this article.
- Does inability to undo all harm invalidate repentance? No; but indifference to remediable harm can contradict professed repentance.

# GAP L — PAID / PARASOCIAL / COUNTERFEIT DIGITAL INTIMACY

## L1. Governing thesis

Pornography is not always experienced as passive viewing. Livestreams, direct messages, paid custom content, sexualized influencers, creator subscriptions, and AI/parasocial environments can package sexual gratification as apparent reciprocity: “she/he noticed *me*,” “this is our private interaction,” “I am supporting a person who knows me.”

The theological category proposed for Product is **counterfeit intimacy**: a synthesis describing purchased/simulated relational cues without covenantal mutuality. It is not a clinical diagnosis or an empirically standardized construct.

## L2. Scripture map

- **Matthew 6:19–24** — treasure and mastery; money exposes allegiance. Do not imply every creator subscription is sexual or sinful.
- **1 Timothy 6:6–10** — love of money and destructive desire; applies especially where sexualized commerce exploits both consumer and creator.
- **1 Corinthians 6:12** — “I will not be dominated by anything”: useful for compulsive platform/sexual consumption without pseudo-addiction diagnosis.
- **Proverbs 5–7** — wisdom framework for illicit sexual allure and costly consequences; do not map ancient prostitute/adulteress imagery directly onto every female creator or make gendered blame assumptions.
- **1 Thessalonians 4:3–6** — holiness and not wronging another.
- **Ephesians 5:5** — sexual immorality/covetousness linkage can illuminate desire + money, but exegete carefully.

## L3. Empirical limits

Parasocial research shows that adolescents form one-sided relational attachments to media figures/influencers and that these relationships can influence attitudes, identity, body image, consumption, and perceived companionship. However:

- general parasocial research is not equivalent to research on sexualized paid interaction;
- influencer/body-image findings should not be rebranded as evidence of pornography effects;
- there is not yet a sufficiently strong basis in this packet for a broad prevalence claim such as “most teen porn use is now relational.”

Use platform/product descriptions and direct studies where available; otherwise keep the point structural, not statistical.

## L4. Hidden money as part of double life

A sexualized digital life may involve:
- prepaid/gift cards;
- app-store purchases;
- creator subscriptions;
- microtransactions/donations;
- crypto/payment intermediaries;
- charges disguised from parents;
- theft or unauthorized use of household payment methods.

This adds greed, theft/deception, and stewardship to the moral picture. Do not assume every unexplained teen payment is sexual.

## L5. Cultural/gender nuances

- Creator-consumer gender patterns vary across platforms and communities.
- Male-focused narratives can obscure girls’ sexualized parasocial consumption and boys’ victimization/exploitation.
- In cultures with strong shame around direct sexual contact, online/paid pseudo-intimacy may be experienced as “safer” while remaining secretive/exploitative.
- Economic vulnerability of creators raises exploitation questions, but Product should not speculate about individual creators’ coercion or agency.

## L6. Hard questions

- Is paying for a personalized but non-nude flirtatious message pornography?
- Is a teenager’s romantic attachment to a streamer sinful if no sexual content is present?
- What if a creator responds directly and the interaction becomes reciprocal?
- What if an AI companion produces sexual content after the teen prompts it?
- Does hidden spending require financial restitution to parents separately from sexual repentance?
- How should parents respond if the conduct is legal but violates household rules and Christian ethics?
