# 9. Puritan historical research newly deepened

## William Gouge

Important new material:

- explicitly distinguishes children grown and no longer under parental government from children still under it;
- says honor remains;
- parents' care/admonition continues in changed form;
- uses grown/separate children examples.

Guard against importing all seventeenth-century patriarchal civil assumptions.

## Richard Baxter

Especially important:

- asks how long parental command/government lasts;
- describes several acts/degrees/end-goals of government;
- near oversight ends as its purposes are attained;
- recognizes full-age child is increasingly at own disposal;
- still preserves duties toward parents.

This makes Baxter highly useful against both:

- `adult = no family duty`;
- `adult = still fourteen forever`.

## Thomas Watson

Useful balance:

- real parental authority/discipline;
- no indulgence of wickedness;
- but no tyranny;
- do not provoke through abusive speech, excessive correction, deprivation, partiality, shameful parental conduct, or commands against conscience.

## Flavel

Useful for enduring honor/love and parental accountability.

# 21. New core formulations to preserve

> **Release from child-level control is not release from love, truth, prayer, or every initiative toward reconciliation.**

> **Adulthood enlarges the sphere in which a person bears his own decisions; it does not shrink the sphere of God's lordship.**

> **Do not send the child into the far country. But if a competent adult chooses it, parents may need to stop financing it.**

> **Do not keep the prodigal comfortable in sin with Christian money; do not manufacture misery as if parental pain could regenerate him.**

> **A parent can leave consequences in God's hands without leaving the child outside the parent's heart.**

> **Receiving the person is not identical with restoring every former privilege.**

> **The father in Luke 15 is not a proof-text for parental disappearance.**

> **There is a time to speak, a time to stop arguing, a time to help, a time to refuse help that feeds sin, and sometimes a time to require separate residence. Scripture gives moral principles; wisdom must apply them to the actual case.**
