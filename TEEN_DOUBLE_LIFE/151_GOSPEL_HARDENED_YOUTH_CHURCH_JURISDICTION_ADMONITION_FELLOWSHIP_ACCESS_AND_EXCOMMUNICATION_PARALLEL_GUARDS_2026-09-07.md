# 0. The disputed pastoral situation

The church knows a particular youth well.

He is not merely an unknown visitor from the street. He may have:

- been raised among church families;
- attended Bible studies for years;
- spoken Christian language;
- sought baptism or presented himself as moving toward profession;
- been accepted socially as part of the Christian youth circle;
- heard repeated personal admonition;

and now:

- openly or secretly lives in serious sexual sin;
- blasphemes / uses filthy speech;
- lies persistently;
- treats correction with contempt;
- perhaps recruits peers into the same patterns;
- perhaps continues appearing at youth gatherings/choir/social settings as though nothing has changed.

The weak pastoral formula is:

> “He is not regenerate / not a member, so we treat him as an ordinary unbeliever and just let him stay everywhere in case he eventually believes.”

This module tests that claim.

### Governing verdict

> **Formal excommunication and pastoral treatment are not the same category. A never-admitted nonmember cannot simply be “excommunicated” as though the church were removing membership it never granted. But lack of formal censure jurisdiction does not require the church to normalize close fellowship, representative roles, confidential youth access, or corrupting influence.**

The closest biblical analogy is often:

> **missionary openness to the Word + explicit admonition + withdrawal of validating/entrusting fellowship where appropriate**.

Not:

> unrestricted social normalization.

---

# 1. 1 Corinthians 5 — formal jurisdiction is real and bounded

Primary:

- 1 Cor. 5:9–13.

Calvin:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/1_corinthians/5.htm

Gill:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/1_corinthians/5.htm

Paul explicitly distinguishes:

- the person **called/reckoned a brother** while living scandalously;
- those **outside**.

Church judicial/censorial action concerns those within its ecclesial sphere.

Calvin states that the special jurisdiction/chastisement belongs to those of God’s household in the church; Gill similarly treats the offender as a member and the church’s excommunication as applying to those within.

### Therefore

If the teenager:

- was never baptized;
- never received membership;
- was never formally affirmed by the congregation as a Christian brother/member,

then the church must not create a fiction:

> “We formally excommunicated him from a membership he never possessed.”

This protects Baptist ecclesiology and due process.

---

# 2. But “outside formal jurisdiction” does not mean “pastorally anonymous”

1 Cor. 5 answers the question:

> **Who is subject to formal church judgment/censure?**

It does not answer every question about:

- pastoral admonition;
- family responsibility;
- access to minors;
- small-group confidentiality;
- choir/platform stewardship;
- organized youth events;
- protection from corrupting influence.

A church may lack authority to **excommunicate** a visitor while plainly possessing responsibility to:

- tell him the truth;
- refuse to validate a false profession;
- protect children/youth;
- set rules for church-organized activities;
- exclude unsafe/corrupting behavior from particular settings;
- ask him to leave a specific meeting if he persistently disrupts/corrupts it;
- continue inviting him to appropriate public gospel hearing where safety/order permit.

### Strong distinction

> **No church-censure jurisdiction does not equal no church-stewardship responsibility.**

---

# 3. A youth may be formally “outside” while socially functioning as an insider

This is especially important for children raised in the church.

A teenage nonmember may nevertheless function socially as:

- “one of our church kids”;
- a regular small-group participant;
- a choir member;
- a trusted older teen among younger children;
- a potential romantic partner assumed to be Christian;
- a person whose parents’ membership gives others a false sense of safety;
- someone who previously spoke of baptism/faith.

That social reality does **not** turn him into a member.

But it means leaders must not pretend that his influence is equivalent to a first-time outsider sitting quietly in the back row.

### Key pastoral question

> **What does his continued unrestricted inclusion communicate to the other children about his profession, conduct, and the church’s view of holiness?**

This is an influence/stewardship question, not an invented membership category.

---

# 4. The excommunication parallel: real, but analogical rather than juridically identical

There is a meaningful parallel between:

## A. Excommunicated professing member

The church has formally withdrawn its affirmation of his profession because of serious unrepentant sin.

and

## B. Never-member church-raised youth who once moved toward profession but now knowingly rejects truth

The church may never have formally affirmed him in the first place, so it cannot formally withdraw membership.

### Shared pastoral logic may include

- no false reassurance about spiritual condition;
- serious warnings;
- refusal to treat protected rebellion as compatible with Christianity;
- changed character of close Christian fellowship;
- prayer for repentance;
- continued gospel proclamation;
- protection of others from corrupting influence.

### Non-shared juridical facts

- no membership to remove;
- no Lord’s Table standing to revoke if he was never admitted;
- no formal “restoration to membership” process after a censure that never happened.

### Locked phrase

> **The parallel is pastoral and moral, not a fiction of church jurisdiction.**

# 5. 2 Thessalonians 3:14–15 — withdrawal and admonition can coexist

Primary:

- 2 Thess. 3:6, 14–15.

Calvin/CCEL:

- https://www.ccel.org/wwsb/2Thessalonians/3/15

Paul combines:

- distancing/withdrawal in response to disorder;
- refusal to treat the offender as an enemy;
- continued admonition as a brother.

Calvin emphasizes that church severity is meant for recovery, not destruction; brotherly love is shown not by flattery but by admonition.

### Direct scope

This text concerns people in the Christian community, not a random unbelieving visitor.

### Analogical value

It destroys the false binary:

> either full normal fellowship **or** hostile rejection.

Biblically there can be:

> **reduced fellowship + real love + explicit admonition + hope of recovery.**

For a nonmember hardened youth this is an analogy, not formal discipline procedure.

---

# 6. Matthew 18:17 — reclassification does not mean gospel silence

Primary:

- Matt. 18:15–17.

Calvin/CCEL:

- https://ccel.org/study/Matthew_18%3A15-35

Calvin reads the final stage as treating the irreclaimable/despising person as outside ordinary sacred fellowship until repentance.

Gill has a somewhat different technical account of Matt. 18 as a rule especially for private offences and does not identify the phrase itself as the formal definition of excommunication:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical.iii.vi.html

### Project use

Do not overbuild policy on the disputed details of Matt. 18.

The broader NT pattern remains clear:

- correction is real;
- persistent refusal changes the relational/ecclesial posture;
- the goal remains repentance;
- outsider status is not the same thing as hatred or refusal to proclaim Christ.

# 7. 9Marks as a contemporary Baptist polity cross-check

Useful contemporary Baptist summaries:

- https://www.9marks.org/answer/what-is-church-discipline/
- https://www.9marks.org/article/good-and-bad-excuses-not-to-practice-church-discipline/
- https://www.9marks.org/article/church-discipline-primer/

They define corrective discipline as ultimately excluding a **professing Christian/member** from membership/Table for serious unrepentant sin and specifically warn that churches cannot formally discipline someone they never affirmed as a believer/member.

At the same time, their discipline primer ordinarily allows an excluded person to attend public preaching where no safety threat exists, while ordinary church members’ fellowship posture changes markedly.

### Why this matters

This contemporary Baptist practice independently converges with the project’s distinction:

> **public hearing of the Word ≠ normal Christian fellowship ≠ membership privileges ≠ trusted youth access.**

It also prevents us from inventing “excommunication-lite” terminology for nonmembers.

# 8. What should leaders actually do with a hardened church-raised nonmember?

Assuming facts are established and this is not mere rumor:

## Step 1 — clarify what he actually professes now

Ask directly:

- Do you call yourself a Christian?
- Do you believe the gospel you were taught?
- Do you intend to submit to Christ in this area?
- Why are you continuing to attend?
- Do you want help fighting sin, or do you reject the church’s judgment that it is sin?

This can materially change the category.

### Possible result A

He still **bears the name brother / publicly claims Christianity** while defending scandalous sin.

This strengthens the relevance of 1 Cor. 5’s false-profession logic, though actual formal censure still depends on membership/polity.

### Possible result B

He explicitly says:

> “I am not a Christian and I do not intend to obey Christianity.”

Then stop pretending his presence is Christian profession.

He is an outsider regarding membership, but still a highly instructed/privileged outsider who may need strong warnings and carefully bounded access.

---

# 9. Public preaching should ordinarily remain the widest door

1 Cor. 14:23–25 already gives the project an outsider-at-public-gathering category.

9Marks similarly ordinarily welcomes an excluded person to public preaching unless physical/safety risk changes the case.

### Therefore

A church should not normally say:

> “Because you are hardened, you are forbidden to hear the Word.”

The Word is one of God’s appointed means.

2 Tim. 2:24–26 explicitly retains hope that God may grant repentance to opponents.

### But

Public hearing is not a right to:

- every small group;
- every youth social circle;
- every overnight trip;
- confidential discipleship setting;
- direct messaging with minors;
- choir/platform;
- leadership-like influence.

---

# 10. Small groups are purpose-specific, not sacramental entitlements

Ask what the group is for.

## Open evangelistic Bible study

A hardened person may often attend if:

- he does not disrupt;
- he accepts direct correction;
- he does not prey on/recruit others;
- leaders can maintain order.

But leaders should not pretend his status is spiritually normal.

## Confidential discipleship/accountability group

Higher threshold.

If he:

- mocks disclosures;
- gathers private information;
- sexualizes others;
- recruits secret sin;
- teaches deception;
- uses the group to find romantic/sexual targets,

restriction or removal may be necessary.

## Youth/minor group

Protection of minors and formation of the group make stewardship especially weighty.

### Principle

> **Evangelistic openness does not require unrestricted access to intimate fellowship.**

---

# 11. “The whole church should admonish him” — needed correction

The instinct behind the phrase is understandable:

> the whole community must not silently normalize known rebellion.

But do **not** turn it into:

- coordinated harassment;
- every adult confronting him every Sunday;
- public humiliation without due reason;
- rumor-driven rebukes;
- a social punishment campaign designed to make attendance unbearable.

### Better model

The whole church should be **corporately truthful**, while admonition is carried by appropriate people and relationships.

Possible agents:

- parents;
- pastors/elders;
- youth leaders;
- mature members who actually have relationship/responsibility;
- formal church action where jurisdiction applies.

Heb. 3:13 establishes mutual exhortation as a community duty, but wisdom/order still governs implementation.

### Strong line

> **Corporate holiness does not require a mob. It requires that no responsible layer of the church quietly certifies what it knows to be false.**

---

# 12. If repeated admonition makes him stop attending

This outcome must be interpreted carefully.

A church must not **aim** to make the person uncomfortable merely so he leaves.

But neither can it preserve attendance by promising:

> “We will never address what you are knowingly doing.”

Possible outcomes of honest admonition:

- repentance;
- request for help;
- honest admission of unbelief;
- anger;
- reduced attendance;
- departure.

### Safe conclusion

> **If a person will remain only on condition that known serious rebellion never be named, the church cannot purchase his presence with moral silence.**

But:

> **his departure is not itself the pastoral goal. Repentance is.**

---

# 13. Why silence can strengthen hypocrisy

Owen on apostasy from gospel holiness is especially relevant:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/apostasy.i.xiv.html

He warns that outward Christian profession joined to profligate life falsely represents Christ and the gospel, as though Christ approves licentiousness.

The project application is strong:

If church leaders know a youth:

- retains Christian social identity;
- appears in Christian roles;
- protects serious rebellion;
- resists correction,

and respond only:

> “He is unsaved, let him continue everything unchanged,”

the institution may unintentionally teach both him and peers:

> **Christian form and protected rebellion can coexist indefinitely without relational/ecclesial consequence.**

This is not an argument for arbitrary exclusion.

It is an argument against **institutionalized pretending**.

---

# 14. Corrupting influence changes the access question

The moral status of his own soul is one question.

His treatment of others is another.

If he is known to:

- distribute pornography;
- send obscene material;
- normalize blasphemy/sexual joking;
- recruit peers into deception;
- coach others around parent filters;
- seek sexual partners through church trust;
- pressure younger children;
- groom/manipulate;

then the church has a duty to protect others even before solving every soteriological question.

### Critical line

> **“We do not know whether he is regenerate” is not a reason to expose other children to known corrupting conduct.**

This follows the project’s longstanding safeguarding and stewardship distinction.

# 15. Proposed access matrix

| Situation | Public Word | Open Bible study | Close/confidential youth group | Choir/platform | Private/minor access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ordinary unbelieving visitor, respectful | OPEN | ordinarily OPEN | purpose-dependent | usually not representative role without policy basis | normal safeguards |
| church-raised unbelieving youth, heavily instructed, receives correction | OPEN | OPEN with truthful conversation | case-specific | role-specific | case-specific |
| church-raised hardened youth, rejects correction, no corrupting conduct | OPEN | may remain if orderly; direct admonition continues | may restrict if group assumes discipleship/confidential trust | normally restrict representative role if known life contradicts it | risk-based |
| hardened youth actively corrupting/recruiting others | OPEN only insofar as safety/order permit | supervised/context-dependent | **RESTRICT/REMOVE as needed** | **REMOVE while pattern persists** | **STRONG RESTRICTION** |
| professing member in serious unrepentant scandal | Word hearing normally remains possible | discipline context | governed by church discipline/safety | remove | restrict as warranted |
| credible repentance | OPEN | OPEN | rebuild appropriately | reassess with fruit/time | re-entrust separately by domain/safety |

This is a pastoral matrix, not an inspired statute.

---

# 16. The teenager seeking a spouse/romantic target

Never infer motive merely because a young person socializes with the opposite sex.

But where concrete evidence shows he:

- cultivates a Christian façade to gain trust;
- hides ongoing sexual conduct;
- pursues believers under false pretenses;
- manipulates youth settings to obtain romantic/sexual access,

leaders should not hide behind:

> “He is just an unbeliever; maybe he will convert.”

They may need to:

- confront him;
- warn/protect particular vulnerable people carefully;
- restrict close-access contexts;
- avoid unnecessary gossip/public disclosure;
- use safeguarding process where coercion/grooming exists.

---

# 17. Theologically exact comparison with an excommunicated person

## Similarities

Both may be treated as needing:

- repentance;
- gospel warning;
- no false affirmation of Christian standing;
- changed validating fellowship;
- boundaries around corruption;
- prayer and hope.

## Differences

The excommunicated person:

- previously held formal member/church privileges;
- has undergone a defined church judgment;
- has something ecclesially formal to be restored to.

The never-member youth:

- may have received immense gospel light;
- may have made informal/partial profession;
- may have functioned socially as insider;
- but was never formally admitted.

### Final formula

> **Do not call the two cases identical. Do not call them unrelated. Their church jurisdiction differs; their pastoral logic can substantially overlap.**

# 18. Publication claims

### GREEN

- A nonmember cannot be formally excommunicated as though he were a member.
- A nonmember may still be directly and repeatedly admonished.
- Public hearing of the Word is distinct from intimate/representative/trusted access.
- Serious known corrupting conduct may justify restrictions independent of membership status.
- Church-raised hardened unbelief should not be normalized by the phrase “he is just unsaved.”
- The goal of restrictions/admonition is truth, protection, and repentance—not social revenge.

### RED

Do not write:

- every church-raised unbelieving child should be publicly shamed;
- every nonmember apostate should be “excommunicated”;
- every member must personally confront him repeatedly;
- make church attendance unbearable so he leaves;
- outsiders have a right to every church social setting;
- the church can read his final election/reprobation;
- safety restrictions are identical to church discipline.

---

# 19. Locked synthesis

> **Церковь не должна выдумывать отлучение человека, которого она никогда не принимала в членство. Но из этого не следует, что воспитанный под Евангелием, ожесточённый и развращающий других юноша должен пользоваться тем же уровнем доверительного общения, что мирный посетитель, впервые слушающий Слово. Формальная юрисдикция определяется членством и исповеданием; пастырская серьёзность определяется также полученным светом, известным поведением, реакцией на увещевание и влиянием на других. Дверь к проповеди может оставаться открытой, пока двери к ложному подтверждению, представительской роли и опасному влиянию закрываются.**
