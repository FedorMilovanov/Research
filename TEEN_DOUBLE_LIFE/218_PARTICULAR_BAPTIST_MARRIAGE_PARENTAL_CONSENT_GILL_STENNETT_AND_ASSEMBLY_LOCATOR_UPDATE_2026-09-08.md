## 1. Executive verdict

The previous project statement

> strong parental involvement is Particular Baptist-compatible, but explicit adult-marriage parental-consent evidence has not yet been located

is now **too weak**.

A more accurate evidence ladder is:

1. **1689 LBCF ch. 25** — explicitly requires that parties be capable of giving consent and that marriage be in the Lord; the confession itself does **not** contain the Westminster Directory's explicit adult-first-marriage parental-consent procedure.
2. **Hercules Collins / Benjamin Keach** — strong fifth-commandment, parental-authority, child-obedience and non-provocation theology remains located; an exact Keach text requiring parental permission for every adult child's marriage is still **not located**.
3. **John Gill, A Body of Practical Divinity** — **directly located**: marriage should be entered with the consent of all parties concerned, including `parents and guardians, under whose care single persons may be`, while the man's and woman's own voluntary consent remains indispensable and no superior may force them into marriage.
4. **Samuel Stennett, Discourses on Domestic Duties (1783)** — **located through a source with exact primary page locator**: Stennett advises young people forming a marriage to consider the `concurrence of parents and friends`; the same discussion places marriage around the period when persons are considered `sui juris`, and explicitly does not endorse arranged unions.
5. **Particular Baptist General Assembly 1689/1692/etc.** — exact assembly rule requiring parental consent of all competent adults at first marriage remains **not located**. The HOLD therefore remains, but its meaning is narrower.

### New locked formulation

> **Direct parental-consent teaching is demonstrably present in mature eighteenth-century Particular Baptist practical theology. It must no longer be described as merely a Presbyterian/Westminster borrowing. But this does not authorize attributing the Westminster Directory's exact procedure to the 1689 Confession, Benjamin Keach, or the Particular Baptist General Assembly without a located primary text.**

## 2. John Gill — direct Particular Baptist evidence

Primary source:

- John Gill, *A Body of Practical Divinity*, Book III / relative duties, marriage section: https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.i.html
- parallel modern transcription: https://www.joelkell.com/resources/other/gill-body-of-practical-divinity/section-4-1/

Gill defines marriage as the union of one man and one woman and then states that the state is entered by mutual consent; indeed by the consent of all parties who have a concern in it, specifically including **parents and guardians under whose care single persons may be**. He immediately adds the balancing proposition that the parties' **own consent is especially necessary** and that no one, including superiors, may force them into marriage against their wills.

### What this proves

Allowed:

> `Gill explicitly teaches parental/guardian consent as part of orderly entry into marriage.`

Allowed:

> `Gill simultaneously preserves real personal consent and rejects forced marriage.`

Allowed:

> `A strong parental-consent principle is demonstrably Particular Baptist in Gill's mature practical theology.`

### What this does not yet prove

Do **not** silently expand Gill into:

- `every parent has an unlimited veto over every competent adult child forever`;
- `the father alone may choose the spouse`;
- `an adult daughter's own consent is secondary or unnecessary`;
- `1689 ch. 25 itself contains Gill's wording`;
- `Gill's rule is identical in every procedural detail to Westminster's Directory for Public Worship`.

Gill's own wording contains both **family/guardian consent** and **personal voluntary consent**.

## 3. Gill's separate adulthood control must remain beside the marriage text

Primary source:

- John Gill, *A Body of Practical Divinity*, duties of children to parents: https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html

Gill says sons and daughters remain under filial obligations through life, but he also explicitly states that **the power of parents over children is less when they are grown up**, while observance, gratitude and filial reverence do not cease.

This is critical to interpretation of his marriage paragraph.

### Locked synthesis

Do not read Gill as either:

`adult = parents become morally irrelevant`

or

`grown child = parental power unchanged from minority in every domain`.

Instead Gill supplies a differentiated model:

`continuing filial duty + diminished general parental power when grown + strong parental/guardian involvement in marriage + indispensable voluntary consent of the marrying parties`.

## 4. Samuel Stennett — a second Baptist witness

Secondary scholarly source with exact primary locator:

- Matthew D. Haste, “Nurseries of Heaven: Samuel Stennett on Marriage and Family,” *Churchman* 129/1, pp. 63–64: https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/churchman/129-01_055.pdf
- Haste cites Samuel Stennett, *Discourses on Domestic Duties*, p. 127 (or *Works of Samuel Stennett*, vol. 3, p. 81 in the dissertation pagination discussed below).

Additional source:

- Matthew D. Haste, *Marriage in the Life and Theology of John Gill, Samuel Stennett, and Andrew Fuller* (SBTS dissertation, 2015): https://repository.sbts.edu/handle/10392/4946

Haste reports that Stennett:

1. recommends pursuing marriage around the age mankind generally considers persons **`sui juris`**;
2. then advises young people to consider the **`concurrence of parents and friends`**;
3. does **not** approve arranged unions;
4. treats parental wisdom as a serious factor in forming the marriage connection.

Haste notes the legal background: Hardwicke's Marriage Act required parental permission below age 21; Stennett's `sui juris` recommendation therefore matters because his parental/friend concurrence advice is not merely reducible to a rule for young minors.

### Evidence classification

`STENNETT_PARENT_FRIEND_CONCURRENCE = LOCATED_SECONDARY_WITH_EXACT_PRIMARY_LOCATOR`

It is stronger than a vague historical summary, but until the exact primary *Domestic Duties* page is directly inspected in this research lane, do not represent the quote as independently transcribed from the 1783 primary scan.

### Important nuance

Stennett's word `concurrence` should not automatically be converted into the strongest possible modern legal meaning of an absolute paternal veto. His treatment is presented as wise formation of a marriage by persons capable of acting for themselves, while retaining serious family participation.

This makes Stennett especially useful against two errors:

- autonomous-couple courtship that treats parents as irrelevant;
- forced/arranged marriage that erases the marrying person's own judgment and consent.

## 5. Particular Baptist General Assembly — HOLD narrowed, not removed

Located Assembly publications:

- *A Narrative of the proceedings of the General Assembly...* (1689): https://books.google.com/books?id=l1rCWI1Sz2AC
- *A Narrative of the proceedings of the General Assembly...* (1692): https://books.google.com/books?id=2LO_ubcDgWgC

These establish accessible primary Assembly corpora and include Benjamin Keach among the participants. The currently surfaced searchable metadata does **not** provide an exact marriage/parental-consent decision.

### Therefore retain

`PARTICULAR_BAPTIST_GENERAL_ASSEMBLY_ADULT_PARENTAL_CONSENT_RULE = LOCATOR_HOLD`

But redefine the HOLD:

> It no longer means “no Particular Baptist parental-consent evidence exists.” It means only: **the project has not yet located an exact General Assembly rule that can be cited as Assembly policy, nor an exact Keach rule requiring parental permission of every competent adult child at first marriage.**

### Anti-overclaim guard

Absence from current search results is not proof that no such Assembly discussion exists. Do not write `the General Assembly never taught this` unless the complete relevant proceedings corpus has been exhaustively audited.

## 7. Theological significance for the family-government companion

The new evidence strengthens the project's conservative case in a precise way.

It supports:

- marriage is not merely a private romantic contract between two isolated adults;
- substantially faithful parents have a serious moral place in courtship/marriage discernment;
- secret romantic bypass of functioning parents is historically alien to significant Baptist practical theology;
- becoming `sui juris` does not automatically make parental concurrence morally irrelevant;
- real personal marital consent remains indispensable;
- strong family participation is compatible with rejecting forced marriage.

It does **not** settle every question about:

- a competent adult who has lived independently for many years;
- an unjust parental refusal;
- abusive parents;
- parents who oppose marriage for sinful reasons;
- an adult daughter still living at home versus a long-independent adult daughter;
- whether `concurrence` always means a binding veto;
- the exact civil/legal significance of parental objection today.

Those remain governed by `193`, `203`, `205` and the status/jurisdiction controls.

## 8. Product-safe synthesis

A strong but evidence-honest formulation is:

> **Historic Baptist family theology did not generally imagine marriage as an entirely private choice made by two detached individuals. John Gill explicitly included parents and guardians among those whose consent properly belonged to the marriage process, while preserving the indispensable voluntary consent of the man and woman themselves. Samuel Stennett likewise urged young adults to seek the concurrence of parents and friends. This strengthens the case for serious parental involvement without turning adult children into property or making every parental objection infallible.**

A stronger claim such as:

> `The 1689 Confession or General Assembly gave fathers an absolute veto over every adult daughter's marriage`

remains **not established**.
