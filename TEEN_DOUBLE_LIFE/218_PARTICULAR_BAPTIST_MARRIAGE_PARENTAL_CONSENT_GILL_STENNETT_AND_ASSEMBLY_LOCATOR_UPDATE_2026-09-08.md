## 1. Executive verdict

The previous project statement

> strong parental involvement is Particular Baptist-compatible, but explicit adult-marriage parental-consent evidence has not yet been located

is now **too weak**.

A more accurate evidence ladder is:

1. **1689 LBCF ch. 25** — explicitly requires that parties be capable of giving consent and that marriage be in the Lord; the confession itself does **not** contain the Westminster Directory's explicit adult-first-marriage parental-consent procedure.
2. **Hercules Collins / Benjamin Keach** — strong fifth-commandment, parental-authority, child-obedience and non-provocation theology remains located; an exact Keach text requiring parental permission for every adult child's marriage is still **not located**.
3. **John Gill, A Body of Practical Divinity** — **directly located**: marriage should be entered with the consent of all parties concerned, including `parents and guardians, under whose care single persons may be`, while the man's and woman's own voluntary consent remains indispensable and no superior may force them into marriage.
4. **Samuel Stennett, Discourses on Domestic Duties (1783)** — **`VERIFIED_PRIMARY` as of 2026-11-15**: the 1783 London first edition was inspected directly at printed **p. 162** (HathiTrust `uc2.ark:/13960/t8nc5v03c`, seq 178); see the verification card in §4.1. Stennett advises young people forming a marriage to consider the `concurrence of parents and friends`; the same discussion places marriage around the period when persons are considered `sui juris` (p. 161), and his own graded teaching at pp. 269 and 276 limits parental command at mature age to respectful counsel. He neither makes parental consent a condition of validity nor endorses arranged unions.
5. **Particular Baptist General Assembly 1689/1692/etc.** — exact assembly rule requiring parental consent of all competent adults at first marriage remains **not located**. The HOLD therefore remains, but its meaning is narrower.

### New locked formulation

> **Direct parental-consent teaching is demonstrably present in mature eighteenth-century Particular Baptist practical theology. It must no longer be described as merely a Presbyterian/Westminster borrowing. But this does not authorize attributing the Westminster Directory's exact procedure to the 1689 Confession, Benjamin Keach, or the Particular Baptist General Assembly without a located primary text.**

## 2. John Gill — direct Particular Baptist evidence

Primary source:

- John Gill, *A Body of Practical Divinity*, Book III / relative duties, marriage section: https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.i.html
- parallel modern transcription: https://www.joelkell.com/resources/other/gill-body-of-practical-divinity/section-4-1/

Gill defines marriage as the union of one man and one woman and then states that the state is entered by mutual consent; indeed by the consent of all parties who have a concern in it, specifically including **parents and guardians under whose care single persons may be**. He immediately adds the balancing proposition that the parties' **own consent is especially necessary** and that no one, including superiors, may force them into marriage against their wills.

### What this proves

Allowed:

> `Gill explicitly teaches parental/guardian consent as part of orderly entry into marriage.`

Allowed:

> `Gill simultaneously preserves real personal consent and rejects forced marriage.`

Allowed:

> `A strong parental-consent principle is demonstrably Particular Baptist in Gill's mature practical theology.`

### What this does not yet prove

Do **not** silently expand Gill into:

- `every parent has an unlimited veto over every competent adult child forever`;
- `the father alone may choose the spouse`;
- `an adult daughter's own consent is secondary or unnecessary`;
- `1689 ch. 25 itself contains Gill's wording`;
- `Gill's rule is identical in every procedural detail to Westminster's Directory for Public Worship`.

Gill's own wording contains both **family/guardian consent** and **personal voluntary consent**.

## 3. Gill's separate adulthood control must remain beside the marriage text

Primary source:

- John Gill, *A Body of Practical Divinity*, duties of children to parents: https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html

Gill says sons and daughters remain under filial obligations through life, but he also explicitly states that **the power of parents over children is less when they are grown up**, while observance, gratitude and filial reverence do not cease.

This is critical to interpretation of his marriage paragraph.

### Locked synthesis

Do not read Gill as either:

`adult = parents become morally irrelevant`

or

`grown child = parental power unchanged from minority in every domain`.

Instead Gill supplies a differentiated model:

`continuing filial duty + diminished general parental power when grown + strong parental/guardian involvement in marriage + indispensable voluntary consent of the marrying parties`.

## 4. Samuel Stennett — a second Baptist witness

Secondary scholarly source with exact primary locator:

- Matthew D. Haste, “Nurseries of Heaven: Samuel Stennett on Marriage and Family,” *Churchman* 129/1, pp. 63–64: https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/churchman/129-01_055.pdf
- Haste cites Samuel Stennett, *Discourses on Domestic Duties*, p. 127 (or *Works of Samuel Stennett*, vol. 3, p. 81 in the dissertation pagination discussed below).
- **Locator correction (verified 2026-11-15, see the card in §4.1):** `p. 127` is **not** the 1783 London first edition. In the 1783 first edition the passage stands on printed **p. 162**; *Works* (1824) is **vol. 2, p. 81**; *Domestic Duties* is not in vol. 3 at all, whose contents list runs the Scripture discourses and the occasional sermons. Haste's `p. 127` matches the **Edinburgh 1800 reprint**, which was verified directly. Haste's *Works* volume number for this treatise is wrong; and because a volume number in a citation can point to the wrong volume without looking wrong, his volume numbers must not be copied unchecked. (Haste's other *Works* citation, `Works, 3:175`, is **correct** — verified against the vol. 3 contents list, which runs the funeral sermons and occasional discourses, Gill's funeral sermon at p. 147 and Howard's at p. 279, with *A Trip to Holyhead* there. Correcting our own earlier overstatement: that endnote is not an error, and it is not evidence of a pattern.)

Additional source:

- Matthew D. Haste, *Marriage in the Life and Theology of John Gill, Samuel Stennett, and Andrew Fuller* (SBTS dissertation, 2015): https://repository.sbts.edu/handle/10392/4946

Haste reports that Stennett:

1. recommends pursuing marriage around the age mankind generally considers persons **`sui juris`**;
2. then advises young people to consider the **`concurrence of parents and friends`**;
3. does **not** approve arranged unions;
4. treats parental wisdom as a serious factor in forming the marriage connection.

Haste notes the legal background: Hardwicke's Marriage Act required parental permission below age 21; Stennett's `sui juris` recommendation therefore matters because his parental/friend concurrence advice is not merely reducible to a rule for young minors.

**Transcription protocol of record.** The pages were read through the text layer / page images served by HathiTrust and Internet Archive. Anyone needing a diplomatic transcript must consult the plate.

**Verified 1783 text (p. 162), long-s normalised, spelling otherwise unchanged:**

> "The concurrence of parents and friends will ever be an object of great consequence with virtuous and considerate young people. The tender affection of those from whom under God they received their existence, the just authority with which nature has invested them, and the prudence and experience they derive from age, are all striking reasons why children should pay a religious regard to their judgment, advice, and consent in all affairs, but especially in these matters. And it is an argument of very commendable good sense and discretion in youth, so to conduct themselves in the management of this important business, as to secure, if possible, the approbation and respect of all around them."

Immediately following (p. 163): "But what demands the principal attention of those who mean to form this connection, is the subsistence of a sincere friendship and cordial affection between themselves."

Immediately preceding (p. 161), the rule this paragraph qualifies:

> "Here we will venture to recommend, in the first place, an early period of life as the fittest for entering into this relation. By early life I mean that age when a person, by the general consent of mankind, is deemed *sui juris*, or at his own disposal. Alliances formed previous to this are often followed with very pernicious consequences, as they are in some instances the result of the interested views of others, rather than the judgment and attachment of the parties themselves …"

**Textual correspondence 1783 → 1800 → 1824:** the three witnesses agree word for word apart from insignificant stop-punctuation and the 1800/1824 modernisation of long-s, `-connexion` and a few spellings. 1824 vol. 2 reads `is deemed sui Juris, or at his own disposal` and then the identical `concurrence of parents and friends` paragraph at p. 81. No doctrinal drift between editions.

**Normative force — the answer to question 2.** Not a legal condition, not an absolute paternal veto, and not merely optional advice. Stennett's own grammar places it inside the fifth commandment's `religious regard`, i.e. a genuine moral duty of the child, but one he explicitly *graduates by age and capacity* elsewhere in the same book (printed pp. 269–273, 276–277):

> "In the early part of life, when children are totally incapable of governing themselves, absolute and unlimited obedience is required. When reason opens, and they can discern good and evil, they are still to be obedient in all things, so far as is consistent with a good conscience. And ever after, on the liberal grounds of friendship, they are to accommodate themselves to the wishes and views of their parents, provided these do not clash with the duty they owe to superior authority." (p. 269)

> "Further, when children arrive at mature age, though they are to be justified in disobeying such commands as are directly opposed to the clear dictates of their own reason and judgment, yet they ought to listen respectfully to the counsels of their parents, and in cases of a doubtful nature, on which age and experience are better capable of deciding, it is both their wisdom and duty to yield to their opinion." (p. 276)

The correct category is therefore **graded, conscience-bounded filial duty of serious consultation and regard**, strongest in minority and loosening to "wisdom and duty" counsel at mature age. Stennett never makes the parents' consent a condition of the marriage's validity, never gives the father a veto, and never treats the child's own judgment as dispensable — the persons addressed are already `at their own disposal`.

**Addressee — the answer to question 3.** Not minors and not "all adults" indiscriminately. The paragraph is addressed to "virtuous and considerate young people" who are forming the marriage connection at the age when "by the general consent of mankind" a person "is deemed *sui juris*, or at his own disposal" (p. 161). It is a rule for *competent young adults at the point of betrothal*, and the phrase "in all affairs, but especially in these matters" deliberately does not restrict it to courtship.

**Historical-legal control (not a substitute for Stennett's theology).** Hardwicke's Marriage Act 1753 governed England and Wales from 1754: a party under 21 needed parental consent to marry **by licence**, while marriage by banns was valid unless a parent actually forbade the banns; the Act did not sweepingly void all minority marriages lacking consent. Two consequences for use of Stennett: (a) his `sui juris` recommendation sits at or above the statutory age of majority, so his counsel is *not* the statute restated; (b) he argues from "the just authority with which nature has invested them" and "religious regard", i.e. from nature and the fifth commandment, not from the Act. Do not derive his position from the law, and do not derive the law from his position.

**One permissible reader formulation:**

> Stennett, writing in 1783 for young adults already of an age to dispose of themselves, treats serious consultation of parents and friends — their judgment, advice and consent — as a religious duty belonging to the fifth commandment, while insisting that the decisive thing in forming the marriage is the couple's own sincere friendship and cordial affection.

**One impermissible over-interpretation:**

> Stennett required a father's permission for a valid marriage and gave parents an absolute veto over a competent adult child's choice of spouse.

**Why that over-interpretation fails on the page:** Stennett's `consent` is one item in a list of `judgment, advice, and consent` that the child is to *regard*, not a permission the child must *obtain*; his own later teaching limits parental command at mature age to `counsels` in `cases of a doubtful nature`; and the section is a set of prudential `rules to be observed in the forming this important connection`, not a doctrine of the validity of marriage.

**Residual caution.** The locator chain in the secondary literature is unreliable in exactly the places this lane had been quoting: `p. 127` is the 1800 Edinburgh reprint, not the 1783 first edition, and `Works` vol. 3 does not contain *Domestic Duties* at all. Future citations should use **1783, p. 162** (with 1800 p. 127 and *Works* 1824, vol. 2, p. 81 as concordant witnesses).

### 4.2 What this card does not settle

Recorded so the reviewer does not have to discover it:

- **`sui juris` is not a fixed calendar age.** Stennett defines it relativistically: `that age when a person, by the general consent of mankind, is deemed sui juris`. He nowhere states 21, 25, or any number, and he does not mention Hardwicke's Act. Haste's inference that this means `early twenties` is a reasonable reading of Stennett's argument, not a statement on Stennett's page. Do not put a number in his mouth.
- **The duty/advice boundary is inferred from Stennett's own system, not from a single sentence.** The p. 162 paragraph calls for a `religious regard` but does not itself say `duty`; the grading language that makes the classification precise comes from the fifth-commandment discourse at pp. 269 and 276. The classification is sound, but it is a synthesis across the book and should be presented as such.
- **`children` at p. 162 is not restricted to minors.** Stennett uses `children` for the filial relation as such, and the paragraph's own addressees are `young people` already `at their own disposal`. A reader who takes `children` there to mean `minors` will misread the passage.
- **The 1824 *Works* is not a critical edition.** It is a repagination with modernised typography; its agreement with 1783 is evidence of textual stability, not a collation of substantive variants. No claim is made here about copies of the 1783 edition other than the two inspected.
- **One passage was read in two witnesses, not all.** The p. 269 / p. 276 grading passages were read in the 1783 UC copy and cross-checked in the Edinburgh 1800 reprint by pagination shift; they were not separately located in the second 1783 copy by leaf.
- **Nothing here speaks to Particular Baptists generally.** This is one author's practical theology in one book, cited for what he actually wrote.

### Evidence classification

`STENNETT_PARENT_FRIEND_CONCURRENCE = VERIFIED_PRIMARY` (1783 first edition, p. 162, directly inspected; concordant in Edinburgh 1800 p. 127 and *Works* 1824 vol. 2 p. 81).

The quote may now be transcribed directly from the 1783 primary scan rather than borrowed from Haste. What the upgrade does **not** license is the stronger legal reading — see the impermissible formulation above.

### Important nuance

Stennett's word `concurrence` should not automatically be converted into the strongest possible modern legal meaning of an absolute paternal veto. His treatment is presented as wise formation of a marriage by persons capable of acting for themselves, while retaining serious family participation.

This makes Stennett especially useful against two errors:

- autonomous-couple courtship that treats parents as irrelevant;
- forced/arranged marriage that erases the marrying person's own judgment and consent.

## 7. Theological significance for the family-government companion

The new evidence strengthens the project's conservative case in a precise way.

It supports:

- marriage is not merely a private romantic contract between two isolated adults;
- substantially faithful parents have a serious moral place in courtship/marriage discernment;
- secret romantic bypass of functioning parents is historically alien to significant Baptist practical theology;
- becoming `sui juris` does not automatically make parental concurrence morally irrelevant;
- real personal marital consent remains indispensable;
- strong family participation is compatible with rejecting forced marriage.

It does **not** settle every question about:

- a competent adult who has lived independently for many years;
- an unjust parental refusal;
- abusive parents;
- parents who oppose marriage for sinful reasons;
- an adult daughter still living at home versus a long-independent adult daughter;
- whether `concurrence` always means a binding veto;
- the exact civil/legal significance of parental objection today.

Those remain governed by `193`, `203`, `205` and the status/jurisdiction controls.

## 8. Product-safe synthesis

A strong but evidence-honest formulation is:

> **Historic Baptist family theology did not generally imagine marriage as an entirely private choice made by two detached individuals. John Gill explicitly included parents and guardians among those whose consent properly belonged to the marriage process, while preserving the indispensable voluntary consent of the man and woman themselves. Samuel Stennett likewise urged young adults to seek the concurrence of parents and friends. This strengthens the case for serious parental involvement without turning adult children into property or making every parental objection infallible.**

A stronger claim such as:

> `The 1689 Confession or General Assembly gave fathers an absolute veto over every adult daughter's marriage`

remains **not established**.
