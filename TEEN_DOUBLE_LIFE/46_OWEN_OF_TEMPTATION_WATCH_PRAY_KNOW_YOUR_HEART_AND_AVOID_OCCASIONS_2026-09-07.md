## 0. Why Owen matters for this project

The project needs a biblical explanation for:

- why “just be strong” is inadequate;
- why individual weaknesses matter;
- why parents may need different boundaries for different children;
- why remaining near a known occasion of sexual sin is foolish;
- why temptation must be addressed before the final sinful act;
- why prayer/watchfulness are not optional add-ons.

Owen’s *Of Temptation* provides exactly this framework from Matthew 26:41.

# 1. Temptation is broader than the final solicitation

Owen defines temptation broadly as anything—state, way, condition, company, affection, reputation, opportunity, etc.—that has efficacy to draw the heart away from obedience into sin.

### Application

A smartphone is not intrinsically “the sin.”

But for a particular child it can become part of a temptation structure when it supplies:

- privacy;
- access;
- pornography;
- a specific peer group;
- romantic/sexual messaging;
- an account hidden from parents;
- nighttime opportunity.

Likewise:

- a friendship;
- sleepover;
- game chat;
- music scene;
- route home;
- bedroom privacy;
- certain social event

can function as an occasion fitted to a known lust.

---

# 2. Owen distinguishes being tempted from “entering into temptation”

One cannot avoid all temptation.

But “entering into temptation” means becoming specially entangled so that:

- temptation/lust is unusually active;
- the heart is engaged in its reasoning/allurement;
- escape becomes harder;
- fear and attraction may operate together.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.v.html

### Important pastoral implication

The parental goal cannot be:

> “My child must never encounter any evil idea.”

The more realistic and biblical goal:

> **Teach him not voluntarily to enter the conditions where his known lust gains unusual power.**

This fits graded exposure better than either total isolation or naive freedom.

---

# 3. “Watch and pray” is prevention, not merely recovery

Owen grounds the entire treatise in Christ’s command:

> “Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation.”

The command aims not merely at resisting after entanglement, but at **preventing entry**.

### Project importance

Much modern advice begins too late:

`explicit site is open → now exercise self-control`.

Owen moves the battle earlier:

`know weakness → notice season → notice company/opportunity → watch → pray → avoid entry`.

---

# 4. Know your own heart

Owen’s Chapter VII is unusually important for child-specific parenting.

He tells a person to know:

- natural frame/temper;
- peculiar lusts;
- corruptions;
- natural/spiritual weaknesses.

Then:

> finding where weakness lies, keep distance from occasions suited to it.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.x.html

### Parenting implication

A wise father/mother needs to know the child well enough to ask:

- Is he highly approval-seeking?
- Does he lie under pressure?
- Is sexual curiosity already strong?
- Does isolation feed secret sin?
- Does one specific friendship repeatedly produce corruption?
- Does he become reckless when unsupervised at night?
- Does he react to shame by further concealment?
- Does he have unusual strength and proven self-government in an area where a sibling does not?

These are not psychological diagnoses. They are ordinary pastoral observations about **particular temptations and weaknesses**.

### Core line

> **Equal love does not require identical exposure. A parent who knows two children have different weaknesses may rightly give them different freedoms.**

---

# 5. Do not flatter the weakness by renaming it

Owen warns that people often give flattering names to their own natural distempers and excuse rather than kill them.

Modern adolescent parallels:

- lust → “curiosity”;
- cowardly peer conformity → “just joking”;
- vanity → “confidence”;
- secretiveness → “privacy”;
- manipulative sexual attention → “relationship”;
- uncontrolled device use → “everyone needs it.”

### Guard

Not every curiosity/privacy/confidence is sinful.

The point is self-deceptive renaming of a known sinful pattern.

---

# 6. Occasions and opportunities fitted to lust are dangerous even before subjective alarm

Owen’s argument is exceptionally direct:

When a person’s lust meets fitting opportunities/occasions, he may already be in temptation **whether he perceives it or not**.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.vii.html

### Application

This supports parental prevention even when a teen says:

> “Nothing happened yet.”

Examples:

- private contact with a person already involved in sexual pressure;
- hidden browser after pornography history;
- keeping an app used repeatedly for explicit content;
- joining a peer group known to trade sexual material;
- sleeping with unrestricted phone after repeated night relapse.

The goal is not punishment for imaginary future sin.

It is recognition that **occasion/opportunity is part of temptation**.

---

# 7. Owen explicitly warns about young men and corrupt company

Owen notes how young men venture into evil company initially abhorring its practices, then become delighted with the company and eventually with its evil.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.viii.html

This maps directly onto the project’s peer-world lane.

### Strong but careful line

> **Many young people do not begin by loving every sin of the group. They begin by loving the group. The group can become the bridge by which previously hated sins become familiar.**

This is Puritan pastoral observation, not a prevalence statistic.

---

# 8. Temptation despised will conquer

Owen stresses the danger of taking temptation lightly.

The attitude:

> “I know it is risky, but I can handle it”

is itself dangerous.

### Modern equivalents

- “I can keep the hidden account and just not use it badly.”
- “I can stay in the sexualized chat and not participate.”
- “I can keep the porn-capable device beside the bed and exercise discipline.”
- “I can keep meeting this person alone; nothing has happened.”

This connects directly to Matt. 5:29–30 and Rom. 13:14.

---

# 9. Prayer is not decoration

Owen says:

> he that would be little in temptation should be much in prayer.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.viii.html

Prayer:

- recognizes inability to preserve ourselves;
- seeks Christ’s help;
- keeps heart in opposition to temptation;
- joins watchfulness rather than replaces it.

### Important balance

Wrong:

> “Just pray, but keep every occasion.”

Also wrong:

> “Install controls; prayer is irrelevant.”

Biblical/Puritan:

> **watch + pray**.

---

# 10. Seasons of unusual vulnerability

Owen identifies seasons such as:

- outward prosperity/self-confidence;
- spiritual slumber/formality;
- neglect of communion with God.

Source:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.ix.html

For adolescents, future pastoral application may include:

- new freedom/device;
- unsupervised travel;
- first romantic relationship;
- new peer group;
- family conflict;
- long periods of spiritual indifference;
- post-discovery overconfidence (“problem solved”).

Do not present this list as Owen’s exact examples; clearly distinguish adaptation.

---

# 11. Privacy and opportunity can destroy strong resolutions

In *Grace and Duty of Being Spiritually Minded*, Owen notes that privacy, secrecy and opportunity can become occasions of temptation, even overturning good beginnings/resolutions.

Source:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/spirituallyminded/spirituallyminded.i.v.ix.html

This is exceptionally relevant to:

- bedroom devices;
- secret accounts;
- disappearing messages;
- unsupervised late-night use.

### Strong line

> **A sincere resolution made in public may be much weaker than a secret opportunity prepared in private.**

Again, pastoral inference, not scientific law.

---

# 12. Parent application: child-specific protection is compatible with Christian wisdom

Owen does not write a modern parenting manual, but the inference is strong:

If mature believers are commanded to:

- know their peculiar weaknesses;
- avoid fitting occasions;
- watch specific seasons;
- distrust self-confidence;
- pray for preservation,

then parents responsible for immature children have good reason to help them do the same until self-government matures.

### This supports

- different boundaries for siblings;
- temporary stricter restrictions after demonstrated weakness;
- gradual restoration of privilege;
- requiring transparency around known occasions;
- protecting a child from a corrupting company.

### This does NOT support

- arbitrary domination;
- humiliating surveillance;
- punishment for unproven fantasies;
- never allowing any age-appropriate autonomy;
- assuming controls regenerate the heart.

---

# 13. Integration with Matthew 5:29–30

Owen helps explain the practical logic of radical amputation:

Christ commands radical dealing with what repeatedly becomes a gateway to sin.

Owen explains:

> identify the ways/companies/opportunities that give advantage to corruption and set oneself against them.

Modern application:

> **Do not worship access. If a tool repeatedly functions as an occasion of grave sin for this person, the wise question is not “Is the tool technically lawful?” but “Why insist on keeping the occasion while praying not to enter temptation?”**

---

# 14. Integration with accountability

Voluntary accountability can be framed through Owen without modern therapy language:

A person who knows his weakness and watches against temptation should not insist on preserving secrecy around the very occasions that have repeatedly defeated him.

Possible pastoral question:

> “If you say you want to flee this lust, why are you also fighting to preserve the secret access by which it repeatedly reaches you?”

This can expose contradiction between professed desire for holiness and practical provision for the flesh.

---

# 15. Publication-safe core propositions

1. **Temptation is not identical to the final sinful act.**
2. **Wisdom fights before entanglement.**
3. **Particular weaknesses require particular watchfulness.**
4. **Companies/opportunities can be part of temptation itself.**
5. **Self-confidence is not spiritual maturity.**
6. **Prayer and avoidance belong together.**
7. **Different children may rightly need different boundaries.**
8. **External boundaries do not regenerate; they can nevertheless prevent foolish entry into temptation.**
