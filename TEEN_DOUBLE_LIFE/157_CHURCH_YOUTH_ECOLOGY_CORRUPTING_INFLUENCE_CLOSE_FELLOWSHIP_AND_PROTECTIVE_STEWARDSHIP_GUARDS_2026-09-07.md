# 0. Research question

What should a church do when a church-raised youth is not formally a member under discipline, yet is known to:

- defend serious sexual sin;
- use filthy/blasphemous speech;
- recruit peers into deception;
- circulate pornography;
- normalize secret sexualized chats;
- use church social spaces to gain romantic/sexual access under a Christian image;
- mock holiness and correction;
- remain socially embedded among younger or more impressionable youth?

The weak answer is:

> “He is an unbeliever, so just let him be present everywhere. Maybe exposure will eventually convert him.”

The opposite weak answer is:

> “Remove him from every Christian setting, make everyone shun him, and ensure he feels unwelcome.”

Both collapse necessary distinctions.

### Governing thesis

> **The church may keep the gospel door wide while narrowing the doors of trust, intimacy, representation, and influence when known conduct threatens the holiness or safety of others.**

---

# 1. 1 Corinthians 15:33 — close association can corrupt

Primary:

- 1 Cor. 15:33.

Commentary control:

- https://www.biblehub.com/commentaries/1_corinthians/15-33.htm

Paul warns the Corinthians not to be deceived about the formative power of bad company/associations.

Immediate context concerns resurrection-denying influences and their moral consequences.

### Safe project use

The text supports the general truth:

> **ideas, speech, companionship and examples within a community can shape conduct.**

### Guard

Do not use the verse to forbid all contact with unbelievers.

Paul explicitly rejects that interpretation in 1 Cor. 5:9–10.

The issue is not mere contact but formative association and corrupting influence.

---

# 2. 1 Corinthians 5:6 — tolerated scandal has community consequences

Primary:

- 1 Cor. 5:6.

Calvin:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom39.xii.ii.html

Gill/commentary:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_corinthians/5-6.htm

Immediate context is a scandalous **insider/member situation**, therefore formal discipline application belongs to members.

But Paul's rationale matters:

> tolerated notorious evil does not remain purely private in a community.

### Project inference

A youth ministry cannot reason:

> “His conduct is his private issue; as long as he shows up, it has no bearing on the rest of the group.”

If he actively spreads/normalizes the conduct, leaders have a stewardship problem even if he is not a formal member.

### Guard

Do not transfer 1 Cor. 5 judicial language wholesale to a nonmember.

Use the contagion/community principle as inference, not as fictional excommunication.

---

# 3. Acts 19:8–10 — ministry setting may change when hardened opposition corrupts hearers

Primary:

- Acts 19:8–10.

The sequence:

1. Paul reasons/persuades openly for months.
2. Some become hardened/disobedient.
3. They speak evil of the Way before the crowd.
4. Paul withdraws the disciples from that environment.
5. He continues intensive teaching elsewhere.

### Key principle

> **Protecting disciples from a hardened corrupting environment is compatible with continuing gospel ministry.**

This is not equivalent to modern youth-group policy, but it demonstrates that Christian ministry need not leave disciples indefinitely embedded in a setting where hardened opposition is actively poisoning the environment.

# 4. Public Word vs close fellowship

This distinction must remain explicit.

## Public preaching / ordinary worship attendance

Generally widest access, unless safety/order requires otherwise.

Purpose:

- hear God's Word;
- be convicted;
- observe Christian worship;
- receive gospel invitation/warning.

## Open evangelistic group

Broad access by design.

Leaders still retain conduct rules.

## Discipleship / confession-oriented small group

Higher trust requirement because participants may disclose:

- sexual temptation;
- family conflict;
- private sin;
- vulnerability.

Known exploitative/corrupting behavior can make ordinary inclusion unwise.

## Youth/minor group

Additional stewardship because:

- age/status asymmetries matter;
- younger adolescents may imitate older ones;
- grooming/coercion risks may exist;
- leaders have responsibility for organized environment.

## Trips / overnight / private chats / device groups

Higher access threshold still.

## Choir/platform/visible ministry

Representative trust, not mere gospel hearing.

### Locked line

> **There is no biblical right of every visitor to every layer of Christian intimacy.**

---

# 5. Administrative restriction must not become secret punishment

Because a nonmember may fall outside formal discipline jurisdiction, leaders may be tempted to invent a shadow discipline system:

- unexplained exclusion;
- informal blacklisting;
- private rumor networks;
- indefinite restrictions without conversation;
- no appeal/review;
- punishment disguised as “safety.”

This is dangerous.

### Protective stewardship should ordinarily include

- established facts;
- direct conversation where appropriate;
- clear reason for restriction;
- proportionate scope;
- need-to-know confidentiality;
- review path where change is possible;
- separate safeguarding process when abuse/coercion is alleged.

### Rule

> **Prudence is not permission for arbitrary power.**

---

# 6. Influence risk is conduct-specific

Do not classify a youth as “corrupting” because he:

- is awkward;
- has doubts;
- dresses differently;
- is from an unbelieving family;
- once used profanity;
- fell sexually and repented;
- has a bad reputation unsupported by facts.

Concrete corrupting conduct looks more like:

- deliberately sends porn/sexual material to peers;
- encourages others to hide accounts from parents;
- coaches deception;
- targets younger youth for sexualized contact;
- mocks/pressures peers who pursue chastity;
- uses confidential disclosures against people;
- repeatedly violates clear group safety boundaries.

### Guard

> **Restrict conduct and access on evidence; do not create a caste of suspected “bad kids.”**

---

# 7. The church's silence sends a message too

If known serious conduct is never addressed while the youth continues normal representative/social privileges, peers may reasonably infer:

- leaders do not care;
- holiness is mainly adult rhetoric;
- church identity and secret sexual life are compatible;
- lying successfully preserves status;
- correction applies only when scandal becomes public.

This is especially damaging where youth already know the facts.

### Important nuance

The answer is not public disclosure of private facts.

Leaders may preserve confidentiality while still:

- confronting the offender;
- changing role/access;
- teaching holiness generally;
- protecting particular individuals;
- correcting false public impressions only to the extent needed.

### Strong line

> **Silence can preserve confidentiality; it must not become moral ambiguity.**

---

# 8. “Let him keep coming; maybe he will repent” — true aim, incomplete policy

Yes:

- continued Word hearing may be good;
- God can save later;
- no one should desire the youth's damnation/departure.

But conversion hope does not imply:

- unrestricted group access;
- unchanged social trust;
- platform privilege;
- access to vulnerable peers.

### Locked distinction

> **Evangelistic hope answers why the door to Christ remains open. Stewardship answers which other doors should be open while rebellion persists.**

---

# 9. If admonition makes attendance uncomfortable

There is a morally important difference between:

## A. Conviction discomfort

The person feels exposed because:

- sin is named;
- leaders no longer flatter the facade;
- boundaries have consequences;
- preaching confronts him.

This may be appropriate and even merciful.

## B. Humanly manufactured hostility

- mockery;
- gossip;
- coordinated coldness;
- humiliating him publicly without biblical process;
- making every conversation accusatory.

This is not justified simply because the youth is guilty.

### Rule

> **The church should be hard to inhabit comfortably as an honored hypocrite, but not hard to approach as a sinner who may yet repent.**

---

# 10. Relationship roles matter

Not every member has the same responsibility to confront repeatedly.

Most appropriate actors may include:

- parents;
- elders/pastors;
- youth leaders;
- mature adult with genuine relationship;
- person directly harmed, where safe/appropriate and not forced.

The whole congregation should share a truthful moral culture, but this does not mean a queue of personal confrontations.

### Guard

Corporate holiness ≠ mob discipline.

---

# 11. Restoration after change

If credible repentance appears:

- do not keep the youth permanently marked;
- distinguish forgiveness from access/trust;
- use modules `130–132` for progressive re-entrustment;
- remove restrictions whose rationale no longer exists;
- retain safeguarding limits that still have real grounds.

### Strong line

> **A protective boundary without a conceivable review path can quietly become a permanent sentence. Where safety permits, restoration should have a real path.**

---

# 12. Final locked synthesis

> **Церковь не обязана выбирать между двумя крайностями: либо выгнать ожесточённого подростка от Слова, либо позволять ему бесконтрольно оставаться внутри всех молодёжных связей и ролей. Публичное слышание Евангелия может оставаться открытым, тогда как близкое fellowship, представительские роли и доступ к уязвимым людям регулируются отдельно. Надежда на обращение не отменяет защиты других; защита других не должна превращаться в месть или тайное псевдоотлучение.**
