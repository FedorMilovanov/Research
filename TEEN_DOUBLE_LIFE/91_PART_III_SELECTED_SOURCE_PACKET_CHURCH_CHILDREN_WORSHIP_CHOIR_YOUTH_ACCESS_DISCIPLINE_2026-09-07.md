# 0. Part III promise

Part III must answer:

> **How should a church respond when a child or teenager is welcome to hear the gospel but is known to live in serious, defended, or actively corrupting sin—and what do our practices around singing, children’s choir, youth group, small groups, ministry roles, discipline, and restoration teach about God’s holiness?**

Core movement:

`children are morally responsible`  
`→ outward worship cannot cover rebellion`  
`→ hearing Word differs from trusted/representative access`  
`→ outsiders differ ecclesiologically from professing members`  
`→ corrupting influence requires protection of others`  
`→ church discipline has due process and restorative purpose`  
`→ weak/repentant sinners must not be crushed`  
`→ safeguarding remains a separate duty`.

# 1. Biblical source packet

## A. Ephesians 6:1–4 — children are addressed now

### Direct claim

Children themselves receive a moral imperative to obey parents; fathers simultaneously receive the command not to provoke but to raise them in the Lord’s discipline/instruction.

### Part III use

Opening correction to:

> “Church moral requirements do not really apply to children until they are converted/adults.”

### Guard

The passage does not directly legislate choir/ministry eligibility.

Its direct value is child responsibility + adult shepherding duty.

---

## B. Psalm 50:16–21 — religious lips + hatred of correction

### Direct claim

God addresses the wicked person who:

- recites God’s statutes;
- takes covenant language on his lips;
- hates correction;
- casts God’s words behind him;
- joins/approves wrongdoing;
- uses speech deceitfully.

### Classical verification

Calvin, Psalm 50 commentary:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/comment3/comm_vol09/htm/xvi.iv.htm

Calvin explicitly identifies hypocritical duplicity: profession with the lips contradicted by life, and rejection of God’s Word precisely where it restrains sinful affections/conduct.

### Publication use

This is the **strongest main text** for the user’s concern:

> child/teen sings/speaks Christian truth while knowingly protecting rebellion and rejecting correction.

### Strong synthesis

> **The problem is not that God’s words are absent from the lips; it is that the life throws those words behind the back.**

Synthesis.

### Guard

Psalm 50’s covenant setting must be respected; do not pretend it is a direct “children’s choir policy” text.

---

## C. Isaiah 1:10–17 — worship without repentance/righteousness

### Direct claim

God rejects multiplied sacrifices, assemblies, prayers, and ritual when hands/life remain morally corrupt; he calls for washing, ceasing evil, learning good, justice.

### Publication use

Corroborates that **religious activity cannot function as moral cover**.

### Guard

Do not imply commanded worship forms were intrinsically bad. The indictment is hypocritical worship severed from obedience/repentance.

---

## D. Amos 5:21–24 — songs/ritual rejected in covenant injustice

### Direct claim

God rejects feasts, offerings, and songs in the context of covenant unfaithfulness/injustice and calls for justice/righteousness.

### Publication use

Short corroboration only; Psalm 50 should carry more weight.

### Guard

Do not make Amos primarily about private sexual sin or children’s music ministry.

---

## E. Matthew 15:7–9 — lips/honor vs heart

### Direct claim

Jesus applies Isaiah to hypocritical religious leaders/people whose lips honor God while hearts are far from him and whose worship is corrupted by human teaching.

### Publication use

Brief NT confirmation of the lips/heart contradiction.

### Guard

Immediate controversy concerns traditions and purity; do not flatten it into generic “any sinner who sings is a hypocrite.”

---

## F. 1 Corinthians 14:23–25 — outsiders may enter/hear

### Direct claim

Paul envisions outsiders/unbelievers entering the gathered church and being convicted/exposed by intelligible prophetic speech.

### Publication use

Primary corrective against:

> “Known sinner/unbeliever should be barred from hearing the Word.”

### Direct inference

Public gospel hearing is not reserved for already credible believers.

### Guard

The text does **not** give outsiders entitlement to:

- membership;
- Lord’s Table;
- ministry platform;
- confidential youth access;
- leadership.

---

## G. 1 Corinthians 5:1–13 — known scandal among one “called a brother”

### Direct claim

Paul condemns tolerated sexual immorality among the church and commands action toward the person bearing Christian-brother identity; he explicitly distinguishes judging those **inside** from those outside.

### Publication use

Essential ecclesiological distinction:

- professing/member insider under settled scandal;
- outsider/unprofessing teen who needs admonition/evangelism but is not automatically the same formal discipline case.

### Key lines to preserve conceptually

- tolerated scandal is not morally neutral for the body;
- church jurisdiction has an insider/outside boundary;
- formal exclusion is ecclesial, not private parental punishment.

### Guard

Do not apply “remove the evil person” directly as a rule to expel every difficult unbaptized child from public gathering.

---

## H. 1 Corinthians 5:6 — leaven, with context guard

### Direct claim

Tolerated scandal affects the body; a little leaven leavens the lump.

### Publication use

Supports concern over **corrupting influence**, especially where the behavior is not merely private weakness but recruitment/normalization.

### Guard

Member/scandal context remains controlling.

For an outsider child, use as ecclesial principle of tolerated corruption, not fictional excommunication procedure.

---

## I. 1 Corinthians 15:33 — bad company corrupts good morals

### Publication use

Directly relevant to youth-group access and the church’s duty toward other children.

### Guard

Do not label every friendship with a sinner as forbidden; Jesus himself associated with sinners without sharing their sin.

Issue is morally formative/corrupting companionship.

---

## J. 2 Timothy 2:22 — holy companionship

### Direct claim

Flee youthful passions and pursue righteousness with those calling on the Lord from a pure heart.

### Publication use

Church must provide more than exclusion:

> **positive counter-formation and holy belonging**.

Youth ministry should not merely say “avoid bad children”; it should cultivate companions and adults among whom holiness is socially livable.

---

## K. Galatians 6:1–2 — restoration with gentleness/self-watchfulness

### Direct claim

Those who are spiritual restore a caught person in a spirit of gentleness while watching themselves.

### Publication use

Important restoration/discipline tone.

### Guard

Gentleness ≠ absence of consequences/qualification standards.

---

## L. Matthew 12:18–21 / Isaiah 42 — bruised reed guard

### Direct claim

The Servant’s manner is gentle toward the weak/bruised; Christ does not crush the bruised reed or quench smoking flax.

### Publication use

Protect against a church culture that reacts to one exposed weak sinner as though severe public humiliation proves holiness.

### Guard

Do not use tenderness toward weak grace to excuse settled protected rebellion or active corruption of others.

# 2. 1689 confessional packet

Primary stable text:

- https://baptistconfession.org/

---

## 2.1 Chapter 16.6–7 — weak believer / unregenerate outward duty

### Verified text/function

16.6:

God accepts believers’ sincere works in Christ even though accompanied by weakness/imperfection.

16.7:

Works of unregenerate people may materially be things God commands and useful, but do not please God spiritually because they do not proceed from faith/right manner/end; yet neglect of such commanded duties is more sinful.

### Publication use

This is the key guard around the user’s “do not sing to God as if everything is fine” concern.

Correct call is **not**:

> “If you are unregenerate, stop every prayer/song until conversion.”

Better:

> **Do not use outward duty as spiritual cover. You are still commanded to Godward duty, but you need repentance/faith and true worship.**

---

## 2.2 Chapter 19 — moral law binds all

### Publication use

Children/unregenerate attenders do not live in a moral exemption zone.

This provides confessional backbone for age-appropriate conduct expectations in church settings.

---

## 2.3 Chapter 22.5 — singing as worship

### Verified text

The confession includes teaching/admonishing in psalms, hymns, spiritual songs and singing with grace in the heart among parts of religious worship, to be performed with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear.

### Publication use

Singing is not morally neutral stage performance merely because the singer is a child.

### Guard

22.5 is **not** an explicit rule saying only regenerate/baptized children may participate in any educational music activity.

Function matters:

- congregational singing;
- catechetical singing;
- formal choir;
- worship-leading platform

are not institutionally identical.

---

## 2.4 Chapter 26 — church membership, privileges, government

### Function

Visible church order includes real membership, discipline/government, and communion categories.

### Publication use

Supports the distinction:

`visitor/hearer` ≠ `member in good standing` ≠ `trusted minister/leader`.

### Guard

Do not infer a detailed youth-program policy directly from the confession.

Primary:

### Verified contribution

Calvin treats the target as hypocrites whose life/work denies the godliness their lips profess; especially relevant is hatred of correction and refusal to let God’s Word regulate sinful affections/conduct.

### Part III use

Best classical voice for the worship/hypocrisy section.

One short quotation or close paraphrase is enough.

---

## 3.2 Richard Baxter — children/youth / filthy speech / corrupt company

Primary accessible family text:

- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43800/43800-h/43800-h.htm

### Verified contribution

Baxter directly addresses children/youth as morally responsible according to their capacity and warns against company that teaches:

- cursing/swearing;
- lying;
- filthy speech;
- idleness/licentiousness;
- contempt for duty.

### Part III use

Historical witness that church/family concern over a corrupting youth subculture is not an invention of the internet age.

### Guard

Baxter’s paedobaptist/family-polity assumptions are subordinate and not controlling for 1689 ecclesiology.

---

## 3.3 Charles Spurgeon — child conversion / no lower repentance

Primary:

- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child

### Verified contribution

Spurgeon rejects:

- postponing concern for child conversion;
- receiving child profession without real examination;
- treating terror as automatically repentance;
- treating religious joy as automatically faith;
- requiring adult demeanor beyond genuine child-appropriate faith/repentance.

### Part III use

This gives the exact Baptist balance needed for choir/youth/church membership discussions:

> neither hereditary Christianity nor adultized perfectionism.

---

## 3.4 John Owen — church discipline / due process / recovery

Primary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/evangelicalchurches/evangelicalchurches.iii.xiv.html

### Verified high-value material

Owen explicitly limits church power to church members and treats excommunication as exclusion from **church-granted privileges**, not natural/civil rights.

For obstinate scandal he requires:

- evident sin;
- prior admonition;
- patient waiting for its effect;
- judgment in love/compassion;
- recovery of offender as primary aim.

He rejects instant ritualized “first/second/third admonition” rush to exclusion.

### Publication use

Critical guard against turning the article’s strictness into summary punishment.

### Strong synthesis

> Biblical seriousness about holiness includes seriousness about process, patience, facts, and restoration.

---

## 3.5 John Gill — Baptist discipline distinctions

Primary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical.iii.vi.html

### Verified contribution

Gill states excommunication:

- does not impose civil punishment;
- does not break natural/civil relationships;
- does not forbid attendance on the external ministry of the Word.

### Part III use

Especially useful for:

> exclusion from church privileges ≠ ban from hearing preaching.

This strengthens the project’s distinction with explicit Particular Baptist historical support.

---

## 3.6 Richard Sibbes — tenderness toward weak grace

Historical scan options:

- https://ebooks.regent-college.edu/Sibbes-Bruised-Reed-1825/
- https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Bruised_Reed_and_Smoking_Flax.html?id=Hzw3AAAAMAAJ

### Verified core

Sibbes emphasizes:

- grace can be very small at first;
- weak grace can coexist with much remaining corruption;
- Christ’s manner is to cherish rather than crush the smoking flax;
- ministers/governors should imitate Christ’s tenderness toward weak beginners.

### Publication use

Essential counterweight after severe sections on hypocrisy/discipline.

### Guard

Weak grace ≠ settled peace with deliberate corruption.

Do not use Sibbes to erase church qualification/safeguarding duties.

# 4. Church-policy taxonomy — evidence categories must be explicit

## DIRECT BIBLICAL/CONFESSIONAL

- children are morally accountable;
- worship cannot cover defended rebellion;
- outsiders may hear public Word;
- professing insiders can face formal discipline;
- bad company is corrupting;
- restoration should be gentle and truth-oriented;
- worship is holy, not morally neutral.

## STRONG PASTORAL / ECCLESIAL INFERENCE

- a church may suspend a child/teen from formal choir/platform when serious known rebellion or corrupting conduct contradicts the role;
- trusted/confidential youth access may require higher conduct/safety thresholds than public attendance;
- restoration to a visible role may require credible change over time.

## PRUDENTIAL POLICY

- exact choir admission form;
- baptism/profession requirement for a particular choir;
- duration of suspension;
- trip/device/chat rules;
- exact supervision ratio;
- restoration review procedure.

### Publication requirement

Never slide from category 3 to:

> “The Bible commands exactly this ministry policy.”

# 5. Specific children’s-choir thesis

### Safe strong claim

> Scripture gives no inspired children’s-choir eligibility list, but a church may biblically require age-appropriate conduct for a visible worship/ministry role and may temporarily remove a child whose known serious, defended, or corrupting behavior makes continued public representation spiritually incoherent or unsafe.

### Why

- children have real duties;
- singing can be worship;
- religious form cannot cover defended rebellion;
- church has stewardship of influence/others;
- public ministry is not identical to public gospel hearing.

### Must also say

- choir is not a reward for sinless children;
- ordinary childish sin is not automatic suspension;
- repentance/restoration are real goals;
- a weak/confessing struggler is not treated like a corrupter who recruits others.

---

# 6. Youth/small-group access thesis

### Safe strong claim

> Evangelistic openness does not require unrestricted access to every intimate or vulnerable youth setting.

When a participant is known to:

- distribute pornography;
- sexualize/harass peers;
- collect confessions for gossip/manipulation;
- teach children to deceive parents;
- groom/pressure others;
- actively recruit peers into serious sin,

leaders may need to:

- supervise;
- restrict private contact;
- redesign participation;
- temporarily exclude from a specific trip/group/role;
- invoke safeguarding procedures.

### Guard

Do not punish based on rumor or mere social dislike.

Facts/conversation/due process matter.

# 7. Struggling sinner vs corrupting influence

Part III must visibly contrast:

## A. Weak/struggling child

- confesses;
- receives correction;
- wants help;
- may relapse;
- is not recruiting others;
- is willing to lose access/privilege to fight sin.

Pastoral posture:

- truth;
- boundaries;
- tenderness;
- restoration;
- no permanent branding.

## B. Settled/corrupting child

- defends the conduct;
- deliberately conceals;
- rejects correction;
- recruits others;
- uses church access/status for sinful opportunity;
- repeatedly violates trust without willingness to change.

Pastoral posture:

- direct admonition;
- stronger restriction of trusted/platform access;
- protection of others;
- formal church discipline if person is actually under church jurisdiction;
- continued gospel call.

### Guard

These are pastoral conduct categories, not infallible decrees about election.

# 13. Part III backbone in one paragraph

Not publication prose; editorial logic test:

> Children do not enter church as morally neutral beings waiting for adulthood before God’s commands apply. Yet the church’s response must distinguish the open door of gospel hearing from the narrower doors of membership, Table, platform, intimate youth access, and leadership. Scripture condemns religious lips used to coexist with hatred of correction, so knowingly allowing settled rebellion to wear visible ministry as camouflage can teach hypocrisy. At the same time, outsiders should still hear the Word, and every weak sinner must not be treated as a formal 1 Corinthians 5 case. The church must distinguish a bruised, confessing struggler from a child who protects sin and corrupts others; it must protect other youth, use due process, separate safeguarding from discipline, and keep restoration as a real aim. Holiness does not mean only perfect children may serve; grace does mean the church cannot pretend known rebellion and worship are morally unrelated.

If future draft cannot preserve these distinctions, Part III is not ready.
