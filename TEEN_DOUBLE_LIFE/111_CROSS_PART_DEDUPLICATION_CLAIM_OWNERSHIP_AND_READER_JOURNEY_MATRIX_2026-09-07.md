# Cross-Part Deduplication — Claim Ownership and Reader-Journey Matrix

**Status:** PRE-DRAFT EDITORIAL CONTROL / RESEARCH ONLY  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Architecture:** three numbered core articles + adult-child companion  
**Purpose:** prevent the large Research corpus from producing four articles that repeat the same theology, warnings, statistics and pastoral conclusions.

---

# 0. Governing principle

Each major claim needs one **home article**.

Elsewhere it may be:

- **BRIDGE** — 1–3 sentences to connect argument;
- **CALLBACK** — a short reminder with link/reference to the home article;
- **NO REPEAT** — do not re-explain.

The series should feel cumulative:

> **Part I explains the hidden life. Part II explains parental duty. Part III explains church duty. The companion explains changed jurisdiction after adulthood.**

If all four articles independently explain heart, pornography, repentance, boundaries, church discipline and Luke 15, the series has failed editorially even if every paragraph is true.

# 1. Reader journey

## Part I asks

> **Что происходит в самом подростке и вокруг него, когда возникает тайная жизнь?**

Reader leaves Part I understanding:

- source in the heart;
- attraction/temporary sweetness;
- deceit;
- corrupting company;
- digital opportunity;
- secrecy and learned concealment;
- progressive moral insensitivity;
- false profession vs serious fall must not be collapsed;
- light/confession is the direction out, but parental procedure is not yet explained.

## Part II asks

> **Что должны делать родители, если только Бог может дать новое сердце?**

Reader leaves Part II understanding:

- sovereignty does not create parental passivity;
- means/formation matter without guaranteeing regeneration;
- unregenerate child remains accountable;
- boundaries may be radical and individualized;
- authority is bounded by non-provocation;
- truth channel matters;
- discovery requires fact/safety classification;
- being caught is not repentance;
- love remains while freedoms/trust may change.

## Part III asks

> **Что должна делать церковь, когда проблема уже не только семейная?**

Reader leaves Part III understanding:

- children are morally visible to the church;
- worship cannot function as camouflage;
- public hearing of Word differs from trusted/representative privilege;
- outsider differs from professing insider/member;
- weak struggler differs from corrupting recruiter;
- choir/youth access requires role-specific prudence;
- discipline requires facts/process/patience/restoration;
- safeguarding remains separate from ecclesial discipline.

## Adult companion asks

> **Как любить и говорить правду, когда ребёнок уже взрослый и детская власть родителей закончилась?**

Reader leaves understanding:

- adulthood changes jurisdiction, not moral truth or family relation;
- honor is not identical to child-level obedience;
- contact and admonition can change mode without changing truth;
- do not finance the far country;
- do not manufacture suffering as a conversion technique;
- household support can carry conditions;
- return home is not automatically repentance;
- forgiveness, relationship, trust, access and church standing are distinct;
- hope rests in sovereign grace, not control.

# 3. Theology dedup rules

## 3.1 Regeneration

### Full treatment

**Part II.**

This is where to explain:

- God alone gives new heart;
- 1689 effectual calling;
- Word/Spirit means;
- parental duty does not regenerate;
- inability/depravity does not create moral exemption.

### Part I

Maximum function:

> Church upbringing and religious vocabulary are not the same as regeneration.

Then move on.

### Part III

Maximum function:

> Church structures cannot regenerate children, but church responsibility therefore does not disappear.

### Adult companion

Use sovereignty mainly as ground of hope and prayer after control ends.

Do not re-teach the whole ordo salutis.

---

## 3.2 Repentance

### Full treatment

**Part II**, because discovery naturally raises the distinction:

`exposure`  
`≠ fear`  
`≠ tears`  
`≠ apology`  
`≠ saving repentance`.

### Part I

End with darkness/light/confession and explicitly defer practical discernment to Part II.

### Part III

Use repentance only to decide posture toward restoration/discipline/role.

### Adult companion

Use repentance to evaluate return/restoration without replaying the entire theology.

## 3.3 Hardening

### Full treatment

**Part I.**

Part II and III must not each create a second sermon on Hebrews 3 / Ephesians 4.

Later articles can say:

> As Part I showed, repeated protected sin can train a person to resist truth more efficiently.

Then move to their own jurisdictional duty.

## Adult companion

# 7. Repetition-budget rules

A callback to a prior article should usually be no more than:

- one sentence of remembered thesis;
- one link;
- one new implication.

Bad:

> Part II spends 700 words re-explaining how the phone extends the peer group into the bedroom.

Good:

> Part I showed why the peer group no longer ends at the front door. The parental question is what to do with that access without confusing restriction with regeneration.

Bad:

> Part III re-teaches the full 1689 doctrine of regeneration.

Good:

> The church, like the parents, cannot regenerate a child. But inability to regenerate does not mean inability to teach, admonish, protect or regulate entrusted ministry access.

# 9. Safeguarding distribution

Safeguarding is one of the few themes that must appear in multiple articles because different jurisdictions encounter it differently.

But each article needs a distinct job.

## Part I — category guard

Purpose:

> sexualized digital harm may involve victimization/coercion, not voluntary sexual sin.

Do not build an emergency manual.

## Part II — discovery triage

Purpose:

> before moral lecture, determine whether there is coercion, sextortion, abuse, threat, image redistribution, adult involvement or immediate danger.

Then separate victimization from the child’s voluntary acts.

## Part III — institutional duty

Purpose:

> church discipline does not replace safeguarding, reporting, supervision or protection of others.

## Adult companion — household/legal reality

Purpose:

> household boundaries and eviction/support questions can become jurisdiction-specific legal matters; Scripture gives principles but local law governs many procedures.

No article should pretend pastoral theology replaces professional/legal emergency response.

# 12. Consequence distribution

Avoid a generic “10 consequences” section in every article.

## Part I

Consequences of hidden life itself:

- deeper concealment;
- corrupting habits/company;
- loss of sensitivity;
- sexual/social harm;
- possible exposure/coercion;
- increasing distance between profession and practice.

## Part II

Consequences of parental errors:

- permissiveness can fund opportunity;
- tyranny can provoke/discourage;
- surveillance-only strategy can improve concealment without changing heart;
- premature restoration can make consequences meaningless;
- despair/fatalism can abandon duty.

## Part III

Consequences of church errors:

- tolerated hypocrisy catechizes everyone watching;
- role access can extend harm/influence;
- summary punishment can crush weak grace or bypass truth;
- confused discipline/safeguarding can fail victims and offenders.

## Adult companion

Consequences of confused post-adult control:

- endless pursuit can become coercive/control-seeking;
- unconditional financing can enable;
- total retaliatory shunning can violate enduring family duties;
- consequence-manufacturing can become vengeance disguised as wisdom.

# 13. Article endings must not be interchangeable

## Part I ending

From darkness toward light:

> secrecy is not destiny; exposure alone is not repentance; the next question is what parents can faithfully do without pretending they can regenerate.

## Part II ending

From parental duty toward church responsibility:

> a family cannot solve every problem privately when conduct harms others or contradicts visible church roles; next comes the church’s stewardship.

## Part III ending

Complete core adolescent arc:

> holiness, truth, protection and restoration must coexist; church cannot save the child but must not teach him that public religion makes defended sin irrelevant.

Then separately link adult companion:

> adulthood changes the jurisdiction; it does not erase truth, love, prayer or hope.

## Adult companion ending

Sovereign hope without control:

> parents can no longer govern as they did in childhood; they can still love without lying, help without enabling, speak without revenge, pray without despair, and receive repentance without pretending trust is automatic.

# 14. Draft audit questions

After each article draft, run these questions:

1. Did this article answer its own question or merely re-summarize the project?
2. Is any section actually owned by another article in the matrix above?
3. Did a callback exceed three sentences without new argument?
4. Did we repeat the same historical quotation elsewhere?
5. Did we repeat the same statistic elsewhere?
6. Did we use the same metaphor at full strength twice?
7. Did we anticipate the adult companion so heavily that it no longer needs to exist?
8. Did we withhold necessary safeguarding merely to avoid repetition?
9. Does each transition make the next article necessary rather than redundant?
10. Could a reader read only one article and still understand its bounded thesis without being forced to read all four?

---

# 15. Final editorial verdict

The series is strongest when its progression is not:

`same problem → same theology → same warning → different audience`.

It should be:

> **Part I: anatomy.**  
> **Part II: parental stewardship.**  
> **Part III: ecclesial stewardship.**  
> **Companion: stewardship after jurisdiction changes.**

This ownership model is now the default deduplication control for future drafting.
