# Comparative Traditions — Daughter, Parental Authority, Courtship, Marriage, and Adult Independence

**Status:** COMPARATIVE THEOLOGICAL / PASTORAL AUDIT / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Purpose:** map major conservative approaches without pretending there is one undifferentiated “traditional Christian” model.

---

## 1. Why a comparative map is needed

The project now contains a real disputed question:

> What does it mean for an unmarried daughter to remain under her father, and what changes at adulthood and marriage?

Christians who all affirm:
- biblical authority;
- fifth-commandment honour;
- male headship in marriage;
- sexual holiness;

still disagree about:
- whether adult single children remain under direct parental command;
- whether parental consent is morally required for marriage;
- whether adult daughters should normally remain in the parental home until marriage;
- how much direct contact/courtship may occur without parents;
- when parents may be resisted.

This file prevents one model from being mislabeled as the only historically orthodox one.

---

## 2. Puritan household-government model — strong parental authority, but not static childhood forever

### Richard Baxter

Primary:
- *Christian Economics*: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43800/43800-h/43800-h.htm

Baxter strongly teaches:
- parents should govern children’s company and formation;
- children should not choose a calling without parental choice/consent while under their disposal;
- marriage should not be entered without parental consent;
- parental judgment should carry major weight.

He says parents are often better judges of a match than inexperienced youth.

But Baxter also recognizes that:
- parental government has different acts/degrees;
- when a child is grown and from under parental disposal/government, some earlier direct control changes.

### Required synthesis

Baxter is strongly anti-individualist, but he does not require us to pretend adulthood never alters jurisdiction.

---

## 3. William Gouge — parental consent in marriage is extremely weighty

Primary/modernized source:
- https://christianreader.app/books/of-domestical-duties/07-treatise-5-duties-of-children
- EEBO/PDF references already preserved in prior corpus.

Gouge gives parents a substantial role in:
- counsel;
- consent;
- marriage choice;
- household government.

He treats clandestine or contemptuous bypassing of parents in marriage as serious disorder.

Yet Gouge also explicitly distinguishes grown children no longer under the same government from those still under direct parental governance.

### Project implication

A “Puritan” position should not be reduced either to:
- `parents are merely optional advisors`, or
- `adult children never transition from childhood jurisdiction`.

## 4. John Gill — strong continuing filial duty with reduced parental power in adulthood

Primary:
- Gill, *Body of Practical Divinity*, parents/children: https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html
- marriage duties: https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.i.html

Gill states:
- sons and daughters are equally bound to filial duty;
- parental power is **less when children are grown up**;
- observance, gratitude and filial reverence do not cease;
- obedience is “in the Lord,” and parents cannot bind the conscience to sin;
- marriage should be entered by voluntary consent, with the consent of parents/guardians where they have a real concern.

Gill also says parents provoke children when they force marriage to persons contrary to their inclination.

### Strong synthesis

> **Adult responsibility grows; filial honour remains; forced marriage is not the biblical answer to individualism.**

## 5. Modern conservative complementarian model — adult autonomy under God, strong counsel, no perpetual child-level government

### Ligonier / Reformed teaching

Ligonier’s Ephesians 6 treatment says the direct obedience command primarily addresses children who have not yet reached maturity, while adult children continue to owe honour and can draw principles from the passage.

Source:
- https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/obeying-parents-in-the-lord

Sinclair Ferguson similarly distinguishes:
- obedience as the form of honour while young;
- continuing honour after adult independence/new household.

Source:
- https://learn.ligonier.org/podcasts/things-unseen-with-sinclair-ferguson/honor-your-father-and-mother

### John Piper

Piper strongly resists adolescent autonomy and counsels submission even to some overprotective but non-sinful parental restrictions while a daughter remains under parental care. He also says the relation becomes more complex as she grows and becomes a more independent unit of life.

Source:
- https://www.desiringgod.org/interviews/to-what-extent-should-a-daughter-submit-to-an-extremely-overprotective-father

### Project use

This modern complementarian approach is generally:

`strong obedience while dependent/minor`  
→ `increasing adult responsibility`  
→ `lifelong honour`  
→ `new primary household at marriage`.

It is conservative, but not identical to a strict “daughter remains under father’s direct command until marriage” model.

## 6. Conservative biblical counseling — strong parental involvement, especially in courtship, but no imposed marriage

Jim Newheiser / IBCD:
- https://ibcd.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/newheiser.parenting_your_young_adult_children_2.pdf
- https://ibcd.org/adult-children-and-parents/

Newheiser says ideally:
- both sets of parents are significantly involved in courtship;
- adult children seek/hear parental wisdom;
- all parties agree on spouse/timing where possible.

But he also says:
- parents do not have the right to impose marriage;
- tyrannical authority can provoke young people to marry merely to escape;
- adult-child relations differ from small-child relations.

His adult-child notes also allow a daughter to choose to remain under parental protection before marriage without making that arrangement universal divine law.

### Strong project value

This is a useful middle model:

> **high parental involvement without treating adulthood or consent as fiction.**

## 7. Strict courtship / father-led model

Some conservative Christian subcultures teach a stronger pattern:
- daughters normally remain at home until marriage;
- father screens/approves romantic approaches;
- direct male-female courtship is heavily mediated by parents;
- parental blessing/consent is treated as near-essential.

### Potential strengths

- takes family authority seriously;
- resists secret romance and sexualized dating culture;
- makes marriage communal/accountable rather than purely private;
- can protect inexperienced young people from predatory or foolish relationships.

### Potential dangers

- converting prudence into universal divine law;
- confusing fatherhood with husband-headship;
- allowing adult responsibility to remain artificially undeveloped;
- enabling coercion or concealment when the father is foolish/abusive;
- making parental approval function as infallible revelation;
- creating secret rebellion precisely because legitimate disagreement has no lawful channel.

### Research rule

Do not caricature this model, but do not canonize its cultural details either.

---

## 8. Modern individualist dating model

At the opposite end:
- adult child chooses partner privately;
- parental counsel is optional;
- family learns late;
- direct romantic relationship takes priority before any covenant exists;
- objections are treated as controlling unless they concern obvious abuse.

### Biblical weaknesses

This model often underweights:
- fifth-commandment honour;
- wisdom of elders;
- family consequences of marriage;
- need for communal discernment;
- danger of secret sexual/romantic bonds creating facts before wise counsel can act.

### Guard

Adult responsibility is real; the critique is not that every adult decision needs parental permission, but that biblical wisdom does not treat parents as irrelevant outsiders.

## 9. Russian Evangelical Baptist / “our” context — family primacy is official, exact courtship protocol is not uniform

The Russian Union of Evangelical Christians-Baptists’ Social Concept says:
- family is the primary and most important social unit;
- parents bear primary responsibility for raising children;
- school, church and other institutions should **help the family, not replace parents as primary educators**;
- the father’s role in spiritual upbringing is specifically emphasized;
- modern autonomy from extended family can increase, but total loss of familial bonds is morally unhealthy.

Official source:
- https://baptist.su/faith

The RS ECB confession also affirms parental responsibility for spiritual upbringing:
- https://baptist.org.ru/o-baptistah/baptistskoe-verouchenie/

### Important limit

These official documents do **not** establish one detailed national courtship rule such as:
- every adult daughter must live at home until marriage;
- every romantic communication must pass through father;
- parental consent legally determines every adult marriage.

Russian Baptist local practice is heterogeneous.

### Article-safe conclusion

> **In the Russian ECB context there is official support for family primacy and parental responsibility, but exact adult-daughter/courtship practice must not be presented as if one local tradition were the formal doctrine of the whole Union.**

## 10. Comparative matrix

### Model A — strong continuing parental command

Emphasis:
- father-led household;
- courtship mediation;
- consent central;
- single daughter often remains at home.

Risk:
- adulthood distinctions can be flattened.

### Model B — Puritan/Reformed household authority with maturity transition

Emphasis:
- serious parental government and consent;
- household solidarity;
- adulthood changes some direct governance;
- filial honour remains.

Best witnesses:
- Gouge, Baxter, Gill (with differences among them).

### Model C — modern conservative complementarian / biblical counseling

Emphasis:
- child obedience while under parental authority;
- adult responsibility;
- lifelong honour;
- significant parental counsel/involvement;
- no imposed marriage;
- limits for tyrannical parents.

### Model D — modern individualist

Emphasis:
- adult autonomous choice;
- parents advisory only;
- direct couple relationship primary before marriage.

Risk:
- underweights family authority, wisdom and accountability.

## 11. Project verdict

The series should not teach either extreme as the whole Bible.

The strongest controlled position is:

1. family authority and parental honour are real and countercultural;
2. a minor/dependent child should not bypass lawful parental commands merely because they are inconvenient;
3. adulthood materially changes the form/degree of governance;
4. parental counsel/consent in marriage should be treated with very high seriousness;
5. adult marriage cannot be imposed against the person’s will;
6. marriage creates a new primary household and husband-headship is not simply father-headship transferred mechanically;
7. third parties should not casually undermine a righteous parental household;
8. when parents command sin, abuse authority, or oppose obedience to God, loyalty to God governs.

---

## 12. Core formulation

> **Biblical family order is neither adolescent individualism nor parental omnipotence. It gives parents real authority and lasting honour, gives adult sons and daughters real responsibility before God, gives marriage a new household priority, and requires every lower authority to remain under Christ.**
