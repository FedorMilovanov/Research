# Pastoral Failure Modes
## Covenant Presumption vs Baptist Ecclesial Vacuum

**Status:** RESEARCH / ADVERSARIAL COMPARATIVE PASS / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `273–283`

> Compare the strongest versions first, then identify how each system can be misused in practice. A misuse is not automatically entailed by the confession.

## 1. Paedobaptist failure mode — covenant presumption

### Drift

`child of believer -> baptized -> visible covenant member -> family/church language of belonging -> practical assumption that spiritual state is basically safe`.

Possible symptoms:

- conversion testimony treated as mere maturation rather than urgent personal faith;
- baptism recalled as evidence of salvation rather than covenant obligation/sign;
- serious defended sin softened because the person is a `covenant child`;
- catechesis produces doctrinal competence that adults misread as regeneration;
- Table admission occurs with low discernment because baptismal belonging carries excessive assurance.

### Confessional guard

This is **not required** by Westminster:

- WCF 28.5 denies that all baptized are undoubtedly regenerated.

URCNA synodical discussion has also rejected a simple presumption that baptized children are to be held regenerate until they demonstrate otherwise.

### Healthy corrective

`BAPTISMAL MEMBERSHIP -> GREATER CALL TO PERSONAL FAITH/REPENTANCE/OBEDIENCE`, not `automatic saving certificate`.

PCA’s own age-of-discretion language exemplifies this stronger model.

## 2. Credobaptist failure mode — ecclesial vacuum

### Drift

`child of believers -> not personally professing -> not baptized -> not formal member -> no member discipline -> treated as merely parent’s private problem / ordinary outsider`.

Possible symptoms:

- elders know little about children of members;
- no one owns catechesis beyond generic Sunday school;
- serious hidden sin remains a family-only matter until it harms church peers;
- unbaptized teen retains broad youth/choir/social privileges without direct moral confrontation because “he isn’t a member anyway”;
- baptism becomes the first moment the church begins taking profession/conduct seriously;
- civil adulthood becomes a practical “now he decides for himself” release with little theology of prior responsibility.

### Confessional guard

This is **not required** by 1689:

- moral law binds all;
- law is useful to others/unregenerate;
- neglect of materially commanded duties remains sinful;
- prayer/family worship/public assembly remain meaningful duties/means;
- parents retain real authority.

### Healthy corrective

`NO FORMAL MEMBERSHIP YET -> STILL NAMED PASTORAL RESPONSIBILITY`, while preserving keys/jurisdiction distinctions.

## 3. Credobaptist compensatory failure — premature certification

A church senses the membership vacuum and solves it by lowering the threshold for child baptism.

### Drift

`child belongs socially -> child gives expected answers -> baptism -> membership -> church/family anxiety relieved`.

Then baptism becomes an **identity stabilizer** rather than a response to credible personal profession.

Later defended sin is met with:

> “But you were baptized at eleven.”

This recreates the very sacramental/covenant presumption the Baptist intended to avoid, only downstream of a weak profession.

Use `161` controls.

## 4. Paedobaptist compensatory failure — indefinite noncommuning adulthood

A polity can formally retain baptized children/adults as noncommuning members for long periods.

Potential danger:

- status exists without credible profession;
- pastoral responsibility is real but may become administratively passive;
- `member` language can obscure the severity of continued refusal to confess Christ.

### Strong-polity response

PCA explicitly warns adult noncommuning members about neglect of covenant obligations; URCNA provides exclusion of mature baptized members. Thus the system has internal remedies when actually applied.

## 5. Both traditions — shared hypocrisy failure

Both can produce a double life if adults reward outward religious conformity more than truth.

### Paedobaptist version

> “You are a covenant child; behave like one so our assumptions remain comfortable.”

### Baptist version

> “You prayed/professed/got baptized; behave like a Christian so we do not have to question that certificate.”

Shared cure:

- honest assessment;
- law and gospel;
- no forced spiritual speech;
- real consequences;
- no heart-reading certainty;
- no reputation management.

## 6. Both traditions — shared permissiveness failure

A sophisticated doctrine of sovereign regeneration can be distorted into passivity:

> “Only God changes hearts, therefore external formation/discipline is spiritually unimportant.”

Both Reformed traditions reject that conclusion through ordained means, parental duty and moral law.

## 7. Both traditions — shared authoritarian failure

High accountability can also be distorted:

> “Because the child is under covenant/parents/church, every parental preference becomes divine law.”

Use existing conscience-category controls:

- direct moral command;
- biblical wisdom;
- lawful house rule;
- parental preference;
- civil law.

`HIGH ACCOUNTABILITY ≠ UNLIMITED AUTHORITY`.

## 8. Practical diagnostic test

Ask of any church:

### Membership clarity

- What exactly is this child’s status?
- What privileges attach?
- What discipline/jurisdiction attaches?

### Soteriological clarity

- Does status imply regeneration in the church’s rhetoric?
- Can a child honestly say “I do not believe” without adults manufacturing a profession?

### Formation clarity

- Who catechizes?
- Who directly admonishes?
- Are parents accountable for neglect?

### Responsibility clarity

- Does unbelief excuse conduct?
- Does age 18 function as a theological boundary it should not have?

### Authority clarity

- Are divine law, household rules and parental preferences distinguished?

## 9. Comparative conclusion

The practical advantage of paedobaptism is real only when covenant membership produces actual catechesis, oversight and discipline rather than presumption.

The practical advantage of credobaptism is real only when regenerate-membership doctrine produces careful profession discernment rather than pastoral neglect before profession.

### Locked line

> **Every ecclesiology has to answer two questions at once: “Whom do we call a member?” and “Whom are we responsible to shepherd?” A Baptist may answer those questions with different boundaries without answering the second with “only members.”**
