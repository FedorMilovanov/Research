## 1. Executive verdict

The project must not accept either of these equations:

`AUTHORITY = INTIMIDATION`

or

`CALMNESS = MORAL SOFTNESS`.

A father or mother may:

- command;
- forbid;
- warn;
- remove privileges;
- impose proportionate consequences;
- insist on truth;
- interrupt dangerous conduct;
- speak with grave moral seriousness.

None of those requires:

- explosive volume;
- insults;
- humiliating labels;
- theatrical rage;
- threats the parent does not intend or cannot lawfully carry out;
- making the child fear physical or relational abandonment;
- using the child's panic as evidence that correction "worked".

### Locked distinctions

> **AUTHORITY ≠ INTIMIDATION.**
>
> **REVERENCE ≠ PANIC.**
>
> **SERIOUSNESS ≠ RAGE.**
>
> **CONSEQUENCE ≠ THREATENING.**
>
> **ANGER MAY BE MORALLY INTELLIGIBLE; ANGER DISCHARGE IS NOT A DISCIPLINE METHOD.**
>
> **CHILD FEAR ≠ PROOF OF PARENTAL FAITHFULNESS.**
>
> **PARENTAL CALM ≠ MORAL INDIFFERENCE.**

And the child-side mirror remains binding:

> **PARENTAL SIN ≠ CHILD LICENSE TO DISOBEY LAWFUL COMMANDS.**

## 2. Ephesians 6:1–4 — the same passage commands obedience and limits parental mode

Paul does not solve parental abuse of authority by abolishing authority.

Children are commanded to obey parents in the Lord.

Fathers are then forbidden to provoke/exasperate and positively commanded to raise children in the Lord's discipline/instruction.

The project therefore may not isolate only one half:

- child obedience without parental restraint on the use of power;
- or non-provocation without real parental government.

### Product-safe synthesis

> **The apostolic answer to abusive authority is righteous authority, not no authority. The apostolic answer to childish resistance is obedience, not parental rage.**

Evidence class: `DIRECT_SCRIPTURE + CANONICAL_SYNTHESIS`.

## 3. Colossians 3:20–21 — discouragement is a named parental danger

Children again receive a real command to obey.

Fathers are warned not to provoke/embitter them lest they become discouraged/disheartened.

This gives a direct biblical reason to reject a correction culture in which the child learns:

- nothing will ever be enough;
- every mistake proves bad character;
- every disclosure will become a new explosion;
- no restoration path exists;
- parental affection is available only after emotional submission.

### Guard

`DISCOURAGEMENT RISK ≠ CHILD MUST NEVER FEEL SORROW, FEAR OR DISPLEASURE`.

Biblical correction can hurt. The prohibition concerns parental provocation/embittering, not the abolition of painful consequences.

## 4. James 1:19–20 / Ephesians 4:26–32 — anger is not a parenting sacrament

James commands Christians to be slow to anger and states that human anger does not produce God's righteousness.

Ephesians permits anger without sin while immediately prohibiting corrupt speech, bitterness, wrath, clamor and malice.

Therefore:

- not every experience of anger is itself identical with sin;
- anger never makes a parental action righteous merely by making it intense;
- anger must remain under truth, self-control, love and justice;
- verbal violence cannot be baptized as "moral clarity."

### Product kill-switch

Reject sentences such as:

- `A godly parent should never feel angry.`
- `If the parent is angry, the discipline is automatically invalid.`
- `The child needs to see how furious sin makes us, so explosive anger is useful.`
- `Raised volume proves seriousness.`

Use instead:

> **A parent may be genuinely grieved or angered by evil, but must not make the child absorb the parent's uncontrolled wrath as the mechanism of correction.**

Evidence class: `CANONICAL_SYNTHESIS`.

## 5. William Gouge — unusually direct historical witness

Primary locator:

- William Gouge, *Of Domesticall Duties*, exposition of Eph. 6, §117, `Of parents provoking children`.
- EEBO/University of Michigan TOC: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A68107.0001.001?view=toc
- searchable modern scan: https://www.digitalpuritan.net/Digital%20Puritan%20Resources/Gouge%2C%20William/Of%20Domestical%20Duties.pdf

Gouge's treatment is especially valuable because it is neither permissive nor anti-authority.

He treats Eph. 6:4 as forbidding abusive extremes in the exercise of real parental government, including in substance:

- excessive austerity/severity;
- threatening/reviling speech;
- overly harsh handling/correction;
- excessive restraint;
- sudden swings from indulgence into ferocity.

He also explicitly warns that parents themselves need the fear of God because children are under parental power while parents can easily feel less externally restrained.

### Historical-theology conclusion

> **Strong early-Reformed household authority did not require parents to treat intimidation as a virtue.**

Evidence class: `HISTORICAL_PRACTICE_STRONG / CLASSICAL_EXEGESIS_STRONG` for Gouge's own position; do not universalize one writer into every Reformed author.

## 7. Fear of God vs fear of parent

Use `110` as controlling theology.

A teen may fear:

- being shouted at;
- losing a phone;
- being exposed;
- being expelled from an activity;
- family embarrassment;
- physical retaliation;
- emotional withdrawal.

That fear can restrain conduct.

It can also train more sophisticated concealment.

Biblical fear of God is different: it governs secret conduct because the moral horizon is God, not merely detection.

### Locked formula

> **PARENTAL AUTHORITY MAY RESTRAIN OPPORTUNITY; PARENTAL INTIMIDATION CANNOT MANUFACTURE THE FEAR OF GOD.**

And:

> **EXTERNAL RESTRAINT IS STILL REAL STEWARDSHIP; IT MUST NOT BE MISTAKEN FOR REGENERATION.**

## 8. 2026 longitudinal disclosure evidence — secondary, not anthropology

Primary source:

Şule Selçuk et al., 2026, *Journal of Youth and Adolescence* 55(2):516–535.  
PMID 41499072; PMCID PMC12894135; DOI 10.1007/s10964-025-02311-8.  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41499072/

Design:

- 1,215 adolescents;
- 50.3% girls;
- assessed at ages 13, 15 and 16;
- eight countries differing in average individualism/collectivism.

Reported pattern:

- perceived psychological control predicted greater perceived neglect/overcontrol;
- perceived overcontrol then predicted lower disclosure;
- the indirect pathway was significant;
- rule-setting itself did not predict lower disclosure over time;
- when voluntary disclosure was isolated from secrecy items, parental solicitation predicted greater disclosure.

### Why this matters

This strongly supports a distinction already required biblically:

`RULE-SETTING ≠ PSYCHOLOGICAL CONTROL`.

The empirical literature does **not** justify saying:

> `rules make teenagers secretive.`

The newer cross-cultural longitudinal evidence is more compatible with:

> **lawful structure and information-seeking are not the same construct as manipulative psychological overcontrol.**

### Evidence boundary

This study does not define:

- biblical authority;
- sin;
- obedience;
- regeneration;
- abuse thresholds.

It is secondary evidence about disclosure dynamics.

## 9. Threats — four categories

### A. Truthful warning of a probable consequence

Example:

> `If you continue using this account to contact that person secretly, access to the app will stop.`

Can be legitimate if proportionate and within authority.

### B. Conditional statement about a decision not yet made

Example:

> `If this continues, we may need to change where/how the device is used.`

Can be legitimate if presented honestly as a possible decision rather than false certainty.

### C. Bluff

Example:

> `Do this once more and you will never have a phone again.`

when parent does not mean it or knows it is impossible.

Use `259`: `AUTHORITY ≠ LICENSE TO BLUFF`.

### D. Intimidatory threat

Examples:

- threat of unlawful violence;
- threat of abandonment of a dependent minor;
- threat to expose sexual details publicly for humiliation;
- threat to destroy the child's reputation unrelated to legitimate protection;
- coercive use of affection or belonging (`we will not love you / you are no longer our child`) as punishment.

These are not rendered righteous merely because the child actually sinned.

## 10. Raised voice / urgent intervention — do not overcorrect

The project should not publish a simplistic decibel ethic.

A loud command may be appropriate in immediate danger:

- `Stop!` near traffic;
- interrupting violence;
- preventing sexual contact/exposure;
- stopping destruction or imminent flight.

The question is not:

> `Was the voice ever loud?`

but:

- what was happening;
- what was the purpose;
- was the speech truthful;
- was it degrading;
- was the parent self-controlled;
- was the intensity proportionate to urgency;
- did the parent later repair any sinful excess?

### Locked

`LOUDNESS ≠ AUTOMATIC ABUSE`.

`LOUDNESS ≠ AUTOMATIC RIGHTEOUSNESS`.

## 11. Fear-based compliance vs obedience

A child can comply because:

- parent is watching;
- parent may explode;
- privileges are at risk;
- siblings may report;
- church reputation may collapse.

That compliance may be externally useful as restraint.

It is not thereby the inward obedience of faith or the fear of God.

Likewise, a child can resent a lawful command and still be obligated to obey it.

Therefore avoid both:

- `fear-based compliance proves the heart is changed`;
- `because motive is bad, parents should stop requiring the conduct`.

## 12. Repair after parental anger

When a parent sins in anger:

1. name the parent's sin specifically;
2. do not attach an excuse (`I yelled because you made me`);
3. preserve the truth of the child's sin if it remains true;
4. preserve lawful consequences if they remain just;
5. withdraw or correct any unjust threat;
6. distinguish forgiveness from cancellation of all household structure;
7. do not demand that the child immediately comfort the parent.

### Locked

> **PARENTAL REPENTANCE ≠ MORAL CAPITULATION.**

and

> **CHILD'S SIN ≠ PARENTAL IMMUNITY.**

Use `215` / `217` / `263`.
