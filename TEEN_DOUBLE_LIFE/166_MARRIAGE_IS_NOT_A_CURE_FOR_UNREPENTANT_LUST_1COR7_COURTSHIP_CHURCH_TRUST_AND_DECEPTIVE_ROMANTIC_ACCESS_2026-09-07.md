# 0. Why this gap matters

A church-raised teenager or young adult may be:

- intensely lust-driven;
- repeatedly using pornography;
- sexting;
- lying about sexual conduct;
- seeking romantic access inside church/youth circles.

Adults may react with a superficially biblical slogan:

> “Paul says it is better to marry than to burn. Maybe he just needs a wife.”

This can place another believer in the role of **treatment for someone else's protected sin**.

The opposite error also exists:

> “Marriage has no relationship at all to sexual desire; 1 Cor. 7:9 is irrelevant.”

### Governing thesis

> **Scripture truly presents marriage as the proper covenantal context for sexual union and, for those lacking the gift of continence, a legitimate remedy against fornication. But marriage is not a substitute for repentance, truthfulness, sanctification or self-control, and another person must never be recruited as therapy for defended pornography, deception or predatory conduct.**

# 1. 1 Corinthians 7:1–9 — marriage really is relevant to sexual desire

Primary:

- 1 Cor. 7:1–9.

Calvin:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom39.xiv.iii.html

Gill:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_corinthians/7.htm

Paul recognizes:

- not everyone has the same gift;
- marriage is legitimate/good;
- spouses have mutual sexual obligations;
- prolonged abstinence can create temptation;
- unmarried persons without continence may marry;
- “better to marry than to burn.”

Calvin explicitly calls marriage a remedy for unchastity in this context.

### Do not weaken the text

The final article should not react against bad marriage advice by pretending Paul sees no connection between lawful marriage and sexual temptation.

He plainly does.

---

# 2. But Paul's remedy is marriage, not “a spouse as behavior-management device”

Marriage is:

- covenantal union;
- mutual bodily obligation;
- exclusive sexual fidelity;
- service/love;
- a new household.

It is not:

> “Provide sexual access to a lustful person and the moral problem disappears.”

### Why this matters

A person who has trained himself in:

- secrecy;
- pornography;
- objectification;
- lying;
- coercion;
- entitlement

can carry those sins **into marriage**.

The existence of a lawful spouse does not itself mortify indwelling sin.

### Strong line

> **Marriage gives lust no right to fornication; it does not give lust a new heart.**

# 3. 1 Thessalonians 4:3–8 — sexual holiness concerns sanctification and the good of the other person

Primary:

- 1 Thess. 4:3–8.

Commentary control:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/1_thessalonians/4.htm

Paul frames sexual purity as:

- God's will: sanctification;
- abstaining from sexual immorality;
- possessing/controlling the body in holiness and honor rather than passion like those who do not know God;
- not transgressing/wronging another in this matter;
- God calling to holiness, not impurity.

### Project consequence

The potential spouse is not merely a means of managing the sinner's desire.

Sexual sin can **wrong another person**.

A future husband/wife deserves truthful, holy treatment rather than being inserted into a concealed pattern and told later:

> “You were supposed to fix me.”

# 4. 1 Corinthians 7:39 — “only in the Lord” and Christian romantic discernment

Primary:

- 1 Cor. 7:39.

Commentary:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_corinthians/7-39.htm

The immediate case concerns a widow free to remarry, “only in the Lord.”

Many interpreters understand this as marriage within Christian allegiance / to a Christian.

### Project use

Do not construct a universal dating manual from a widow verse.

But it strongly resists treating Christian marriage as religiously neutral.

### Relevance to false profession

If a church-raised youth uses a Christian facade to gain access to a believing spouse while knowingly rejecting Christ's authority, the deception is spiritually material.

---

# 5. The church must not certify a romantic candidate it does not actually affirm

A subtle failure occurs when everyone knows:

- he is living in defended serious sin;
- leaders do not presently regard his profession as credible;

but socially he is still introduced/treated as:

> “one of our good Christian boys.”

This can mislead:

- potential spouse;
- the spouse's parents;
- peers.

### Church duty

Without gossiping intimate details, leaders should avoid giving a false spiritual reference.

If directly asked appropriately:

> “Do you regard him as a Christian in good standing?”

leaders should not lie to protect appearances.

### Guard

Do not disclose private sexual details beyond legitimate need.

Use `165` confession/privacy controls.

---

# 6. Known deception in courtship is not merely “private sin”

If a youth knowingly tells a believing girl/boy:

- “I share your faith” while privately rejecting it;
- “pornography is behind me” while currently protecting the pattern;
- “leaders/parents approve my life” when they do not,

then the other person is making relational decisions on false information.

### Biblical categories

- Eph. 4:25 — put away falsehood, speak truth;
- 1 Thess. 4 — do not wrong/defraud in sexual matters;
- Matt. 7 / profession-life integrity themes.

### Strong line

> **A church must not protect one young person's privacy in a way that knowingly turns another young person's trust into the instrument of deception.**

This requires careful need-to-know judgment, not public exposure.

---

# 7. A pastor/parent must not arrange a marriage as discipline or treatment

Wrong logic:

> “He cannot control himself; let's find him a godly wife quickly.”

Problems:

- converts another person into a therapeutic resource;
- may conceal serious character issues;
- places enormous pressure on the spouse;
- can move hidden sexual entitlement inside a covenant with greater access and vulnerability.

### Better logic

If a mature Christian genuinely desires marriage and lacks the gift of singleness:

- marriage may be good;
- it may remove some illegitimate contexts of sexual temptation;
- premarital discipleship can address purity/truth.

But if the person is **settled, deceptive and unrepentant**, the immediate need is not faster marriage.

It is repentance and truth.

---

# 8. Marriage does not eliminate pornography temptation automatically

The project should not make a modern empirical claim here unless needed.

Biblically enough:

- indwelling sin remains after changes of circumstance;
- marriage creates legitimate sexual union but does not sanctify every desire automatically;
- spouses owe one another fidelity and love, not use.

### Strong line

> **A wedding changes what sexual relationship is lawful; it does not automatically change what the heart worships.**

---

# 9. What if a struggling, repentant young person wants marriage?

Do not create a new perfectionism:

> “Anyone who ever struggled with pornography can never marry.”

Wrong.

Questions include:

- Is there credible repentance?
- Is lying decreasing / truth increasing?
- Are safeguards voluntary?
- Is the prospective spouse being deceived about materially relevant facts?
- Is there present exploitation/coercion?
- Is the person seeking marriage as covenantal service or chiefly as sexual escape?

### Guard

No universal disclosure checklist is established here.

Material past/present facts require wise pastoral/counseling discernment; do not demand graphic detail.

---

# 10. Parents and leaders must not use romantic opportunity as reward for outward compliance

Bad system:

> “Behave like a Christian and you get access to Christian girls/boys.”

This can reinforce the exact double-life machinery:

- perform holiness;
- preserve social privileges;
- hide actual heart/conduct.

### Better culture

Teach:

- marriage as covenant before God;
- another person's dignity;
- truthfulness;
- chastity;
- patience;
- no entitlement to romance.

---

# 11. If the youth is actively corrupting or exploiting peers

Leaders may need to restrict:

- dating-oriented youth social access;
- private chats;
- overnight events;
- contact with specific vulnerable persons,

when facts/risk warrant it.

This is governed by `151`, `157`, safeguarding modules.

### Guard

Do not ban ordinary opposite-sex presence merely because a teenager experiences attraction.

Restriction follows concrete conduct/risk, not fear of sexuality itself.

# 12. Article-ready formulations

> **«Лучше вступить в брак, нежели разжигаться» — не значит «дайте нераскаянному человеку супруга, чтобы супруг лечил его похоть».**

> **Брак — Божье место для сексуальной близости, но не замена покаянию.**

> **Жена не является фильтром от порнографии, а муж — терапией от распущенности.**

> **Нельзя защищать приватность одного молодого человека так, чтобы другой вступал в серьёзные отношения на основании известной церкви лжи.**

> **Свадьба меняет законный контекст сексуальной близости; она не автоматически меняет объект поклонения сердца.**

# 13. P0 guards

Do not write:

- 1 Cor. 7:9 means marriage cures pornography;
- every sexually tempted teen should marry quickly;
- churches should disclose everyone's sexual history to potential partners;
- former pornography use permanently disqualifies marriage;
- 1 Cor. 7:39 alone is a complete dating policy;
- attraction itself equals corrupting conduct.

Do maintain:

- marriage as legitimate God-given sexual covenant;
- purity/truth before and within marriage;
- credible profession where Christian marriage is claimed;
- protection from deception;
- privacy proportionality;
- repentance before using another person as attempted solution.

# 14. Final locked synthesis

> **Павел действительно предлагает брак тем, кто не имеет дара воздержания, и этим нельзя пренебрегать. Но он предлагает брак — не человеческое тело как лекарство от нераскаянной похоти. Поэтому церковь не должна торопить обманывающего, защищающего порнографию или распутство молодого человека в брак под лозунгом 1 Кор. 7:9. Сначала нужно перестать лгать, перестать использовать других и обратиться к святости; иначе будущий супруг рискует стать следующей жертвой той же двойной жизни.**
