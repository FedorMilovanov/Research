# 0. Part II promise

Part II should answer:

> **If only God gives a new heart, what are parents actually responsible to do—and how can they combine serious formation, protection, discipline, truth and gospel hope without either abandoning an unregenerate child to the world or trying to control regeneration?**

Part II must avoid both:

### Fatalism

> `Only God saves, therefore upbringing and boundaries barely matter.`

### Parental sovereignty

> `If we design the perfect home, we can secure regeneration.`

Core Reformed synthesis:

> **God alone regenerates. God commands real means. Children remain responsible. Parents remain responsible for their own stewardship.**

---

# 1. Sovereign regeneration — John 3 / 1689 chapter 10

Biblical anchors:
- John 3:1–8;
- Eph. 2:1–10;
- Titus 3:3–7.

Confessional:
- 1689 LBCF chapter 10: https://the1689.org/chapters/10

### Direct theological claim

The new heart/new birth is God’s work, not a product of parental technique, external behavior modification or inherited Christian culture.

### Publication line

> **Parents can command a bedtime; they cannot command a new birth.**

Synthesis, not quotation.

### Guard

Do not move from divine sovereignty to practical fatalism.

The same God who regenerates sovereignly commands parents to teach, discipline, pray and bring truth to the child.

---

# 2. Deuteronomy 6 — formation belongs in ordinary life

Text:
- Deut. 6:4–9.

### Direct claim

Israelite parents are commanded to keep God’s words upon the heart and diligently teach them in the ordinary rhythms of life:
- sitting;
- walking;
- lying down;
- rising.

### Part II use

This is stronger than a model in which spiritual education is outsourced to:
- Sunday school;
- youth pastor;
- annual camp.

### Strong synthesis

> **Biblical formation is not one weekly religious appointment added to an otherwise secular family life.**

### Historical-grammatical guard

Do not turn Mosaic covenant household forms into an unqualified modern checklist. Transfer the enduring parental-teaching principle through the broader canonical witness.

---

# 3. Ephesians 6:1–4 — child duty and parent duty in the same paragraph

Text:
- Eph. 6:1–4.

### Child side

Children are directly commanded to obey/honour.

### Parent side

Fathers/parents are not given unlimited power; they must not provoke to anger but bring children up in the discipline and instruction/admonition of the Lord.

### Core Part II balance

> **Parental authority is real, and therefore parental abuse of authority is also morally real.**

### Strong line

> **The Bible does not solve permissiveness by sanctifying parental irritation.**

### Guard

`Do not provoke` does not mean:

> `Never impose a boundary that makes the child angry.`

Child anger is not itself proof of parental sin.

The question is whether authority is exercised:
- justly;
- proportionately;
- consistently;
- for the child’s good;
- without humiliating arbitrariness.

---

# 4. Colossians 3:20–21 — discipline must not crush

Text:
- Col. 3:20–21.

Parents must not embitter/provoke children lest they become discouraged.

### Part II use

This guards:
- constant criticism;
- impossible standards;
- moving goalposts;
- public humiliation;
- discipline driven by reputation panic.

### Equal guard

Do not infer that every serious correction `discourages` and is therefore forbidden.

Paul commands child obedience and parental non-provocation together.

# 5. No moral waiting room for the unregenerate child — 1689 chapter 19

Confessional source:
- https://the1689.org/chapters/19

### 1689 19.5

The moral law binds all people, justified and others, because of God the Creator’s authority.

### 1689 19.6

The law remains useful in exposing sin and directing duty, though it cannot justify.

### Core thesis

> **Unregeneration explains inability to produce saving obedience from a fallen heart; it does not produce innocence or suspend duty.**

### Therefore reject

> `He is not converted, so let him live however he wants until God saves him.`

### Equal rejection

> `We will train him to perform Christian behavior and call the performance conversion.`

Use law and gospel distinctly.

# 6. Proverbs 22:6 — wisdom pattern, not mechanical salvation guarantee

Text:
- Prov. 22:6.

### Project use

Treat as a genuine wisdom statement about formative direction, not:
- empty proverb with no practical force;
- covenant guarantee that faithful parents cannot have wayward adult children.

### Safe synthesis

> **Training matters because formation matters; Proverbs does not turn parenting into sovereign election.**

### Guard

Do not weaponize Prov. 22:6 against grieving parents as proof that every prodigal demonstrates parental failure.

# 7. Baxter — family government is active, not prayer-only passivity

Primary sources from module 48:
- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43800/43800-h/43800-h.htm
- https://ccel.org/ccel/baxter/practical.toc.html
- https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6?rgn=div1%3Bview%3Dfulltext

### Best Part II uses

Baxter treats children/youth as morally accountable according to immature capacity and repeatedly urges parents to:
- instruct;
- watch;
- shape company;
- correct;
- take filthy speech seriously;
- provide better formative environments where possible.

### Strong historical contrast

Baxter feared corrupt peer company centuries before smartphones.

Therefore:

> **The internet did not invent corrupt companionship; it can remove the old closing hour.**

Synthesis.

### Guard

Baxter can rhetorically overstate parental causal power. Use him against passivity, not as deterministic proof that faithful upbringing always succeeds.

# 8. Know the child you actually have — Owen, *Of Temptation*

Primary sources from module 46:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.v.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.vii.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.viii.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/owen/temptation.i.x.html

### Owen’s useful categories

Know:
- natural frame/temper;
- peculiar lusts;
- weaknesses;
- companies;
- occasions;
- conditions that give a corruption special advantage.

### Major parenting synthesis

> **Equal love does not require identical exposure.**

Two children may legitimately receive different freedoms where:
- one has demonstrated reliable self-government;
- another has a repeatedly proven weakness in the same setting.

### Guard

Do not turn individual discernment into favoritism or arbitrary inequality.

Explain the morally relevant reason for differing boundaries where appropriate.

# 9. Graded responsibility — neither bubble nor exposure-as-training

Biblical framework:
- wisdom literature;
- Eph. 6;
- Owen on occasions;
- Matt. 5 radicality.

### Reject extreme A

> complete lifelong insulation from every difficult idea/person.

### Reject extreme B

> expose the child to pornography, predation or known corrupting environments so he becomes strong.

### Better model

> **Expand freedom with demonstrated readiness; do not call intrinsically corrupting exposure a training exercise.**

### Strong distinction

Learning to navigate disagreement is legitimate exposure.

Being deliberately exposed to pornography is not character training.

---

# 10. Matthew 5:29–30 — radical removal without literalism or abuse

Text:
- Matt. 5:27–30.

Supporting:
- 1 Cor. 6:18;
- 2 Tim. 2:22;
- Rom. 13:14.

### Direct moral force

Jesus’ language demands radical seriousness toward sexual sin.

### Parent/application level

Possible case-specific applications:
- phone outside bedroom;
- app removal;
- loss of private browser after proven misuse;
- device curfew;
- ending access to a corrupting chat/account;
- declining a trip/sleepover strongly tied to repeated sin.

### Required classification

These are **prudential applications**, not direct translations of `pluck out the eye`.

### RED

Never use Matt. 5 to justify:
- self-harm;
- physical abuse;
- humiliating destruction;
- arbitrary maximal punishment.

---

# 11. Owen — prevention begins before the explicit site is open

Owen’s central `watch and pray` framework moves self-government upstream:

`know weakness`  
→ `notice season/company/opportunity`  
→ `avoid entry`  
→ `pray/watch`.

### Strong Part II line

> **Self-control often begins hours before the moment we usually call “the temptation.”**

For parents this means:
- do not wait until the explicit content is already open before discussing access;
- build habits/settings before crisis;
- treat known occasion as morally relevant.

### Guard

The occasion is not identical to the sin.

Restrict because of demonstrated risk/wise prevention, not because future guilt is assumed with omniscience.

---

# 12. Strong rules must have intelligible moral purpose

Part II should explicitly distinguish:

### Boundary

A restriction connected to:
- safety;
- known temptation;
- demonstrated misuse;
- maturity;
- household stewardship.

### Arbitrary control

A demand primarily serving:
- parental fear;
- reputation;
- domination;
- humiliation;
- refusal to allow age-appropriate growth.

### Strong line

> **A boundary should be able to answer the question: “What good are we guarding, and what danger has made this restriction necessary?”**

Synthesis.

# 13. Build a truth channel before discovery

Biblical background:
- Deut. 6 ordinary-life teaching;
- Prov. parental instruction;
- Heb. 3 mutual exhortation principle;
- 1 John 1 light/truth.

### Parent practices

Before scandal:
- speak explicitly about lust/pornography/sexual speech;
- make questions possible without immediate panic;
- tell child what boundaries exist and why;
- model confession/apology as a parent;
- distinguish temptation from chosen sin;
- invite help **before** the fall.

### Core distinction

`caught`  
≠ `confessed`  
≠ `voluntarily disclosed`  
≠ `asked for help before falling`.

### Strong line

> **Voluntary accountability attacks secrecy as a strategy, not merely one detected episode.**

---

# 14. Trust does not mean immunity from consequences

Reject:

> `If you tell me the truth, nothing bad will happen.`

That trains confession as an immunity card.

Also reject:

> `Truth will be punished so savagely that successful concealment is obviously safer.`

### Better parental posture

> **The truth will not make me stop loving you. But the truth may show that some freedom is not safe for you right now.**

### Core formula

Love is not on probation.

Freedom can be evidence-sensitive.

---

# 15. Discovery — first classify the case before giving the sermon

The first response should distinguish at least:

1. **safety/victimization** — coercion, sextortion, abuse, self-harm risk?
2. **facts** — what is actually known?
3. **voluntary conduct** — what did the child choose?
4. **others** — did the child distribute/corrupt/pressure others?
5. **access** — what immediate avenue still needs to be secured?
6. **spiritual posture** — defense, fear, confession, help-seeking?

### Strong principle

> **Do not make a moral lecture so fast that you fail to notice the child is also being extorted or abused.**

### Equal guard

Victimization does not erase responsibility for a distinct voluntary sinful act that preceded it.

Keep moral categories separate.

---

# 16. Parental investigation — truth seeking without interrogation frenzy

Parents have legitimate authority to establish facts for:
- safety;
- household discipline;
- protection of others.

But discovery can trigger uncontrolled surveillance that becomes:
- humiliating;
- speculative;
- endless;
- unrelated to the actual risk.

### Part II principle

> **Seek enough truth to act faithfully; do not confuse exhaustive knowledge with faithful parenting.**

### Guard

Minors and adults differ materially in privacy/jurisdiction; adult-child surveillance belongs in the companion article, not here.

# 17. Being caught is not repentance — 2 Corinthians 7 + Watson

Biblical center:
- 2 Cor. 7:10–11;
- Prov. 28:13;
- 1 John 1.

Thomas Watson primary sources:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/commandments.vi.ii.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson/prayer.viii.html

### Watson’s highly useful false positives

Not sufficient by themselves:
- tears;
- terror/fear;
- crisis sorrow;
- good intentions;
- vows;
- leaving one sin while protecting another.

### Strong line

> **A vow made while the fire alarm is ringing is not yet proof of what the heart will love when the house becomes quiet again.**

Project synthesis.

### Guard

Do not despise tears or fear; they can accompany real conviction. They simply do not prove repentance by themselves.

# 18. Repentance changes the relationship to the sin

Watson + 2 Cor. 7 / Prov. 28.

Observable direction may include:
- calls sin what God calls it;
- stops defending/minimizing;
- confesses beyond detected facts;
- accepts proportionate consequences;
- removes access/occasion;
- seeks help;
- makes restitution where applicable;
- does not recruit others;
- demonstrates change beyond immediate crisis.

### Key distinction

> **Temptation may remain strong while the person’s allegiance toward the sin changes from protection to warfare.**

### Guard

This is not a rigid salvation checklist and not proof of election.

# 19. Struggling sinner vs settled protected rebellion

Keep the project’s central contrast.

### Struggle/repentance pattern

- truth;
- warfare;
- confession;
- help-seeking;
- surrender of access;
- grief over sin;
- returning after failure.

### Settled protected pattern

- planned concealment;
- defending sin;
- manipulating adults;
- building loopholes;
- mocking correction;
- recruiting/corrupting others;
- using church persona as cover.

### Core warning

Do not comfort settled rebellion with:

> `Christians struggle too.`

Do not crush the struggling believer with:

> `If you fell again, nothing was ever real.`

# 20. Parents also come into the light

Biblical anchors:
- Eph. 6:4;
- Col. 3:21;
- Matt. 7:3–5 as general self-examination guard, handled contextually;
- James 5:16 where appropriate.

Potential real parent sins:
- hypocrisy;
- pornography/sexual hypocrisy;
- rage;
- neglect;
- absenteeism;
- reputation idolatry;
- favoritism;
- inconsistent discipline;
- refusal to apologize.

### Required balance

If a parent sinned:
- confess actual sin;
- seek forgiveness;
- change conduct.

But do not say:

> `Because I sinned as a parent, my child is no longer accountable for his chosen sexual/deceptive sin.`

### Strong line

> **Parental repentance tells the truth about the parent; it does not rewrite the moral agency of the child.**

# 21. Spurgeon — no inherited conversion, no delayed gospel

Primary sources from module 53:
- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/sermons10.xviii.html

### Best Part II use

Spurgeon rejects:
- automatic conversion by Christian family inheritance;
- careless interpretation of child emotion as saving faith;
- waiting until adulthood to seek real child conversion.

### Core synthesis

> **Do not presume regeneration because the child knows the culture; do not postpone the call to repentance because he is young.**

# 22. What parents cannot promise

Do not promise:
- `Follow these steps and your child will convert.`
- `A strong filter prevents sexual sin.`
- `A close relationship guarantees disclosure.`
- `Perfect parenting prevents apostasy.`
- `If the child rebels, your parenting caused it.`

### What they can say

> `God has given us duties, not sovereignty over the outcome.`

That is the theological center of Part II.

---

# 23. Positive replacement — do not make holiness only subtraction

Part II should not become:

`remove phone / remove app / remove friend / remove door / remove privacy`.

Biblical formation also includes:
- Scripture;
- prayer;
- worship;
- work;
- skill;
- service;
- exercise/embodied life;
- family conversation;
- good friends;
- mentors;
- ordinary lawful joys.

### Important theology

Restrictions can remove occasions.

They cannot create love for God.

But a well-governed home should offer something more substantial than constant defensive policing.

# 27. Final drafting theorem

> **Christian parents are neither saviors nor spectators. They cannot give the child a new heart, but they are commanded to teach, govern, protect, know, warn, pray, model repentance and remove serious occasions of sin with wisdom. Their authority must not become tyranny; their love must not become indulgence. When hidden sin is exposed, they must distinguish safety from guilt, being caught from repentance, and a struggling sinner from protected rebellion. Then, having used the means God gave them, they must entrust to God the work He never gave them: raising the spiritually dead.**
