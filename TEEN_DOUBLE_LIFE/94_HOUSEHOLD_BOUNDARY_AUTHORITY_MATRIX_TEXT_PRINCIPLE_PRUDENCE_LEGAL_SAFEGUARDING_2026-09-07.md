# Adult Child in the Parental Home — Authority Matrix
## TEXT → PRINCIPLE → PRUDENCE → LEGAL/SAFEGUARDING

**Status:** CONTROL MATRIX / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07

Purpose: prevent future prose from presenting every conservative household rule as though Scripture states it verbatim.

---

# 1. Why this matrix is necessary

The user wants a serious answer to questions such as:

- may an openly sexually immoral adult child remain at home?
- what if he/she brings sexual partners into the house?
- what if the child refuses work and spends parents’ money?
- what if the child corrupts younger siblings?
- when may parents require separate residence?
- what conditions may be attached to financial help?

These are real pastoral questions.

But Scripture usually supplies:
- moral norms;
- household/family duties;
- principles of work, sexual holiness, stewardship and protection;

rather than a modern lease agreement.

Therefore classify every claim.

---

# 2. LEVEL 1 — TEXT / direct moral norm

These claims may be stated strongly.

## Sexual immorality

Texts:
- 1 Cor. 6:9–20;
- Eph. 5:3–7;
- Heb. 13:4;
- 1 Thess. 4:3–8.

Direct claim:

> Sexual immorality is not morally neutral because the person is 18+ or unbelieving.

## Drunkenness/debauchery

Texts:
- Eph. 5:18;
- 1 Cor. 6:10;
- Prov. 23:20–35.

Direct claim:

> Parents need not call drunkenness acceptable adult self-expression.

## Theft/deceit

Texts:
- Eph. 4:25,28;
- Col. 3:9;
- Ex. 20:15–16.

Direct claim:

> Adult status does not create a right to steal from or systematically deceive the household.

## Work / deliberate idleness

Texts:
- 2 Thess. 3:6–12;
- Eph. 4:28;
- Prov. 6:6–11.

Direct claim:

> Scripture condemns able-bodied deliberate idleness and commands productive responsibility.

## Fifth-commandment family duty

Texts:
- Ex. 20:12;
- Mark 7:9–13;
- 1 Tim. 5:4,8;
- Eph. 6:1–3.

Direct claim:

> Maturity changes the form of subjection but does not make contempt for parents a virtue.

## Parent must not prefer child above God

Texts:
- 1 Sam. 2:29;
- Matt. 10:37.

Direct claim:

> Fear of losing a child must not require a parent to bless what God condemns.

# 3. LEVEL 2 — PRINCIPLE / strong biblical synthesis

These claims are not usually one-verse rules, but they follow strongly from multiple texts.

## A. A household is not morally ownerless

The person(s) responsible for a household may establish lawful conditions for its common life.

Biblical background:
- household stewardship;
- parental responsibility;
- property stewardship;
- order rather than disorder;
- protection of vulnerable members.

### Allowed claim

> Parents may require adult residents to live by morally lawful household rules.

### Guard

Do not imply parents can command sin or exercise total ownership over an adult conscience.

---

## B. Help need not be unconditional

Biblical background:
- stewardship;
- work responsibility;
- wisdom about company;
- refusal to participate in evil;
- concern for genuine good rather than immediate appeasement.

### Allowed claim

> Financial or residential help may be conditioned on conduct directly relevant to the safe and moral use of that help.

Example:

`You may stay here while seeking work, but not while using the house to host sexual encounters or distribute pornography.`

This is a prudential application of moral stewardship, not a verbatim biblical sentence.

---

## C. Parents need not subsidize known rebellion

### Allowed claim

> Love does not impose a duty to provide resources reasonably known to be functioning as infrastructure for serious sin.

Examples:
- cash repeatedly used for drugs;
- vehicle repeatedly used for criminal exploitation;
- parental home deliberately used for sexual encounters against household rules;
- devices/accounts being used to target younger children.

### Guard

Do not claim parents can know every future use of money or are guilty for all misuse after ordinary good-faith support.

---

## D. Protection of other household members is a real duty

If an adult child is:
- sexually targeting minors;
- distributing pornography;
- violent;
- threatening;
- stealing;
- bringing dangerous people/substances into the house;
- deliberately teaching younger children deception,

parents must consider duties to **all** household members, not only the wayward adult.

### Strong line

> **One adult child’s demand for unrestricted access cannot automatically outrank the parents’ duty to protect younger children.**

---

# 4. LEVEL 3 — PRUDENCE / case-specific application

These may be wise but should normally be written with `may`, `sometimes`, `depending on circumstances`.

Examples:

- charge rent;
- require contribution to food/utilities;
- require active job search;
- set curfew for use of shared property;
- prohibit overnight sexual partners;
- keep illegal drugs out of the house;
- remove access to shared vehicle;
- stop providing unrestricted cash;
- provide groceries rather than cash;
- require separate digital network/device conditions if minors are at risk;
- require treatment/counsel where there is a concrete safety/addiction issue;
- require separate residence after persistent serious violation.

### Editorial language

Good:

> `In some cases parents may need to require separate residence.`

Bad:

> `The Bible commands every parent to evict an immoral 18-year-old.`

---

# 5. LEVEL 4 — LEGAL / SAFEGUARDING

Some questions cannot be answered solely as private family discipline.

Examples:
- eviction law;
- tenancy rights;
- domestic violence;
- child sexual abuse;
- possession/distribution of illegal sexual images;
- extortion;
- threats;
- stalking;
- drug crimes;
- emergency mental-health danger.

### Project rule

If publication gives operational advice in these areas:
- verify jurisdiction;
- do not advise illegal self-help eviction;
- do not tell parents to conceal crime in the name of church/family restoration;
- emergency protection can take priority over ordinary relational cadence.

---

# 6. Returning child — separate hospitality from restored trust

A returning child may need:
- food;
- temporary shelter;
- medical care;
- transport;
- conversation;

before there is enough evidence for:
- unrestricted money;
- unsupervised access to minors;
- keys to every room/property;
- vehicle access;
- ministry roles;
- digital privileges involving younger children.

### Key distinction

> **Mercy can be immediate while some forms of trust remain evidence-sensitive.**

This must never become:

> `prove yourself worthy of grace`.

Grace/forgiveness and stewardship/trust answer different questions.

---

# 7. Repentant but high-risk history

A real repentance claim should be welcomed seriously, not cynically.

But where conduct involved:
- predation;
- coercion;
- abuse;
- repeated theft;
- violence;
- deliberate corruption of minors,

restored affection/fellowship does not automatically answer:

> `What access is wise today?`

### Rule

> **Do not demand omniscient proof of the heart; assess the observable stewardship question before you.**

---

# 8. Non-Christian adult child

Unbelief does not create a moral vacuum.

Therefore parents may say:

> `I know you do not profess Christ. I am not asking you to fake conversion. But this house still cannot be used for pornography distribution / sexual exploitation / drunken violence / theft.`

This avoids both errors:

1. forcing a false Christian profession;
2. acting as though God’s moral law disappears until conversion.

---

# 9. Possible household policy structure

Not a divine statute; sample prudential framework only.

### Essential conditions
- no violence/threats;
- no illegal conduct;
- no sexual exploitation;
- no pornography distribution to others/minors;
- no deliberate corruption of younger household members;
- no theft;
- no sexual partners using the family home as a place for immorality;
- respect for shared property/basic household order.

### Responsibility conditions where appropriate
- work/study/job search;
- contribution to household;
- agreed financial plan;
- transparent use of specific parental resources where prior abuse is proven.

### Restoration conditions
- clear route to re-evaluate restrictions;
- no arbitrary moving target;
- distinguish consequences from humiliation;
- do not demand theatrical religiosity as rent payment.

---

# 10. Final audit formulas

> **A parent may own the house without owning the adult child’s conscience.**

> **An adult child may own his decisions without acquiring a right to use someone else’s home as infrastructure for them.**

> **Not every consequence is revenge; not every rescue is mercy.**

> **Household holiness is not regeneration. External order cannot give a new heart—but the absence of a new heart does not require a Christian home to become morally neutral.**

> **When Scripture gives a principle, say “principle.” When wisdom supplies an application, say “may.” When law governs the action, check the law.**
