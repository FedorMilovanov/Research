## 0. The question

Conservative churches can make a serious category error when they take a biblical text governing **church reconciliation/discipline** and make it function as a complete protocol for:

- suspected crime;
- child sexual abuse;
- grooming;
- assault;
- evidence preservation;
- civil investigation;
- mandated reporting;
- immediate danger;
- institutional safeguarding.

The opposite error also exists: once police/civil authorities are involved, a church may act as though it has no remaining responsibility for:

- membership;
- the Table;
- pastoral admonition;
- ministry/platform access;
- safeguarding restrictions;
- repentance/restoration.

### Governing thesis

> **Church jurisdiction and civil jurisdiction are distinct God-ordained spheres. A serious case may belong to both at the same time.**

Locked formula:

`CIVIL REPORT / INVESTIGATION ≠ ECCLESIAL VERDICT`

and

`ECCLESIAL DISCIPLINE ≠ SUBSTITUTE FOR CIVIL JUSTICE`.

A church must not become a private police department. A police outcome must not become the church's entire theology of repentance, membership, trust, or ministry fitness.

# 1. The 1689 Baptist Confession gives civil government a real, positive jurisdiction

## 1.1 Chapter 24 is not optional background

1689 LBCF ch. 24.1 says God ordained civil magistrates under Him for His glory and the public good, and armed them with the power of the sword for:

- defense/encouragement of those who do good;
- punishment of evildoers.

Primary/confessional text:
- https://1689baptistconfession.com/confession/24-of-the-civil-magistrate/
- https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-24
- historical-text mirror: https://www.ccel.org/creeds/bcf/bcfc24.htm

Its proof text is Romans 13:1–4.

### P0

A Particular Baptist church cannot coherently say:

> “Because this is a church matter, civil authority has no God-given role in criminal wrongdoing.”

That claim collides directly with the confession's separate civil-magistrate doctrine.

## 1.2 Chapter 26 does not swallow chapter 24

The confession's church chapter establishes real church authority, ordinances, discipline, officers, and communion.

But church power is **ecclesiastical**, not the civil sword.

### Confessional synthesis

> **The same confessional system that gives the church keys also gives the magistrate the sword.**

Do not use one chapter to erase the other.

# 2. Romans 13:1–7 — civil authority exists in part to restrain and punish public evil

Romans 13:4 calls the civil ruler God's servant/ministry for good and an avenger who bears the sword against wrongdoing.

## 2.1 Calvin

Calvin says magistrates are appointed for public good and armed to repress wrongdoing and inflict punishment; private persons receive protection from injuries through this God-ordained civil function.

Primary commentary:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom38.xvii.ii.html
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/romans/13.htm

Calvin also distinguishes ecclesiastical and civil power in the *Institutes*: the church has no right to the civil sword, prison, or coercive punishments of the magistrate.

Primary source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.vi.xii.html

## 2.2 Gill

Gill explicitly says Romans 13's “higher powers” are **civil magistrates**, not elders/pastors; church officers do not bear the temporal sword or wield corporeal civil punishment.

Primary source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.iv.html

### P0

> **Pastoral authority is not a private substitute for the magistrate's criminal jurisdiction.**

A pastor may admonish, investigate ecclesial fitness, protect church access, and shepherd. He does not thereby acquire police powers, criminal evidentiary jurisdiction, or the right to suppress lawful civil process.

# 3. John Gill gives a direct Particular Baptist limit on Matthew 18

Gill's *Body of Practical Divinity*, chapter on church discipline, is one of the strongest primary sources for this project.

Primary source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical.iii.vi.html

## 3.1 Gill: Matthew 18 is a private-offense rule

Gill says Matt. 18:15–17 is a rule for a **private trespass**, a secret fault known initially to the parties.

He explicitly distinguishes it from:

- public sin known to church/world;
- atrocious public crimes;
- notorious heresies;
- cases requiring a different and more immediate ecclesial response.

### P0

This alone blocks the simplistic formula:

> `EVERY SERIOUS SIN → PRIVATE ONE-ON-ONE CONFRONTATION FIRST`.

That is not Gill's Baptist reading of the text.

## 3.2 Gill: excommunication itself does not interfere with civil magistrate business

In the same treatment, Gill says excommunication:

- does not affect temporal estate/civil affairs;
- does not impose fines/imprisonment/death;
- **does not interfere with the business of the civil magistrate**;
- does not erase natural/civil relations.

### Locked Particular Baptist synthesis

> **Ecclesial discipline and civil justice are not rivals. Gill expressly limits church discipline to its own sphere.**

This is especially useful because it prevents the project from depending only on modern abuse-prevention ministries for the jurisdiction distinction.

# 4. Matthew 18:15–20 — what the text clearly gives and what it does not give

## 4.1 What it clearly gives

Within the disciple/community context, Jesus gives an escalating process aimed at gaining a sinning brother:

1. private approach;
2. one or two others if he will not listen;
3. tell the church;
4. changed ecclesial treatment if he refuses the church.

The process protects against:

- gossip;
- needless publicity;
- immediate mob judgment;
- failure to seek repentance/reconciliation;
- unilateral escalation of ordinary private grievances.

## 4.2 What it does not say

The text does not say:

- suspected crimes may not be reported to civil authority until all steps finish;
- a child must privately confront an alleged abuser;
- the victim must investigate the offense personally;
- civil evidence must be gathered by church members first;
- immediate safety action must wait;
- a reporter must remain alone with the accused;
- the accused must be warned before evidence/safety is secured;
- mandatory-reporting law is suspended by church membership.

### P0

> **Do not turn a reconciliation/discipline passage into a gag order.**

## 4.3 Modern conservative exegetical corroboration

D. A. Carson has warned against treating Matthew 18 as a universal procedure for every public or serious wrong.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/editorial-on-abusing-matthew-18/

Covenant Baptist Theological Seminary similarly distinguishes private sins from public/grievous sins in discussing church discipline.

Source:
- https://content.cbtseminary.org/church-discipline-the-way-of-forgiveness/

TGC Canada explicitly notes criminal conduct as a case where reporting to police is not delayed for the Matthew 18 sequence.

Source:
- https://ca.thegospelcoalition.org/columns/ad-fontes/5-situations-matthew-18-not-apply/

### Authority guard

These modern sources are corroborative/position-map sources. Gill + Scripture + confessional jurisdiction remain stronger controls for this project's doctrinal argument.

# 5. 1 Corinthians 6 — believer lawsuits are not a blanket ban on civil criminal process

1 Cor. 6 rebukes believers for taking disputes with one another before unbelieving tribunals instead of handling matters appropriately among the saints.

The text is serious and must not be neutralized.

But a common misuse expands it into:

> “Christians must never involve civil authorities when another professing Christian commits a crime.”

That conclusion conflicts with Romans 13 and the confession's magistrate doctrine.

## 5.1 Required distinction

`PRIVATE / CIVIL DISPUTE BETWEEN BELIEVERS`

is not automatically identical to

`ALLEGED CRIME AGAINST A CHILD OR OTHER PERSON`.

The church can be ashamed when Christians weaponize courts for petty self-interest **and** still recognize the magistrate's God-given criminal/public-order jurisdiction.

## 5.2 Publication guard

Do not overstate the opposite direction either.

1 Cor. 6 does not contain a modern penal-code taxonomy or an explicit sentence:

> “Every criminal allegation must be reported in exactly this way.”

The positive reporting/legal duty must be established from:

- Romans 13/civil jurisdiction;
- relevant safeguarding duty;
- current civil law;
- immediate-protection duties.

### P0

> **1 Corinthians 6 cannot be used to erase Romans 13. Romans 13 cannot be used to trivialize Christian duties of reconciliation and church discipline.**

# 6. “Two or three witnesses” — evidence control is not a silence-before-intake rule

This topic requires exceptional care because several texts use witness language:

- Deut. 19:15;
- Matt. 18:16;
- 2 Cor. 13:1;
- 1 Tim. 5:19.

They must not be flattened into one modern procedural slogan.

## 6.1 Deuteronomy 19:15

The Mosaic judicial text protects against establishing serious legal guilt on a single witness alone.

The principle is important:

- accusations matter;
- evidence matters;
- false testimony is serious;
- punishment should not be based on careless assertion.

But an **evidentiary threshold for establishing guilt** is not identical to:

> “One disclosure cannot be received, documented, referred, or investigated.”

If no inquiry can begin until two already-formed witnesses exist, many hidden offenses could never be responsibly examined.

## 6.2 Matthew 18:16

The one/two additional persons in Matt. 18 serve within the escalating private-offense process. The verse cites witness language, but its immediate function must be read in that process.

Do not transform it into a universal criminal intake rule.

## 6.3 1 Timothy 5:19 — elder accusations deserve careful evidentiary protection

Paul says an accusation against an elder should not be received except on the evidence/support of two or three witnesses.

This is especially important because elder office creates both:

- vulnerability to malicious accusations;
- serious institutional power if wrongdoing is real.

### Lexical/procedural HOLD

There is debate about exactly how the witness phrase should operate procedurally and what counts as corroborating witness/evidence in modern application.

Therefore Product must **not** state without a dedicated Greek/exegetical pass:

- `1 Tim 5:19 requires two eyewitnesses to the same act`;
- `a single complainant may not be heard`;
- `a safeguarding report may not be passed to authorities`;
- `the church must publicly believe every allegation immediately`.

### Safe P0

> **A standard for establishing/adjudicating a charge is not the same thing as a prohibition on receiving a concern and seeking lawful corroboration.**

TGC's pastoral treatment of elder moral failure makes a similar practical distinction by calling leaders to seek corroborating evidence/witnesses after an allegation rather than requiring the complainant to arrive with a completed case.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/moral-failings-pastor/

### Evidence-policy note

This modern article is `B1/C position-map`, not the basis for the Greek conclusion. The Greek/procedural detail remains `LOCATOR/EXEGESIS_HOLD` until separately audited.

# 7. Reporting is not the same as publicly declaring guilt

A major cultural confusion is the binary:

`BELIEVE VICTIM PUBLICLY WITHOUT PROCESS`

vs

`PROTECT ACCUSED BY KEEPING IT INTERNAL`.

Biblical truthfulness requires better categories.

## 7.1 A safeguarding/civil report can mean

> “A serious allegation/concern has been disclosed; competent authorities need to assess/investigate it.”

It need not mean:

> “The church has already finally adjudicated every fact and publicly condemned the accused.”

## 7.2 Presumption against reckless accusation remains

The church must resist:

- gossip;
- social-media trial;
- embellishment;
- false certainty;
- retaliatory leaks;
- using a report as permission for public character destruction before facts are established.

## 7.3 Safety can require interim restrictions before final adjudication

Where credible risk exists, prudent interim measures may include:

- no unsupervised access to minors;
- temporary removal from child/youth role;
- supervised contact;
- preservation of relevant records;
- separation of involved minors;
- suspension of high-trust responsibilities.

### P0

> **Interim safeguarding is not identical to a final declaration of guilt.**

Likewise:

> **Due process is not identical to unrestricted access while facts are investigated.**

# 8. Church, police, safeguarding professionals, and parents ask different questions

A single case may involve several authorities.

## Civil authority may ask

- was a law broken?
- what evidence exists?
- what criminal/civil procedure applies?
- is immediate protection/order required?

## Church may ask

- is there credible sin/scandal requiring ecclesial action?
- is the person repentant?
- may the person remain in membership/Table according to polity?
- is ministry/platform/trusted access appropriate?
- what pastoral care is needed?

## Parents may ask

- how do we protect our child/siblings?
- what household access changes now?
- what truth do we need to know?
- what medical/mental-health support is needed?

## Safeguarding professionals may ask

- who is at risk now?
- what contact restrictions are needed?
- what developmental/power/coercion factors matter?
- how do we avoid contaminating evidence or retraumatizing a child?

### Locked formula

> **One authority's decision does not automatically answer every other authority's question.**

Examples:

- `NO CRIMINAL CHARGE` does not necessarily equal `FIT FOR CHILDREN'S MINISTRY`;
- `CHURCH REPENTANCE CLAIM ACCEPTED` does not equal `CIVIL CASE CLOSED`;
- `POLICE INVESTIGATION OPEN` does not by itself prove ecclesial guilt;
- `NOT GUILTY BEYOND REASONABLE DOUBT` does not automatically create a right to unrestricted high-trust church role where safeguarding facts remain concerning;
- `VICTIM FORGIVES` does not end the magistrate's public-interest jurisdiction.

# 9. Immediate danger changes the sequence

If a child/person is in immediate danger, the first pastoral question is not:

> “Have you completed step one of Matthew 18?”

It is:

> **How do we stop imminent harm and involve the proper protective authority?**

Possible categories:

- threatened violence;
- active sexual abuse;
- adult grooming a minor;
- imminent meeting with exploiter;
- credible suicide/self-harm emergency tied to sextortion;
- child sexual images actively circulating;
- kidnapping/unlawful confinement;
- severe domestic violence.

### P0

Emergency protection is not revenge and not an ecclesial shortcut. It answers a different stewardship question.

Exact emergency/reporting actions depend on current jurisdiction and must be verified at publication/use time.

---

# 10. Do not conduct a homemade forensic interview

One danger of church-first handling is that well-intentioned adults can contaminate evidence or intensify trauma by repeatedly questioning the child.

Official safeguarding guidance commonly emphasizes:

- listen;
- avoid leading/probing questions;
- do not promise absolute confidentiality;
- record accurately;
- pass concerns through appropriate channels.

Ofsted/GOV.UK example:
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ofsted-safeguarding-policy/safeguarding-concerns-guidance-for-inspectors

### Church application

Pastor/youth leader should not say:

> “Tell me every graphic detail so I can decide whether this is serious enough.”

The needed standard is:

> **Enough truthful information to protect/refer; not amateur interrogation.**

See also `165_CONFESSION_SCOPE...`.

---

# 11. Confidentiality: bounded, truthful, and role-specific

A church leader should not promise:

> “Whatever you tell me will never leave this room.”

if the content may involve:

- child abuse;
- serious danger;
- criminal conduct;
- risk to others;
- mandatory reporting.

Better:

> “I will not spread this. I will tell only the people who need to know in order to protect you and act responsibly, and I will explain what I can about the next step.”

### P0

`CONFIDENTIALITY ≠ SECRECY PACT`.

But the opposite also matters:

`SAFEGUARDING REPORT ≠ LICENSE FOR CONGREGATIONAL GOSSIP`.

Need-to-know discipline remains a Christian duty.

---

# 12. Church investigation and civil investigation can run in parallel—but must not sabotage each other

Potential collision points:

- accused warned before evidence secured;
- church persuades witnesses to reconcile before statement;
- multiple leaders interview child repeatedly;
- records deleted in “repentance cleanup”;
- public discipline announced in a way that contaminates active proceedings;
- police delay causes church to leave unrestricted ministry access indefinitely;
- church waits for criminal conviction before doing any safeguarding.

### Better operating principle

> **Coordinate without collapsing jurisdictions.**

When a criminal/safeguarding investigation is active, church leaders should seek competent legal/safeguarding advice about what internal fact-finding can safely proceed.

### P0

No universal timing formula belongs in a generic theological article.

---

# 13. A civil “not guilty” or no-charge outcome does not mechanically determine church trust

Criminal courts often operate under high proof standards and defined criminal elements.

A church's high-trust role decision asks a different prudential question:

> Is unsupervised access to vulnerable persons wise and safe given all responsibly established facts?

### Guard

This must not become:

> “If police did not convict, church leaders may invent guilt from rumor.”

The church still needs evidence, fairness, and clear reasons.

### Locked distinction

`CRIMINAL LIABILITY`

≠

`ECCLESIAL MEMBERSHIP JUDGMENT`

≠

`MINISTRY FITNESS`

≠

`SAFEGUARDING ACCESS`.

These may overlap factually while remaining different judgments.

---

# 14. A criminal conviction does not by itself prove repentance is impossible

The opposite collapse is equally wrong.

A convicted offender may genuinely repent and be forgiven in Christ.

But:

`FORGIVENESS / REPENTANCE`

still does not equal

`RIGHT TO RETURN TO THE SAME TRUSTED ROLE`.

Some role/access restrictions can be extremely long-term or permanent because safeguarding stewardship asks about risk, vulnerability, and trust—not the sufficiency of Christ's atonement.

### P0

> **Permanent role restriction can coexist with genuine spiritual restoration.**

This should be especially explicit around children/youth.

---

# 15. The accused also remains a neighbor who must not be falsely condemned

Victim-centered safeguarding does not authorize false witness.

Christian duties include:

- accurately distinguish allegation from established fact;
- do not exaggerate;
- document what was actually said;
- avoid retaliatory publication;
- correct false statements;
- protect against mob justice;
- if an allegation is found knowingly false, address that wrongdoing too.

### P0

The project must never choose between:

`PROTECT VULNERABLE`

and

`DO NOT BEAR FALSE WITNESS`.

It must do both.

---

# 16. The reporter must not be punished for using a legitimate safety channel

A dangerous conservative-culture pattern is:

> “You should have come to the accused privately; by telling another adult/police you were rebellious/disloyal.”

This can create a powerful silence mechanism.

### Guarded correction

Where the matter is an ordinary private offense, Matthew 18-style privacy can be wise and required.

Where the matter is serious abuse/crime/danger, using a legitimate protection/reporting channel must not itself be labeled rebellion merely because the accused is:

- a father;
- pastor;
- elder;
- youth leader;
- respected church member;
- influential family.

### P0

> **Respect for authority does not require reporting danger first to the person accused of causing the danger.**

# 17. Four-case procedure matrix

This is a **pastoral research matrix**, not legal advice.

## Case A — ordinary private noncriminal offense between church members

Examples:

- insulting private remark;
- unpaid small personal obligation/dispute;
- private relational trespass.

Ordinary direction:

`PRIVATE APPROACH → WITNESSES/MEDIATION → CHURCH IF UNREPENTANT`

with forgiveness/restoration aims.

## Case B — public scandalous but non-immediate-danger church sin

Examples:

- publicly teaching destructive heresy;
- publicly known gross immorality;
- open corrupting conduct.

Private Matt. 18 sequence may not be the controlling first step. Church officers/congregation act according to polity and public-sin texts.

Gill is explicit on this distinction.

## Case C — suspected child sexual abuse / assault / serious crime

Immediate direction:

`PROTECT → RECEIVE/RECORD WITHOUT INTERROGATION → REQUIRED/APPROPRIATE CIVIL/SAFEGUARDING REFERRAL → INTERIM CHURCH SAFETY MEASURES → COORDINATED ECCLESIAL PROCESS`

Do not force child-victim confrontation.

## Case D — allegation against elder/pastor

Need simultaneous care for:

- 1 Tim. 5 evidentiary caution;
- independence from the accused's control;
- victim/reporter safety;
- corroboration/evidence;
- civil referral if crime/danger;
- church accountability.

### P0

An accused elder must not be the sole controller of the process evaluating his own accusation.

# 18. Theological red-team: common slogans and corrections

## “Matthew 18 says go to him alone first.”

**Correction:** for private trespass. Gill explicitly distinguishes public/atrocious cases; criminal jurisdiction is separate.

## “Christians shouldn't take Christians to secular authorities.”

**Correction:** 1 Cor. 6 addresses believer disputes; Romans 13/1689 ch. 24 give civil magistrates a God-ordained punitive role.

## “There aren't two witnesses, so we cannot do anything.”

**Correction:** do not establish guilt recklessly; but receiving/referencing/investigating a concern is not identical to final adjudication. 1 Tim. 5 mechanics need exact exegetical treatment.

## “Police found no charge, so restore ministry.”

**Correction:** criminal charge/conviction and high-trust ministry fitness are different judgments.

## “The church forgave him, so police should drop it.”

**Correction:** ecclesial forgiveness does not own the magistrate's public jurisdiction.

## “The victim forgave, so contact should resume.”

**Correction:** forgiveness ≠ proximity/access/safeguarding.

## “Reporting ruins the church's witness.”

**Correction:** concealing public evil to protect reputation can itself corrupt witness.

## “If we report, we're assuming guilt.”

**Correction:** a report can communicate a serious allegation needing competent assessment without a public final verdict.

# 24. Final synthesis

> **Matthew 18 teaches the church not to turn ordinary private sin into instant public warfare. Romans 13 teaches the church that it is not the magistrate. Those truths belong together.**

> **The conservative church does not protect biblical authority by making every authority one authority. Fathers, elders, churches, and magistrates all stand under God, but they do not receive the same office, tools, evidence questions, or jurisdiction.**

> **A truthful church can say simultaneously: “We will not condemn without evidence; we will not silence a serious report; we will protect the vulnerable; we will cooperate with lawful civil authority; and we will still do our own biblical work of admonition, discipline, forgiveness, and restoration.”**

> **The safest church is not the church that sends every conflict to police, nor the church that keeps every serious matter inside. It is the church that knows which authority God assigned to which task and refuses to use confidentiality, forgiveness, submission, or Matthew 18 as a shield for institutional comfort.**
