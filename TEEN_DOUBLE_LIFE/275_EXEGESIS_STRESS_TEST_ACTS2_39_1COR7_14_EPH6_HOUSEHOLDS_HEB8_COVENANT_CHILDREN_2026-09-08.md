# Exegesis Stress Test — Acts 2:39, 1 Cor 7:14, Eph 6, Household Baptisms, Jeremiah 31 / Hebrews 8

**Status:** RESEARCH / EXEGESIS STRESS TEST / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `273`, `274`

> Purpose: identify what the major texts actually establish before importing a complete paedobaptist or credobaptist system into them. Historical interpreters are witnesses to readings, not substitutes for exegesis.

## 0. Thesis control

No single disputed proof text presently closes the baptism question by itself. The real disagreement is cumulative and covenant-theological:

- How continuous is Abrahamic household inclusion with New Covenant visible-church membership?
- Does Jeremiah 31 / Hebrews 8 redefine covenant membership around saving knowledge of God, or describe the eschatological efficacy/fullness of a covenant that still has an outward administration?
- Does New Testament child/household language preserve covenant membership before profession, or only demonstrate household exposure, responsibility, and gospel reach?

The project must not pretend that the pastoral-attractiveness question settles these exegetical questions.

# 1. Acts 2:38–41 — “the promise is for you and for your children”

## Textual observations

Peter addresses a Jewish audience after Pentecost and calls for repentance and baptism in the name of Jesus Christ. He then says the promise is:

1. `to you`;
2. `to your children`;
3. `to all who are far off`;
4. followed by `as many as the Lord our God will call`.

Verse 41 then explicitly describes those receiving Peter’s word as baptized.

## Strong paedobaptist reading

Calvin takes the “you and your children” language as covenantal continuity, explicitly connecting it to the Abrahamic promise and treating children as included with believing parents in ecclesial privilege. His Institutes likewise appeals to Acts 2:39 as New Testament continuation of God’s promise to believers and their seed.

### Real strength

Peter does not merely say “the promise is to every called individual.” He uses recognizably intergenerational language to Jewish hearers at a major redemptive-historical transition. It is therefore too quick for Baptists to act as though `your children` is a meaningless redundancy.

## Strong Baptist reading

Gill attaches the closing clause `as many as the Lord our God shall call` to every preceding group. Thus neither descent nor belonging to the audience’s children guarantees possession of the promise; the promise reaches those among parents, children and far-off Gentiles whom God effectually calls.

Verse 41 directly identifies word-receivers as baptized, not infants as a separate class.

### Real strength

The passage never says:

- infants are in view specifically;
- children are to receive baptism independently of repentance/faith;
- any infant was baptized that day.

## Stress-test verdict

`Acts 2:39 = STRONG COVENANT-CONTINUITY DATUM / NOT STANDALONE INFANT-BAPTISM PROOF`.

Publication guard:

- Baptist side must explain why Peter retains explicit `children` language.
- Paedobaptist side must show why promise-language entails sacramental entitlement before personal faith rather than simply intergenerational gospel promise/calling.

# 2. 1 Corinthians 7:14 — “your children are holy”

## Context

Paul answers whether a believer should remain married to an unbelieving spouse. Instead of saying contact with the unbeliever defiles the believer, he says the unbelieving spouse is `sanctified` in relation to the believer and the children are `holy` rather than `unclean`.

The immediate point is the legitimacy/continuance of the mixed marriage and the surprising direction of holiness rather than contamination.

## Historic paedobaptist reading

Calvin and other Reformed interpreters read the child’s holiness as relative/covenantal ecclesial holiness rather than inward regeneration. On this reading, children of believers are set apart from children of the unbelieving world and therefore stand within covenant/church privilege.

This reading coheres naturally with Heidelberg Catechism Q74 and Belgic Confession Article 34.

## Historic Particular Baptist reading — Gill

Gill argues that `sanctified` refers to lawful espousal/marriage and `holy` to legitimate offspring rather than covenant membership.

### Problem requiring caution

The legitimacy-only solution should not be repeated merely because Gill used it. It must explain why Paul uses holiness/uncleanness vocabulary in precisely this way and whether children of two unbelievers would thereby be “unclean” in a legitimacy sense. The lexical/contextual case needs more than denominational inheritance.

## Contemporary Baptist refinement — Thomas Schreiner

Schreiner (2024) rejects the narrow need to reduce `holy` to legitimacy. He grants that unbelieving spouse and children are placed in a real **realm of holiness** through relation to the believer, yielding special gospel exposure/opportunity, while denying that this status is saving or baptism-entitling.

His key argument is structural:

- unbelieving spouse is `sanctified` but is not therefore baptized;
- children are `holy` in the same semantic field;
- therefore holiness itself does not establish baptismal entitlement.

This is a substantially stronger Baptist reply for this project than simply repeating “holy = legitimate.”

## Stress-test verdict

`1 COR 7:14 = REAL SPECIAL SET-APART STATUS / BAPTISMAL INFERENCE DISPUTED`.

The series should likely abandon any overconfident statement that the verse *merely* means legitimacy unless further lexical research decisively warrants it.

### Project significance beyond baptism

Even on Schreiner’s credobaptist reading, the text supports the project’s larger point:

> a child living under believing parentage is not pastorally equivalent to a random outsider; there is a real derivative sphere of holiness/gospel privilege without automatic salvation.

That fits `150_NOT_AN_ORDINARY_UNBELIEVER` exceptionally well.

# 3. Ephesians 6:1–4 / Colossians 3:20–21 — apostolic address to children

## Direct observation

Paul directly commands children to obey parents and fathers to raise children in the Lord’s discipline/instruction. Children are therefore treated as moral hearers of apostolic teaching now, not as beings whose Christian moral relevance begins at conversion or age 18.

Gill grounds obedience in natural right, God’s law and the Lord’s command; Calvin on Col 3 treats parental authority as providentially ordained while limited by God.

## Paedobaptist inference

Because Paul addresses children within letters to churches and couches their duties `in the Lord`, paedobaptists often see this as fitting their covenant-membership status.

## Baptist counter

Direct apostolic address does not logically entail baptism or formal membership. Children can be present in Christian households/assemblies, addressed under God’s Word, and obligated to obey without having received the initiatory ordinance.

A notable older critical commentary (Meyer) explicitly says infant baptism does not follow from Eph 6:1 and that children can stand in a holy household relation without baptism.

## Stress-test verdict

`EPH 6 = DECISIVE AGAINST MORAL-WAITING-ROOM / NON-DECISIVE ON INFANT BAPTISM`.

This text is one of the strongest controls for the user’s practical concern regardless of which baptism position wins.

# 4. Household baptisms — Acts 16; 1 Corinthians 1

## Lydia — Acts 16:15

Lydia and her household are baptized. The narrative does not state whether she had children, whether any were infants, or who exactly comprised the household. Even paedobaptist-friendly historical commentaries acknowledge that infant presence is not demonstrable from the text.

Calvin’s application is strongly household-governance oriented: the godly householder should order the home under Christ and seek household participation in the faith. That pastoral principle remains useful even apart from the infant-baptism inference.

## Philippian jailer — Acts 16:31–34

The word is spoken to the jailer **and to all in his house**; the household is baptized and the narrative connects rejoicing/believing with the house. Gill therefore argues that this household is positive evidence for hearers/believers rather than infants.

## Stephanas — 1 Corinthians 1:16 + 16:15

Paul baptized the household of Stephanas. Later that household is described as firstfruits of Achaia and devoted to service of the saints. This strongly limits claims that the example proves infant baptism, though it cannot prove every household member was necessarily an adult at the baptismal moment.

## Stress-test verdict

`HOUSEHOLD BAPTISMS = CUMULATIVE HOUSEHOLD PATTERN / INFANT PRESENCE UNPROVEN`.

Do not write:

- “households prove infant baptism”;
- “households prove there were no infants.”

The first overreads silence; the second also overreads silence. Jailer/Stephanas contexts do, however, provide positive faith/hearing data that strengthens the credobaptist reading in those specific households.

# 5. Jeremiah 31:31–34 / Hebrews 8:6–13 — the deepest covenant-membership dispute

## Textual center

The promised New Covenant differs from the covenant made at the Exodus:

- law written on mind/heart;
- God-person relation;
- `all shall know me, from least to greatest`;
- definitive forgiveness.

Hebrews explicitly deploys this prophecy to demonstrate the superiority/newness of Christ’s covenant.

## Strong credobaptist argument

If `all` New Covenant members know the Lord savingly and possess forgiveness/internal law, then legal covenant membership should be restricted, as far as the church can discern, to credible professors. A believer-only baptism becomes the visible sign of a regenerate-professor community rather than a mixed genealogical body.

Gill reads Heb 8:11’s knowledge as spiritual/experimental knowledge attended by faith, love, fear, and obedience. Contemporary Baptist covenant theology develops this into a membership argument.

### Strength

The promised blessings are not merely external privileges. The text describes realities ordinarily associated with regeneration and forgiveness.

## Strong paedobaptist response

Reformed paedobaptists deny that Jeremiah/Hebrews abolishes all distinction between outward administration and inward saving participation during the inaugurated-but-not-consummated age. Common responses include:

1. `all` is expansive across classes (`least to greatest`) rather than mathematically excluding all hypocrites/nonregenerate persons from the visible administration;
2. the prophecy has already/not-yet dimensions, reaching complete realization in consummation;
3. Hebrews itself contains covenant-community apostasy warnings, which paedobaptists argue are difficult to reconcile with an absolutely unmixed visible covenant administration;
4. the primary contrast is with Mosaic mediation/inefficacy, not necessarily the abolition of believer-and-household covenant administration rooted in Abraham.

Calvin treats the new covenant as genuinely better/different relative to the Mosaic covenant while also defending substantive continuity of the covenant of grace and infant inclusion.

### Strength

The visible Baptist church is empirically also mixed at the level of fallible profession; therefore `all know me` cannot simply mean the institutional church succeeds in identifying every regenerate person infallibly.

### Baptist rejoinder

Credobaptists need not claim infallibility. Their claim is normative constitution: the church admits **only those it has positive reason to regard as professors**, whereas paedobaptism knowingly admits persons without personal profession. The fact that hypocrites deceive a Baptist church does not erase the normative distinction.

## Stress-test verdict

`JER 31 / HEB 8 = HIGH-LEVERAGE COVENANT-THEOLOGY TEXT; BOTH SIDES REQUIRE SYSTEMIC ARGUMENT`.

This is probably the central doctrinal fork. It should receive more weight in the eventual appendix than household-baptism speculation.

# 6. Genesis 17 and Abrahamic continuity

## Paedobaptist core

God explicitly establishes covenant promise with Abraham and his seed, commands the covenant sign for infant males, and identifies himself as God to Abraham and his seed. Calvin’s classic argument is that unless the New Testament revokes the child-inclusive principle, the continuity of the covenant of grace supports applying the corresponding initiatory sign to believers’ children.

## Particular Baptist core

Gill distinguishes the Abrahamic/circumcision covenant’s national, typological and temporal dimensions from the New Covenant. He argues that covenant interest, even if granted, does not itself confer right to a positive ordinance without a New Testament command; baptism’s named prerequisites are discipleship/faith/profession.

### Particularly strong Baptist point

Even if one conceded some covenant privilege to believers’ children, this still would not logically prove baptism unless Scripture establishes that baptism is the sign to be administered to that class. `COVENANT RELATION -> BAPTISM` is itself an inference needing warrant.

### Particularly strong paedobaptist point

The Baptist must explain why an explicitly child-inclusive covenant structure rooted before Moses becomes profession-only in visible administration without a comparably explicit exclusion statement, especially when Acts 2:39 and 1 Cor 7:14 retain child/household language.

## Stress-test verdict

`GEN 17 = FOUNDATIONAL CONTINUITY/DISCONTINUITY DISPUTE, NOT A SIMPLE TYPOLOGY DISMISSAL`.

# 7. What this means for the series even before baptism question is resolved

Several propositions survive both strong systems:

1. A child is presently accountable to God before conversion.
2. Parents have present authority/duty to instruct and discipline.
3. Children under sustained gospel light occupy a materially different pastoral history from a minimally exposed outsider.
4. Neither baptism nor Christian parentage proves regeneration.
5. No civil age automatically creates moral responsibility.
6. Christian adults must seek present repentance/faith rather than defer the gospel until adulthood.
7. Church-raised children should receive organized, named pastoral care even where a Baptist church correctly withholds membership before profession.

These common conclusions are strategically important: the series can strongly attack Baptist permissiveness **without first requiring the reader to change baptismal theology**.

# 8. Provisional weighting of disputed texts

| Text | What it strongly establishes | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Acts 2:39 | explicit intergenerational promise language + divine calling | infant baptism / automatic covenant salvation |
| 1 Cor 7:14 | real set-apart/holy relation around believing spouse/parent | saving holiness; automatic baptism entitlement |
| Eph 6:1–4 | present child moral duty + parental Christian formation | formal church membership / baptism |
| Household baptisms | household scope of gospel/baptism narratives | actual infant presence or absence in every case |
| Gen 17 | explicit Abrahamic child inclusion + covenant sign | direct identity of circumcision recipients with NT baptism recipients without further argument |
| Jer 31 / Heb 8 | profound New Covenant internalization, knowledge, forgiveness, superiority | by itself, uncontested polity for visible membership before consummation |

## Locked editorial rule

Do not resolve the practical child-accountability question by overclaiming the baptism texts. The series’ immediate pastoral thesis is much more secure than either side’s shortest sacramental proof text.
