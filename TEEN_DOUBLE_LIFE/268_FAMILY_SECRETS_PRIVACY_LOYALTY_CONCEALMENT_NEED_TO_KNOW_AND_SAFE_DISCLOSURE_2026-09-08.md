## 1. Executive verdict

The Russian family phrase `не выноси сор из избы` can express two very different moral instincts.

It can mean something legitimate:

> do not humiliate your family, gossip, publicize every conflict, or distribute private details to people who have no right or need to know.

It can also become a corrupt rule:

> never disclose anything that would embarrass the family, even when truth, justice, protection, restitution, church discipline or civil safeguarding require disclosure.

The project must never treat those as the same.

### Locked distinctions

> **PRIVACY ≠ SECRECY.**
>
> **CONFIDENTIALITY ≠ CONCEALMENT OF EVIL.**
>
> **LOYALTY ≠ FALSE WITNESS.**
>
> **FAMILY HONOR ≠ IMAGE MANAGEMENT.**
>
> **NEED-TO-KNOW ≠ EVERYONE-GETS-TO-KNOW.**
>
> **DISCLOSURE ≠ PUBLICATION.**
>
> **SAFE DISCLOSURE ≠ GOSSIP.**
>
> **A CHILD MAY KEEP A SURPRISE; A CHILD MUST NOT BE TRAINED TO KEEP DANGEROUS SECRETS FROM SAFE ADULTS.**

And:

> **A family does not become truthful by abolishing privacy, and it does not preserve honor by protecting a lie.**

## 2. Five categories that must not collapse

### Category A — ordinary privacy

Examples:

- private thoughts not involving danger/deception;
- age-appropriate personal conversations;
- a diary not implicated in serious known risk;
- ordinary bodily privacy;
- a lawful surprise/gift;
- family financial or medical facts that outsiders do not need.

Default posture: protect dignity; no moral presumption of guilt.

### Category B — entrusted confidence

Examples:

- a friend shares an embarrassing but non-dangerous struggle;
- a sibling confesses an ordinary personal concern;
- a pastor/mentor receives information that can remain within a legitimate care circle.

Default posture: do not gossip; explain limits before promising confidentiality where safety may arise.

### Category C — concealed wrongdoing

Examples:

- hidden sexual account;
- secret pornography use;
- repeated lying about whereabouts;
- covering theft;
- helping another child bypass safeguards.

Default posture: truth must come to the persons with legitimate responsibility; privacy cannot be invoked as a right to maintain deception.

### Category D — dangerous secrecy / coercion / abuse

Examples:

- `don't tell your parents what I did to you`;
- sexual images or contact involving coercion;
- adult-minor sexual behavior;
- credible threat of violence;
- sextortion;
- grooming secrecy;
- credible self-harm/suicide emergency;
- instruction to hide abuse of a sibling.

Default posture: safety and lawful safeguarding override ordinary secrecy expectations. Jurisdiction matters.

### Category E — publicity / broad disclosure

Examples:

- telling the whole church;
- posting online;
- announcing sexual details to relatives;
- forcing public confession unrelated to the scope of harm.

Default posture: not justified merely because a fact is true.

## 3. Proverbs 11:13 / 25:9–10 — Scripture values guarded speech

Biblical truthfulness is not indiscriminate disclosure.

Proverbs condemns talebearing and commends trustworthy keeping of confidence; it also warns against revealing another's secret in a dispute.

These texts establish an anti-gossip norm.

They do **not** establish:

> `all secrets must be kept regardless of danger or justice`.

### Product-safe synthesis

> **Faithfulness with another's private information is a virtue until secrecy begins serving falsehood, danger or injustice.**

Evidence class: `CANONICAL_SYNTHESIS`.

---

## 4. Ephesians 4:25 / 5:11–13 — truth and light are also real duties

The new-life ethic rejects falsehood.

Christians are not permitted to create a family culture where maintaining an image requires lying, suppressing victims, or denying what happened.

At the same time, `bring to light` must not be turned into:

> `broadcast every detail to every person`.

The New Testament also contains ordered processes, limited audiences and jurisdictional distinctions.

### Locked

`LIGHT ≠ PUBLIC SPECTACLE`.

`PRIVACY ≠ DARKNESS`.

`DECEPTION ≠ PRIVACY`.

## 5. Matthew 18 — widening can be proportionate

Use existing `167` and `242`.

Matthew 18 begins with private confrontation where appropriate and widens the circle when unresolved.

That supports an important principle:

> **the circle of disclosure can grow according to moral/jurisdictional need rather than curiosity.**

But the text is not:

- a universal abuse-reporting statute;
- a reason to delay civil safeguarding;
- proof that every family matter begins with the alleged offender privately;
- a reason to keep dangerous allegations inside the family/church.

## 7. Child-facing "safe secrets" taxonomy

A future parent-facing article can teach a child simple categories without requiring technical terminology.

### Safe privacy / surprise

- birthday gift;
- harmless surprise party;
- ordinary private thought;
- another person's non-dangerous confidence.

### Secret that should trigger adult help

If someone says secrecy is required around:

- sexual touching;
- sexual images;
- violence;
- threats;
- drugs/weapons in an immediate-risk context;
- self-harm danger;
- adult requests for hidden private contact;
- requests to hide where/when meetings occur;
- requests to delete evidence because adults would stop the relationship;
- threats that disclosure will destroy the family.

Then the child should be taught that telling a safe responsible adult is not betrayal.

### Locked

> **SECRECY DEMANDED BY A MORE POWERFUL PERSON CAN ITSELF BE A RISK SIGNAL; IT IS NOT BY ITSELF PROOF OF ABUSE.**

Avoid teaching children that every request for discretion is grooming.

## 8. "Don't tell Mom/Dad" — parent-side mirror

Parents can themselves create corrupt secrecy.

Examples:

- `don't tell your father how much this cost`;
- `don't tell your mother I let you do this`;
- `tell them I was at church when I wasn't`;
- `don't tell anyone your brother did that because it will shame us`;
- `don't tell the pastor because people will think badly of our family`.

This trains the child in divided loyalty and selective truth.

### Locked

`PARENTAL UNITY ≠ COMPLICITY` from `256` applies here.

A spouse or child must not be recruited into false witness to preserve another family member's image.

## 9. Disclosure after sexual abuse — why fear of family reaction matters

Secondary empirical evidence can document disclosure barriers; it does not define biblical morality.

### Hershkowitz et al. — alleged child sexual abuse disclosure

PMID 17316793  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17316793/

Small interview study:

- 30 alleged victims plus parents;
- more than half delayed disclosure from one week to two years;
- fewer than half first disclosed to parents;
- many children reported fear/shame about parental response;
- parental anger/blame appeared in reported responses.

Use only as qualitative/observational evidence of disclosure barriers.

Do not use it for prevalence claims about all abuse victims.

### Qualitative adolescent study

PMID 22821848  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22821848/

Reported reasons for not telling parents included lack of trust and not wanting to burden them; peer disclosure was common.

### Older epidemiological disclosure study

PMID 19038448  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19038448/

Useful category-level evidence that abuse can remain largely outside adult/professional knowledge even when peers know.

### Product implication

> **A church/family culture that teaches "embarrassing things stay inside" can unintentionally give an abuser's secrecy demand religious cover.**

This is a prudential safeguarding inference, not a causal prevalence claim.

## 10. Disclosure/secrecy research — do not collapse the constructs

Recent and older adolescent-information-management work repeatedly distinguishes voluntary disclosure from secrecy.

### 2026 Zeringue & Laird

PMID 42529927  
DOI 10.1002/jad.70241  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42529927/

Sample:

- 242 youth;
- mid-adolescence mean age 15.4;
- late adolescence mean age 18.7.

Key caution:

- lower disclosure and higher secrecy related differently to adjustment;
- secrecy, not mere low disclosure, showed the more persistent longitudinal association with antisocial behavior.

### Frijns et al. four-wave work

PMID 19573902  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19573902/

Likewise supports treating secrecy and disclosure as distinct constructs.

### Project rule

Do not write:

> `A healthy teenager tells parents everything.`

Use:

> **The project should be especially concerned with active concealment of morally/safety-relevant information, while preserving a real category of ordinary privacy.**

## 11. Need-to-know matrix

### Parents/guardians

Need can be high where:

- minor safety is involved;
- household rules/resources are being used deceptively;
- medical/legal care requires involvement;
- another child in the home is at risk.

But `parent` does not automatically mean:

- safe recipient in every abuse case;
- entitlement to every erotic detail;
- entitlement to all pastoral confidences regardless of purpose.

### Pastor/elder

Need can be high where:

- church membership/discipline is implicated;
- church role/access needs review;
- pastoral care is requested;
- another congregant may be at risk.

But church office does not create universal investigative jurisdiction.

### Civil/safeguarding professional

May become necessary where law/safety requires.

Exact reporting rules are jurisdiction-dependent.

### Siblings/extended family

Usually should receive only information necessary for:

- their own safety;
- practical household arrangements;
- truthful explanation of visible changes.

Do not load younger siblings with unnecessary sexual detail.

### General congregation/public

Need is much narrower.

Truth may need public correction where harm/office/public falsehood was public, but broad publication is not the default response to private sin.

## 12. "Tell me everything" is not a safe universal parenting rule

After discovering a hidden sexual/digital life, parents may understandably want exhaustive knowledge.

But the project already has `165` and `266` because exhaustive detail can become:

- voyeuristic;
- unnecessary;
- humiliating;
- harmful to victims;
- legally/investigatively unwise;
- a false test of repentance.

Parents need enough truth to:

- protect;
- govern;
- understand scope;
- interrupt access;
- seek competent help;
- determine restitution/repair;
- rebuild trust.

They do not need every explicit image, fantasy or sexual detail merely because they are parents.

## 13. Safeguarding red line

Never publish:

- `Families should solve abuse privately first.`
- `Going outside the family dishonors parents.`
- `Forgiveness means authorities do not need to know.`
- `Matthew 18 must be completed before reporting.`
- `A child who tells a teacher/pastor about abuse betrayed the family.`
- `Parents have an absolute right to control every disclosure a minor makes.`

Exact civil/legal duties require current jurisdiction verification.

## 15. Product overclaim kill-switch

Reject or rewrite:

- `All secrets are bad.`
- `Children should never keep anything from parents.`
- `Privacy is a modern individualist invention.`
- `Family loyalty means problems stay in the family.`
- `If something is true, it should be publicly disclosed.`
- `Confidentiality means never telling anyone.`
- `Disclosure always goes to parents first.`
- `A child who tells an outsider is dishonoring father and mother.`
- `Safeguarding means parents lose all legitimate information rights.`

Preserve:

> **Truth without gossip; privacy without deception; loyalty without complicity; confidentiality without dangerous secrecy; disclosure to the smallest legitimate circle capable of doing the next right thing.**
