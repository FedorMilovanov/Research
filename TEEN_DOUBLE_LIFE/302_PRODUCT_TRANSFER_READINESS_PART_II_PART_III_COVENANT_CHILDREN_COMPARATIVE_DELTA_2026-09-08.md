# 2. Material Part II delta — historic Baptist recovery

## Problem in current draft

Part II correctly states a high-accountability Baptist model, but the historical support visible to the reader is weighted toward:

- Owen;
- Baxter;
- Watson;
- Spurgeon.

The new primary-source research can prove more precisely that **seventeenth/eighteenth-century Particular Baptists themselves** joined believer-only baptism with intensive child formation.

This matters because the article otherwise risks leaving a sophisticated reader with the impression:

> `The author is confessionally Baptist but imported the household-accountability intensity from Puritan/paedobaptist writers.`

That impression would now be historically unnecessary.

## Recommended import — `YES / SMALL`

Add a compact historical Baptist paragraph or boxed note near either:

- `Нет моральной комнаты ожидания для необращённого ребёнка`, or
- `Воспитание начинается не в день скандала`.

### Reader-facing content target

Use only two witnesses in the main article:

1. **Hercules Collins** — even while rejecting infant baptism, he called godly parentage a great mercy because children receive the advantages of good education, prayer and good example; his *Orthodox Catechism* preface presses parents to pray, instruct, exemplify and care for children’s everlasting welfare.
2. **John Gill** — child obedience remains an objective duty, while parents must teach Scripture, pray, correct early vice, guard company and bring children under the Word/means of grace; at the same time Gill warns against training children into first-person religious profession before actual knowledge/faith.

Optional third witness:

3. **Spurgeon** — already present and can remain the later bridge to immediate genuine child conversion without premature certification.

### Product thesis

> **Баптистская граница у крещения не означает паузы в воспитании до крещения. Сильная Particular Baptist традиция различала две вещи: ребёнка нужно сейчас учить, исправлять, приводить под Слово и звать ко Христу — и при этом нельзя превращать семейную религиозность или выученные слова в свидетельство нового рождения.**

This is editorial synthesis, not a historical quotation.

# 5. Material Part III delta — membership vs pastoral ownership clarified by comparison

## Why any comparison might help

Current Part III states the Baptist rule positively, but a short fair comparison can sharpen **why** the Baptist church must intentionally build pre-membership pastoral ownership.

The new Research shows that serious paedobaptist polities solve this institutional problem structurally:

- PCA — child of believers is a non-communing member with baptism, oversight, instruction/government and discipline language;
- OPC — baptized children are noncommunicant members on the church’s roll, under care/discipline;
- URCNA — mature baptized members can be admonished and eventually excluded for persistent delinquency.

A Baptist rejects the inherited membership/sign premise, but should notice the institutional strength:

> **the child already has an explicit ecclesial address.**

## Recommended import

`OPTIONAL / HIGH VALUE IF KEPT VERY SHORT`.

### Best location

Immediately after or within the existing section that says:

> a nonmember cannot be excommunicated, but the church is not therefore unable to admonish or restrict its own roles/access.

### Reader-facing target

`~150–250 Russian words` maximum.

Possible thesis:

> **Здесь полезно увидеть, почему пресвитерианские и некоторые реформатские церкви часто выглядят институционально определённее: ребёнок верующих уже записан как неконфирмированный/некоммуницирующий член и находится под названным надзором. Баптист не обязан принимать крещение младенцев, чтобы признать силу этого вопроса. Если мы говорим: «членом он станет после личного исповедания», мы всё равно должны ответить: «а кто пастырски знает и ведёт его сейчас?» Граница членства может остаться баптистской; пастырская безадресность — не обязана.**

### Mandatory guard

Immediately preserve:

> `pastoral ownership ≠ church keys`.

Do not imply that the unbaptized Baptist child is secretly a quasi-member.
