# 0. Part III promise

Part III should answer:

> **How should a church treat a child or teenager who is welcome to hear the gospel but is known to live in serious, defended, or corrupting sin—and what do choir, youth group, small group, membership, platform, discipline and safeguarding teach about the holiness of God?**

The article must reject both:

1. **moral neutrality:** `He is only a child / unbeliever; let him live however he wants while he hears the Word.`
2. **false ecclesiology:** `Every sinful outsider should be treated as if formally excommunicated.`

# 1. Governing distinction — hearing the Word is not entitlement to every church privilege

Biblical anchors:
- 1 Cor. 14:23–25;
- 1 Cor. 5;
- Acts 17 as broad gospel-hearing precedent if needed.

### Direct claims

1 Cor. 14 explicitly envisages an unbeliever/outsider entering the gathered church and hearing truth.

1 Cor. 5 explicitly addresses a scandalous person **called a brother** and distinguishes judging insiders from outsiders.

### Core taxonomy

`PUBLIC PREACHING / HEARING`  
≠ `MEMBERSHIP`  
≠ `LORD’S SUPPER`  
≠ `CHOIR / REPRESENTATIVE PLATFORM`  
≠ `LEADERSHIP`  
≠ `CONFIDENTIAL YOUTH ACCESS`  
≠ `UNSUPERVISED ACCESS TO MINORS`.

### Publication line

> **An open door to the sermon is not a blank cheque to every role, room and relationship in the church.**

### Guard

Do not imply an unbelieving teen is formally subject to 1 Cor. 5 excommunication merely because he attends youth meetings.

# 2. Children are morally accountable — Eph. 6 and 1689 law/gospel control

Texts:
- Eph. 6:1–4;
- Col. 3:20–21;
- 1689 LBCF 19.5–6;
- 1689 ch. 10/14/15 as regeneration/faith/repentance background.

### Direct claim

Paul addresses children as moral agents under actual divine commands.

### Confessional claim

1689 19.5 says the moral law binds all people, justified and others.

### Core synthesis

> **Unregeneration explains why external training cannot create a new heart; it does not erase duty, guilt, parental authority, or the call to repentance.**

### RED

Do not write:

> `Because he is not saved, we should expect nothing morally until God converts him.`

### Equal-and-opposite RED

Do not coerce:

> `Behave externally, therefore we will call you a Christian.`

Use law to name sin and gospel to call to Christ.

# 3. Spurgeon — no hereditary religion, no postponed child conversion

Primary sources already verified in module 53:
- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/sermons10.xviii.html
- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/a-pastoral-visit
- https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/baptismal-regeneration

### Best Part III uses

Spurgeon strongly rejects:
- automatic Christianity by Christian parentage;
- receiving child profession carelessly;
- treating tears/religious emotion as automatic conversion;
- postponing serious concern for child conversion until adulthood.

He insists on real repentance and real faith **in kind**, while not demanding adult maturity or vocabulary.

### Strong synthesis

> **Christian familiarity is not Christian birth.**

> **A child can be young enough to need patient teaching and old enough to need real repentance at the same time.**

Both are project synthesis, not direct quotation.

### Choir relevance guard

Spurgeon speaks directly about conversion/church membership, not a modern children’s-choir admission code.

Use his theology to prevent automatic inherited Christian status, not to invent a rule he never wrote.

# 4. Singing is worship — 1689 chapter 22

Source:
- https://the1689.org/chapters/22

### Confessional points

1689 22.5 treats teaching/admonishing in psalms, hymns and spiritual songs and singing with grace in the heart as part of religious worship.

Worship is to be rendered with:
- obedience;
- understanding;
- faith;
- reverence;
- godly fear.

### Project implication

If a children’s choir is functioning inside gathered worship, it should not be treated as spiritually meaningless stage entertainment.

### Strong publication line

> **Holy lyrics are not a moral solvent. A child cannot make protected rebellion acceptable to God by singing Christian words over it.**

Synthesis.

### Critical guard

1689 does **not** contain a children’s-choir eligibility rubric.

Exact choir rules remain ecclesial prudence.

---

# 5. Do not overcorrect into “unconverted children should stop worshipping”

1689 22.3:
- prayer with thanksgiving is required of all men.

Related biblical principle:
- God’s moral claim is universal.

### Therefore reject

> `You are unconverted; stop praying/singing until conversion.`

### Better summons

> **Do not use prayer or song as camouflage. God has a claim on you, and you need repentance and faith so that you worship Him truly rather than preserving a religious mask.**

This is stricter than moral neutrality without inventing a period in which God has no claim on the child.

# 6. Psalm 50 — the central “religious lips + rebellious life” text

Text:
- Ps. 50:16–23.

### Direct concern

God addresses the wicked person who recites His statutes/covenant language while hating correction and participating in evil.

### Part III use

This should probably be the primary text for the user’s concern:

> `the child sings/speaks Christian language while knowingly protecting profanity, sexual sin, deceit and contempt for correction.`

### Strong synthesis

> **The problem is not that worship is too holy for sinners who need grace. The problem is using holy words as though they made peace with a life that refuses correction.**

### Guard

Psalm 50 is covenant lawsuit/worship indictment, not a children’s-choir statute.

Apply the worship/hypocrisy principle, not an invented admission procedure.

- Isa. 1:10–17;

Use Psalm 50 as central and these as canonical reinforcement.

# 8. Owen — churches must not treat children as spiritually invisible

Primary source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/evangelicalchurches/evangelicalchurches.iii.v.html

Verified in module 95.

Owen argues that churches should care for children/persons connected with member households who are not yet fit for full communion through:
- prayer;
- catechetical instruction;
- counsel to parents;
- family visiting;
- encouragement/admonition;
- preparation toward fuller communion;
- withholding special privileges where visibly unfit.

He criticizes careless education conforming children to the world and churches allowing them to drop away with little inquiry.

### Strong transferable principle

> **Nonmembership does not make a child pastorally invisible.**

> **Church care includes instruction and encouragement, but also admonition and differentiated privilege.**

### CRITICAL BAPTIST GUARD

Owen is paedobaptist and grounds part of his argument in baptized-child covenant/church standing.

Do **not** import:
- infant-baptism membership assumptions;
- Owen’s covenant-child rights as if identical to 1689 Baptist ecclesiology.

Transfer only principles independently defensible from Scripture and Baptist polity.

# 9. 1689 chapter 26 — visible saints, profession and walk

Source:
- https://the1689.org/chapters/26
- alternate: https://rbs.org/2lbc

### Confessional center

1689 26.2 describes local visible saints as professing gospel faith and obedience without destroying that profession through foundational error or unholiness of life.

26.6 speaks of members visibly manifesting/evidencing obedience to Christ in profession and walk.

### Core distinction

Wrong A:

> `We know from this sin that the child is certainly reprobate.`

Wrong B:

> `Because only God knows the heart, visible conduct can never affect visible church privileges.`

### Better formula

> **The church does not judge the secret decree; it must still steward visible roles from visible profession and visible walk.**

### Important scope guard

This directly controls membership/visible church order.

Choir/small-group/platform application is prudential inference, not verbatim confession language.

# 10. The children’s choir problem — classify the role before setting the threshold

There is no explicit NT `children’s choir` office.

Therefore first ask what the choir actually is.

### Model A — catechetical/instructional singing

Purpose:
- teach Scripture/truth/music;
- children may include unconverted attendees;
- participation does not necessarily communicate credible church membership/testimony.

### Model B — public representative ministry / worship-leading role

Purpose/communication may include:
- representing Christian profession;
- leading/contributing visibly to gathered worship;
- trusted ministry status.

Then visible-conduct expectations can reasonably be higher.

### In either model

Serious known conduct that:
- corrupts other children;
- mocks correction;
- uses choir status as religious camouflage;
- creates safety risk

cannot simply be ignored because the offender is a child.

### Editorial rule

Do not write one universal choir policy without first explaining the role model.

# 11. Struggling child vs settled/corrupting pattern

This distinction belongs near the center of Part III.

### Struggling/repentance-oriented indicators

- calls sin sin;
- confesses beyond what was discovered;
- asks for help;
- accepts reasonable restrictions;
- removes occasions;
- grieves offense against God;
- does not recruit others;
- returns to truth after failure.

### Settled/protected/corrupting indicators

- planned concealment;
- defends sin or demands recognition;
- mocks/evades correction;
- repeatedly finds ways around boundaries;
- distributes pornography;
- pressures peers sexually;
- teaches deception;
- uses church/social setting for sexual access;
- corrupts younger children.

### Guard

This is pastoral pattern assessment, not infallible heart-reading.

### Strong line

> **The same outward sin can require different pastoral responses depending on whether the child is fighting it, protecting it, or recruiting others into it.**

# 12. Youth group is formative company, not neutral recreation

Texts:
- 1 Cor. 15:33;
- 2 Tim. 2:22;
- Proverbs company/enticement themes.

### Direct principle

Company can corrupt; Paul also tells Timothy to pursue righteousness with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart.

### Part III argument

Church youth culture should not merely ask:

> `Are kids physically present in church?`

It should ask:
- what language is normalized?
- what private chats emerge?
- who sets status norms?
- are godly older mentors present?
- does the ministry reward bravado/sexual joking?
- are leaders aware of corrupting subgroups?

### Guard

Do not treat every friendship with a sinful child as contamination.

The church exists to evangelize/sanctify sinners; the issue is trusted/unchecked corrupting influence.

# 13. Known corrupting outsider — admonition and access restriction without fictional excommunication

If an unbelieving/nonmember teenager:
- shares pornography;
- sexually pressures peers;
- teaches children to deceive parents;
- creates obscene hidden chats;
- repeatedly targets youth-group members romantically/sexually under a false Christian persona;
- refuses direct correction,

church leaders may have reason to restrict:
- confidential groups;
- trips;
- private chats;
- representative roles;
- unsupervised access to minors.

### Why

Not because 1 Cor. 5 formally excommunicates every outsider.

Because leaders have stewardship duties over:
- order;
- holiness;
- safety;
- other children.

### Public Word

Where safe/orderly, hearing the Word remains a distinct category.

# 14. Professing insider — 1 Corinthians 5 becomes directly relevant

Text:
- 1 Cor. 5.

### Direct claim

Paul addresses serious scandal among a person called a brother and commands ecclesial action rather than passive normalization.

### Part III use

If a teenager/adult youth:
- is a professing church member;
- persists in serious scandal;
- rejects correction,

formal church discipline may become relevant according to local-church polity.

### Guard

Do not use 1 Cor. 5 to say:

> `any teen who curses once should be banned from church`.

Context is serious, known, protected scandal and church responsibility.

# 15. Safeguarding is not the same question as church discipline

Important categories:

A child may:
- sin sexually;
- then become a victim of coercion/extortion/redistribution.

Or a child/young adult may become a perpetrator of:
- grooming;
- coercion;
- abuse;
- illegal image distribution.

### Rule

`repentance / forgiveness / discipline`  
≠ `safety assessment / reporting / access control`.

### Strong line

> **Forgiving an offender does not transfer the risk to the next child.**

### Guard

If legal reporting/safeguarding duties apply, church counseling does not replace them.

---

# 16. Forgiveness / restoration / trust / access — five questions, not one

Carry forward module 87 distinction:

1. Has the offender been forgiven by the offended person(s) in the relevant biblical sense?
2. Is relational reconciliation possible/appropriate now?
3. Is church standing restored?
4. Is trust rebuilt?
5. Is this specific access/role safe and wise?

These can move on different timelines.

### Strong synthesis

> **Mercy is not naivety, and safeguarding is not revenge.**

### Equal guard

Do not turn a past fall into permanent branding where current risk/role rationale no longer exists.

# 17. Restoration — Sibbes/Spurgeon tenderness guard

Part III must not end as a policy crackdown.

Biblical anchors:
- Gal. 6:1;
- 2 Cor. 2:5–11 where contextually useful;
- 2 Cor. 7 repentance fruit;
- Luke 15 only as gospel joy/repentance theme, not policy.

### Principle

A weak/repentant child should be restored with gentleness appropriate to actual circumstances.

Role restoration may be gradual; spiritual welcome should not become institutional humiliation.

### Strong line

> **The church that is serious about holiness must also be serious about restoration, because holiness is not the art of permanently identifying people by the worst thing they did.**

Synthesis.

# 18. What church procedures teach children

This should be the closing institutional challenge.

A church teaches doctrine not only by sermons but by what it tolerates/rewards.

Possible messages accidentally taught:

### If known corruption is ignored

> `Christian appearance and serious protected sin can coexist without consequence.`

### If every struggling child is publicly shamed

> `Truth is dangerous; concealment is safer than confession.`

### If inherited church identity is presumed

> `Christianity is a family role.`

### If restoration is impossible

> `Grace is preached but permanent suspicion governs.`

### Desired message

> **God is holy; sin is real; Christ receives sinners; truth comes into light; vulnerable people are protected; repentance bears fruit; and church privileges are handled honestly rather than as props.**

# 23. Final drafting theorem

> **The church should neither make children prove Christianity by performing a religious role nor teach them that religious roles are morally weightless. It should preach Christ openly, instruct children seriously, name sin honestly, distinguish hearing the Word from trusted privilege, protect the vulnerable, refuse to certify protected rebellion as normal Christian life, and rejoice when real repentance comes. Only God gives a new heart; precisely for that reason the church must faithfully use the means and steward the responsibilities He actually gave it.**
