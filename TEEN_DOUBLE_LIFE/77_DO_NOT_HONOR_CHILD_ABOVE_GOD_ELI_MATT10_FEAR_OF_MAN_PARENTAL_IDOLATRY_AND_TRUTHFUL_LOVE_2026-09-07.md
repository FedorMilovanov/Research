# Do Not Honor the Child Above God — Eli, Matthew 10, Fear of Man, Parental Idolatry, and Truthful Love

**Status:** SCRIPTURE-FIRST THEOLOGICAL SYNTHESIS / RESEARCH ONLY  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  

---

## 1. Why this matters

The adult-prodigal lane has strongly emphasized:

- keep loving;
- do real good;
- maintain appropriate contact;
- do not impose automatic shunning;
- avoid parental control masquerading as faithfulness.

That needs an equally strong opposite guard:

> **The relationship with the child must never become the god that determines what the parent is willing to call good or evil.**

Parents can idolize family peace just as surely as they can idolize control.

---

## 2. Eli’s indictment is relationally explicit: “you honor your sons above Me”

Key text:

- 1 Sam. 2:29–30.

Source:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_samuel/2-29.htm

The problem is not that Eli possesses natural affection for his sons.

The problem is the **ordering** of loyalties.

He knows their sacrilege and corruption and nevertheless allows them to remain in a position where God’s worship continues to be dishonored.

Commentators emphasize his connivance/weakness and unwillingness to deal effectively with them.

### Central project question

> **What is the parent unwilling to obey because he fears losing the child’s favor?**

This question may expose an idol more precisely than merely asking whether the parent is “strict enough.”

---

## 3. Matthew 10:34–39 — Christ explicitly names son/daughter in the hierarchy of love

Primary text:

- Matt. 10:37.

Calvin:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom31.ix.lxxxix.html

Gill/commentary hub:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/matthew/10-37.htm

Jesus does **not** abolish natural affection.

Calvin explicitly says Christ does not command us to lay aside human affection or cease relational duties.

The demand is ordering:

> family love must not overpower reverence/obedience due to Christ.

Gill likewise stresses that natural family duties remain, but Christ must be loved above every created relation.

### Adult-child application

A Christian parent cannot say:

> “I know Scripture calls this sexual conduct sin, but if I say so my daughter may stop speaking to me, therefore I will redefine it.”

Nor:

> “If I remove him from ministry, he may leave the church; therefore I will leave him on the platform.”

That is precisely where family affection can become disordered.

---

## 4. Luke 14:26 — “hate” is not a command to emotional hostility toward family

Christ’s stronger wording in Luke 14 must be interpreted alongside Matt. 10:37.

Calvin’s synthesis (also cited in his John commentary):

- God/Christ receives highest place;
- human relations remain real;
- human duties are performed under, not over, divine obedience.

Source:

- Calvin cross-reference discussion:
  https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom35/calcom35.ix.vi.html

### Publication guard

Never use Luke 14 to justify:

- cruelty;
- spite;
- disowning a child for emotional punishment;
- refusing ordinary family duty.

Its point is **supreme allegiance to Christ**, not sanctified hostility.

---

## 5. Proverbs 29:25 — fear of losing human favor can become a snare

Source:

- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/proverbs/29-25.htm

Gill identifies fear of man in part as fear of:

- losing favor/friendship;
- reproach;
- displeasure;
- consequences from people.

That fear can lead a person to conceal or drop truth.

### Parent-specific forms

- “If I tell the truth, he won’t call me.”
- “If we impose a house condition, she’ll move out.”
- “If the pastor confronts him, he’ll leave the youth group.”
- “If we remove her from choir, everyone will know something is wrong.”
- “If I refuse the money, he’ll say I never loved him.”

These possibilities may be genuinely painful.

Pain does not itself decide obedience.

---

## 6. Galatians 1:10 — pleasing people cannot govern fidelity to Christ

Calvin:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom41/calcom41.iii.iii.iii.html

Paul’s immediate context concerns gospel ministry, not parenting.

Therefore this is an **analogical discipleship principle**, not a direct household command.

Calvin’s useful qualification:

- Christians/ministers should not seek to displease people for its own sake;
- charity toward people is good;
- but human favor cannot be bought at the price of truth/obedience.

### Excellent project balance

Do not create conflict unnecessarily.

Do not fear conflict so much that truth disappears.

---

## 7. “Keeping the relationship” can itself become an idol

This is a needed correction to modern counseling language.

A relationship is a good gift.

But good gifts become idols when they function as ultimate governing goods.

Signs:

- parent lies to protect child from deserved exposure;
- funds sin to keep access;
- silences siblings/victims;
- changes doctrine to preserve peace;
- pressures church leaders not to discipline;
- refuses to acknowledge unbelief because “he prayed a prayer at six”;
- continues visible ministry to avoid embarrassment;
- accepts sexual partners/behavior into the household contrary to conscience solely because refusal might end contact.

### Strong formulation

> **A parent can lose biblical love for a child precisely by becoming unwilling to lose the child’s approval.**

---

## 8. But “God first” must not become religious cover for parental ego

The opposite abuse is common:

> “I’m standing for God,”

when the actual issue is:

- wounded pride;
- family reputation;
- desire to control an adult;
- anger that the child embarrassed the parent;
- personal preferences falsely elevated to divine law.

Therefore every claimed “stand for holiness” must be tested:

### Is this actually commanded by God?

or

### Is this my preference/status/fear dressed in religious language?

This is why the project’s `command / principle / prudential application` hierarchy is essential.

---

## 9. The parent must be willing to lose both idols: control and approval

Two symmetrical temptations:

### Control-idolatry

> “I must make the adult child do what I believe.”

### Approval-idolatry

> “I must make the adult child keep liking/talking to me.”

Biblical faithfulness may require surrendering both outcomes to God.

The parent instead controls:

- his own speech;
- his own money/resources;
- his own household;
- his own repentance;
- his own prayer;
- his own treatment of others;
- his own obedience.

### Core line

> **You are not sovereign over whether your adult child returns, and you are not sovereign over whether he approves of your faithfulness.**

---

## 10. This directly explains “non-enabling” in theological terms

Rather than beginning with the counseling term `boundaries`, begin with worship:

> Whom am I honoring above whom?

Then stewardship:

> What has God actually entrusted to me?

Then love:

> What real good can I do without participating in evil?

Then humility:

> What outcome belongs to God rather than to me?

This is more biblical than organizing the entire problem around psychological self-protection.

---

## 11. Application to church access and children’s choir

Eli is especially relevant because the sin was carried inside sacred office.

A church can effectively “honor the child above God” if leaders know a young person is:

- aggressively profane;
- sexually corrupting peers;
- sharing pornography;
- mocking correction;
- living a protected double life,

and yet keep him in visible worship/ministry solely because:

- parents will be offended;
- child will leave;
- youth numbers will decrease;
- confrontation seems unwelcoming.

### Guard

Visible ministry standards remain prudential ecclesial application; there is no NT children’s-choir qualification chapter.

But fear of losing attendance is not a biblical reason to knowingly misrepresent serious rebellion as spiritually inconsequential.

---

## 12. Application after a child secretly leaves home

Potential parent temptations:

### Fear-driven pursuit

> “I need to call constantly because if I lose contact I cannot survive.”

### Fear-driven indulgence

> “I will pay for anything if he keeps answering.”

### Fear-driven silence

> “I will never mention repentance again because he may block me.”

### Fear-driven retaliation

> “If he rejects my authority, I will show him I can reject him harder.”

All four may arise from a heart ruled by the child’s response rather than God.

The faithful alternative can include:

- measured contact;
- truthful words;
- no revenge;
- no participation in sin;
- genuine good;
- willingness to bear relational pain;
- prayer;
- trust in God.

## 13. Calvin’s particularly useful hierarchy

Calvin, discussing family duty under divine duty, says in substance:

- God’s command/worship/service takes precedence;
- afterward we give human relations everything rightly due to them.

This is crucial.

The answer is not:

`God instead of family`.

It is:

`God first → therefore family loved in the right order`.

### Strong formulation

> **Christ does not free the father from loving his son; He frees the father from having to worship his son’s approval.**

---

## 14. How to tell whether “relationship preservation” has crossed into compromise

Questions:

- Am I denying or renaming clear sin?
- Am I lying for the child?
- Am I silencing a harmed person?
- Am I funding what I believe God forbids?
- Am I granting church/platform access that exposes others?
- Am I ignoring younger children’s safety?
- Am I refusing necessary admonition solely because I fear rejection?
- Am I treating a prudential preference as God’s law?
- Can I state my position without rage or manipulation?
- Would I make the same moral judgment if this were not my child?

---

## 15. Article-ready formulations

> **Christian parents are not commanded to choose between holiness and love. They are commanded to love under the lordship of Christ.**

> **Sometimes the fear of losing a child makes a parent try to control him. Sometimes it makes the parent surrender every conviction. Both fears can become snares.**

> **Eli’s tragedy was not that he loved his sons. It was that, when love of sons collided with the honor of God, his conduct preferred the sons.**

> **The prodigal must never become the parent’s god—not even under the beautiful name “keeping the relationship.”**

---

## 16. Guards

Do not use this module to justify:

- unnecessary relational cruelty;
- prideful culture-war confrontation;
- parental micromanagement of adult preferences;
- breaking contact merely to prove zeal;
- declaring every disagreement a choice between child and Christ.

The conflict must concern real obedience/truth, not parental ego.

Remember Rom. 12:

`genuine love` + `abhorrence of evil` + `peace where possible` + `doing good` + `no vengeance`.
