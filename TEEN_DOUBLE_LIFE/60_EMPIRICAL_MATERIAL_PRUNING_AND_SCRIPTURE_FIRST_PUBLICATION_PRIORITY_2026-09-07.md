# Empirical Material Pruning and Scripture-First Publication Priority

**Status:** RESEARCH CONTROL / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** prevent the final article from becoming a psychology/public-health essay with biblical decoration. This ledger decides what empirical material deserves main-text space, what belongs in notes/sidebar, and what should be removed unless specifically needed.

---

## 0. Governing rule

Use empirical research where it answers questions Scripture does not quantify directly:

- how many;
- how often;
- through which platforms;
- at what age;
- what contemporary risks exist;
- what technical affordances changed;
- what safeguarding patterns are documented;
- whether a modern factual claim is true.

Do **not** use empirical research as the controlling authority for:

- what sin is;
- whether a conscience is hardening;
- whether a person is regenerate;
- whether worship is acceptable;
- whether repentance is genuine;
- whether parents have authority;
- whether children are morally responsible;
- whether the church must admonish;
- what mortification is.

Those categories belong first to Scripture.

---

# 1. KEEP IN MAIN ARTICLE / SERIES — empirical material that genuinely adds modern factual weight

## 1.1 Digital opportunity architecture

Keep.

Needed to substantiate that the modern environment changes **opportunity**, not human nature:

- near-continuous access;
- portability;
- privacy;
- direct messages/group chats;
- recommendation systems;
- easy production/redistribution of sexual images;
- night access;
- scale;
- low friction.

Preferred sources already in corpus:

- Pew teen technology/social media usage;
- WHO/HBSC continuous online contact;
- Ofcom children’s online experience;
- Children’s Commissioner pornography reports;
- UNICEF Disrupting Harm / 2026 technology-facilitated sexual harm;
- NCMEC/FBI sextortion.

Use these to answer **what changed structurally**.

Do not convert them into a moral coefficient such as `internet ×20 sin`.

---

## 1.2 Pornography access/exposure pathways

Keep, but concise.

Strong publication value:

- exposure can occur through mainstream platforms/messages, not only dedicated pornography websites;
- first exposure in surveyed populations often occurs during adolescence and sometimes earlier;
- contemporary access is private and portable;
- longitudinal outcome evidence is heterogeneous, so avoid universal causal chains.

Do not let prevalence statistics dominate the theological argument.

---

## 1.3 Sexting, image redistribution, sextortion

Keep strongly.

This is one area where current empirical/official data are indispensable because the threat architecture is genuinely new in scale/form.

Main distinctions:

- voluntary sexual sin;
- foolish/self-endangering behavior;
- coercion;
- image-based abuse;
- extortion;
- victimization.

Especially retain NCMEC/FBI/UNICEF evidence because it changes pastoral emergency response.

---

## 1.4 Intergenerational religious transmission

Keep, but subordinate to theology.

Use to refute practical fatalism:

> “Upbringing makes basically no difference because regeneration is sovereign.”

Empirical findings can show that family religious practice, parental importance, congregational involvement and relationship quality are associated with later religious continuity.

Guard:

`religiosity` ≠ `regeneration`.

The theological case that God uses means must stand independently from the statistics.

---

## 1.5 Parent-child connectedness / disclosure

Keep selectively.

Useful factual point:

- parents often know about adolescent life through voluntary disclosure, not surveillance alone;
- relational connectedness is associated with several protective outcomes;
- covert monitoring can have different relational effects from transparent supervision.

But the final article should frame this biblically as **truth, confession, parental knowledge, wise authority and love**, not in psychological jargon.

---

## 1.6 Peer influence / company

Keep only the strongest, simplest evidence.

The biblical case is already abundant:

- Prov 13:20;
- 1 Cor 15:33;
- Ps 1 patterns;
- 2 Tim 2:22;
- Puritan warnings about company.

Empirical meta-analyses can serve one sentence proving that peer effects are measurable, but should not be the engine of the section.

---

# 2. KEEP AS SECONDARY / FOOTNOTE / SIDEBAR

## 2.1 Psychological control vs behavioral control

Retain as secondary corroboration only.

Primary frame should be Eph 6:4 / Col 3:21 / Baxter:

- real discipline;
- no arbitrary provocation;
- tender love + severity against fault.

Modern terminology may help clarify, but is no longer necessary to structure the argument.

---

## 2.2 Boredom/loneliness and problematic technology use

Move to secondary status.

These can help explain why an empty, disconnected life makes digital escape attractive, but they are not central to biblical anthropology.

Avoid building a major causal section around correlations.

Possible use:

> “Modern research also suggests boredom/loneliness can accompany problematic technology use, which reinforces the practical need to replace merely removed screens with embodied family life, work, competence and community.”

Then move on.

---

## 2.3 Self-control / proactive temptation avoidance studies

Keep only if needed to illustrate a prudential point already established biblically by Matt 5, 2 Tim 2:22 and Owen.

Do not say science discovered the biblical principle.

---

## 2.4 Moral disengagement

Keep in research notes; use sparingly in publication.

Scriptural language—deceit of sin, suppressing truth, calling evil good, hardness—should dominate.

Empirical moral-disengagement work can corroborate that humans employ rationalizing mechanisms, but the term should not replace biblical self-deception.

---

## 2.5 False-self / self-presentation discrepancy research

Demote strongly.

It was useful during exploration but risks psychologizing a fundamentally moral/spiritual issue.

Final text should prefer:

- hypocrisy;
- walking in darkness;
- fear of man;
- deceit;
- double-mindedness where contextually warranted;
- false profession;
- hidden sin.

Only retain modern identity research if a precise factual claim requires it.

---

# 3. KEEP ONLY AS CLINICAL/SAFETY GUARDS

## 3.1 PPU / CSBD / “porn addiction” literature

Keep as a guard, not theological framework.

Purpose:

- do not diagnose every pornography user with a clinical disorder;
- distinguish moral guilt from diagnostic criteria;
- know when serious compulsivity, suicidality, trauma or psychiatric symptoms require competent professional involvement.

Never allow clinical classification to decide whether pornography is sinful.

---

## 3.2 Moral incongruence

Keep only to prevent false clinical claims.

Do not let it become:

> “Religious morals are what create the problem.”

It may explain variation in self-perceived addiction/distress; it cannot adjudicate God’s moral law.

---

# 4. USE MAINLY AS COUNTER-EVIDENCE / ANTI-SENSATIONALISM

## 4.1 Offline sexual behavior declines

Keep one compact factual correction.

Purpose:

> Do not claim that every measurable form of adolescent sexual behavior is worse today than in previous decades.

This actually strengthens credibility when arguing that **new digital forms of sexual opportunity/secrecy can expand while some offline indicators decline**.

Do not let this countertrend swallow the article.

---

## 4.2 Smartphone age studies with mixed findings

Keep mainly as a guard against a magical universal age.

The practical article should emphasize:

- powers granted;
- private/night access;
- child readiness;
- demonstrated weakness;
- parental supervision;
- staged responsibility.

Do not build a doctrinal recommendation around one observational age threshold.

---

# 5. REMOVE FROM MAINLINE UNLESS SPECIFICALLY NECESSARY

## 5.1 “Dopamine damage / burned receptors”

Remove/reject.

No publication value. It invites pseudo-neuroscience and is unnecessary for the biblical argument.

---

## 5.2 Universal pornography escalation

Remove as general claim.

Can say some users develop habitual/compulsive/escalating patterns; cannot say everyone inevitably seeks harder content.

---

## 5.3 “Brain switched off”

Remove as scientific language.

If used rhetorically, immediately translate into biblical categories:

- living by passions;
- diminished practical resistance;
- deceit/hardening;
- yielding to sin.

---

## 5.4 Generic adolescent neuroscience of reward

Usually remove.

The fact that adolescents can be sensitive to social reward is not needed to establish the moral seriousness of peer approval. Use only if a later article specifically examines adolescence as developmental stage.

---

## 5.5 Large blocks of psychological vocabulary

Terms to avoid as organizing headings unless strictly necessary:

- identity discrepancy;
- self-concept integration;
- autonomous internalization;
- moral disengagement;
- executive control;
- reward circuitry;
- sensation seeking.

Translate the useful factual insight into ordinary language and then govern it biblically.

---

# 6. Main article source-budget recommendation

A future flagship article should not visibly carry dozens of empirical citations per section.

Recommended hierarchy per major section:

1. **Biblical text in context**.
2. **One or two Reformed/Puritan voices** where historically useful.
3. **One strong empirical/official source** only where a modern factual claim needs verification.
4. Additional research stays in notes/research apparatus.

This keeps the article readable without weakening verification.

---

# 7. Empirical claims that remain high-value enough for final verification

Before publication, re-check exact wording/date/sample for:

1. WHO/HBSC continuous-online-contact figures.
2. Pew current teen online/social-media usage.
3. Children’s Commissioner pornography exposure findings and exact national sample.
4. Current UNICEF technology-facilitated sexual exploitation/abuse study.
5. Current NCMEC sextortion numbers and report-vs-prevalence distinction.
6. Sexting prevalence meta-analysis definitions/age ranges.
7. Pornography longitudinal systematic review heterogeneity.
8. Intergenerational religion transmission figures.
9. Parent-child connectedness meta-analysis exact outcomes.
10. Peer influence meta-analysis effect-size wording.

Everything else is optional unless a particular article section needs it.

---

# 8. The final governing sentence

> **Science can help us describe the new roads, speeds, doors and risks through which temptation now travels. Scripture must tell us what the traveler is, what sin is, why the road is evil, what repentance is, and where life is found.**
