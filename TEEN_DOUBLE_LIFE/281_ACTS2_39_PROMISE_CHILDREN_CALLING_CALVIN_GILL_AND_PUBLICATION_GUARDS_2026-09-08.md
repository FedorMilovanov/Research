# Acts 2:39 — Promise, Children, Calling
## Calvin, Gill, and Publication Guards

**Status:** RESEARCH / TARGETED EXEGESIS / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `273–280`

## 1. Why the verse matters

Acts 2:39 sits at Pentecost immediately after Peter commands repentance and baptism. Its wording preserves explicit intergenerational language:

`to you -> to your children -> to all who are far off -> as many as the Lord our God calls`.

Any serious Baptist account must explain why `your children` is named. Any serious paedobaptist account must explain how this promise language yields infant baptism prior to personal reception of the Word.

## 2. Calvin’s reading

Calvin sees `you and your children` as continuity with Abrahamic covenant promise. In his broader theology:

- believers’ children are included in covenant privilege;
- Pentecost does not announce their expulsion;
- baptism corresponds to circumcision as covenant sign;
- therefore children of believers should receive the sign.

### Strength

This reading takes the Jewish covenantal audience and the intergenerational formula seriously.

### Dependency

The baptism conclusion depends on Calvin’s broader continuity premise. The verse itself does not narrate infant baptism.

## 3. Gill’s reading

Gill interprets the final calling clause as the controlling limitation:

- among `you`;
- among `your children`;
- among `those afar off`;
- the saving promise belongs to those God calls.

He therefore denies that natural descent gives the promise or baptismal right automatically.

### Strength

This integrates Romans 9-style distinction between natural descent and children of promise/effectual call.

### Difficulty

If the final clause simply erases every distinctive force of `your children`, Peter’s explicit intergenerational wording risks becoming rhetorically under-explained. A Baptist reading should preserve real significance for children even if it denies automatic baptism.

## 4. Better Baptist formulation for this project

A stronger formulation than “children means only future believers” is:

> Peter announces the gospel promise in a deliberately intergenerational horizon: it reaches his Jewish hearers, their descendants/children, and the far-off Gentiles. Yet its saving realization is tied to the Lord’s call, and the immediate baptism narrative explicitly identifies receivers of the word. Therefore Christian parents have strong reason to place their children continually under this promise and summons, but the verse by itself does not establish administering baptism before personal faith.

This gives `children` positive weight without conceding the sacramental inference.

## 5. Verse 41 control

Luke says those who received Peter’s word were baptized.

This supplies positive credobaptist pattern evidence:

`WORD RECEIVED -> BAPTIZED -> ADDED`.

But it should not be overclaimed as a direct exclusion statement about infants, since Luke’s immediate focus is the adult Pentecost audience.

## 6. Relation to Acts 3:25 / Abrahamic promise

Paedobaptists rightly note that Acts retains covenant/seed language in apostolic preaching. Baptists should not act as though Abraham disappears from the New Testament.

The real question is how Abrahamic promise is fulfilled in Christ:

- genealogical covenant administration continuing into believers’ children?
- or promise fulfilled through Christ and received by faith, with offspring included as they are called/believe?

This cannot be settled by deleting either Abraham or personal calling.

## 7. Project use

### Strong use

- children remain explicit objects within apostolic promise horizon;
- gospel is not an adulthood-only summons;
- Christian parents should address children as present hearers of promise/command;
- family evangelism/catechesis is not optional.

### Controlled use

- supports paedobaptist continuity argument;
- supports Baptist call/profession argument.

### Forbidden use

- “Acts 2:39 directly commands infant baptism”;
- “Acts 2:39 proves children of believers have no special relation to the promise until adult conversion”;
- “the calling clause makes the word children irrelevant.”
