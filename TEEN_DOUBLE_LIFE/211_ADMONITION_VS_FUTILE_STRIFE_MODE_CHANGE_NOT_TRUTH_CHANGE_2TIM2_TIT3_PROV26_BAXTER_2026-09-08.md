# Admonition vs futile strife — MODE CHANGE, NOT TRUTH CHANGE

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** RESEARCH-ONLY / EXEGETICAL-PASTORAL CONTROL / NOT PUBLICATION APPROVAL  
**Series:** `TEEN_DOUBLE_LIFE` / adult-child & family-government companion  
**Purpose:** answer the difficult pastoral question: when should parents/church continue warning an adult child or professing believer who defends serious sin, and when should repeated conversation stop becoming a daily courtroom without changing the moral verdict?

---

## 0. Executive finding

The biblical pattern is neither:

> `say it once, then never speak again`

nor:

> `repeat the same argument at every contact until the sinner yields`.

Scripture gives both commands:

- continue patient, gentle correction where truth may still be heard;
- avoid foolish controversies and quarrels that merely reproduce strife;
- answer folly when silence would strengthen deception or harm others;
- do not answer in a way that makes the counselor participate in the fool's manner;
- in ecclesial cases, repeated formal admonition can reach a point where avoidance/rejection is required;
- natural family duties are not thereby erased.

**Locked synthesis:**

> **The content of God's verdict does not change merely because the sinner has heard it many times. The mode, frequency, length, and setting of the parent's admonition may and sometimes should change when repetition no longer serves truth but only fuels strife.**

Project shorthand remains:

`MODE CHANGE, NOT TRUTH CHANGE`.

---

# 1. 2 Timothy 2:23–26 — avoid quarrels while continuing correction

## 1.1. Immediate context

Paul tells Timothy to avoid foolish/ignorant controversies because they generate quarrels (v. 23).

He immediately refuses the false alternative:

> avoiding quarrels does not mean refusing all correction.

The Lord's servant must instead be:

- non-quarrelsome;
- gentle;
- able to teach;
- patient under evil/opposition;
- correcting opponents with meekness;
- dependent on God to grant repentance and knowledge of truth.

### P0

The passage distinguishes:

`CONTENDING FOR TRUTH`

from

`QUARRELSOME STRIVING`.

The parent/pastor does not prove faithfulness by maximizing conversational friction.

## 1.2. Calvin control

Calvin reads the passage as a prohibition of contentious questions that generate fighting while still requiring meek correction of resistance.

Primary/near-primary source:
- https://ccel.org/c/calvin/comment3/comm_vol43/htm/iv.iii.v.htm

## 1.3. Gill control

Gill says the servant of the Lord must not strive over profitless words in a litigious manner or for mastery, while he may and ought to contend for the faith. He also emphasizes patient instruction of stubborn opponents and God's sovereign gift of repentance.

Source:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/2_timothy/2.htm

### Family transfer

This text directly concerns Timothy's ministerial conduct, not a special parent-child procedure. But its moral wisdom transfers strongly:

> **A father is not more faithful because he can keep the same fight alive for three hours.**

The question is whether he is still **teaching/correcting in hope**, or merely **striving for mastery**.

# 2. Proverbs 26:4–5 — there is a time to answer and a time not to answer

The paired sayings deliberately create situational wisdom:

- v. 4: do not answer a fool according to his folly, lest you become like him;
- v. 5: answer a fool according to his folly, lest he become wise in his own eyes.

## 2.1. Gill's useful synthesis

Gill explains that sometimes a fool should not be answered, especially in his own railing/foolish manner. At other times he should be answered wisely:

- where there is hope of doing him good;
- where others may be harmed by his claims;
- where God's glory, truth, or the church's good requires an answer;
- where silence would let him imagine his falsehood unanswerable.

Source:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/proverbs/26.htm

### P0

> **Silence and speech can both be wise or foolish depending on purpose, manner, audience, and likely fruit.**

This is a direct guard against mechanical family rules.

Not every provocative message requires a theological essay.

Not every lie should be left unanswered.

---

# 3. Proverbs 29:9 and Ecclesiastes 3:7 — some disputes have no conversational resolution

Proverbs 29:9 portrays the wise man contending with a fool and reaching no quiet resolution.

Ecclesiastes 3:7 preserves a general wisdom category: a time to keep silence and a time to speak.

These are wisdom texts, not formulas assigning a fixed number of warnings.

### Transfer

When every conversation follows the same cycle:

`parent names fact`
→ `child denies/reframes`
→ `parent re-proves every historical detail`
→ `child counters with old grievance`
→ `three-hour argument`
→ `no new admission / no new action / no new truth`

then continuing the **same format** may be foolish even though the parent's moral judgment remains correct.

# 4. Titus 3:9–11 — important but NOT a private family two-warning algorithm

## 4.1. Context

Paul first commands avoidance of foolish controversies, genealogies, quarrels, and disputes about the law because they are unprofitable.

Then he addresses the divisive/factious person after first and second admonition.

## 4.2. Calvin's jurisdiction guard

Calvin explicitly treats this as solemn ecclesial/ministerial admonition under church authority, not as any private person's generic warning counter.

Sources:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43.v.v.iv.html
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/titus/3.htm

## 4.3. Gill's similar guard

Gill likewise reads the admonition as public/church admonition and the offender as heretical/schismatic and divisive.

Source:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/titus/3-10.htm

### P0

Do **not** publish:

> `Warn your adult son twice; after that Scripture commands no further contact.`

Titus 3 does not establish that rule.

### Valid transfer

It does establish a broader ecclesial principle:

> **Christian correction is not required to remain an endless debate with a person whose settled activity is division and obstinate resistance.**

But the actor, jurisdiction, offense, and consequence must be preserved.

# 5. Matthew 7:6 — sacred truth is not to be weaponized into endless contemptuous exchanges

Jesus forbids giving what is holy to dogs / casting pearls before swine where the response is trampling and attack.

This text has often been misused as permission to write people off rapidly. That is unsafe.

Its legitimate pastoral force is narrower:

- holy things are not to be endlessly offered in a mode that only generates contempt and profanation;
- prudence may alter how truth is delivered;
- the text does not authorize hatred or declare a family member beyond God's saving power.

### Guard

`CURRENT RESISTANCE ≠ FINAL REPROBATION`.

Parents do not know God's decree.

---

# 6. Jesus' own answering pattern — not every hostile question receives the demanded format

The Gospels repeatedly show Jesus:

- answering sincere or morally important questions directly;
- answering hostile traps with counterquestions;
- exposing hidden premises rather than accepting the opponent's framing;
- sometimes refusing to give the demanded answer where the question itself was corrupt.

Examples for later exegesis:

- Matthew 21:23–27;
- Matthew 22:15–46;
- John 8 controversies;
- Luke 20.

### Transfer

A parent may say:

> “We have already answered whether we believe this conduct is sinful. I will gladly talk about what repentance, practical next steps, safety, work, housing, or reconciliation would look like. I am not going to spend tonight re-litigating whether God calls sexual immorality sin.”

This is not evasion.

It can be **refusal to accept a false conversational frame**.

# 7. Richard Baxter — persistence, differentiation, and church discipline

Baxter supplies a very useful balance.

## 7.1. Persistent exhortation

In *The Saints' Everlasting Rest*, Baxter urges frequent exhortation because one warning often does not awaken a sinner. But he also insists on prudence:

- choose a fitting time;
- account for the person's condition/temper;
- deal differently with obstinate, timid, ignorant, and convinced people;
- ground reproof in God's authority rather than personal irritation.

Source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/baxter/saints_rest.iii.IX.html

## 7.2. Reformed Pastor — private reproof before discipline, then consequences for impenitence

Baxter says ministers must personally admonish those living offensively/impenitently and ordinarily attempt private reproof before public discipline. But if impenitence persists, discipline itself becomes part of pastoral faithfulness.

Source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor.all.html

### P0

Baxter does not support either:

`ONE WARNING ONLY`

or

`INFINITE PRIVATE ARGUING WITH NO CHANGE OF MODE`.

He supports **persistent means ordered toward an issue**, with escalating/changed pastoral action when impenitence becomes established.

# 8. A practical diagnostic: is this still admonition or already strife?

The following is **pastoral application**, not a direct biblical checklist.

### Indicators the conversation may still be fruitful

- the child asks a real question rather than only setting traps;
- admits some facts without coercion;
- distinguishes his sin from parental sins rather than using one to erase the other;
- asks what repentance/restoration would require;
- accepts concrete consequences even while emotionally struggling;
- requests Scripture or counsel;
- returns later to something previously said;
- expresses uncertainty rather than only defiant certainty;
- is willing to meet with a mature third party;
- shows new truth, not merely new rhetoric.

### Indicators the **same conversational format** is becoming futile

- every conversation is used to erase the moral issue through semantic debate;
- known facts are repeatedly denied despite settled evidence;
- any admission lasts only until access/money/freedom is restored;
- parental sins are invoked solely as immunity from discussing the child's sin;
- the child demands that relationship be conditioned on parents affirming his moral account;
- conversations predictably end in mutual rage, insults, threats, or hours of circularity;
- new evidence/new questions are absent;
- the only objective is to force the parent to say “you were right / this was not sin.”

### Result

The wise response can become:

- shorter;
- less frequent;
- more written than verbal;
- more fact-specific;
- more mediated;
- focused on practical consequences rather than re-proving settled doctrine;
- open to genuine questions;
- closed to endless re-litigation.

# 9. What “mode change” may look like

## Stage A — discovery / early crisis

Usually high information need:

- establish facts;
- distinguish voluntary sin from coercion/abuse;
- clarify safety;
- name sin plainly;
- hear the child's account;
- correct major lies;
- explain immediate household consequences.

## Stage B — repeated deception / false repentance

More structured:

- less reliance on promises;
- more attention to fruit;
- concrete conditions;
- fewer repetitive lectures;
- consequences tied to conduct;
- specific restoration path.

## Stage C — defended rebellion / adult departure

Possible mode:

> “You know our conviction and why. I am not going to pretend this is good. I also do not want every contact to be a courtroom. I am willing to talk honestly, help where helping does not finance sin, and discuss repentance/reconciliation whenever you want. I will not repeatedly debate whether God calls this sin.”

This preserves:

`TRUTH + RELATION + OPEN DOOR`

without preserving:

`ENDLESS ARGUMENT + FALSE NORMALCY`.

## Stage D — new openness / repentance evidence

Mode changes again:

- listen more;
- distinguish grief from repentance but do not cynically pre-judge;
- ask concrete questions;
- rebuild truthful disclosure;
- retain proportionate consequences;
- do not punish the person for finally bringing hidden things to light;
- restoration can be gradual without turning repentance into justification-by-probation.

# 10. Important guard: silence must not become passive-aggressive punishment

A parent can misuse “I won't argue” to mean:

- icy contempt;
- strategic silence intended to hurt;
- refusing basic courtesy;
- withholding necessary information;
- making the child guess what would permit reconciliation;
- emotional manipulation.

That is not the wisdom of Proverbs 26.

A changed mode should be **explained truthfully** where possible.

Example principle:

> “I am not refusing you as my son/daughter. I am refusing to repeat the same quarrel in the same form. My conviction has not changed, and my willingness to hear honest questions has not changed.”

---

# 11. Important guard: “God gives repentance” does not justify passivity

2 Timothy 2:25 simultaneously teaches:

- God gives repentance;
- Timothy must instruct.

Therefore sovereignty excludes both:

- manipulation (`I can argue you into a new heart`);
- fatalism (`there is no point saying anything`).

The proper frame is:

> **use the means God commands without pretending the means are sovereign.**

# 13. Product-safe theses

Strong enough for future publication:

1. Scripture commands both correction and avoidance of quarrelsome/foolish controversy.
2. Faithfulness is not measured by how long a parent can keep an argument going.
3. Parents may stop re-litigating a settled moral question while remaining open to real questions, repentance, and reconciliation.
4. God's sovereignty in repentance is a reason for patient means and a limit on manipulative control.
5. The frequency/form of admonition is a wisdom question sensitive to fruit, audience, risk, and status.
6. Titus 3:10 is an ecclesial text about a divisive person, not a universal “two warnings then cut off your adult child” rule.
7. Silence can be cowardice or wisdom; speech can be faithfulness or strife. Proverbs 26 requires discernment.

# 14. Claims that must NOT be published

- `The Bible commands parents to warn an adult child exactly twice.`
- `If someone rejects counsel repeatedly, God has certainly hardened him beyond repentance.`
- `Not debating means approving.`
- `Continuing contact means compromising.`

- `Once a child has heard the gospel, further loving invitation is pointless.`
- `A parent who keeps trying to speak is necessarily trying to play the Holy Spirit.`
- `A parent who stops circular arguments is necessarily abandoning the child.`

# 15. Locked pastoral synthesis

> **Tell the truth clearly enough that the child does not have to guess what you believe. Repeat it when love, new circumstances, new questions, the protection of others, or the glory of God requires repetition. Refuse the form of conversation when its only fruit is circular strife. Keep the door open to truth, repentance, practical help that does not fund sin, and reconciliation. Trust God with what no parent can produce: repentance and a new heart.**

And more compactly:

> **Do not change the truth to preserve the relationship. Do not destroy the relationship merely to prove that the truth has not changed. Change the mode when wisdom requires it.**
