# Admonition: MODE CHANGE ≠ TRUTH CHANGE

**Status:** EXEGESIS / PASTORAL SYNTHESIS / RESEARCH ONLY  
**Date:** 2026-09-07

---

# Question

When a rebellious teenager or adult child has heard the same truth many times, when does continuing to speak become faithful persistence, and when does it become fruitless quarrelling?

The project must reject both slogans:

> `Never stop confronting every time you speak.`

and

> `Once an adult child resists, say nothing more because only God can change him.`

Scripture gives a more differentiated pattern.

---

# 1. 2 Timothy 2:23–26 — do not quarrel, yet keep instructing

Primary Calvin source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43/calcom43.iv.iii.v.html

Paul puts several requirements together:

- avoid foolish controversies that breed quarrels;
- the Lord’s servant must not be quarrelsome;
- be gentle;
- be able to teach;
- patiently endure evil;
- correct opponents with gentleness;
- hope that God may grant repentance.

Calvin emphasizes that even apparently nearly hopeless people should still be approached with meekness because conversion is God’s work.

### Project synthesis

The opposite of quarrelling is **not silence**.

The opposite of silence is **not endless argument**.

Biblical correction can be:
- brief;
- calm;
- infrequent;
- direct;
- invitation-based;
- repeated when a real hearing opens.

### Strong line

> **A parent can stop arguing without pretending the argument was won by sin.**

---

# 2. Titus 3:9–11 — withdrawal exists, but context matters

Primary Calvin source:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom43.v.v.iv.html

Calvin understands the first/second admonition in formal ministerial/church context concerning a divisive/heretical person.

Therefore Titus 3 **cannot** function as:

`conversation 1`  
`conversation 2`  
`→ permanently stop contacting your adult child`.

That is a category error.

### What the passage legitimately contributes

It proves that Christian faithfulness does not require endless engagement with a person who has become self-condemned in persistent divisiveness.

It supports the broad category:

> there are times when further argumentative engagement should cease or materially change.

But family application is analogical/prudential, not direct procedural transfer.

---

# 3. Proverbs on the scoffer — wisdom requires audience discernment

Important texts:
- Prov. 9:7–9;
- Prov. 23:9;
- Prov. 26:4–5;
- Prov. 29:9.

Proverbs intentionally refuses a single speech technique for every fool.

Prov. 26:4–5 is especially useful:

- sometimes do not answer a fool according to his folly;
- sometimes answer a fool lest he be wise in his own eyes.

This is not contradiction. It is wisdom requiring discernment of circumstance and effect.

### Family application

There are times when another hour-long confrontation:
- gives the rebel a stage;
- multiplies anger;
- trains both sides in scripted hostility;
- produces no new truth;
- allows the child to make parental tone the entire issue.

A parent may then move from **debate mode** to **testimony mode**:

> `You know what I believe about this. I love you. I am not changing what God calls this. I will not argue for three hours. If you want an honest conversation or help turning from it, I am ready.`

That is not moral surrender.

---

# 4. Romans 12:9–21 — changed mode must remain genuine love

A changed mode may never become revenge disguised as `boundaries`.

Romans 12 keeps together:
- love without hypocrisy;
- abhorrence of evil;
- honourable conduct;
- peace as far as it depends on us;
- no personal vengeance;
- actual good toward an enemy.

Therefore:

`stop debating` ≠ `start punishing emotionally`.

`refuse money for sin` ≠ `refuse humane good`.

`do not permit residence` ≠ `you are dead to me`.

`do not answer abusive messages immediately` ≠ `I hope you suffer`.

---

# 5. James 5:19–20 — restorative initiative remains a biblical good

James praises turning a wanderer from error.

This destroys another overcorrection:

> `If you ever initiate a conversation to bring someone back, you are trying to be the Holy Spirit.`

No. Scripture itself calls human beings to seek restoration.

But human initiative is a **means**, not sovereign regeneration.

### Reformed synthesis

> **God alone grants repentance; therefore we neither manipulate as if regeneration were ours nor become passive as if God never used human admonition.**

---

# 6. Practical mode ladder

This is pastoral synthesis, not a biblical statute.

### MODE A — full conversation

Use when the person:
- asks genuine questions;
- admits uncertainty;
- listens;
- shows grief;
- seeks help;
- is willing to examine Scripture.

### MODE B — concise admonition

Use when:
- truth is already known;
- resistance is predictable;
- a clear word is appropriate but extended debate would be fruitless.

Example:

> `I love you, but I cannot tell you this is acceptable before God. You know why. If you want help turning from it, I am here.`

### MODE C — answer only when opened

Use when every unsolicited conversation becomes a fight, but ordinary relationship remains possible.

Continue:
- prayer;
- ordinary kindness;
- truthful answers;
- refusal to validate evil.

### MODE D — restricted communication

May become necessary when contact itself is:
- abusive;
- threatening;
- sexually predatory;
- manipulative around minors;
- relentlessly obscene;
- used for extortion or harassment.

This is a different issue from mere theological disagreement.

### MODE E — emergency/safeguarding contact

Regardless of ordinary relational mode, danger can require action:
- self-harm risk;
- exploitation;
- disappearance under suspicious conditions;
- criminal coercion;
- danger to minors.

Do not use `respecting adulthood` to ignore an actual emergency.

---

# 7. What determines a mode change?

Not a fixed number of conversations.

Pastoral questions:

1. Has any new truth actually been introduced?
2. Is the person listening or merely rehearsing defenses?
3. Does the conversation produce clarity or only heat?
4. Is continued speaking becoming an excuse not to take necessary action?
5. Is silence becoming cowardice because the parent fears rejection?
6. Is frequent correction provoking needless anger because the truth is already unmistakably known?
7. Is there a fresh opening created by suffering, questions, fear, guilt, or new circumstances?
8. Is there danger requiring action rather than another theological talk?

---

# 8. Strong publication lines

> **Changing the frequency of admonition is not changing the moral verdict.**

> **You do not have to preach the same sermon at every dinner in order to remain faithful to the truth.**

> **Persistent love is not measured by the number of arguments you can survive.**

> **A closed mouth can be cowardice, but an endlessly fighting mouth can also be folly. Wisdom asks what faithfulness requires now.**

> **The goal of admonition is not that the parent has the last word. The goal is that the child hears the truth before God.**

---

# 9. Audit guards

Do not use this module to justify:
- passive indifference;
- never naming known sexual sin;
- fear-driven avoidance;
- total no-contact for ordinary disagreement;
- treating Titus 3 as a parental countdown;
- harassment under the label `persistent admonition`.

Final balance:

> **Truth remains fixed. Love remains real. The mode may change.**
