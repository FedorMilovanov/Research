# Conscience category map — divine command, wisdom, house rule, parental preference, civil law

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** RESEARCH-ONLY / Scripture-first / confessional / Part II conscience overlay  
**Series:** `TEEN_DOUBLE_LIFE`

---

## 0. Why this matters

A major source of confusion in Christian homes is the collapse of different kinds of authority into one sentence:

> `God says so.`

Sometimes God **does** say so directly.

Sometimes Scripture supplies a principle requiring wisdom.

Sometimes a parent makes a lawful household rule in an indifferent matter.

Sometimes the parent merely has a preference.

Sometimes civil law creates a real external obligation.

If these categories are flattened, two opposite errors follow:

1. **parental absolutism** — every preference is presented as direct divine law;
2. **autonomist antinomianism** — anything not stated verbatim in Scripture is treated as non-binding for a child.

Both are wrong.

### Locked center

> **THE DUTY TO OBEY A LAWFUL PARENTAL RULE MAY BE DIVINE EVEN WHEN THE SPECIFIC RULE IS NOT ITSELF A UNIVERSAL DIVINE MORAL LAW.**

> **PARENTAL AUTHORITY CAN BIND CONDUCT WITHIN ITS LAWFUL SPHERE; IT CANNOT TURN HUMAN PREFERENCE INTO GOD'S UNIVERSAL COMMAND.**

---

# 1. Confessional guard — God alone is Lord of the conscience

1689 London Baptist Confession, chapter 21.2:

> God alone is Lord of the conscience and requires no implicit faith or absolute/blind obedience to human commands.

Sources:
- https://1689baptistconfession.com/confession/21-of-christian-liberty-and-liberty-of-conscience/
- https://founders.org/library/chapter-21-christian-liberty-and-liberty-of-conscience/

The confession must not be weaponized to abolish all lawful human authority.

The same Reformed moral tradition explicitly affirms obedience to **lawful commands** of superiors.

Westminster Larger Catechism 127:

- inferiors owe willing obedience to lawful commands and counsels.

WLC 130 simultaneously says superiors sin by:

- commanding unlawful things;
- commanding what inferiors lack power to perform;
- correcting unduly;
- acting unjustly, indiscreetly, rigorously or remissly.

Source:
- https://www.wscal.edu/westminster-larger-catechism/

### P0

`LIBERTY OF CONSCIENCE ≠ LIBERTY FROM EVERY LAWFUL HUMAN AUTHORITY`.

`LAWFUL AUTHORITY ≠ POWER TO CREATE NEW UNIVERSAL MORAL LAW`.

---

# 2. Particular Baptist / Puritan practical synthesis

John Gill on Ephesians 6:1 and Colossians 3:20 says children obey parents:

- in lawful, just and good things;
- not in things sinful;
- also in indifferent matters;
- even when difficult/disagreeable.

Sources:
- https://www.biblestudytools.com/tmba/ephesians/6-1.html
- https://www.biblestudytools.com/commentaries/gills-exposition-of-the-bible/colossians-3-20.html

William Gouge likewise gives parents broad authority in lawful/indifferent matters while limiting obedience by God's contrary command.

Source:
- https://christianreader.app/books/of-domestical-duties/07-treatise-5-duties-of-children

### Key synthesis

A parent can lawfully say:

> `In this home, phones stay out of bedrooms overnight.`

The specific 10 p.m. / kitchen-counter rule is not a universal sixth/ seventh/tenth-commandment ordinance for all Christians.

But a minor child under lawful household government may still sin by defying that rule because God commands children to obey lawful parental authority.

### Locked formula

`SPECIFIC RULE NOT UNIVERSAL DIVINE LAW` **does not imply** `CHILD MAY DISOBEY IT`.

# 3. Category A — DIRECT DIVINE MORAL COMMAND / PROHIBITION

These are matters where Scripture itself morally binds the person.

Examples relevant to this series:

- do not lie;
- do not steal;
- sexual immorality is sin;
- drunkenness is sin;
- honor father and mother;
- do not murder/assault;
- do not bear false witness;
- do not participate in idolatry;
- pursue truth and holiness.

### Parent's role

Parents:

- teach;
- enforce lawful household consequences;
- protect;
- admonish;
- model;
- distinguish repentance from mere detection.

But parents do **not create** these duties.

### P0

`PARENT AGREEMENT DOES NOT MAKE SIN HOLY`.

`PARENT DISAGREEMENT DOES NOT MAKE GOD'S COMMAND OPTIONAL`.

A parent cannot grant moral permission to lie, steal, fornicate, abuse or worship an idol.

---

# 4. Category B — BIBLICAL WISDOM / PRUDENTIAL APPLICATION

Scripture supplies real moral ends and wisdom principles, but does not always specify one modern implementation.

Examples:

- how much screen time;
- whether this specific friend group is prudent;
- whether a particular smartphone/social platform is wise at a given age;
- exact curfew;
- exact dating/courtship procedures;
- exact clothing measurements used to express modesty;
- which educational option is best;
- how much privacy is appropriate after a history of deception;
- whether a teen should take a particular job.

### P0

`WISDOM ≠ MORAL RELATIVISM`.

Some choices are genuinely wiser or more dangerous than others.

But:

`WISE APPLICATION ≠ INSPIRED UNIVERSAL PROCEDURE`.

Parents may make binding prudential rules for dependent minors within their sphere, but should distinguish:

> `I believe Scripture requires this exact universal technique`

from:

> `God requires holiness/wisdom; as your parent I judge this rule prudent for you now.`

---

# 5. Category C — LAWFUL HOUSEHOLD RULE

A household needs order beyond verbatim biblical case law.

Examples:

- dishes go in a particular place;
- device charging occurs in the kitchen;
- family leaves for church at a set time;
- no shoes on a certain floor;
- chores assigned by schedule;
- guests require parental permission;
- a minor cannot use a parent-owned car without permission;
- quiet hours apply after a set time.

These can be morally binding **as parental commands** while the child is under that jurisdiction.

### P0

`HOUSE RULE ≠ UNIVERSAL CHRISTIAN ETHIC`.

A child visiting another faithful Christian family need not conclude their different kitchen/device routine is sinful.

### Critical conscience distinction

If a child breaks a lawful household rule, the possible sin is not necessarily:

> `the underlying neutral act is inherently evil`.

It may be:

> `I knowingly disobeyed lawful parental authority.`

This distinction protects both authority and conscience.

---

# 6. Category D — PARENTAL PREFERENCE / COUNSEL

Parents frequently have desires that they have **not** actually made commands.

Examples:

- preferred hairstyle;
- preferred music genre where content is otherwise lawful;
- preference for one sport/hobby;
- preference for one clothing color/style within lawful modesty;
- preference for one academic subject;
- hope that a child chooses a particular profession;
- preference for a particular non-moral social custom.

### P0

`PARENTAL PREFERENCE ≠ AUTOMATIC COMMAND`.

Parents should say what they mean:

- `I command this`;
- `This is our house rule`;
- `I strongly advise this`;
- `I prefer this`;
- `I am open to your judgment here`.

Ambiguity invites either false guilt or strategic exploitation.

### Equal guard

A child should not contemptuously dismiss parental counsel merely because it is not a command.

Honor includes taking wise counsel seriously.

But counsel should not be retrospectively converted into command after the child chooses differently.

---

# 7. Category E — CIVIL LAW / EXTERNAL LAWFUL AUTHORITY

Some household rules overlap with civil law or institutional rules.

Examples:

- seat belts;
- age restrictions;
- driving/licensing law;
- school attendance requirements where applicable;
- property rules;
- lawful reporting/safeguarding duties;
- employment rules.

Parents may rightly enforce these.

But legal status and exact obligations vary by jurisdiction and time.

### P0

`PARENTAL RULE ≠ CIVIL LAW`.

`CIVIL LAW ≠ AUTOMATIC DIVINE MORAL IDEAL IN EVERY DETAIL`.

`LAWFUL CIVIL AUTHORITY STILL MATTERS`.

Current-law verification is required before Product gives operational legal advice.

# 8. Why 1689 liberty of conscience does not erase Category C

A superficial reading of 1689 21.2 could be misused:

> `This rule is not written in Scripture, therefore nobody can tell me to obey it.`

That reading would make ordinary family government, civil government, employment and many church-order decisions impossible.

The better distinction is:

- **God alone defines ultimate moral/religious obligation of conscience**;
- God himself institutes lawful human authorities with bounded spheres;
- therefore a lawful human command can create a derivative duty of obedience because God commands respect for that authority;
- the human authority still may not claim divine infallibility or invent new universal articles of faith/morality.

### P0

`DERIVATIVE DUTY OF OBEDIENCE ≠ DIVINIZATION OF THE HUMAN RULE`.

---

# 9. Clothing example — moral principle vs metric

This is a high-conflict domain.

Possible layers:

### Divine moral layer

- modesty;
- chastity;
- refusal of sexually provocative self-presentation;
- humility / not making status display an idol.

### Prudential layer

- how those principles apply in a given garment/context/culture;
- age and setting;
- fit/transparency/function.

### Household-rule layer

Parents may set concrete limits for a dependent minor.

### Preference layer

Parents may simply dislike a lawful fashion.

### P0

Do not say:

> `The Bible says skirts must be exactly X centimeters.`

unless Scripture actually says that — it does not.

A parent may still set a concrete family rule without pretending the metric itself is universal revelation.

---

# 10. Music example — content, association, wisdom and preference

Do not flatten all music questions into:

> `secular = sin`

or:

> `music is morally neutral regardless of content/context`.

Possible analysis:

1. **Direct moral content** — blasphemy, pornographic glorification, incitement to evil.
2. **Prudential pattern** — a genre/scene/artist may predictably function as a gateway to corrupt community/content for this child.
3. **House rule** — parents may prohibit certain media while the child is a dependent minor.
4. **Preference** — parents may simply dislike a lawful style.

### P0

`PARENTAL DISLIKE ≠ PROOF OF SIN`.

`NOT INTRINSICALLY SINFUL ≠ PARENT MUST ALLOW IT`.

---

# 11. Device example — an especially clean category demonstration

### Direct divine law

Pornography, sexual exploitation, lying, theft and malicious cruelty remain sin regardless of device policy.

### Wisdom

A particular teen with prior secret sexual use may need tighter access than a sibling without that history.

### House rule

`Phones charge downstairs after 22:00.`

### Preference

Parent prefers Android over iPhone.

### Civil/safeguarding

Certain sexual-image conduct involving minors may trigger criminal/safeguarding law; exact jurisdiction must be verified.

### P0

Category clarity prevents both:

- `phone upstairs after 22:00 is inherently sexual immorality`;
- `because the Bible says nothing about charging locations, the child may ignore the rule`.

Both are false.

# 12. Adolescents themselves naturally distinguish domains — descriptive empirical support

Social-domain research repeatedly finds that adolescents and parents distinguish moral, conventional, prudential, multifaceted and personal issues and disagree especially around personal/multifaceted boundaries.

Sources:
- Smetana JG, Asquith P. *Adolescents' and parents' conceptions of parental authority and personal autonomy.* Child Development. 1994. PubMed PMID 7956471.
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7956471/

- Cumsille P et al. *Heterogeneity and change in the patterning of adolescents' perceptions of the legitimacy of parental authority.* Child Development. 2009. `N=2,611` Chilean adolescents.
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19467001/

- Gingo M, Roded AD, Turiel E. *Authority, Autonomy, and Deception.* 2017.
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29152872/

### Evidence guard

This literature is descriptive social-developmental theory.

It does **not** define biblical jurisdiction.

Safe use:

> category distinctions are psychologically salient, and perceived illegitimacy of authority can interact with deception/conflict; therefore parents should be especially careful not to confuse moral law, prudence and preference.

Do not import `personal domain = biblically sovereign child zone`.

# 13. A practical five-question classifier

When a parent wants to say `you must`, ask:

### 1. Is this directly commanded/forbidden by God?
If yes, cite the actual text/principle accurately.

### 2. Is this a wisdom judgment applying a biblical end?
If yes, say it is a judgment and explain the risk/reason.

### 3. Is this a lawful household rule within present parental jurisdiction?
If yes, state it plainly as a house rule.

### 4. Is this actually only my preference/counsel?
If yes, do not manufacture a command merely because the child chose differently.

### 5. Is another lawful authority involved?
If civil/school/employer/church/safeguarding rules matter, identify the actual jurisdiction rather than attributing everything to parental authority.

---

# 14. Child-side classifier

Teach the teen to ask internally:

- `Is God directly forbidding this?`
- `Is my parent making a wisdom judgment?`
- `Is this a lawful house rule I must obey while under their government?`
- `Is this counsel/preference I should honor but may have growing discretion over?`
- `Is civil/church/school authority also involved?`

### P0

The purpose is **not** to create a teenage courtroom for evading obedience.

The purpose is to form a conscience capable of adult moral reasoning.

---

# 15. Double-life relevance

Category confusion can feed hypocrisy in two ways.

### Overbinding

When parents label every preference `sin`, the child may discover one false attribution and begin to doubt true moral prohibitions as merely another family taste.

### Underbinding

When the child says `show me the exact Bible verse for 10 p.m. curfew` as though lawful parental authority has no derivative force, biblical honor is hollowed out.

### Locked synthesis

`DO NOT CALL PREFERENCE GOD'S LAW.`

`DO NOT CALL LAWFUL PARENTAL GOVERNMENT MERE PREFERENCE.`

---

# 16. Parent repentance when categories were confused

Possible repair:

> `I told you God forbade this exact thing, but Scripture does not say that. I still believe the household rule was within my responsibility to make, but I should have called it our rule and explained my reasoning instead of putting God's name on my judgment.`

This is not surrender.

It teaches the child:

- God's Word is more authoritative than Dad's rhetoric;
- Dad's real authority does not require false claims of revelation;
- correction can coexist with humility.

### P0

`PARENTAL REPENTANCE FOR OVERCLAIM ≠ RETROACTIVE PERMISSION FOR CHILD DECEPTION/DISOBEDIENCE`.

Cases may contain both sins.

# 17. Product kill-switch

Reject sentences like:

- `God says no secular music.`
- `The Bible says phones may not be in bedrooms.`
- `Any clothing parents dislike is immodest before God.`
- `Because Scripture does not specify a curfew, parents cannot set one.`
- `Every parental preference is binding obedience.`

- `Christian liberty makes dependent minors self-governing in all indifferent matters.`
- `A household rule remains universally binding after the jurisdiction ends.`

## Final synthesis

> **A mature Christian conscience must know not only whether to obey, but what kind of claim is being made. God's moral law is universal; wisdom applies that law; household rules govern a real but bounded relation; parental counsel deserves honor without automatic divinization; civil authority has its own sphere. Good parenting teaches these distinctions so the child does not grow into either an antinomian who rejects every unquoted rule or a scrupulous adult who cannot distinguish God's voice from a parent's preference.**
