# Church Mediation in Parent / Adult-Child Conflict — Matthew 18, 1 Corinthians 6, 1689 Jurisdiction, Different Churches, and the Unbeliever Case

**Status:** RESEARCH-ONLY / SCRIPTURE-FIRST / CHURCH-JURISDICTION AUDIT / NOT PUBLICATION APPROVAL  
**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Project:** `TEEN_DOUBLE_LIFE` adult-child / family-government companion  
**Question:** When conflict between Christian parents and an older/adult child becomes entrenched, what role may or must pastors/churches play? What if both sides are members, one side is unregenerate/non-member, or they belong to different churches? How can mediation avoid becoming either an appellate court designed to defeat parents or an automatic ecclesiastical endorsement of parental claims?

---

## 1. Executive verdict

The church has a real pastoral and disciplinary role in conflicts involving its members, but **jurisdiction must be identified before intervention**.

The project must reject two opposite models.

### Model A — privatized family absolutism

> `This is a family matter. No pastor may ask questions or correct a father.`

Unsafe.

Christian parents remain church members under Christ's Word and church oversight. If they sin by rage, lying, oppression, abuse, unjust accusation, refusal to forgive, or other church-disciplinable conduct, fatherhood does not create ecclesiastical immunity.

### Model B — ecclesiastical replacement parenting

> `The adult child told a pastor his side, so the pastor now becomes the authoritative judge of every household decision and may nullify parental order without hearing the parents.`

Also unsafe.

The church does not receive a general divine commission to become the day-to-day household governor of every member's adult children. Church authority, parental authority, adult conscience, civil law and household ownership/stewardship are distinct relations.

### Controlling synthesis

> **MEDIATE BEFORE COALITION. ESTABLISH FACTS BEFORE MORAL VERDICT. IDENTIFY JURISDICTION BEFORE EXERCISING AUTHORITY.**

Where both parties profess Christ and are church members, the church may have direct pastoral/disciplinary responsibility toward both. Where the adult child is an unbeliever/non-member, church leaders may still teach, admonish, counsel, protect, mediate and call to repentance, but church censures/keys do not apply to the non-member as though he had covenanted into that church.

## 2. Proverbs 18:17 — the first hearing is not the final truth

Biblical control:

- Prov. 18:17.

Family estrangement almost guarantees narrative asymmetry.

The first party may tell many true facts and still omit:

- chronology;
- earlier warnings;
- his/her own deception;
- parental admissions/apologies;
- household conditions;
- civil/safety factors;
- what was actually said versus inferred;
- what happened after the quoted incident.

The parents can do the same.

### Locked mediation rule

> **ONE-SIDED PASTORAL CARE MAY BEGIN WITH ONE SIDE; A TWO-SIDED MORAL VERDICT NORMALLY REQUIRES HEARING BOTH SIDES WHEN THAT IS POSSIBLE AND SAFE.**

Exceptions include urgent safeguarding where immediate protective action is necessary before ordinary mediation.

## 3. Matthew 18:15–17 — real escalation, but context matters

Primary biblical unit:

- Matt. 18:15–20.

Historical commentary:

- John Gill on private admonition / church discipline: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical.iii.vi.html
- Calvin on Matthew 18: https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/matthew/18.htm

Gill treats the rule as applicable to differences/trespasses between persons and church members, especially private offences, with the sequence:

`private reproof → one/two witnesses → church`.

He also limits the simple sequence:

- not every trivial word becomes a discipline case;
- public/atrocious sin may require a different immediate response;
- the church-stage belongs to church order.

### Family application

If a believing adult child says:

> `My father sinned against me`,

Matthew 18 does not naturally teach:

> `Immediately recruit an outside pastor who has never spoken to the father and build a permanent coalition.`

Where safe and appropriate, direct truth-telling and then mediated conversation fit the text better.

Likewise, a parent alleging a private sin against an adult believing child should not automatically publicize the case across the church before ordinary private/limited steps.

### Guard — crime/abuse/safeguarding

Matthew 18 is not a secrecy statute for crime, violence, child abuse, sexual abuse, stalking or other matters requiring civil/safeguarding response.

`MATTHEW 18 ≠ REPLACEMENT FOR CIVIL JURISDICTION`.

## 4. 1689 LBCF ch. 26 — church authority is real and bounded

Primary:

- 1689 LBCF ch. 26: https://www.the1689confession.com/1689/chapter-26

### 26.4 / 26.7 — Christ governs the church

Christ is supreme Head; particular churches receive the authority needed to carry out the worship and discipline Christ instituted.

Therefore church discipline is not mere optional friendship advice where the church actually has jurisdiction.

### 26.10 — pastors watch for souls

Pastors attend to Word/prayer and watch for the souls entrusted to their church.

A father or adult child who is a member cannot reasonably say:

> `My family status makes my conduct none of the elders' concern.`

### 26.12 — church privileges and church government belong together

Those admitted to church privileges are under its censures and government according to Christ's rule.

This is extremely important for the adult-child project.

> **CHURCH DISCIPLINE JURISDICTION TRACKS CHURCH RELATION, NOT MERE BIOLOGICAL RELATION.**

If the adult child is not a member/professor, he may be exhorted as a human being under God's law, but the church cannot pretend he has submitted to membership government that he never entered.

### 26.15 — counsel between churches without external domination

In difficult disputes, churches in communion may send messengers to consider and advise, but such messengers do not acquire church power/jurisdiction over the churches concerned.

This is a valuable analogy for family mediation:

> **HELP CAN BE REAL WITHOUT REPLACING THE ACTUAL GOVERNING RELATION.**

## 5. 1 Corinthians 6 — believers should possess wisdom to mediate ordinary disputes

Primary:

- 1 Cor. 6:1–8.

Useful modern expositions:

- Ligonier, `Settling Disputes Among Believers`: https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/settling-disputes-among-believers
- Andrew David Naselli / Crossway, `Should Christians Sue Each Other?`: https://www.crossway.org/articles/should-christians-sue-each-other-1-corinthians-6/

Paul rebukes believers for taking ordinary intra-Christian disputes to pagan civil courts rather than having wise believers judge/mediate ordinary matters.

### What it supports

- the church should have mature people capable of helping believers resolve disputes;
- reconciliation and truth are real ecclesial goods;
- two believing family members need not remain locked forever in private accusation when mature church help is available.

### What it does not support

Do not turn 1 Cor. 6 into:

> `Church elders replace police/courts in crimes or safeguarding matters.`

The text concerns ordinary disputes between believers, not abolition of the civil magistrate.

Nor does it make pastors infallible arbitrators of every prudential family decision.

## 6. Galatians 6:1 — restoration, not team victory

Primary:

- Gal. 6:1.

The person caught in transgression is to be restored in gentleness, while the corrector watches himself.

### Mediation aim

The goal is not:

- prove Dad won;
- prove daughter won;
- preserve the church's image;
- force reunion at any cost;
- produce a public confession that satisfies spectators.

The goal is:

> **truth + repentance of each guilty party + protection of the harmed + restoration where possible + right ordering of relations under Christ.**

This is especially important in mixed cases (`215`):

- parent may need to repent of rage/harshness;
- child may need to repent of deceit/sexual sin/rebellion;
- neither repentance cancels the other's.

## 7. Scenario A — parent and adult child are members of the same church

This is the clearest church-jurisdiction case.

If both profess Christ and are members:

- both are under the same church's pastoral oversight;
- elders should not assume the parent is right merely because older/father;
- elders should not assume the adult child is right merely because presenting as wounded;
- both may be called to specific repentance;
- serious unrepentant conduct may become discipline matter according to its nature/publicity;
- family status does not exempt either from church government.

### Good process

`initial hearing → identify concrete allegations → distinguish sin from preference → hear other party → check evidence/witnesses where needed → private mediation where possible → specific repentance/restoration → discipline only where warranted`.

### Guard

Do not make the entire congregation consume private sexual/family details unnecessarily.

`CHURCH ACCOUNTABILITY ≠ MAXIMUM PUBLICITY`.

---

## 8. Scenario B — parents are church members; adult child is unbelieving/non-member

This is one of the user's central cases.

The church may:

- preach the Word;
- call the child to repentance and faith;
- admonish known sin;
- meet with him/her voluntarily;
- mediate a family conversation;
- help verify competing factual claims;
- protect vulnerable people;
- counsel the parents about their own conduct;
- help the parents distinguish lawful household order from overreach;
- refuse to provide a parallel Christian support system whose function is enabling defended sin.

But the church may not honestly say:

> `We excommunicate this person from membership`

if he was never a member/professing brother in the relevant sense.

1 Cor. 5 itself distinguishes the outsider from the one `called a brother`.

### Key formula

> **NON-MEMBER ≠ OUTSIDE GOD'S MORAL LAW; NON-MEMBER ≠ UNDER MEMBER DISCIPLINE.**

## 9. Scenario C — adult child is a member elsewhere

This creates a major risk of competing one-sided pastoral universes.

Useful contemporary case:

- 9Marks, `Churches Cooperating in Pastoring`: https://www.9marks.org/article/journalchurches-cooperating-pastoring/

The article specifically describes parents and adult children in different churches: one pastor hears the adult children's side, another hears the parents' side; the pastors can remain one-sided or communicate and mediate.

### Strong project principle

> **PASTORAL CONFIDENTIALITY SHOULD NOT BECOME A SYSTEM FOR MAINTAINING TWO INCOMPATIBLE FACTUAL REALITIES.**

This does **not** mean pastors indiscriminately share confidential disclosures.

It means that where a member is asking his church to render moral judgments about another Christian family member, pastors should consider whether fair pastoral care requires:

- consent to speak with the other party;
- direct invitation to the other party;
- pastor-to-pastor communication;
- carefully scoped fact verification.

### If the counselee refuses any verification

A pastor may still care for the person.

But should be cautious about issuing strong conclusions such as:

> `Your parents are abusive/legalists and you owe them no reconciliation`

on a complex disputed record he has never tested.

## 10. Scenario D — child recently left and a new church immediately becomes the replacement family

High-risk pattern:

`secret departure → new friend/romantic interest/church → immediate full credibility → parents treated as villains → practical support removes every consequence → no attempt at fact reconciliation`.

This can become the ecclesial version of the project's existing P0:

> **Do not build a Christian-looking escape hatch from righteous parental correction.**

### But the mirror danger is real

A church must not send a person back into:

- violence;
- sexual abuse;
- dangerous confinement;
- forced marriage;
- criminal activity;
- demands to deny Christ/lie/sin;
- other real danger

merely because parents ask for `submission`.

### Therefore

`RETURN HOME` is not a default sacrament.

The church should investigate:

- why the person left;
- legal/adult status;
- safety;
- actual parental demand;
- actual child's conduct;
- whether return is wise, necessary, possible or unsafe;
- whether repentance/reconciliation can occur without immediate co-residence.

---

## 11. Scenario E — parents themselves need church correction

Examples:

- father falsely accused child;
- demanded access he did not lawfully possess;
- used obscene insults/threats;
- struck/assaulted an adult child unlawfully;
- demanded false confession;
- manipulated church leaders with selective facts;
- refuses to acknowledge demonstrated parental sin;
- blocks lawful marriage for sinful reasons;
- treats every adult disagreement as rebellion against God.

The correct church response is not:

> `But he is the father, therefore preserve authority by saying nothing.`

Authority under God is strengthened, not destroyed, by repentance.

Use `215`:

`PARENTAL REPENTANCE ≠ MORAL CAPITULATION`.

The church can correct the father while also correcting the child.

---

## 12. Scenario F — child is genuinely in defended gross sin, parents are substantially faithful

Examples:

- ongoing fornication;
- pornography distribution;
- lying/theft;
- corrupting siblings;
- substance abuse;
- predatory sexual conduct;
- deliberate household sabotage.

A pastor should not become `nice neutral adult` whose function is:

- affirm the child while avoiding the actual sin;
- frame all parental discomfort as control;
- provide money/housing without examining whether it enables the pattern;
- advise secrecy from substantially faithful parents merely to gain rapport.

### Better role

- tell the truth about sin;
- call to repentance/faith;
- verify parental claims rather than blindly repeat them;
- encourage truthful reconciliation;
- distinguish relationship from co-residence;
- help formulate lawful/proportionate household terms;
- protect others;
- resist both enabling and revenge.

## 13. Privacy / confidentiality in mediation

A pastor needs confidentiality, but confidentiality is not absolute secrecy from every person touched by the matter.

The project should distinguish:

### Personal pastoral confidence

Not every temptation/confession needs to be disclosed to parents.

### Factual allegation against another person

If the counselee asks the pastor to conclude:

> `Dad did X`,

fairness may require speaking with Dad before treating that allegation as established.

### Safety disclosure

Abuse/crime/self-harm danger may trigger safeguarding/civil duties depending on jurisdiction.

### Church discipline evidence

Church action should not rest merely on unverifiable private assertions where evidence/witnesses are required and available.

### Locked formula

> **CONFIDENTIALITY PROTECTS PASTORAL CARE; IT MUST NOT BECOME A LICENSE FOR UNTESTED ACCUSATIONS TO ACQUIRE ECCLESIASTICAL AUTHORITY.**

---

## 14. Mediation is not always reconciliation

Possible outcomes include:

1. full repentance/reconciliation;
2. relationship restored but trust rebuilding slowly;
3. peace/limited contact without co-residence;
4. acknowledged disagreement over prudential matters without moral condemnation;
5. continuing discipline of one/both church members;
6. protective separation because of danger;
7. adult child remains unbelieving but contact becomes more truthful;
8. no current reconciliation because one party refuses truth.

### Romans 12 control

Believers seek peace `so far as it depends on you`.

Therefore:

> **A failed reconciliation attempt does not automatically prove failed faithfulness.**

The goal is obedience to God, not coercive production of a relational outcome.

## 15. Church mediation must distinguish categories before deciding

Before giving a verdict, ask:

### Status

- minor?
- legal adult resident/dependent?
- recently departed?
- long-independent?
- married?

### Church relation

- believer/member?
- professing non-member?
- unbeliever?
- member of another church?

### Alleged issue

- explicit sin?
- lawful household rule?
- prudential disagreement?
- safety/crime?
- marriage/courtship?
- money/property?
- privacy?

### Parent's conduct

- faithful correction?
- overreach?
- rage/humiliation?
- actual abuse?

### Child's conduct

- warfare/repentance?
- ambiguity?
- defended rebellion?
- manipulation of third parties?

### Desired remedy

- confession?
- restoration?
- return home?
- separate residence?
- money?
- church discipline?
- safety plan?

Without these distinctions `support the child` and `support the parents` are both too crude.

## 16. What pastors must not do

### Do not baptize the first narrative

`First story heard ≠ final fact pattern`.

### Do not erase family authority with adult-age slogans

`18 ≠ family relation ceased`.

### Do not absolutize parental authority

`Father ≠ infallible / husband of daughter / civil magistrate / elder over every adult conscience`.

### Do not weaponize Matthew 18 for secrecy

Crime/safeguarding belongs to appropriate civil processes too.

### Do not make church membership irrelevant

Church discipline belongs to members/professing insiders in its proper scope.

### Do not make the unbeliever morally neutral

He remains under God's moral law and may be admonished.

### Do not require co-residence as proof of repentance

Relationship/reconciliation and physical residence are different questions.

### Do not use mediation to preserve appearances

A public `happy family` performance is not the goal.

---

## 17. Product-safe pastoral sequence

For a disputed adult-child conflict without urgent danger:

`1. hear initial party compassionately`

`2. identify exact claims rather than labels`

`3. distinguish sin / house order / prudence / safety`

`4. identify member/non-member/church jurisdiction`

`5. seek the other side where appropriate and safe`

`6. compare facts/evidence`

`7. name each party's actual sin separately`

`8. call each guilty party to repentance without barter`

`9. mediate restitution/reconciliation where possible`

`10. distinguish restored relation from residence/trust/access/role`

`11. apply church discipline only within real church jurisdiction`

`12. use civil/safeguarding processes where those jurisdictions apply`

## 19. Locked P0s

1. `MEDIATE BEFORE COALITION`.
2. `FIRST STORY HEARD ≠ FINAL FACT PATTERN`.
3. `CHURCH DISCIPLINE JURISDICTION TRACKS CHURCH RELATION, NOT BIOLOGICAL RELATION`.
4. `NON-MEMBER ≠ OUTSIDE GOD'S MORAL LAW; NON-MEMBER ≠ UNDER MEMBER DISCIPLINE`.
5. `CONFIDENTIALITY ≠ ECCLESIASTICAL LICENSE FOR UNTESTED ACCUSATIONS`.
6. `MATTHEW 18 ≠ REPLACEMENT FOR CIVIL/SAFEGUARDING JURISDICTION`.
7. `PARENTAL AUTHORITY ≠ IMMUNITY FROM CHURCH CORRECTION`.
8. `PASTORAL HELP ≠ REPLACEMENT HOUSEHOLD GOVERNMENT`.
9. Different churches should consider pastor-to-pastor cooperation rather than maintaining parallel one-sided moral universes.
10. Reconciliation may occur without immediate return to co-residence.
