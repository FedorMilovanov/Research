# Owen / Gill / Edwards — Church Discipline, Family Relations, and the Limits of Total Shunning

**Status:** PRIMARY-SOURCE VERIFICATION / RESEARCH ONLY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** verify the Reformed/Baptist claim that formal church discipline does not dissolve natural/civil family obligations, and apply that distinction carefully to wayward adult children.

---

# 1. Why this source pass matters

The adult-child project can easily commit a jurisdiction error:

> `unrepentant person` → `1 Corinthians 5` → `avoid him` → `parents must cease ordinary family relationship/contact`.

That chain is too fast.

There are at least three questions:

1. What does formal church discipline require toward a professing church member?
2. What natural/civil/family duties existed before the discipline?
3. Does the church's act cancel those duties?

A current 9Marks article argued no and cited John Owen, John Gill, Jonathan Edwards, and older Baptist material.

This file verifies the three principal older sources directly.

Modern synthesis source:

- https://www.9marks.org/article/relating-to-family-members-under-church-discipline/

# 2. John Owen — direct primary verification

Primary source:

- John Owen, *An Inquiry into the Original, Nature, Institution, Power, Order, and Communion of Evangelical Churches*.
- CCEL: https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/evangelicalchurches/evangelicalchurches.iii.xiv.html

Owen discusses the practical force of separation from an excommunicated person.

His distinctions are unusually precise.

## 2.1 What is restricted

Owen treats the rule as forbidding ordinary elective/familiar converse of a certain kind.

But he immediately limits that restriction.

He distinguishes:

- chosen/ordinary converse from unavoidable/occasional converse;
- merely secular familiar converse from spiritual admonition;
- optional relations from obligations previously established by justice/relationship.

He says the disciplined person **may and ought still to be admonished while he will hear admonition**.

This alone is important for the project:

> ecclesiastical separation is not a ban on every attempt to reclaim the sinner.

## 2.2 Natural/moral relations are not dissolved

Owen explicitly says duties arising from pre-existing natural or moral relations are **not released or weakened** by excommunication.

He gives examples:

- husband/wife;
- parents/children;
- magistrates/subjects;
- masters/servants;
- neighbours/blood relations.

Primary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/evangelicalchurches/evangelicalchurches.iii.xiv.html

### Strong project conclusion

If **formal excommunication** does not cancel parent/child duties, then it is even less defensible to claim that a merely wayward/unbelieving adult child automatically becomes someone toward whom the parents must suspend every natural family expression.

This does not mean “nothing changes.”

It means changes must respect jurisdiction.

# 3. John Gill — Baptist primary verification

Primary source:

- John Gill, *A Body of Practical Divinity*, church discipline/excommunication.
- CCEL: https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical.iii.vi.html

Gill states that excommunication:

- does not affect temporal estate/civil affairs;
- does not impose civil penalties;
- does not intrude on magistracy;
- does not break natural/civil relations between husband/wife, parents/children, masters/servants;
- does not forbid attendance on the external ministry of the Word.

This is extraordinarily relevant to multiple already-locked project distinctions.

## 3.1 Hearing the Word remains open

Gill's statement that excommunication does not forbid attendance on the external ministry of the Word reinforces our earlier conclusion:

`loss of church communion/privileges` ≠ `ban from hearing preaching`.

This supports the project's treatment of:

- public attendance;
- membership;
- Lord's Table;
- close fellowship;
- ministry privilege

as distinct categories.

## 3.2 Natural relation remains

Gill treats parent/child relation as natural and not destroyed by church discipline.

Elsewhere he explicitly distinguishes natural parent-child relation from relations founded mainly in civil compact/consent.

Related:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.iii.i.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.i.html

### Project use

Gill gives strong Particular Baptist historical support for:

> **church censure does not erase family identity or all family duty.**

# 4. Jonathan Edwards — direct verification

Primary:

- Jonathan Edwards, *Works*, vol. 2, material on church discipline/excommunication.
- CCEL: https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/works2/works2.v.html

Edwards asks what kindness/respect may or ought still be shown to excommunicated people.

He includes:

- prayer;
- admonition;
- common offices/duties of humanity;
- relief in sickness/distress;
- necessary social help for life/property;
- natural and civil relational duties.

He then says specifically that excommunication does not release:

- children from duties to parents;
- parents from parental affection/care toward children;
- spouses from marital duties.

Primary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/works2/works2.v.html

### Important

Edwards is simultaneously **strong for real church discipline**.

He does not use continuing family duty to make discipline meaningless.

He argues discipline aims at reclaiming the erring person and protecting the church.

This is the exact balance our project needs.

# 5. Three-source convergence

Owen (Independent/Puritan), Gill (Particular Baptist), and Edwards (New England Reformed) converge on the same structural point:

> **ecclesiastical exclusion does not abolish antecedent natural/civil relations.**

That is significant because the authors differ in church polity and context.

The convergence is not an infallible tradition, but it is a strong historical Reformed witness.

---

# 6. What this means for parents of an unrepentant adult child

## It DOES mean

Parents should not casually be told:

> “Treat him as though he is no longer your son.”

or

> “If he is sexually immoral/unbelieving, biblical separation means no meals, no ordinary care, no contact, no help under any circumstances.”

Those claims need much more argument and collide with the primary tradition above.

Parents may continue, as circumstances permit:

- parental affection;
- prayer;
- admonition;
- humane aid;
- ordinary family responsibility;
- some meals/contact;
- attempts at restoration.

## It does NOT mean

- unrestricted fellowship as if nothing happened;
- church membership in good standing;
- Lord's Supper access;
- ministry privilege;
- unrestricted youth influence;
- financial enabling;
- automatic right to residence;
- ignoring threat/corrupting influence.

Natural relation survives; not every privilege survives.

---

# 7. Especially important for the user's “he is unbelieving anyway” case

If the adult child is openly unbelieving and was never a church member/professing brother, 1 Cor. 5's formal disciplinary category does not directly apply as a church censure case.

Therefore it is even more dangerous to take the strongest separation language from 1 Cor. 5 and use it as an automatic family rule toward an outsider.

The church may still:

- admonish;
- restrict ministry/youth access;
- protect members;
- refuse to affirm sin.

The family may still:

- love;
- admonish;
- relate;
- set house boundaries.

# 8. “Do not eat with such a one” and family meals

1 Cor. 5:11 is often the flashpoint.

The historical Reformed/Baptist tradition represented here does not read this as cancelling necessary family relations.

Owen distinguishes chosen familiar association from natural/moral obligations.

Gill says natural/civil relations are not broken.

Edwards says common duties and parental care remain.

### Project guard

The final article should not attempt a full exegetical settlement of every possible meal scenario in this module alone.

But it can safely state:

> Reformed interpreters have historically denied that 1 Cor. 5 dissolves parent-child or marital duties.

For exact quotation/publication, use primary URLs and short quotation within copyright limits.

# 9. Distinguish family dinner from validating the sinful arrangement

A parent eating soup with an adult son is not automatically saying:

> “Your fornication is righteous.”

But a parent hosting a celebration designed to honor the son's sinful union would be a different moral act.

Likewise:

- caring for him during illness;
- giving a meal;
- speaking at a kitchen table;

must not be collapsed with:

- funding sin;
- ceremonial approval;
- spiritual reassurance;
- public endorsement.

This distinction follows the broader biblical moral logic of actions having different objects/meanings.

---

# 10. Family affection is not church fellowship

This is one of the most useful conceptual clarifications for the entire corpus.

A parent may say:

> “You remain my son, and I love you.”

while church says:

> “We can no longer affirm your profession as a member in good standing.”

Those are not contradictory statements.

Parent-child relation is natural/familial.

Church membership is ecclesial/covenantal and governed by church keys.

Therefore:

> **loss of one relation's privileges need not annihilate another relation's duties.**

---

# 11. This also prevents parents from impersonating the church

Parents have household/family authority.

They do not personally possess the church's corporate authority to excommunicate.

A father cannot simply declare:

> “I have delivered my eighteen-year-old son to Satan.”

as a private parenting technique.

1 Cor. 5 involves a gathered church and professing insider.

The parent's corresponding category is more modest:

- warn;
- refuse participation;
- set house/resource boundaries;
- allow consequences;
- involve the church where church jurisdiction exists.

---

# 12. This also prevents the church from impersonating the family

Church leaders should not casually order parents:

> “Because we disciplined him, you may no longer perform normal parental duties.”

Owen/Gill/Edwards specifically resist that logic.

Church discipline should strengthen truth and holiness, not dissolve God's other institutions.

---

# 13. Protecting younger siblings remains fully compatible with family duty

Continuing parental affection does not require exposing the rest of the household to corruption.

A disciplined/wayward adult child may need limits on:

- overnight residence;
- unsupervised access to minors;
- youth trips;
- devices/shared networks;
- bringing partners/friends into home;
- distributing porn/drugs;
- abusive conduct.

This is not “denying he is your son.”

It is governing the household and protecting others.

The current 9Marks article explicitly applies this principle to parents with younger children.

Secondary:

- https://www.9marks.org/article/relating-to-family-members-under-church-discipline/

# 14. Admonition after discipline: Owen and Edwards both matter

Owen says the disciplined person may still be admonished while willing to hear.

Edwards lists prayer and admonition among continuing kindness/duties.

This strongly supports the user's concern:

> parents/pastors should not interpret “release” as “we are now morally forbidden to speak truth to him.”

### Better distinction

There may be a time to stop **arguing endlessly**.

There is no general command to stop **ever admonishing again**.

Frequency/timing is wisdom; truth-duty remains.

---

# 15. Prayer is not control

Edwards expressly includes prayer.

The project should make this obvious but explicit:

> surrendering an adult child's control to God's providence should intensify, not terminate, prayer.

This also harmonizes with:

- Monica;
- Ligonier prodigal counsel;
- BCC prodigal counsel.

---

# 16. Doing humane good is not automatically enabling

Edwards includes aid in sickness/distress and ordinary human help.

Romans 12:20 commands good even toward an enemy.

Therefore the project should not teach a brittle equation:

`help = enable`.

Instead:

`help that sustains sin` may be enabling;

`help that meets real humane need without sustaining sin` may be commanded or wise.

Examples:

- food;
- urgent medical help;
- safe transport;
- emergency shelter under terms;

cannot be morally classified only by whether the recipient is repentant.

# 18. Publication-grade formulations

> **Church discipline can remove church fellowship; it cannot erase biology, marriage, or every duty God attached to another relationship.**

> **An excommunicated son does not become an orphan by ecclesiastical decree, and his father does not become a stranger.**

> **The church may withdraw its affirmation of a man's profession without requiring his mother to withdraw every act of maternal care.**

> **Biblical separation is not moral anesthesia. Owen still permits admonition; Edwards still requires prayer and humane duty; Gill still preserves natural relations.**

> **Family love must not undo church discipline, but church discipline must not be stretched until it destroys family duties God never revoked.**

Synthesis only, not quotations.

# 19. Audit verdict

### VERIFIED PRIMARY SUPPORT

- Owen: natural/moral relational duties survive excommunication; admonition may continue.
- Gill: excommunication does not break parent-child/natural-civil relations and does not ban hearing external ministry of Word.
- Edwards: parents retain parental affection/care; prayer/admonition/humane duties remain.

### Strong implication

Total familial shunning is **not** the default Reformed/Baptist historic implication of excommunication.

### Guard

This does not settle every modern safety, co-residence, finance, or abusive-contact question. Strong boundaries may still be warranted by the specific duty to protect others and avoid participation in sin.
