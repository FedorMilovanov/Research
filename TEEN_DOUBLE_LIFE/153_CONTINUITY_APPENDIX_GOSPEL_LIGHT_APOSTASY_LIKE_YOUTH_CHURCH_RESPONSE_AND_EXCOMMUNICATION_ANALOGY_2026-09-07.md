# 0. Why this appendix exists

The user raised a publication-critical question after the main late-stage closure overlay was already created.

This requirement must not be lost in future context compression:

> A child raised under extensive gospel light, who knew Scripture, heard preaching, attended Bible groups, moved toward baptism/profession, and later knowingly rejects correction and lives in defended rebellion, must not be flattened into the same pastoral category as an ordinary outsider who has barely heard the gospel merely because both may be unregenerate.

The project must also preserve the user’s strong intuition of a **parallel with excommunication**, while correcting it with precise Baptist ecclesiology.

---

# 1. Mandatory three-axis distinction

Future drafters must separately ask:

## Soteriology

- regenerate/unregenerate?
- true believer fallen grievously?
- false/temporary profession?

Do not pretend infallible heart knowledge.

## Gospel light / responsibility

- quantity/clarity of gospel instruction;
- known Scripture;
- personal admonition;
- former Christian identification;
- baptism candidacy/profession;
- deliberate rejection against known truth.

Greater light can aggravate responsibility (Luke 12:47–48; John 15:22–24; 2 Pet. 2:20–22 with context guard).

## Ecclesial jurisdiction/status

- outsider/visitor;
- child of members;
- baptism candidate;
- informal/public professor;
- member;
- disciplined/excommunicated member.

Formal censure depends on this axis, not merely the amount of guilt/light.

---

# 2. Mandatory core conclusion

Never use:

> `unregenerate = ordinary outsider in every respect`.

Use:

> **Unregenerate describes lack of saving regeneration; it does not erase gospel privilege, prior profession, rejected admonition, aggravated guilt, corrupting influence, or the church’s existing pastoral relationship.**

---

# 3. Excommunication analogy — mandatory guard

## Do preserve

A church-raised hardened nonmember and an excommunicated professing member may share pastoral logic:

- no false affirmation of Christian standing;
- serious warning;
- changed normalizing fellowship;
- continued call to repentance;
- prayer;
- protection of others;
- public Word hearing ordinarily remains possible where safe/orderly.

## Do not collapse

A never-member:

- has no membership to remove;
- cannot be formally “excommunicated” from privileges never granted.

Therefore the final formulation must remain:

> **pastoral/moral analogy, not juridical identity.**

---

# 4. Church-access principle

Locked:

> **Missionary openness does not equal fellowship normalization.**

Public preaching/hearing of Word should ordinarily be the widest door.

Close/confidential/representative access is separately stewarded:

- discipleship small groups;
- youth social circles;
- overnight trips;
- private chats;
- choir/platform;
- access to younger/minor participants;
- leadership-like influence.

Known corrupting conduct can justify restriction even when formal discipline jurisdiction does not apply.

---

# 5. “Whole church admonition” correction

Preserve user’s concern:

> church silence can normalize known hypocrisy/rebellion and teach other children that one can live openly against God while remaining socially/ministerially unchanged.

But do not prescribe:

- a mob;
- repeated confrontations by every member;
- public humiliation as strategy;
- deliberately making attendance unbearable so the person leaves.

Correct category:

> **corporately truthful church + ordered admonition by those with real responsibility/relationship.**

Parents, elders, youth leaders, and appropriate mature members may act; formal congregation-wide discipline only where church jurisdiction/process applies.

---

# 6. “If he stops coming because we admonish him”

Do not make departure the goal.

Do not buy attendance with silence.

Locked line:

> **If the only way to retain him is never to name known serious rebellion, the church cannot purchase his presence with moral silence.**

But equally:

> repentance, not social expulsion, is the aim.

---

# 7. Hebrews 6/10 P0 guard

The user’s category is serious enough that Hebrews apostasy warnings are relevant.

However:

- Heb. 6:4–6 is not every relapse;
- Heb. 10:26 is not every voluntary/gross immoral act;
- Gill explicitly restricts Heb. 10:26 toward resolute/obstinate apostasy against known truth;
- Dennis Johnson reads both passages as deliberate repudiation of once-confessed faith/Christ after visible-community blessings.

Therefore:

> **use Hebrews to warn about trajectory and gravity, not to declare a named living teenager irreversibly beyond repentance.**

Read `152` before drafting any Hebrews paragraph.
