# Scripture-First Parent + Pastor Post-Discovery Decision Tree

**Status:** PASTORAL RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Purpose:** provide a biblically ordered framework after discovery of pornography, sexting, sexualized speech, systematic deception, corrupting peer behavior, or a wider double life.

This is **not** a rigid counseling protocol and does not replace safeguarding/legal duties.

---

# 0. First rule: identify what kind of case actually exists

Do not begin by forcing every discovery into one category.

Possible categories:

A. ordinary/isolated sin + teachable response;  
B. repeated secret sin + genuine struggle;  
C. unregenerate child in open rebellion but still teachable;  
D. professing believer in serious fall;  
E. settled professing hypocritical pattern;  
F. child actively corrupting/recruiting others;  
G. victimization/coercion/sextortion;  
H. mixed case: voluntary sin followed by victimization.

The same screenshot can sit inside very different pastoral situations.

---

# 1. Step one — establish facts without theatrical anger

Biblical controls:

- Prov. 18:13, 17;
- Deut. 19 principle of evidence (use as general justice principle, not direct household procedure);
- James 1:19–20;
- Gal. 6:1 for restoration of a fallen brother.

Ask:

- What actually happened?
- What is verified vs suspected?
- Who else is involved?
- Is anyone in immediate danger?
- Was content voluntary, coerced, fabricated, redistributed?
- Is there an adult predator?
- Is there sextortion/suicide risk?

### Never

- publish/private-shame the child to relatives/church unnecessarily;
- assume every allegation is true;
- erase victimization because the child also sinned;
- perform amateur criminal investigation when competent reporting is required.

---

# 2. Step two — safety before the full moral lecture when danger is acute

If there is:

- sextortion;
- threats;
- grooming;
- nonconsensual image distribution;
- adult sexual contact;
- suicidality/self-harm danger;
- coercion,

then immediate safeguarding takes priority operationally.

This does not deny moral questions.

It sequences them wisely.

### Mixed-case principle

> **A child may need to repent for what he voluntarily did and still need protection from what someone else is now doing to him.**

Do not collapse those into one guilt category.

---

# 3. Step three — call the sin by biblical names

When facts are clear, avoid both euphemism and exaggeration.

Possible biblical categories:

- lust/adultery of heart — Matt. 5:27–30;
- sexual immorality — 1 Cor. 6:18;
- obscene/filthy speech — Eph. 5:3–4; Col. 3:8;
- lying — Eph. 4:25;
- deceit/covering — Prov. 28:13;
- rebellion/disobedience — Eph. 6:1–3;
- corrupting others — Matt. 18 warning themes; 1 Cor. 15:33 (context guarded);
- hypocrisy — Ps. 50; Matt. 15 where sustained religious facade truly fits.

### Avoid

- calling every pornography episode `apostasy`;
- calling every repeated use `clinical addiction`;
- calling every secret `seared conscience`;
- saying “you are a reprobate.”

---

# 4. Step four — distinguish sorrow from repentance

Primary text:

- 2 Cor. 7:8–11.

Paul does not celebrate sorrow merely because it hurts.

The godly sorrow produced observable earnestness/change.

Calvin highlights:

- previous carelessness becomes active concern;
- the Corinthians do not defend themselves by extenuating sin;
- indignation is directed against evil;
- fear, longing and zeal aim toward amendment.

Sources:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom40.xiii.iii.html
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/2_corinthians/7.htm

Watson module 47 adds counterfeit categories:

- tears;
- terror;
- vows;
- temporary motions;
- dropping one sin while protecting another.

### Parent question

Not only:

> “Are you sorry?”

But:

> “What are you sorry for?”
> “What do you now call what you did?”
> “What are you willing to bring into the light?”
> “What avenue are you willing to give up?”

---

# 5. Step five — confession + forsaking, not confession as information dump

Prov. 28:13 gives an especially concise wisdom pattern:

`cover transgression → no prosperity`  
`confess + forsake → mercy`.

NET notes emphasize the contrast between covering/rationalizing and acknowledging + abandoning.

Source:

- https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Pro&chapter=28&tab=commentaries&verse=13

### Project significance

Confession alone can become another religious ritual if the person is simultaneously preserving the infrastructure of the sin.

Ask:

- What must be forsaken?
- Who must be told?
- What must be deleted/surrendered?
- What relationship must change?
- What restitution is needed?

---

# 6. Step six — determine whether the child is fighting or negotiating

## Fighting pattern

May include:

- calls sin sin;
- asks for help;
- discloses beyond evidence;
- accepts removal of occasions;
- does not insist on immediate privileges;
- grieves having offended God/others;
- returns to truth after relapse;
- wants corrupting relationships changed.

## Negotiating/protecting pattern

May include:

- admits only proven facts;
- preserves hidden channel;
- repeatedly finds loopholes;
- defends one “exception”;
- blames all guilt on parents/rules;
- fights hardest to regain the very occasion of repeated sin;
- recruits others;
- despises correction.

### Guard

These are pastoral indicators, not an infallible salvation test.

---

# 7. Step seven — remove occasions proportionately

Biblical/Puritan controls:

- Matt. 5:29–30;
- Rom. 13:14;
- 1 Cor. 6:18;
- 2 Tim. 2:22;
- Owen *Of Temptation*;
- Brooks Device 7.

Potential measures:

- phone out of bedroom;
- temporary feature phone;
- remove app/account;
- supervised browser;
- end/private-contact restriction with corrupting person;
- leave sexualized group chat;
- no overnight youth event for a period;
- pause choir/ministry platform.

### Key question

> Is this boundary connected to a **known avenue** of sin/risk?

Not:

> How can we make punishment maximally painful?

---

# 8. Step eight — do not confuse consequences with payment for forgiveness

Consequences can serve:

- restraint;
- protection;
- truthfulness;
- rebuilding trust;
- church order.

They cannot:

- pay God for sin;
- regenerate;
- purchase parental love;
- create justification.

### Parent language

> “You are not earning forgiveness by losing your phone. The restriction exists because trust and self-government were actually damaged.”

---

# 9. Step nine — preserve relationship while refusing indulgence

Biblical controls:

- Eph. 6:4;
- Col. 3:21;
- Gal. 6:1;
- Luke 15 carefully as gospel/father imagery, not a complete parenting procedure.

Calvin on Gal. 6:1 stresses:

- faithful reproof can be sharp where necessary;
- aim is restoration;
- cruelty/upbraiding is not the same as Christian correction;
- meekness is not indifference to sin.

Source:

- https://calvin.edu/sites/default/files/migrated/centers-institutes-meeter-center-files-john-calvins-works-in-english-Commentary-20041---Epistles-of-Paul-to-the-Galatians-and-Ephesians.pdf

### Crucial balance

> **Gentleness describes the spirit and restorative aim of correction; it does not require mild vocabulary about the evil itself.**

---

# 10. Step ten — distinguish unregenerate child from church member without removing responsibility

## If unprofessing/unbaptized

Say clearly:

- God commands you;
- your sin is guilt, not merely “symptom of being unsaved”;
- parental rules still apply;
- you need Christ/new heart;
- church discipline in formal 1 Cor. 5 sense may not apply.

But church may still restrict:

- ministry platform;
- choir;
- youth access;
- private contact

for concrete conduct/risk.

## If professing member

Settled scandal + refusal of correction can move into Matt. 18 / 1 Cor. 5 church-discipline lane under church polity.

---

# 11. Step eleven — if the child corrupts others, widen the concern

The case is no longer only:

> “How do we restore our child?”

It is also:

> “Whom has our child harmed or recruited?”

Actions may include:

- notifying appropriate parents/leaders consistent with safeguarding/confidentiality;
- ending access to group/ministry;
- stopping distribution channels;
- requiring restitution/apology where appropriate;
- protecting victims.

### 1 Cor. 5 community principle

Tolerated corruption affects more than the offender.

Formal discipline category depends on profession/membership, but leaders must not ignore community risk.

---

# 12. Step twelve — visible ministry should reflect current reality

If a teen is:

- under investigation for serious conduct;
- actively lying;
- refusing correction;
- corrupting others,

it may be appropriate to pause:

- choir;
- worship-team role;
- youth leadership;
- public testimony role.

This is not equivalent to banning church attendance.

### Reason

Visible ministry can function as public affirmation/trust.

The church should not force a double-life child to continue performing “everything is normal” spirituality.

---

# 13. Step thirteen — positive rebuilding, not only subtraction

Removing access without rebuilding life can leave a vacuum.

Biblical positive pursuit:

- 2 Tim. 2:22 — flee + pursue + with pure-hearted companions;
- Eph. 4 — put off + renewal + put on;
- Col. 3 — put off + put on;
- Titus 2 — grace trains us to renounce and live godly.

Rebuild:

- family conversation;
- work/responsibility;
- sleep/order;
- service;
- healthy recreation;
- skill/competence;
- godly friendships;
- mentoring;
- Scripture/prayer;
- meaningful church belonging without premature platform restoration.

---

# 14. Step fourteen — define accountability before crisis fades

Voluntary accountability should answer:

- Who will ask?
- What will be disclosed?
- How soon after temptation/fall?
- What device/account access is transparent?
- What happens after relapse?
- Who can help if parent-child relationship becomes heated?

### Preferred goal

Not perpetual infantilization.

The goal is progressively truthful self-government under God.

Owen’s knowing one’s own peculiar weakness is key.

---

# 15. Step fifteen — test fruit over time, not theatrical intensity

Do not set a magical period.

Observe:

- truth when surveillance decreases;
- willingness to accept continued boundaries;
- changed company;
- cessation of recruiting/corrupting others;
- restored ordinary duties;
- increasingly voluntary disclosure;
- attitude toward correction;
- whether the person seeks God or merely privileges.

### 2 Cor. 7 control

Paul identifies repentance by what godly sorrow **produced**, not merely by how strongly sorrow was felt.

---

# 16. Step sixteen — restoration should be differentiated

Possible order:

1. relationship/family normality;
2. ordinary public church attendance;
3. normal peer participation with supervision;
4. device freedoms;
5. choir/public ministry;
6. leadership/sensitive access.

Not every category must restore at same speed.

### Safeguarding guard

Some sensitive access may remain permanently inappropriate even after forgiveness if risk/history requires it.

---

# 17. Four worked pastoral cases

## Case A — 13-year-old first pornography discovery, confesses readily

- establish scope;
- teach Matt. 5 / sexual holiness;
- remove private access proportionately;
- father/mother keep conversation open;
- no need to declare apostasy/addiction;
- choir removal may be unnecessary if no settled public hypocrisy/corruption and child is teachable, depending church policy.

## Case B — 16-year-old church member, repeated hidden porn/sexting, lies, then asks help

- serious consequences;
- pause sensitive ministry may be prudent;
- accountability;
- church/pastor involvement;
- do not treat relapse alone as proof of no faith;
- assess truth/warfare/fruit over time.

## Case C — 16-year-old nonprofessing church child distributes porn, mocks holiness, recruits youth, attends choir/social events for status/dating

- direct admonition despite unbelief;
- do not say “nothing can be expected until conversion”;
- restrict choir/close youth influence;
- public Word/catechesis remains open absent safety disruption;
- parents exercise household boundaries;
- urgent gospel call.

## Case D — 15-year-old sent image voluntarily, now sextorted and suicidal

- immediate safety/reporting/protection;
- preserve evidence per competent guidance;
- do not lead with shame;
- distinguish voluntary initial act from abuse/extortion;
- later address repentance/sexual wisdom;
- church access/ministry questions secondary to immediate safety.

---

# 18. Questions parents/pastors should not ask too quickly

- “Are you even saved?” as first emotional weapon.
- “How could you do this after everything we taught you?”
- “Who else knows?” merely for family reputation.
- “Promise never again” as sole plan.
- “Do you realize how embarrassed you made us?”

These can drive attention toward:

- identity panic;
- family image;
- vows;
- consequence fear

rather than truth/repentance.

### Better sequence

facts → safety → name sin → hear full truth → call to repentance → remove occasions → consequences → ongoing pastoral care.

---

# 19. Questions that penetrate the double life more deeply

- What did you love about this sin?
- What did you tell yourself to make it easier?
- What did you hide because you knew it would expose the pattern?
- Whose approval were you afraid to lose?
- Which command of God became practically unimportant to you?
- Which person/app/place gives this sin most access?
- What are you still unwilling to surrender?
- Who else has been harmed?
- What would bringing this fully into the light cost you?
- Do you want freedom from the sin or only freedom from the consequences?

These are pastoral questions, not an interrogation script.

---

# 20. Core theological sentence

> **The goal after discovery is not to manufacture a frightened, temporarily compliant church child. It is to tell the truth about sin, protect those at risk, remove provision for the flesh, preserve a path of honest confession, call the child to Christ, and look over time for the fruit of repentance without pretending to possess God’s knowledge of the heart.**

## Audit guards

**PASTORAL SYNTHESIS:** not an inspired algorithm.  
**SAFETY:** victimization/crime overrides amateur church-only handling.

**NO PERFECTIONISM:** relapse alone ≠ settled impenitence.  
**NO PERMISSIVENESS:** unregeneration ≠ immunity from law/consequences.
