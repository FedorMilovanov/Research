## 0. Executive verdict

Digital evidence is often highly useful. It is also easy to overread.

A family may discover:

- screenshots;
- screen recordings;
- profile pages;
- account handles/display names;
- timestamps;
- deleted-message markers;
- location history;
- app logs;
- cloud/email notifications;
- forwarded media;
- AI-generated or edited images/audio/video.

None should be treated as morally irrelevant merely because digital artifacts can be manipulated.

But no single artifact should be asked to prove more than it actually establishes.

### Locked distinctions

> **DIGITAL ARTIFACT ≠ SELF-INTERPRETING FACT.**

> **SCREENSHOT ≠ COMPLETE RECORD.**

> **DISPLAY NAME / AVATAR ≠ PROVED ACCOUNT OWNER.**

> **TIMESTAMP ≠ WHOLE CHRONOLOGY.**

> **DELETED-MESSAGE MARKER ≠ PROVED MOTIVE FOR DELETION.**

> **CROPPED IMAGE ≠ SOURCE CONTEXT.**

> **DIGITAL EVIDENCE CAN BE STRONG WITHOUT BEING INFALLIBLE.**

> **MANIPULATION IS POSSIBLE ≠ THIS ARTIFACT IS FAKE.**

> **PRESERVING AN ORIGINAL ≠ PARENT BECOMES A FORENSIC EXAMINER.**

> **LOW CHILD CREDIBILITY ≠ EVERY DIGITAL ACCUSATION IS AUTHENTIC.**

## 2. A screenshot can establish something without establishing everything

A screenshot may strongly establish that, at some moment, a device/interface displayed particular visual information.

Depending on provenance/context, it may support:

- existence of a conversation fragment;
- presence of an account/profile;
- displayed username;
- displayed timestamp;
- displayed media/text;
- app state at capture.

But a screenshot alone may not establish:

- who physically operated the account;
- whether the display name was changed;
- whether prior/later messages alter meaning;
- whether the image was edited before receipt;
- whether the timestamp uses local/device/server time;
- whether the screenshot depicts an original post versus a forwarded/cached/reposted copy;
- whether apparent deletion was user-directed or platform behavior;
- motive.

### P0

> **THE QUESTION IS NOT “IS A SCREENSHOT EVIDENCE?” BUT “EVIDENCE OF WHAT, WITH WHAT PROVENANCE AND LIMITS?”**

---

## 3. Crop and context

Cropping can be innocent:

- hiding unrelated names;
- focusing on relevant text;
- protecting privacy.

It can also remove material context:

- prior denial/correction;
- message sender identity;
- date;
- app/interface state;
- surrounding sarcasm/quotation;
- evidence that an image was forwarded rather than created by the apparent sender.

### Family rule

Where the issue is materially serious, prefer preserving the most original/complete available object or context rather than relying only on a cropped derivative.

### Guard

`CROP ≠ FRAUD`.

But:

`CROP ≠ FULL CONTEXT`.

---

## 4. Account attribution — handle, display name and avatar are different from identity

Platforms may permit:

- mutable display names;
- similar usernames;
- copied avatars;
- parody/impersonation accounts;
- shared devices;
- shared credentials;
- compromised accounts;
- proxy use by friends.

Therefore:

> `The profile says “Ivan”` is not automatically identical to `Ivan controlled this account at the relevant moment`.

Attribution may become much stronger when multiple facts converge:

- unique account identifier;
- direct device/session evidence;
- admissions;
- known linked phone/email;
- independent message continuity;
- platform records;
- corroborating witnesses;
- consistent device/account history.

### Locked

`NAME MATCH ≠ IDENTITY PROOF`.

`ATTRIBUTION CAN BE CUMULATIVE`.

---

## 5. Timestamp limits

A displayed timestamp can be important.

But chronology can be affected by:

- device time-zone settings;
- platform localization;
- server versus local display time;
- message edits/reposts;
- screenshots taken later than the underlying event;
- imported/exported data formats;
- daylight-saving transitions;
- cached interface state.

### Product guard

Do not publish or pastorally assert:

> `This screenshot says 01:14, therefore we know exactly where and when the underlying act occurred.`

unless the timestamp semantics are actually established.

### Locked

`DISPLAYED TIME ≠ AUTOMATIC EVENT-TIME FORENSICS`.

---

## 6. Deletion is evidence of absence/change, not automatic motive proof

A family may see:

- `message deleted`;
- missing chat history;
- cleared browsing data;
- disappearing-message settings;
- absent media.

This can be materially suspicious, especially after an established concealment pattern.

But deletion can also result from:

- platform auto-expiry;
- sender-side deletion;
- storage cleanup;
- app reinstall/sync behavior;
- normal privacy practices;
- moderation/takedown;
- another account/device user.

### P0

> **DELETION MAY SUPPORT AN INFERENCE; IT DOES NOT BY ITSELF REVEAL THE HEART.**

Do not convert:

`data absent`

into

`child intentionally destroyed it to hide this exact sin`

without supporting evidence.

Use `288` for correction when motive was falsely asserted as fact.

---

## 7. Forwarded evidence and source lineage

A screenshot received from a peer may be valuable.

But distinguish:

1. original platform/account record;
2. direct screenshot by a witness;
3. screenshot forwarded by another person;
4. screenshot of a screenshot;
5. copied text without image/context;
6. rumor describing alleged screenshot no one can currently produce.

Each step can reduce what can safely be claimed about provenance.

### Locked

> **FORWARDED ≠ FALSE.**

> **FORWARDED ≠ ORIGINAL.**

This becomes important for the separate third-party source-lineage question.

## 8. AI/deepfake/manipulation — two opposite errors

`185` owns synthetic sexual harm.

This module owns the evidentiary mirror.

### Error A — naive realism

> `It looks real, therefore it happened.`

AI generation, compositing, image editing, voice cloning and impersonation mean appearance alone can be insufficient in a high-stakes dispute.

### Error B — universal nihilism

> `AI exists, therefore no digital image/audio/video can prove anything anymore.`

Also false.

Authenticity can be supported through provenance, originals, metadata, independent records, source devices/platform data, corroboration and expert examination where needed.

### Locked

> **SYNTHETIC MEDIA RAISES THE VALUE OF PROVENANCE; IT DOES NOT ABOLISH EVIDENCE.**

## 9. NIST professional boundary

NIST SP 800-101 Rev. 1 describes mobile device forensics as recovering digital evidence under forensically sound conditions and separately treats:

- validation;
- preservation;
- acquisition;
- examination;
- analysis;
- reporting.

Source:
- NIST CSRC, `SP 800-101 Rev. 1, Guidelines on Mobile Device Forensics`: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/101/r1/final

### Project significance

This does **not** mean ordinary parents must perform forensic acquisition.

It establishes the opposite boundary:

> professional digital evidence work is a specialized process, so a family should not assume that scrolling through a phone and interpreting one visual artifact is equivalent to forensic certainty.

Evidence class: `PROFESSIONAL / GOVERNMENT TECHNICAL GUIDANCE`.

---

## 10. SWGDE 2025 — preserve integrity, source and documentation

Scientific Working Group on Digital Evidence, `Best Practices for Digital Evidence Collection`, v2.0, 2025-11-20:

- data integrity should be preserved;
- third-party electronic data should have an original/static copy documented where possible;
- source should be documented;
- cryptographic hashing is used to establish/verify a baseline where forensic acquisition applies;
- chain-of-custody/evidence inventory and contemporaneous collection notes matter;
- screenshots can be documentation but are not the whole acquisition model.

Sources:
- https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/18-f-002-2-0/
- https://swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-11-20-Best-Practices-for-Digital-Evidence-Collection-18-F-002-2.0.pdf

SWGDE remote-collection guidance likewise emphasizes definitive source/endpoint identification, acquisition details, hash values, screenshots, acquisition date/errors and chain of custody:
- https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/22-f-003-best-practices-for-remote-collection-of-digital-evidence-from-an-endpoint/

### Strict transfer guard

Do **not** turn this into:

> `Christian parents must hash every screenshot.`

These are professional forensic best practices.

Their safe pastoral implication is narrower:

> **the more serious the allegation and consequence, the more important provenance, context and preservation become — and the less appropriate amateur certainty becomes.**

## 11. Ordinary family preservation vs forensic examination

For an ordinary non-criminal household matter, useful minimal practice may include:

- do not immediately alter/delete the relevant original;
- note where the artifact came from;
- preserve enough surrounding context to understand it;
- distinguish original from forwarded/cropped copy;
- note obvious date/time/account uncertainty;
- avoid repeatedly redistributing sensitive material;
- keep only what has a legitimate stewardship purpose;
- stop once the `281` stopping rule is reached.

This is `PRUDENTIAL_APPLICATION`, not a technical forensic protocol.

### Escalation boundary

If the evidence concerns:

- sexual exploitation;
- illegal sexual imagery;
- grooming;
- sextortion;
- credible violence;
- criminal conduct;
- serious safeguarding allegation;

then ordinary family handling should give way to competent legal/safeguarding/forensic process where appropriate.

Use `283`.

## 12. Never make illicit sexual imagery the family’s authenticity test

If an explicit image of a minor or possible child sexual abuse material is involved, do not reason:

> `We need everybody in the family/church to inspect it so we can decide whether it is real.`

This risks:

- further possession/distribution;
- victim re-exposure;
- evidentiary damage;
- legal/safeguarding problems;
- unnecessary sexual exposure.

### Locked

`AUTHENTICITY QUESTION ≠ PERMISSION TO MULTIPLY THE IMAGE`.

Use `283` and competent process.

---

## 13. Parent-generated screenshots also need humility

Parents may themselves capture evidence during inspection.

They should not later forget the distinction between:

- what was directly visible;
- what they inferred;
- what they remember;
- what the screenshot actually preserves.

Example:

Parent saw a chat briefly, later says:

> `The screenshot proves you planned a meeting.`

But the screenshot preserves only:

> `See you tomorrow?`

The meeting interpretation may be plausible, but the image should not be made to say more than its contents/context support.

### P0

> **PARENTAL SCREENSHOT ≠ PARENTAL INFALLIBILITY.**

---

## 14. Child explanation also is not automatic disproof

Mirror guard:

> `That screenshot is out of context` does not automatically make it harmless.

A context claim can be tested where material:

- ask what context changes the meaning;
- inspect fuller thread if proportionate/available;
- compare independent evidence;
- distinguish explanation from established correction.

### Locked

`CONTEXT CLAIM ≠ AUTOMATIC EXONERATION`.

`REQUESTING CONTEXT ≠ REJECTING EVIDENCE`.

---

## 15. Church receipt of digital material

A pastor/elder may receive a screenshot from parents or another youth.

`282` controls church evidentiary responsibility.

The church should not silently convert:

> `Parent showed us a screenshot`

into

> `Church independently verified the underlying event`.

Questions can include:

- who captured it;
- where it came from;
- whether it is original/forwarded;
- what exact fact it establishes;
- whether the church needs more at all;
- whether safeguarding/civil authorities rather than elders should handle the evidentiary question.

### Locked

`RECEIVED EVIDENCE ≠ INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED EVIDENCE`.

---

## 16. Serious consequence should raise the evidence discipline

The project should avoid a rigid pseudo-legal formula.

But prudentially:

> **the more serious, public, durable or safety-sensitive the consequence, the stronger the need to distinguish established fact from inference.**

Examples:

- temporary device restriction may reasonably rest on a risk history plus current suspicion;
- public accusation of distributing sexual material should require much stronger support;
- church role restriction can sometimes be temporarily precautionary without a final guilt verdict;
- civil/criminal allegations belong to competent process.

### Guard

`HIGHER EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE ≠ NO PRECAUTION UNTIL COURT-LEVEL PROOF`.

Use explicit language:

- `precaution`;
- `unresolved allegation`;
- `established fact`;
- `strongly supported inference`.

## 17. Case matrix

| Artifact/case | What it may support | What it does not automatically prove | Next step if material |
|---|---|---|---|
| cropped chat screenshot | displayed fragment | full conversation/context | preserve fuller source/context if proportionate |
| account with child’s display name | apparent association | controller/owner at relevant time | corroborate attribution |
| `message deleted` marker | content removed/unavailable | who deleted it or why | investigate only if material |
| timestamp | displayed time value | exact event chronology/location | establish platform/time semantics if needed |
| forwarded screenshot from peer | witness-provided digital artifact | original provenance | identify source lineage / original if serious |
| realistic sexual image | existence of image | depicted event actually occurred | do not redistribute; safeguarding/forensic process |
| child says `AI fake` | competing explanation | falsity by assertion alone | provenance/expert process where stakes require |
| parent saw something but screenshot omits key part | parent testimony + partial artifact | exact omitted content | calibrate claim; do not make screenshot bear memory |

## 18. Product overclaim kill-switch

Reject or rewrite:

- `Screenshots do not lie.`
- `If his name/avatar is on the account, it is proved to be his.`
- `A deleted message proves a guilty motive.`
- `The timestamp proves the whole chronology.`
- `A cropped screenshot is worthless.`
- `AI means no digital evidence can be trusted.`
- `If the child says deepfake, parents must believe it.`
- `Parents should forensically image every device after lying.`
- `Every screenshot should be hashed by Christian families.`
- `Pastors can authenticate digital evidence by visual inspection.`
- `To prove whether a sexual image is real, show it to more adults.`

Preserve:

> **Digital artifacts can expose real hidden conduct, but they do not interpret themselves. Strong family and church judgment distinguishes the artifact from the inference: what is original, what is forwarded, what identity is actually established, what the timestamp means, what context is missing, and whether the stakes now require competent forensic or safeguarding help. The possibility of manipulation is a reason for provenance, not a reason for universal disbelief.**

## 20. Final locked synthesis

> **Скриншот может быть сильным доказательством, но не является всеведущим свидетелем. Он показывает то, что сохранил, а не автоматически владельца аккаунта, весь контекст, мотив и полную хронологию. Поэтому после реальной лжи нельзя ни обожествлять цифровой след, ни объявлять его бессмысленным из-за AI. Чем тяжелее обвинение, тем важнее происхождение, контекст и точность формулировки; а когда дело становится safeguarding/forensic, родители и пасторы не должны изображать из себя цифровую лабораторию.**
