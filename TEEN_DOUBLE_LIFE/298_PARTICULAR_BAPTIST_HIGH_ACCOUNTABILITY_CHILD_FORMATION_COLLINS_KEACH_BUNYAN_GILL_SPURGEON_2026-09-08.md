**Purpose:** test whether a high-accountability credobaptist model is a modern synthetic workaround or has real historical Baptist pedigree.

> Governing finding: historic Baptist denial of infant baptism did **not** require a morally or spiritually neutral childhood. # 0. Why this file matters

The project’s comparative question can be badly framed as:

> `Paedobaptists have covenant children with real duties; Baptists have outsiders whom they merely wait to convert.`

Primary Baptist sources falsify that binary.

A historically recognizable Baptist pattern is:

`NO INHERITED BAPTISM / NO INHERITED CHURCH MEMBERSHIP`

plus

`REAL CHILD DUTY + PARENTAL GOVERNMENT + CATECHESIS + PRAYER + SCRIPTURE + CORRECTION + GOSPEL SUMMONS NOW`

plus

`EXTERNAL FORM / REFORMATION ≠ REGENERATION`.

The key question left for the paedobaptist/credobaptist dispute is therefore **formal covenant/church membership and sacramental entitlement**, not whether children should presently live under God-conscious moral and religious formation.

# 1. Hercules Collins — 1680 Orthodox Catechism

## 1.1. Source identity

Hercules Collins, *An Orthodox Catechism* (London, 1680), a Particular Baptist adaptation of the Heidelberg Catechism.

Primary/original-object leads:

- Angus Library / Regent’s Park College scan record:
  https://theangus.rpc.ox.ac.uk/treasures/an-orthodox-catechism-being-the-sum-of-christian-religion-contained-in-the-law-and-gospel/
- modern transcription keyed to the 1680 text:
  https://anorthodoxcatechism.org/

## 1.2. Collins’s preface — children’s souls are a present parental stewardship

The preface is directly relevant to this project. Collins urges Christians to:

- love their children’s souls;
- show that love by praying for them;
- instruct them, invoking Abraham as a model;
- win them toward good by good example;
- use catechetical instruction so youth learn the principles of Christian religion;
- be seriously concerned for their children’s everlasting welfare.

The catechetical form itself is expressly presented as useful for youth.

### Historical significance

This is very close chronologically and confessionally to the Second London Baptist Confession environment.

It makes the following modern slogan historically implausible as a necessary 1689 Baptist consequence:

> `He has not professed faith / been baptized, so spiritual formation is mostly waiting.`

Collins instead assumes **active household means before baptismal profession**.

## 1.3. Same Collins — believer-only baptism

The same catechism rejects infant baptism and identifies proper baptismal subjects as those who actually profess:

- repentance toward God;
- faith in Christ;
- obedience to Christ.

### Crucial conjunction

Collins does not solve child formation by lowering the baptismal boundary.

He holds together:

> **intensive Christian formation of children**

and

> **baptism after actual profession**.

This is an early Particular Baptist form of the model this Research lane is trying to recover.

# 2. Hercules Collins — children of godly parents have a real advantage without baptismal entitlement

## 2.1. Primary source

Hercules Collins, *Believers-baptism from heaven, and of divine institution; infants-baptism from earth, and human invention*.

EEBO / University of Michigan transcription, pp. 47–48:

https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo2/B20542.0001.001/1:5.5?rgn=div2;view=fulltext

## 2.2. The unusually useful concession

In anti-paedobaptist polemic Collins nevertheless explicitly grants that it is a **great mercy** for children to have godly parents because they possess advantages such as:

- good education;
- prayer;
- good examples.

He then refuses the next inference:

> these real advantages do not establish that God appointed baptism for infants.

### Project significance

This is a near-perfect historical control for our emerging distinction:

`SPECIAL CHRISTIAN-HOUSEHOLD PRIVILEGE`

≠

`SAVING STATUS`

≠

`BAPTISMAL ENTITLEMENT`.

Therefore a Baptist need not flatten children of believers into a sociologically or pastorally ordinary outsider in order to defend credobaptism.

### Exegetical caveat

Collins follows the older Baptist `1 Cor 7:14 = legitimate children` reading. Current Research should not make that lexical solution authoritative merely because Collins is confessionally near. Use `279` for the stronger modern Baptist alternative that can admit a real set-apart household relation without deriving baptism from it.

# 3. The Baptist Catechism — fifth-commandment duty remains part of Baptist formation

## 3.1. Source identity

The Baptist Catechism, commissioned in the London Particular Baptist context and commonly associated with William Collins; oldest known surviving edition is 1695.

Accessible historical-text presentation:

https://baptistcatechism.org/

## 3.2. Questions 68–71

The catechism explicitly retains the fifth-commandment framework:

- honor father and mother;
- preserve honor;
- perform duties belonging to the relations God has ordered;
- neglect/opposition to those duties is forbidden.

### Project significance

Credobaptist ecclesiology did not suspend filial moral duty until personal profession or baptism.

A child does not first acquire the fifth commandment at baptism.

### Later Baptist exposition

Benjamin Beddome’s catechetical exposition makes the parental side concrete:

- provide for children;
- instruct them;
- correct where needed;
- avoid severity/passion;
- pray for them;
- pray with them.

This is later testimony and should not be retroactively attributed to William Collins, but it demonstrates continuity of Baptist catechetical family practice.

# 5. Benjamin Keach — *War with the Devil* and the double-life problem

## 5.1. Source identity

Benjamin Keach, *War with the Devil: or, The Young Man’s Conflict with the Powers of Darkness*.

Archival witnesses:

- Folger records identify the work as chiefly intended for the instruction of younger persons:
  https://catalog.folger.edu/record/444479
  https://catalog.folger.edu/record/452752
- accessible text witness:
  https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/keach/War%20With%20the%20Devil_Benjamin%20Keach_crowdedship.pdf

## 5.2. Keach sharply distinguishes outward reform from heart change

The dramatic dialogue repeatedly attacks the assumption:

> visible abandonment of some bad behavior = proof of conversion.

The youth points to an impressive outward change; Truth answers that a person may turn from outward filthiness while remaining inwardly unchanged.

### Direct relevance to DВР

This is historically striking because it holds together the same categories the series needs:

- sin in youth is real and must be confronted;
- outward reform is genuinely distinguishable from gross outward vice;
- yet outward reform does not itself prove regeneration;
- conversion involves a deeper heart reality that external pressure cannot manufacture.

### Locked project principle

> **The Baptist answer to false profession is not to stop requiring outward obedience; it is to refuse to rename outward obedience “new birth.”**

This principle should be independently grounded in Scripture/1689, with Keach serving as historical corroboration.

# 6. John Bunyan — allied baptistic/Puritan witness, with taxonomy guard

## 6.1. Source

John Bunyan, *Christian Behaviour*, section `Duty of Parents to Children`.

Accessible text witnesses:

- https://www.fpchurch.org.uk/publications/the-free-presbyterian-magazine/y1964/m10/christian-behaviour/
- https://www.originalsources.com/Document.aspx?DocID=HIXBG75H7P26U5R

## 6.2. Core structure

Bunyan tells parents that:

- their children have souls;
- natural birth is not enough: they must be begotten of God;
- parents therefore must instruct and correct;
- instruction should be understandable and diligent;
- parental behavior/example matters.

### Project significance

Again the categories remain distinct:

`CHILD NEEDS NEW BIRTH`

and

`PARENT MUST TEACH / GOVERN / CORRECT NOW`.

### Taxonomy guard

Bunyan is an important baptistic Nonconformist/Puritan witness but should not be used as though he were the formal confessional spokesman of the London Particular Baptist churches. Collins/Keach/Gill are closer controls for that purpose.

# 7. John Gill — the strongest mature Particular Baptist synthesis for this project

## 7.1. Primary source

John Gill, *A Body of Practical Divinity*, `Of the Respective Duties of Parents and Children`:

https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html

## 7.2. Child duty exists by nature and God’s command

Gill roots parent-child duties in:

- natural relation;
- gratitude;
- the fifth commandment;
- Ephesians 6;
- God’s own authority.

He treats children as owing:

- love;
- honor;
- reverence;
- gratitude;
- subjection/obedience in lawful things.

He explicitly says parental power changes as children grow, while filial observance/reverence does not simply disappear.

### Project use

This is devastating to the pastoral formula:

> `Until conversion, Christian parents can expect only whatever behavior naturally happens.`

Gill treats filial duty as an objective moral reality, not as a privilege created by regeneration.

## 7.3. Parent duty is explicitly religious

Gill’s parental program includes:

- religious training;
- good example;
- keeping children from corrupting company;
- checking early manifestations of sin;
- naming the evil of lying and corrupt speech;
- praying with and for children;
- bringing them under the means of grace / ministry of the Word;
- teaching Scripture early according to capacity;
- teaching the evil of sin;
- teaching their miserable natural state;
- teaching recovery/salvation through Christ;
- admonishing them concerning duty, fear of God and commandments.

This is extraordinarily close to the controlling anthropology of the present Research series:

`TOTAL DEPRAVITY + SOVEREIGN GRACE + ORDAINED MEANS + FULL RESPONSIBILITY`.

## 7.4. Gill’s anti-false-profession guard is equally strong

Gill then explicitly criticizes forms of child education that teach a child to say things equivalent to:

> `I believe ...`

before the child actually has knowledge/faith, because this can train the child to rest in outward religious form.

### Critical synthesis

Gill therefore refuses **both** errors:

1. `NO REGENERATION YET -> NO SERIOUS RELIGIOUS FORMATION`;
2. `RELIGIOUS FORMATION / RECITATION -> TREAT AS REGENERATED`.

### Article-ready synthesis

> **Teach the child Christianity before conversion; do not teach the child to counterfeit conversion.**

This is project synthesis, not a direct Gill quotation.

# 8. Charles Spurgeon — immediate conversion, real examination, no child discount

## 8.1. Primary source

Charles H. Spurgeon, `Do Not Sin Against the Child`:

https://www.spurgeon.org/sermons/do-not-sin-against-the-child

## 8.2. Spurgeon rejects delayed concern

He insists that God truly can save children and attacks a church instinct that effectively postpones serious expectation of conversion until later life.

### Project use

`CHILDHOOD ≠ PRE-EVANGELISTIC WAITING ROOM`.

Seek actual repentance and faith now.

## 8.3. He also rejects premature certification

Spurgeon will not:

- identify first terror over sin automatically with repentance;
- identify first joy at Christ’s love automatically with saving faith;
- admit a child to church without examination;
- use a lower **kind** of repentance/faith for children than for adults.

He simultaneously opposes needless suspicion of credible child conversion.

### Project synthesis

`NO INFANT BAPTISM / NO HEREDITARY CERTIFICATE`

+

`NO DEFERRAL OF CONVERSION`

+

`NO CHILD DISCOUNT ON REAL REPENTANCE/FAITH`

+

`NO ADULT-MATURITY REQUIREMENT FOR CHILD-SIZED FRUIT`.

# 9. Historical comparison with the paedobaptist model

The historic Baptist sources reveal that the core difference is **not formation intensity**.

### Strong Westminster / Continental model

`BELIEVERS' CHILD -> COVENANT / VISIBLE-CHURCH MEMBER -> BAPTISM -> CATECHESIS / DISCIPLINE -> PERSONAL PROFESSION STILL REQUIRED`.

### Strong Particular Baptist model

`BELIEVERS' CHILD -> NO INHERITED MEMBERSHIP / BAPTISM -> INTENSIVE HOUSEHOLD CHRISTIAN FORMATION + DUTY + MEANS -> PERSONAL REPENTANCE / FAITH -> BAPTISM / CHURCH ADMISSION`.

### Historical verdict

The sentence

> `Baptists historically just treated children as ordinary unbelieving outsiders until they were old enough to choose Christianity`

is false as a general description of the Particular Baptist tradition represented here.

# 10. What the Particular Baptist tradition gains

## 10.1. It protects baptism/member status from natural descent

No biological/cultural inheritance is allowed to substitute for personal credible profession.

## 10.2. It can press real duty without manufacturing assurance

A child can be told truthfully:

- obey your parents;
- stop lying;
- flee immorality;
- hear Scripture;
- pray/seek God;
- come under Christian instruction;
- you need a new heart;
- none of these outward acts by themselves certify that you possess that new heart.

## 10.3. It preserves the use of means under sovereign grace

The sources do not reason:

> `God must regenerate, therefore parental/church means are spiritually irrelevant.`

They reason in the opposite direction: parents teach, pray, correct, exemplify, bring to the Word, and seek present conversion while knowing only God gives life.

# 11. Where Baptist practice can still fail despite this heritage

Historic resources do not automatically become modern institutional practice.

A credobaptist church may still create an **ecclesial vacuum** if it says:

> `parents are responsible for their nonmember child, therefore pastors have no named continuing responsibility until baptism`.

The primary sources here strongly support household duty, but they do not by themselves create a modern church-order mechanism equivalent to PCA/OPC/URCNA baptized-child membership.

Therefore the modern 1689 pastoral question remains legitimate:

> **How can a church give known nonmember children sustained pastoral ownership without pretending they are formal members?**

Use `286` and `287` for the institutional proposal.

# 12. Strong proposed 1689 synthesis after the historical pass

> **A Baptist church does not need to baptize an infant in order to refuse moral neutrality. The Particular Baptist tradition already knew how to distinguish a child’s objective duty, parental government, catechesis, prayer, Scripture, correction and present summons to Christ from the separate question of baptism and church membership. Its failure mode is therefore not credobaptism itself, but forgetting its own theology of household formation and ordained means.**

# 14. Final locked historical finding

> **High-accountability credobaptism has historical Baptist pedigree. Collins can deny infant baptism while calling godly parentage a great mercy because of education, prayer and example. Keach can aim books at spiritually educating youth while warning that outward reform does not equal conversion. Gill can demand obedience, correction, Scripture, prayer and means of grace while explicitly rejecting manufactured “I believe” religion. Spurgeon can demand the same real repentance and faith in children as in adults without demanding adult maturity. The tradition’s categories are therefore not “baptized covenant child” versus “morally neutral unbelieving child,” but “real duty and intensive formation now” versus “church sign/status only upon credible personal profession.”**
