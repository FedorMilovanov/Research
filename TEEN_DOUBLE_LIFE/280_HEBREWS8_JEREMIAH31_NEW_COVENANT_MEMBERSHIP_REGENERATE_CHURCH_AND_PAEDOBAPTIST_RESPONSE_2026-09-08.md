# Hebrews 8 / Jeremiah 31 — New Covenant Membership
## Regenerate-Church Baptist Argument and Paedobaptist Responses

**Status:** RESEARCH / HIGH-LEVERAGE COVENANT THEOLOGY / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `273–279`

> This is the doctrinal fork most likely to control the baptism/member-status question. Treat it with more weight than speculative household-infant arguments.

## 1. New Covenant promises in Jeremiah 31:31–34 / Hebrews 8:6–13

The contrast with the covenant made in the Exodus includes:

- law placed in mind/heart;
- reciprocal God/people relation;
- universal knowledge of God within the promised people (`all shall know me, least to greatest`);
- definitive forgiveness/remembrance of sins no more.

These are not merely external privileges. They are soteriologically rich promises.

## 2. Strong Baptist inference

Contemporary credobaptist covenant theology reasons:

1. all New Covenant members, in the covenant’s proper saving sense, know God;
2. all possess the internalized-law/forgiveness blessings described;
3. therefore the church should administer the New Covenant sign only to persons who give a credible profession of such faith;
4. infant birth to a believer supplies no such profession;
5. thus baptism and visible membership should follow credible faith.

### Important precision

This does **not** claim Baptist elders can see regeneration infallibly.

The normative claim is:

> only credible professors are *admitted as members*, even though some professions may later prove false.

That differs materially from knowingly admitting a person who has made no profession because of parental status.

## 3. Paedobaptist response A — visible administration vs saving substance

Classic Reformed covenant theology distinguishes:

- outward/legal/visible covenant administration;
- inward/vital/saving participation.

Therefore `all shall know me` can describe the saving efficacy/substance promised in the New Covenant without requiring the earthly visible administration to be epistemically unmixed.

### Baptist reply

The question is not whether hypocrites can exist. The question is whether Scripture positively authorizes visible membership **without any profession** in the New Covenant.

## 4. Paedobaptist response B — already/not yet consummation

A Reformed response notes that several promises in Jeremiah 31 / Hebrews 8 are not experienced in complete eschatological fullness now:

- believers still need external teaching;
- law internalization is real but not perfected;
- visible Christian communities still contain apostasy/false profession;
- complete covenant realization awaits consummation.

Thus `all shall know me` may reach its exhaustive fulfillment eschatologically rather than function as a present church-roll admission rule.

### Baptist reply

Even if consummation perfects the promise, Hebrews presents the New Covenant as already inaugurated and superior. The present church should therefore approximate its regenerate constitution through profession rather than intentionally recreate genealogical mixed membership.

## 5. Paedobaptist response C — the target contrast is Mosaic, not Abrahamic

Hebrews says the new covenant is not like the covenant made when God brought Israel out of Egypt. Reformed paedobaptists therefore distinguish:

- Abrahamic covenant of promise, whose believer+seed pattern continues;
- Mosaic/old-covenant administration, whose shadows/priesthood/weakness pass away.

On this account, Jeremiah 31 does not announce the cancellation of Abrahamic child inclusion.

### Baptist reply

The New Covenant is not merely Moses-minus-ceremonies. Its membership/blessings are defined Christologically and internally. Therefore Abrahamic genealogical administration must itself be re-read through the New Covenant rather than automatically carried over.

## 6. Paedobaptist response D — warning passages

Hebrews warns people located in the Christian covenant community against apostasy (Heb 6; 10). Paedobaptists argue that this demonstrates a visible covenant administration wider than final saving participation.

### Baptist reply

Warnings can be addressed to visible professors whose final state is not humanly known. They do not by themselves prove membership of nonprofessing infants.

## 7. Calvin as a historical control

Calvin recognizes real newness/superiority over the Mosaic covenant but maintains substantive continuity in the one covenant of grace. His paedobaptism is therefore not contradicted, in his system, by the internal promises of Jeremiah 31.

This must be represented fairly: he is not ignoring the New Covenant’s internality; he understands it within inaugurated covenant continuity.

## 8. Gill / later Baptist control

Gill reads `all shall know me` in Heb 8:11 as spiritual/experimental knowledge attended by faith, love, fear and obedience. This naturally fits the Particular Baptist insistence that New Testament church membership should consist of visible saints/professors.

Modern Baptist theologians sharpen the contrast by distinguishing:

- an infallibly regenerate church (not claimed);
- a regenerate-church **admission principle** (credible profession required).

## 9. Child-accountability consequence independent of final baptism verdict

Even if the Baptist wins the membership inference, one false practical conclusion must be blocked:

`NOT YET NEW-COVENANT MEMBER -> NOT PRESENTLY RESPONSIBLE`.

Jeremiah/Hebrews never says unbelievers are free from God’s law until covenant admission. 1689 19 explicitly blocks that move.

Likewise, even if the paedobaptist wins child membership, another false conclusion is blocked:

`VISIBLE COVENANT MEMBER -> CERTAINLY REGENERATE`.

Westminster 28.5 and broader Reformed doctrine block that move.
