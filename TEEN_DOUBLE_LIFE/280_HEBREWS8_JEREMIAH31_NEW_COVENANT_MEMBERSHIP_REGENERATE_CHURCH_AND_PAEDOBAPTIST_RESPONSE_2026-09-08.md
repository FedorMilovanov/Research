# Hebrews 8 / Jeremiah 31 — New Covenant Membership
## Regenerate-Church Baptist Argument, Westminster Continuity, and Paedobaptist Responses

**Status:** RESEARCH / HIGH-LEVERAGE COVENANT THEOLOGY / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Depends on:** `273–279`, `284`, `298`

> This is the doctrinal fork most likely to control the baptism/member-status question. Treat it with more weight than speculative household-infant arguments. Do not pretend that the child-accountability question rises or falls with the baptism verdict.

---

# 1. New Covenant promises in Jeremiah 31:31–34 / Hebrews 8:6–13

The contrast with the covenant made in the Exodus includes:

- law placed in mind/heart;
- reciprocal God/people relation;
- universal knowledge of God within the promised people (`all shall know me, least to greatest`);
- definitive forgiveness/remembrance of sins no more.

These are not merely external privileges. They are soteriologically rich promises.

---

# 2. The confessional fork is visible before detailed exegesis

## Westminster Confession 7.5–6

Westminster explicitly describes the covenant of grace as **one and the same covenant in substance under different administrations**.

Under the law it was administered through promises, sacrifices, circumcision and other ordinances; under the gospel through Word, Baptism and Lord’s Supper.

That continuity framework is structurally important for infant baptism.

## Westminster Confession 25.2

The visible church under the gospel consists of those professing the true religion **together with their children**.

Thus Westminster does not derive infant church status from one isolated baptism text; it rests on a broader covenant-administration model.

## 1689 chapter 7

The Second London Baptist Confession significantly does **not** reproduce Westminster 7.5–6’s `one covenant, two administrations` formulation.

Instead it says:

- the covenant of grace freely offers sinners life/salvation in Christ, requiring faith;
- God promises the Spirit to those ordained to eternal life to make them willing/able to believe;
- the covenant was revealed progressively from Genesis 3 until its **full discovery was completed in the New Testament**.

## 1689 chapter 26

Particular congregations are constituted of visible saints — persons professing gospel faith and obedience, with profession not destroyed by foundational error or unholiness.

### Project significance

The Baptist/Westminster difference is therefore not a late dispute about mode of baptism alone.

It reaches into:

- covenant continuity/administration;
- who belongs to the visible church;
- what visible membership presupposes;
- how Old Testament genealogical structures carry into the New Covenant.

### Guard

Do not overclaim that 1689 chapter 7 by itself proves one modern system commonly called `1689 Federalism` in every detail. The confessional textual divergence is direct; later systematizations require their own historical proof.

# 3. Strong Baptist inference from Jeremiah 31 / Hebrews 8

A credobaptist argument reasons:

1. New Covenant members, in the covenant’s proper saving sense, know God;
2. the covenant promises internalized law and forgiveness;
3. its visible sign should therefore be administered to persons who give credible personal profession of faith;
4. birth to a believer does not itself give such profession;
5. church admission should seek visible correspondence with New Covenant realities through profession.

### Important precision

This does **not** claim Baptist elders can see regeneration infallibly.

The normative claim is:

> **only credible professors are intentionally admitted as members, though some professions may later prove false.**

That is materially different from intentionally admitting a nonprofessing person because of parental covenant status.

---

# 4. Paedobaptist response A — visible administration vs saving substance

Classic Reformed covenant theology distinguishes:

- outward/legal/visible administration;
- inward/vital/saving participation.

Therefore `all shall know me` can describe the saving efficacy/substance of the New Covenant without requiring the earthly visible administration to be epistemically unmixed.

### Baptist reply

The dispute is not merely whether hypocrites can appear among professors.

It is:

> **Does Scripture positively authorize visible New Covenant church membership for persons who have made no personal profession, solely through covenant relation to a believing parent?**

False professors do not automatically answer that question because they entered *as professors*, even if wrongly judged.

---

# 5. Paedobaptist response B — already / not yet consummation

A Reformed response notes that Jeremiah 31 / Hebrews 8 promises are not all experienced in exhaustive consummate form now:

- believers still need teaching;
- law internalization is real but imperfectly manifested;
- Christian communities still contain hypocrites/apostates;
- complete covenant realization awaits consummation.

Thus `all shall know me` need not function as a mechanical present church-roll admission statute.

### Baptist reply

Even if eschatological fullness remains future, Hebrews presents the New Covenant as **already inaugurated and superior**.

Therefore Baptist ecclesiology argues that the church should intentionally approximate its professedly regenerate constitution instead of importing genealogical inclusion without a New Testament warrant.

---

# 6. Paedobaptist response C — Jeremiah contrasts Mosaic, not Abrahamic

Hebrews says the New Covenant is not like the covenant made when God brought Israel from Egypt.

Classic paedobaptism therefore distinguishes:

- Abrahamic covenant of promise / believer-and-seed pattern;
- Mosaic administration with shadows, national polity and temporary ceremonies.

On this account, Jeremiah 31 ends the Mosaic form, not the Abrahamic inclusion principle.

### Baptist reply

The New Covenant is not merely `Moses minus ceremonies`.

Its membership/blessings are defined christologically and internally. Therefore Abrahamic genealogical administration itself must be read through fulfillment in Christ rather than automatically mapped into baptism.

This is why Gen 17, Gal 3, Rom 4, Col 2, Acts 2 and Heb 8 must be treated as a **system**, not isolated proof texts.

---

# 7. Paedobaptist response D — one covenant of grace, different administrations

Westminster 7.5–6 provides the cleanest confessional statement:

`ONE COVENANT OF GRACE IN SUBSTANCE -> LAW/GOSPEL ADMINISTRATIONS -> CIRCUMCISION/BAPTISM WITH CONTINUITY`.

That makes infant inclusion a natural systemic expectation unless the New Testament explicitly removes children.

### Baptist counter-structure

1689 retains one salvation by grace in Christ across all ages, but its chapter 7 wording refuses to simply reproduce Westminster’s administration formula.

A strong Baptist can therefore say:

> **all Old Testament saints were saved through the grace of Christ, yet not every historical covenant administration or membership principle is identical with New Covenant visible-church constitution.**

This is a much stronger argument than:

> `Old Testament different, therefore infants out.`

---

# 8. Paedobaptist response E — warning passages and visible covenant life

Hebrews 3, 6 and 10 warn people located within the Christian community against unbelief/apostasy.

Paedobaptists argue this demonstrates a visible covenant administration wider than final saving participation.

### Baptist reply

Warnings can address visible professors whose final spiritual state is unknown to humans.

They demonstrate that visible church and invisible election cannot be equated; they do not by themselves establish membership of nonprofessing infants.

### Project relevance independent of baptism

These warning texts powerfully support `150`:

> **received gospel light can aggravate responsibility even where saving regeneration is absent.**

That point is available to both systems.

# 9. Westminster child membership is not a weak concept

Westminster Larger Catechism 165–167 shows why the paedobaptist model can feel pastorally stronger on accountability.

Baptism is described as:

- solemn admission into the visible church;
- connected to covenant benefits/signification;
- an open/professed engagement to be wholly the Lord’s;
- something to be remembered/improved through repentance, faith, mortification and holy living.

Q166 says infants of one or both professing parents are in that respect within the covenant and are to be baptized.

### Consequence

For Westminster theology the child’s baptism is not supposed to say:

> `nothing is expected until a later free adult choice`.

It says almost the opposite:

> **you have been visibly claimed, marked and obligated within the church; therefore personally believe, repent and live unto the Lord.**

### Guard

This still does not prove the premise exegetically. It accurately states the pastoral force of the system that must be evaluated.

# 10. 1689 child responsibility does not require child covenant membership

Chapter 7.1 begins from a universal Creator-creature relation: reasonable creatures owe obedience to God as Creator even before discussing covenant reward.

Other 1689 chapters already locked by `108` add:

- moral law binds all (`19.5`);
- unregenerate material obedience can still be commanded/useful though not spiritually acceptable as saving obedience (`16.7`);
- prayer is required of all (`22.3`);
- family worship/public assembly remain Christian order (`22.6`);
- visible congregation/member status follows profession/walk (`26`);
- baptism follows actual profession (`29`).

### Therefore

A Baptist can consistently say:

> **You are not yet a member of the New Covenant church in our ecclesiological judgment; nevertheless you already owe God obedience as Creator, owe your parents filial duty, live under extraordinary gospel light, and are presently commanded to repent and believe.**

This is not a theological contradiction. It is exactly the distinction the current series needs.

# 11. Particular Baptist historical corroboration — `298`

The historical evidence now strengthens the confessional argument.

### Hercules Collins

- intense concern for children’s souls, prayer, instruction, example and catechesis;
- calls godly parentage a great mercy because of education/prayer/example;
- nevertheless rejects infant-baptism entitlement.

### Keach

- explicit child/youth Christian education;
- outward reform distinguished from inward conversion.

### Gill

- extensive child obedience + parental religious formation;
- means of grace and Scripture before conversion;
- rejection of manufactured first-person faith language before real faith.

### Spurgeon

- seek genuine child conversion now;
- no lower kind of saving faith/repentance;
- no premature certification.

### Historical conclusion

The Baptist position does not need to choose between:

`NO INFANT COVENANT MEMBERSHIP`

and

`NO SERIOUS CHRISTIAN FORMATION UNTIL PROFESSION`.

Historic Baptists explicitly practiced the third path.

# 12. Why this matters for the user’s original “responsibility” intuition

The original intuition can now be decomposed precisely.

### Is a Presbyterian baptized child under **more basic moral responsibility** than a Baptist unbaptized child?

**No.** Both already owe God and parents real duty.

### Is the Presbyterian child under an **additional formal ecclesial/covenant obligation by that system’s own theology?**

**Yes.** The baptism/member relation adds an objective church-status and covenant-engagement layer.

### Can that additional layer produce stronger institutional accountability?

**Yes.** PCA/OPC/URCNA show real polity consequences.

### Does that pastoral strength prove infant baptism?

**No.** The membership/sign premise still requires covenantal/exegetical proof.

### Can a Baptist reproduce every paedobaptist feature without changing ecclesiology?

**No.** A nonmember cannot truthfully be treated as a member under keys/censure.

### Can a Baptist reproduce much of the pastoral seriousness?

**Yes.** Through household government, catechesis, prayer, preaching, sustained pastoral ownership, parent accountability, direct admonition, role/access stewardship and urgent gospel summons.

# 13. Child-accountability consequence independent of final baptism verdict

Even if the Baptist wins the membership inference, block:

`NOT YET NEW-COVENANT MEMBER -> NOT PRESENTLY RESPONSIBLE`.

Jeremiah/Hebrews never grants unbelievers a moral holiday; 1689 19 explicitly blocks it.

Even if the paedobaptist wins child membership, block:

`VISIBLE COVENANT MEMBER -> CERTAINLY REGENERATE`.

Westminster 28.5 blocks it.

Therefore the secure common pastoral center is:

> **real duty + real means + urgent faith + no presumption + no neutrality.**
