## Parental authority, marriage transition, hierarchy and divine limits

## Purpose

The project already has Puritan/Reformed practical theology. This module widens the historical lens to show whether ordered household authority and relation-specific duties are merely one Puritan subculture or a broader Christian interpretive pattern.

## Chrysostom — differentiated relations and order

Primary:
- Homily 20 on Ephesians: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/230120.htm
- Homily 23 on Romans: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/210223.htm

Chrysostom treats the household/social world as containing differentiated forms of rule/subjection:

- husband/wife;
- father/son;
- elder/younger;
- ruler/ruled;
- teacher/disciple.

On Eph. 5:31 he emphasizes the remarkable creation-order strength of the marriage union: a man leaves those who begot him and is joined to his wife.

### Historical significance

Long before modern feminism/complementarian labels, major Christian interpretation naturally read Scripture as containing **real asymmetrical relations**, not flat authority equality.

But Chrysostom's use of Gen. 2:24 also distinguishes marriage from parental relation rather than merging all female subjection into one generic male jurisdiction.

## Calvin — creation order and priority of marriage

Sources:
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom41.iv.vi.vi.html
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom32.ii.lxvii.html

Calvin says marriage does not cancel filial duty, but the spouse relation has comparative priority over even the natural father/mother bond.

### Locked transition principle

> Marriage reorganizes obligations by creating a stronger one-flesh relation while filial duty continues.

This is not modern autonomous leaving-home ideology. It is covenantal reprioritization.

## Heidelberg Catechism — fifth commandment as continuing authority ethic

Source:
- https://www.heidelberg-catechism.com/en/lords-days/39.html

LD39 requires:

- honour;
- love;
- faithfulness;
- due obedience to good instruction/discipline;
- patience with weaknesses;
- recognition that God governs through legitimate authorities.

This is significantly stronger than modern ceremonial `respect your parents, but they have no special claim after adulthood` language.

At the same time the phrase **good instruction and discipline** and the larger catechetical structure keep authority subordinate to God.

## Heidelberg first-commandment ceiling

Source:
- https://www.heidelberg-catechism.com/lang/lords-days/34.html

The believer must forsake any creature rather than do the least thing against God's will.

Therefore fifth-commandment authority cannot become idolatrous absolute obedience to parents.

### Strong synthesis

> **The same catechism that tells the child God governs through parents first requires the believer to prefer God over every creature.**

This is a clean confessional anti-tyranny limit.

## Westminster Larger Catechism — relation-specific authority

See `182`.

WLC expands the fifth commandment beyond parents into a general theology of superior/inferior/equal relations and makes both sides accountable.

Historical importance:

- Reformed social ethics rejects authority-anarchy;
- it also rejects autonomous superior rule;
- authority is ministerial, relational and morally bounded.

## Reformed historical convergence

Across Calvin / Heidelberg / Westminster / Puritan household manuals:

### Strong agreement

- family authority is real;
- obedience/deference is morally serious;
- modern atomistic self-rule is foreign to their categories;
- marriage creates a distinct and stronger spouse bond;
- no human authority may require sin;
- authorities themselves have duties before God;
- maturity changes how relations operate rather than making honour optional.

### Areas with historical variation

- exact age/maturity boundary;
- father authority over adult unmarried daughter;
- necessity/force of parental consent to marriage;
- exact courtship procedure;
- civil enforcement of household authority.

Therefore historical theology supports a **strong ordered-family thesis** much more clearly than one precise twenty-first-century courtship protocol.

## Implication for feminist critique

It is historically fair to say:

> modern nonhierarchical family assumptions represent a substantial departure from mainstream premodern Christian interpretation.

It is less fair to say:

> every modern practice associated with adult female civil capacity directly contradicts the unanimous historic church.

Historical Christianity itself differentiated statuses and jurisdictions.

## Implication for strong patriarchy critique

Modern strong-patriarchal movements can legitimately claim continuity with older Christianity on:

- hierarchy;
- paternal responsibility;
- strong family involvement;
- sex-differentiated marriage order.

But they must still prove any additional claims such as:

- adult unmarried daughter remains in exactly child-level command obedience until wedding;
- father has an absolute veto over every marriage indefinitely;
- every woman requires a private male governor;
- paid employment outside paternal household is presumptively rebellion.

Historical atmosphere is not enough; exact claims require exact sources.

## P0 conclusion

> **The broad historic Christian tradition is far less egalitarian and individualistic than current Western culture, yet its best sources also understand authority as ordered under God, differentiated by relation, and changed by marriage and maturity.**

This should be the historical-theology baseline for future Product prose.
