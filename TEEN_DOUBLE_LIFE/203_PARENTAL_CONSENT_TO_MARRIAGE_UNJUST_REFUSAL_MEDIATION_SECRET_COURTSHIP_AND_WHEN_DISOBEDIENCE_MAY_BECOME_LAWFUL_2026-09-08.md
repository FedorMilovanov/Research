# Parental consent to marriage — unjust refusal, mediation, secret courtship, and when disobedience may become lawful

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** research-only / family-government / marriage-consent deep pass / not publication approval

---

## 0. Why this file is necessary

The project now has strong evidence for:

- serious parental authority;
- lifelong fifth-commandment honour;
- major historic Reformed expectations of parental consent/blessing in marriage;
- strong family involvement in courtship;
- opposition to atomistic private-couple autonomy.

That evidence becomes unsafe if we do **not** answer the opposite case:

> What if parents refuse marriage without sufficient reason, try to force another spouse, delay indefinitely, or use their authority to preserve control rather than seek the child's good under God?

The historic Reformed sources themselves put real limits on parental refusal.

---

# 1. Westminster Directory — unusually strong adult parental-consent rule, unusually clear anti-tyranny guard

The Westminster Directory for Public Worship says:

- minors' parental consent is to be known/recorded;
- the like is to be observed even for people **of age** with living parents in a first marriage;
- later marriages should also ordinarily be communicated to parents while endeavouring to obtain consent;
- parents must not force children to marry without their free consent;
- parents must not deny consent **without just cause**.

Sources:
- https://westminster1647.com/en/dapublica/10/of-the-solemnization-of-marriage
- https://www.apuritansmind.com/westminster-standards/directory-of-publick-worship/

### Critical synthesis

The same document holds together:

`STRONG ADULT PARENTAL CONSENT EXPECTATION`

and

`FREE CONSENT OF THE PARTIES + PARENTS NEED JUST CAUSE TO REFUSE`.

Therefore a Westminster-style model is not:

> "Father says no, therefore no further moral question exists."

The father's refusal itself is morally assessable.

---

# 2. Second Helvetic — consent of parents is explicit, but marriage remains lawful covenant, not paternal ownership

The Second Helvetic Confession says marriages should be lawfully contracted in fear of the Lord and made with consent of parents or those standing in their place.

Source:
https://ccel.org/creeds/helvetic.htm

This demonstrates that parental consent is not a marginal Puritan eccentricity.

### Guard

The confession does not make the parent the spouse, erase the parties' consent, or authorize forced marriage.

---

# 3. Baxter — probably the most detailed Protestant casuistry on unjust refusal

Richard Baxter is very strong on parental consent.

He ordinarily counsels children not to marry against a peremptory parental prohibition and treats parental will as morally weighty even after the child has formed affections.

Primary source:
https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6?rgn=div1%3Bview%3Dfulltext

Yet Baxter explicitly admits cases where it may be lawful to do what a parent/governor forbids because authority is given **for edification, not destruction**.

He gives the broader principle that parents cannot bind a child to sin, obstruct duties required by God, or use government for the child's destruction.

Source:
https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A8?rgn=div1&view=fulltext

He also distinguishes life stages:

- while young and living under parental government, the parental prohibition carries greater force;
- when age or prior marriage has removed the child from closer government, the adult is "more at his own disposal" and a lesser countervailing reason can justify acting otherwise.

### P0

Baxter is **not** a modern autonomy witness.

But neither is he a paternal-absolutist witness.

His own controlling principle is:

> **authority is for ordered good under God, not destruction.**

---

# 4. Baxter on forced/unfit marriage — authority can forfeit its claim in that specific point

Baxter advises very strongly against marrying without parental consent and even prefers parental judgment over youthful passion where possible.

But he says that if parents would force a child to marry someone:

- ungodly;
- religiously false;
- manifestly unfit;
- likely to make life sinful/miserable,

then the child may humbly refuse.

If a genuine necessity of marriage exists and parents consent only to an ungodly/unfit match, Baxter says the parents can forfeit their authority **in that point** because it was given for edification, not destruction.

Source:
https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6.13?rgn=div2%3Bview%3Dfulltext

### Important precision

`FORFEIT AUTHORITY IN THAT POINT`

is not:

`PARENTS CEASE TO BE PARENTS`.

A sinful use of one jurisdiction does not erase fifth-commandment duties in every other matter.

# 5. Traditional 1 Corinthians 7:36–38 interpretation contains its own anti-tyranny limit

Earlier modules document the historical father/daughter reading in Calvin, Poole and Gill.

If that reading is accepted, it gives substantial paternal authority in marriage decisions.

But the classic commentators themselves limit the authority.

Poole says the father has great power and parental consent ought to matter, yet he has no such power as wholly to hinder marriage where the daughter's condition/welfare makes marriage necessary.

Calvin says a father who attempts to keep a daughter from marriage where she does not have the gift of continence may become a cruel tyrant rather than a faithful father.

Controls already recorded in `183`, `200`.

### Result

> **The traditional father/daughter reading itself cannot honestly be used as proof of an unlimited paternal veto.**

# 6. A father's reason must be morally tested, not merely emotionally disliked

The fact that a young adult dislikes the reason does not make it unjust.

Potentially weighty reasons can include:

- prospective spouse is unbelieving where the child professes Christ;
- notorious immorality;
- violence/coercion;
- severe dishonesty;
- refusal to work/responsibility;
- predatory age/power situation;
- clear evidence of manipulation;
- major unresolved sexual deceit;
- marriage being used as a rapid escape from discipline rather than a sober covenant;
- legal incapacity/serious safeguarding issue.

Potentially weak or sinful refusal reasons can include:

- ethnic/class prejudice without biblical ground;
- status/snobbery;
- "I simply do not want to lose control";
- refusal of every possible spouse indefinitely;
- forced preference for a person the child does not consent to;
- demand to marry an unbeliever/immoral person;
- retaliatory refusal because the child questioned a non-moral parental preference.

### P0

The church or counselor must investigate **reasons**, not merely count votes.

---

# 7. Secret courtship is usually a bad answer to bad or difficult parents

Even when parents are wrong, immediately creating a secret romantic system can worsen the case:

- facts are hidden;
- emotional dependency grows before adjudication;
- the suitor becomes an alternative authority;
- parents receive later disclosure as a fait accompli;
- legitimate concerns become harder to distinguish from control;
- the couple may interpret every objection as persecution.

### Better ordinary pathway

Where safety permits:

1. state intentions honestly;
2. hear specific parental objections;
3. require reasons rather than slogans;
4. involve mature church mediation;
5. test character of both the couple and parents;
6. allow time for facts to become clearer;
7. distinguish temporary delay from indefinite veto;
8. distinguish parent's sin from child's impatience.

This is prudential pastoral sequencing, not a sacrament.

---

# 8. Third-party mediation should not mean replacing the parents

Earlier `179/181` controls remain governing.

A pastor, mentor, relative or elder should not begin with:

> "Your parents are old-fashioned; you are adults; do whatever you want."

Nor with:

> "They are your parents; whatever they say ends the case."

Instead ask:

- Are the parties competent adults?
- Are they living under the parents' active household government?
- What is the actual objection?
- Is it biblical, prudential, prejudicial or controlling?
- Is the proposed spouse godly/safe/responsible?
- Has there been deception already?
- Is the child using marriage to evade deserved correction?
- Are parents using the threat of withholding blessing as spiritual blackmail?
- What does the church know independently?

### P0

> **Mediation exists to expose truth, not to build a coalition.**

---

# 9. When acting without parental consent may become morally defensible

Do not publish a universal checklist as if Scripture gives one.

But historic Reformed casuistry allows the category where all/most of the following become clear:

- competent adult status;
- prolonged serious consideration;
- proposed marriage lawful "in the Lord";
- no substantial moral/safety objection survives examination;
- parental refusal is demonstrably unjust/controlling or indefinitely obstructive;
- repeated respectful attempts at reconciliation/mediation have failed;
- the adult is no longer in the close minor-like stage of parental government;
- decision is not driven by sexual panic, retaliation or secret rebellion;
- trusted church counsel confirms the refusal lacks just cause.

### Guard

This does not make parental opposition irrelevant.

Even where the final act may be lawful, bypass can carry grief, relational cost and possible prior sins in the process that still require repentance.

# 10. A marriage already contracted changes the jurisdictional question

Baxter makes a particularly important distinction.

He can condemn marrying without parental consent as sinful in some circumstances, yet once a real marriage exists, he does not treat parents as able to dissolve it. He appeals to the creation rule of leaving father/mother and cleaving to spouse.

Source:
https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6.3?c=eebo%3Bc%3Deebo2%3Bg%3Deebogroup%3Brgn%3Ddiv2%3Bview%3Dfulltext%3Bxc%3D1%3Bq1%3Dcirca+sacra

### P0

> **A sinful way of entering marriage does not ordinarily make parents the continuing governors of the new marriage.**

Prior sins may need confession/restoration.

The marriage covenant now creates real duties that parents may not simply nullify.

# 11. Particular Baptist confessional guard

`199–201` remain controlling.

The 1689 Confession itself explicitly requires competent marital consent and marriage in the Lord.

It does **not** contain the detailed Westminster Directory adult-first-marriage parental-consent procedure.

Therefore Product may say:

> historic Reformed/Puritan practice often required very strong parental consent, including for adults;

but must not say:

> 1689 chapter 25 explicitly gives fathers an adult-child marriage veto.

That has not been established.

---

# 12. Daughter-specific guard

A daughter must not be treated as morally voiceless.

Genesis 24 preserves extraordinary family mediation and Rebekah's affirmative answer.

Numbers 36 presents bounded real choice.

The 1689 text requires persons able with judgment to give consent.

Therefore:

`STRONG FATHERLY INVOLVEMENT`

can coexist with

`REAL DAUGHTER CONSENT`.

These are not contradictory categories.
