# 1. Calvin — child-inclusive covenant continuity plus strong household government

## 1.1. Core paedobaptist structure

Calvin’s *Institutes* IV.16 makes the covenant-continuity argument explicit:

- circumcision and baptism correspond as initiatory covenant signs;
- the promise to Abraham and his seed is not abolished in Christ;
- Acts 2:39 is read as continuation of promise to believers and children;
- children born to Christians are treated as covenant heirs prior to their own adult confession;
- the sign should therefore not be withheld merely because an infant cannot articulate faith.

This is not a detached proof-text approach. Calvin’s paedobaptism is downstream from a whole covenant theology.

## 1.2. Acts 2:39

Calvin treats `to you and to your children` as retaining the Abrahamic child-inclusive structure at Pentecost. For him, the phrase is positive evidence that conversion to the New Testament economy did not eject believers’ children from covenant privilege.

### Baptist challenge Calvin must answer

The final calling clause and the repentance/baptism sequence must still be integrated. Calvin’s broader covenant framework supplies the bridge; the verse alone does not spell out infant administration.

## 1.3. 1 Corinthians 7:14

Calvin reads the holiness of children as relative/covenantal rather than saving regeneration. The child of a believer is not simply classed with the profane world, and this status supports his baptismal theology.

### Project significance

Calvin is particularly useful because his strongest position is not:

> “Christian parent -> child automatically regenerate.”

It is:

> “Christian parent -> child objectively included in covenant privilege/visible holiness and therefore entitled to the sign, while true saving reality remains dependent on God’s grace.”

## 1.4. Lydia / household government

On Acts 16:15 Calvin presses a broader pastoral principle: the believing householder should govern the household under Christ, remove profanity/superstition, instruct those under his care, and seek a home ordered as an image of the church.

### Baptist-transfer value

This principle does not require infant baptism. It strongly supports the project’s rejection of:

> “If a household member is unregenerate, religious household order is pointless.”

A 1689 Baptist can receive the household-government insight while rejecting Calvin’s sacramental inference.

## 1.5. Jeremiah 31 / Hebrews 8

Calvin takes the New Covenant as genuinely better and distinct from the Mosaic administration while preserving substantive continuity in the covenant of grace. Therefore he does not infer from `all shall know me` that the visible covenant administration must contain only credible adult/child professors.

This is one of the deepest divergences from later Baptist covenant theology.

# 2. John Gill — strong Particular Baptist counter-system

Gill is particularly valuable because he combines high Calvinism, strong parental/household duty, believer’s baptism and regenerate-church ecclesiology. He therefore directly refutes the assumption that strict credobaptism requires permissive treatment of unregenerate children.

## 2.1. Baptismal subjects

In *Body of Practical Divinity*, Gill argues:

- Christ’s commission makes disciples/teaches before baptism;
- candidates should show repentance, faith, discipleship, reception of the Spirit;
- natural birth from believing parents does not confer church membership;
- no positive New Testament command or clear precedent authorizes infant baptism.

## 2.2. Genesis 17 / covenant argument

Gill does not merely say `circumcision is Old Testament, baptism is New Testament`. His argument is more structural:

1. Abrahamic/circumcision arrangements include national/typological features not identical to the pure New Covenant;
2. circumcision was applied to persons not thereby proven regenerate;
3. even established covenant interest does not automatically give a person a right to every ordinance;
4. positive divine appointment determines the subjects of a positive ordinance;
5. baptism’s positive New Testament pattern names disciples/believers/professors.

### Strongest Gill point for this project

Even if a Baptist conceded a special providential/covenantal privilege to Christian children, a separate argument would still be required to establish their baptism.

## 2.3. Acts 2:39

Gill reads the final clause concerning those whom God calls as controlling parents, children and those afar off. Hence biological relationship does not itself secure the saving promise or baptismal right.

## 2.4. 1 Corinthians 7:14

Gill’s classic solution identifies the marriage as lawfully sanctified/espoused and the children as legitimate rather than covenant members.

### Research correction

This project should not adopt Gill’s legitimacy reading as a locked Baptist answer merely because Gill is confessionally close. Contemporary Baptist Thomas Schreiner offers a more textually cautious alternative: children and spouse truly occupy a set-apart sphere through the believer, but this holiness does not itself entail salvation or baptism.

Thus:

`GILL = IMPORTANT HISTORIC POSITION / NOT AUTOMATIC EXEGETICAL OWNER`.

## 2.5. Visible church

Gill explicitly denies infant membership by natural birth or parental faith. He makes the Baptist logic stark: profession -> baptism -> church admission. He also criticizes paedobaptists who call infants members but then fail to apply ordinary member oversight/censure consistently when those children mature.

### Important comparative irony

Modern PCA/OPC/URCNA polity partly answers Gill’s historical criticism by explicitly naming baptized children as members under oversight/discipline, with URCNA even providing an exclusion procedure for mature baptized non-professors.

This is an important historical-development point for the series: the strongest modern paedobaptist model should not be criticized using a caricature that its own church order has explicitly closed.

# 3. Spurgeon — child conversion without sacramental presumption

## 3.1. Mark 10

In *Children Brought to Christ, Not to the Font* (1864), Spurgeon argues that the children brought to Jesus in Mark 10 were brought for Christ’s touch/blessing, not for baptism. Therefore the text should summon parents to bring children to Christ rather than function as an infant-baptism institution narrative.

## 3.2. Immediate child evangelism

Spurgeon is equally important against the opposite Baptist error. He does not say:

> “They are unregenerate children, so wait until adulthood.”

His child-conversion preaching presses for genuine repentance/faith now and rejects delay in seeking children’s conversion.

This creates a valuable Baptist synthesis:

`NO INFANT BAPTISM` + `NO DELAYED GOSPEL RESPONSIBILITY`.

## 3.3. Anti-presumption guard

Spurgeon’s polemic against baptismal regeneration should be used narrowly. His famous Anglican controversy targets liturgical claims he understood to connect baptism with regeneration. It must **not** be used as though it refutes Westminster’s actual claim that every baptized child is certainly regenerate; WCF 28.5 expressly denies that universal inference.

### Editorial rule

Do not make Spurgeon fight a Presbyterian position he was not accurately describing in that particular polemic.

# 4. Historical synthesis relevant to the user’s concern

Calvin and Gill disagree radically over infant covenant membership, yet they converge much more than weak modern practice suggests on household government and present child duty.

### Calvin

`covenant child -> visible privilege/sign -> household + church formation -> personal faith required`

### Gill / strong Baptist

`natural child -> no inherited church membership/sign -> household + Word + moral duty now -> personal faith/profession -> baptism/church admission`

### Spurgeon

`no sacramental shortcut -> seek genuine child conversion now, not at an imagined adult threshold`.

## Key finding

The practical binary

> “Either baptize the child as covenant member, or treat him as merely an unbeliever until he chooses faith later”

is historically false.

Strong Particular Baptist practice has a third category:

> **unbaptized, possibly unregenerate child who nevertheless lives under intense household religious order, divine moral obligation, repeated gospel summons, catechesis, pastoral attention, and present accountability.**

The unresolved question is whether that pastoral status should also be formal visible-church membership. That is the ecclesiological dispute.

# 5. Transfer map

### Safe to transfer from Calvin into a 1689 project

- Christian household is not morally/religiously neutral;
- head/parents should order household under Christ;
- children and dependents should receive instruction and live under God-conscious order;
- children should be brought to the means of grace and gospel, not deferred to adulthood.

### Requires paedobaptist label / cannot silently transfer

- believers’ natural children are covenant heirs in the sense that entails infant baptism;
- baptism formally admits them before personal profession;
- church membership by birth/covenant right.

### Safe to transfer from Gill

- positive-ordinance subjects require scriptural warrant;
- natural birth does not prove regeneration;
- faith/profession and baptism/church admission must remain distinct from parental identity;
- children remain under real parental duty/obedience.

### Gill claims requiring independent re-test

- 1 Cor 7:14 `holy = legitimate` as the decisive meaning;
- some historical/patristic dating assertions in older polemics;
- any conclusion resting mainly on absence arguments rather than positive text.

### Safe to transfer from Spurgeon

- seek child conversion now;
- do not mistake ceremony/religious environment for new birth;
- do not turn Mark 10 into an infant-baptism narrative;
- do not defer real repentance/faith to adulthood.

# 6. Final historical control

> The best paedobaptist sources make children objectively accountable members of the visible covenant community without claiming that baptism guarantees regeneration. The best Baptist sources deny inherited membership without granting a moral holiday before conversion. Any public article that presents only “covenant responsibility” versus “Baptist waiting” would misrepresent both traditions.
