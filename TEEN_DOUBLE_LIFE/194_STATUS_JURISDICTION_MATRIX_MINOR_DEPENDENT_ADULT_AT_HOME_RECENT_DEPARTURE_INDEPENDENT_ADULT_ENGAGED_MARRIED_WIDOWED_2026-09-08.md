# Status / jurisdiction matrix — minor, dependent adult, recently departed child, independent adult, engaged, married, widowed

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Mode:** research-only / pastoral-application control / not legal advice  
**Purpose:** prevent the entire adult-child lane from collapsing into one undifferentiated category called “adult.”

---

## 0. Controlling problem

A civil birthday does not magically erase every prior relation. But neither does the word “child” prove that the same parental power applies at age 28 as at age 12.

The series therefore needs a **status-sensitive** rather than slogan-sensitive model.

Questions to ask before giving counsel:

1. Is the person legally a minor or adult in the current jurisdiction?
2. Is he/she living in the parents' household?
3. Is he/she materially dependent on parents?
4. Has he/she only recently left after conflict or lived independently for years?
5. Is the person unmarried, engaged, married, widowed/divorced?
6. Is the issue a direct sin question, household-rule question, prudential choice, or private adult vocation?
7. Is the family relationship intact, strained, or formally severed?
8. Is there danger, abuse, coercion, predation, addiction, severe mental-health crisis, crime, or safeguarding concern?
9. Is a church discipline/membership relationship present?
10. Are third parties reinforcing truth or forming a coalition around rebellion?

# 1. Biblical baseline

## 1.1. Filial duty is not a childhood-only sentiment

Gill's treatment of Ephesians 6 explicitly says children of both sexes and every age remain obligated to filial observance, gratitude and reverence even though **parental power is less when children are grown**.

Source:
https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html

This is a key historical-theological distinction:

> `GROWN => LESS PARENTAL POWER`
>
> does not imply
>
> `GROWN => NO FILIAL DUTY`.

## 1.2. Obedience is “in the Lord”

Ephesians 6:1 and Colossians 3:20 place child obedience under the Lord's authority. Gill and Poole explicitly limit obedience to lawful/godly matters; sinful demands are not binding.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/ephesians/6.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/poole/ephesians/6.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/colossians/3-20.htm

## 1.3. Natural family duties survive adulthood

1 Timothy 5:4, 8 requires grown children/family members to care for parents and household. Paul does not imagine adulthood as dissolving natural obligations.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_timothy/5-8.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/1_timothy/5.htm

## 1.4. Marriage creates a new primary household relation

Genesis 2:24 / Matthew 19 / Ephesians 5:31 establish leave-and-cleave / one-flesh priority. The project already controls this through `187`.

## 1.5. Widowhood demonstrates a real status shift

1 Corinthians 7:39 says a widow is free to marry whom she wishes, only in the Lord. Gill explicitly contrasts the widow's greater liberty with the traditional guardian reading of the virgin daughter.

Sources:
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/1_corinthians/7-39.htm
- https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/1_corinthians/7.htm

This does not settle every unmarried-daughter question, but proves Scripture/traditional exegesis can distinguish **family statuses rather than treating all women identically**.

# 2. Matrix A — minor child living at home

### Typical jurisdiction

Highest ordinary level of direct parental government in the matrix.

Parents normally govern:

- residence;
- schooling/education arrangements within law;
- technology/access;
- curfews and movement;
- ordinary household conduct;
- friendships/visits proportionately;
- church attendance/formation insofar as civil law permits;
- discipline and protection.

### Child's continuing responsibility

Unregenerate status does not create moral neutrality.

### Parent guards

Authority remains limited by God and civil law. No abuse, sexual coercion, unlawful confinement, forced criminality, forced marriage, or command to sin.

### Third-party rule

Strongest non-undermining presumption. A youth leader, friend, relative or romantic interest should not build a hidden alternative authority structure around the minor except where protection from genuine harm requires intervention.

### Product warning

Adult “leave him to consequences” logic cannot be transferred here as an eviction shortcut.

# 3. Matrix B — legally adult but still resident/dependent in parents' home

This is the most commonly flattened modern case.

### Changed status

Civil adulthood matters. The person has expanded legal competence and responsibility.

### Continuing household authority

Living in another person's household creates real obligations that are not reducible to childhood.

Parents/household heads may ordinarily set conditions concerning:

- no drugs/crime/violence;
- no sexual partners in the home;
- no pornography distribution or corruption of minors;
- no theft;
- truthful communication about matters affecting household safety/property;
- reasonable contribution/work where able;
- respect for other residents;
- technology/property use when parents own/provide it;
- protection of younger siblings.

### Critical distinction

`HOUSEHOLD CONDITION` is not automatically `PARENTAL OWNERSHIP OF EVERY ADULT DECISION`.

The adult's career, calling, finances, private conscience and future marriage require analysis by actual biblical relation, not mere residence alone.

### When co-residence may end

See `172`: chronic deceit, danger, corruption of siblings, use of household as infrastructure for defended gross sin, refusal of lawful conditions, or permanent-surveillance burden may make separate residence a legitimate boundary.

### Guard

Separate residence is not a sacrament of conversion or punishment technology.

# 4. Matrix C — legally adult, recently/secretly departed after family conflict

This case remains relationally different from a person who has lived independently for ten years.

### Why

- practical dependency may remain;
- possessions/finances/education may remain entangled;
- the moral conflict is unresolved;
- the departure may itself be part of deception/rebellion or may be escape from wrongful parental conduct;
- safety facts may still be unclear;
- parent-child relationship is active, not merely historical.

### Proper initial mode

Fact clarification + truthful contact + safety assessment + no automatic moral verdict based solely on `left home`.

### Third-party rule

This is a high-risk stage for triangulation.

Friends/pastors/suitors should avoid becoming a secret replacement family before establishing facts.

### If parents substantially right

Support repentance, truth, restitution/reconciliation, and where prudent return or orderly transition.

### If parents substantially wrong

Protect the adult from sinful coercion/abuse and do not use fifth-commandment language to force return to danger.

### Key formula

> **Recent physical departure does not instantly erase a still-live moral/family relationship.**

# 5. Matrix D — competent adult living independently for years

This status materially changes application.

### What remains strongly

- fifth-commandment honour;
- gratitude;
- care for needy/aged parents;
- openness to wise parental counsel;
- avoidance of contempt, needless humiliation, revenge and calculated estrangement;
- family obligations where real.

### What ordinarily weakens

Direct child-level control over:

- residence;
- daily schedule;
- employment;
- ordinary purchases;
- personal property;
- routine movement;
- lawful adult friendships.

### Marriage question

Historic Reformed evidence (`193`) can still place parental consent/blessing at high moral weight, especially first marriage, without reclassifying a long-independent adult as a minor.

### Guard

Parents cannot turn continued financial gifts into hidden ownership of every decision unless conditions were legitimately agreed and morally lawful.

---

# 6. Matrix E — unmarried adult daughter living with parents

This is the project's most disputed category.

### Directly supportable

- fifth-commandment honour continues;
- household residence carries real household obligations;
- Scripture/history give parents substantial involvement in marriage;
- classic Reformed tradition can expect parental consent even beyond minority;
- father/mother counsel should not be treated as merely interchangeable with random advice;
- economic capability does not erase filial relation.

### Not directly established as universal NT law

- every mature unmarried daughter has exactly the same obedience obligation as a minor;
- father has husband-like headship over her;
- father has a universal veto over every job, move, ministry, purchase or adult friendship;
- every significant conversation with another adult must be routed through father;
- every adult woman requires a generic private male “covering.”

### Stronger household model

A church/family may intentionally practice stronger father-led courtship or home residence as a prudential/confessional application, provided it does not call every inference an explicit divine statute or violate adult conscience/civil law.

# 7. Matrix F — engaged / serious courtship but not married

### P0

`ENGAGED ≠ MARRIED`.

There is no husband headship before marriage merely because emotional commitment is strong.

### Implication

The suitor must not become a secret quasi-husband who:

- displaces parents;
- demands loyalty against the family;
- creates financial dependency;
- orchestrates secret housing;
- isolates the woman from accountability;
- treats parental objections as automatically irrelevant.

### But

Parents also must not weaponize engagement indefinitely to control or humiliate two competent adults when no just objection remains.

### Historic evidence

`193` shows Reformed tradition's strong anti-clandestine and parental-consent instinct.

---

# 8. Matrix G — married daughter / married son

### Relational reordering

Genesis 2:24/Ephesians 5:31 means marriage forms a new one-flesh primary household.

### What remains

- honour to parents;
- gratitude;
- care;
- counsel;
- family affection;
- responsibilities toward aged parents.

### What changes decisively

Parents do not remain the primary governing household authority over the married child.

For a married daughter, her father is not her husband. For a married son, his parents do not outrank obligations to his wife.

### Conflict rule

Parents must not demand betrayal of spouse or sabotage the marital household.

Michal/Saul/David (`192`) supplies a narrative example of wicked paternal demand colliding with marital loyalty, though Michal's lies remain morally problematic.

---

# 9. Matrix H — widow

1 Corinthians 7:39 gives an unusually clear status statement: free to marry whom she wishes, **only in the Lord**.

### Implication

A widow may still owe parents honour where they live, but Scripture does not place her back into automatic child-level paternal jurisdiction after her husband's death.

This is an important guard against a simplistic model:

`father → husband → if husband dies, back to father's complete authority`.

The NT instead treats her as directly accountable to the Lord in her new status, with family/church obligations appropriate to that status.

---

# 10. Matrix I — divorced/separated adult

This requires separate biblical/legal analysis and must not be casually equated with widowhood.

Questions include:

- legitimacy of divorce/separation;
- ongoing marriage bond;
- safety;
- civil orders;
- children;
- church discipline;
- financial obligations.

### HOLD

Do not use this series to construct a complete divorce/remarriage doctrine unless a future owner explicitly opens that lane.

---

# 11. Matrix J — adult child in defended gross sin

Status does not remove moral accountability.

But response changes by jurisdiction.

### If resident/dependent

Household conditions and possible separate residence may be relevant.

### If independent

Parents normally cannot police daily conduct through child-level rules but may:

- admonish;
- refuse funding/enabling;
- alter voluntary fellowship;
- refuse to celebrate/approve sin;
- protect minors;
- communicate truth;
- pursue church processes where applicable.

### Key distinction

> **Loss of jurisdiction is not approval; continued moral witness is not possession.**

# 12. Relationship-status decision tree

Before saying “obey your parents” or “you're an adult,” ask:

### STEP 1 — What relation actually exists?

`minor child / adult resident / independent adult / fiancé(e) / spouse / widow / church member / employee / citizen`

### STEP 2 — What is the disputed matter?

`explicit sin / household use / safety / marriage / prudence / vocation / property / conscience`

### STEP 3 — Does Scripture directly assign authority here?

If yes, obey Scripture.

If not, distinguish strong wisdom/tradition from divine command.

### STEP 4 — Is the human authority acting lawfully and justly?

`authority under God`, never `authority instead of God`.

### STEP 5 — What consequences follow from the actual relation?

A father can refuse use of his house without thereby proving a right to command every off-site adult decision.

An adult child can lawfully live separately without thereby becoming morally free to dishonour parents.

---

# 13. Final synthesis

The project should use this formula repeatedly:

> **Biblical adulthood is a transition in jurisdiction and responsibility, not an emancipation from all inherited obligations.**

And its companion:

> **Biblical parenthood is a real authority relation whose form changes with maturity and marriage; it is not permanent ownership of another adult's conscience.**

This matrix should govern future pastoral examples before Product prose gives any categorical instruction.
