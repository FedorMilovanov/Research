# До смартфона и после: как изменилась не природа греха, а инфраструктура возможностей

**Статус:** RESEARCH / NOT PUBLICATION  
**Дата:** 2026-09-07

---

## 1. Центральная задача

Проверить первоначальную интуицию «интернет усилил всё в разы» без выдуманного коэффициента.

Мы не можем честно измерить общий moral multiplier вроде `×20`.

Но можем сравнить **структурные свойства среды**:

- access;
- privacy;
- portability;
- speed;
- persistence;
- scale;
- searchability;
- recommender delivery;
- peer continuity;
- production/sharing cost;
- adult gatekeeping;
- stopping points.

---

## 2. Что не изменилось

Не изменились базовые категории:

- lust;
- pride;
- desire for approval;
- curiosity;
- rebellion;
- deceit;
- envy;
- status competition;
- exploitation.

Therefore future article must never imply:

> «до интернета дети были естественно чистыми».

Scripture itself destroys that nostalgia.

---

## 3. Что radically изменилось: portability

Pornography/sexualized communication once generally required a physical medium/place/person.

Now a personal smartphone combines:

- camera;
- private display;
- browser;
- social network;
- encrypted/private messaging;
- payment;
- search;
- storage;
- generative AI.

### Analytical significance

The same device used for:

- school;
- Bible reading;
- family contact;
- maps;
- music

can also become the private portal to temptation.

This makes simple category controls harder than in older media ecosystems.

---

## 4. Access changed from episodic to continuous

Old pattern often involved discrete opportunities.

Smartphone pattern can be:

`wake → feed → school group chat → break → DM → home → gaming chat → bedroom → late-night feed`.

The key variable is not merely screen hours, but **continuity of access**.

Ofcom Children’s Media Lives project has followed children’s media habits since 2014; current reports emphasize deeply integrated, personalised feeds and daily-life embedding.

Source:
- https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-children/childrens-media-lives

---

## 5. Gatekeeping weakened

Offline adult sexual media historically involved various barriers:

- shop placement;
- physical ownership;
- TV scheduling/rating;
- family device;
- visible object.

Online pornography is now accessible through multiple routes, including mainstream platforms.

Children’s Commissioner 2023/2025 data:

- average first porn exposure ~13;
- substantial exposure by younger ages;
- accidental exposure common;
- mainstream social platforms are significant pathways.

Sources:
- https://www.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/resource/a-lot-of-it-is-actually-just-abuse-young-people-and-pornography/
- https://www.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/news-and-blogs/press-notice-children-see-violent-pornography-by-accident-via-social-media-and-as-young-as-six-new-research-from-the-childrens-commissioner-reveals/

Common Sense 2023 US teen survey likewise reports majority exposure.

Source:
- https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/teens-and-pornography

---

## 6. Search changed to recommendation

Traditional access model:

> user seeks content.

Modern recommender model:

> user seeks some content, but platform also selects what appears next.

Ofcom current child-safety work explicitly treats algorithms/recommender systems as major pathways through which harmful content may reach children.

Sources:
- https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-industry-bulletins/online-safety-industry-bulletin-march-2026
- https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/transparency-notice-research-on-ai-chatbot-social-media-and-video-sharing-platforms-to-explore-online-safety

### Guard

Do not claim every algorithm leads to pornography.

The structural claim is narrower:

> discovery is no longer purely intentional; recommendation is an additional exposure pathway.

---

## 7. Privacy changed

A physical magazine/video was discoverable as an object.

Digital behavior may be hidden through:

- private mode;
- disappearing messages;
- second accounts;
- deleted history;
- VPN;
- app vaults;
- multiple devices;
- cloud accounts.

Our earlier module `14_SECRET_ACCOUNTS...` documents measurable secret-account behavior without exaggerating prevalence.

### Structural consequence

The adolescent can maintain **high-frequency private behavior with low visible evidence**.

This changes the cost of hypocrisy/secrecy.

---

## 8. Peer world changed from local/periodic to persistent/networked

Offline peer group was bounded by:

- place;
- transport;
- curfew;
- school hours.

Digital peer group is less bounded.

Social media research framework describes transformation of peer relations through:

- increased frequency/immediacy;
- amplified demands;
- changed qualitative dynamics;
- new opportunities.

Source:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29627906/

### Relevance

A teen’s social status can now be negotiated while physically sitting at family dinner.

---

## 9. Audience scale changed

A sexual joke/rumor once might reach a classroom.

Now content can be:

- screenshot;
- forwarded;
- reposted;
- archived;
- distributed across school/community.

This increases both:

- temptation to perform for audience;
- harm to victims.

It also means one impulsive act can have irreversible distribution consequences.

---

## 10. Production cost collapsed

Camera phones lowered cost of creating sexualized images.

Generative AI lowered it further: synthetic explicit content can be created without a genuine nude image.

Common Sense 2026 representative teen survey documents widespread exposure to AI-generated explicit material.

Source:
- https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/teens-and-explicit-deepfakes-in-the-age-of-ai

This is a qualitatively new capability.

---

## 11. Stopping points weakened

Printed object ends.

TV show ends.

Store closes.

Friend goes home.

Infinite scroll/autoplay/streak systems reduce natural stopping points.

Ofcom 2026 persuasive-design research explicitly identifies features children/parents associated with spending more time online than intended.

Source:
- https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-literacy/exploring-the-relationship-between-persuasive-design-on-online-platforms-and-the-time-that-children-spend-on-them

### Theological/pastoral significance

Stopping points are not holiness.

But they create opportunities for reflection.

A system with fewer natural pauses can make repeated impulse-action loops easier.

---

## 12. Social reward became quantified and immediate

Traditional peer approval:

- laughter;
- conversation;
- reputation.

Modern approval adds:

- likes;
- views;
- shares;
- comments;
- follower counts;
- streaks.

Meta-analysis finds social media use and peer popularity linked during adolescence.

Source:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39543815/

Peer-influence meta-analysis 2026 shows strong immediate effect of peer norms in experimental literature.

Source:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42383466/

---

## 13. Yet the historical comparison must resist false declinism

Some traditional offline risk behaviors have improved.

US YRBS 2013→2023:

- ever sex fell;
- current sexual activity fell;
- multiple partners fell.

CDC teen birth rate also reached record lows.

Sources:
- https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/dstr/pdf/YRBS-2023-Data-Summary-Trend-Report.pdf
- https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db535.htm

### Therefore

Modern society is not simply:

> «every measurable behavior is worse».

Better concept:

> **the opportunity architecture has intensified and diversified, while actual behavioral outcomes differ by domain.**

This is more defensible and more interesting.

## 14. Proposed comparison matrix for publication

| Dimension | Pre-smartphone tendency | Smartphone-era tendency |
|---|---|---|
| sexual material access | episodic / physical / shared device | private / portable / multi-platform |
| peer contact | time/place bounded | persistent |
| discovery | mostly active search/peer handoff | search + feed + recommendation + messaging |
| content volume | limited by physical holdings/schedule | effectively vast |
| social audience | local | potentially networked/scalable |
| concealment | physical evidence often present | deletion/ephemerality/multi-account possible |
| production | higher friction | camera/AI low friction |
| stopping points | natural boundaries | infinite/autoplay/streak patterns |
| adult gatekeeping | relatively centralized | fragmented |
| status feedback | interpersonal | interpersonal + quantified metrics |

**Important:** table is structural synthesis, not a numeric causal model.

## 15. Can we say «internet multiplied temptation»?

Yes rhetorically, if immediately defined.

Safer wording:

> **Интернет не умножил человеческую греховность математически, но во многих сферах умножил доступ, приватность, повторяемость, скорость, масштаб и социальное подкрепление искушения.**

This is much stronger than invented `×20` because each multiplier is independently analyzable.

---

## 16. A crucial theological observation

Temptation opportunity and moral guilt are not identical variables.

A person can be more tempted without being less responsible.

Conversely, parents may reduce opportunities without changing heart.

This lets future article hold all levels:

- heart;
- habit;
- opportunity;
- culture;
- technology;
- providence;
- responsibility.

---

## 17. Final research line

> **Смартфон не изобрёл похоть. Он объединил в кармане подростка магазин образов, личную комнату, компанию сверстников, средство производства, канал распространения, счётчик одобрения и систему рекомендаций. Это не делает грех неизбежным — но делает прежнюю родительскую модель “он дома, значит он в безопасности” исторически устаревшей.**
