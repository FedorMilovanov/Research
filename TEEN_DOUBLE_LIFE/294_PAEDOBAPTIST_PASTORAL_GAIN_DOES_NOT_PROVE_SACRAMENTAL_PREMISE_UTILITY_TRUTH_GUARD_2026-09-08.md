# Paedobaptist Pastoral Gain Does Not Prove the Sacramental Premise
## Utility / Truth Guard

**Status:** RESEARCH / ARGUMENTATION P0 / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `273–293`

## 1. Problem

The user’s observation is pastorally perceptive:

> if a church calls children covenant members from infancy, it has a built-in reason to hold them under explicit church accountability.

But a useful consequence cannot by itself establish the truth of the baptismal/covenant premise.

## 2. Invalid argument

1. Paedobaptist polity often produces stronger formal child oversight.
2. Strong child oversight is desirable.
3. Therefore infant baptism is biblical.

Conclusion does not follow.

## 3. Mirror invalid argument

1. Baptist regenerate-membership polity reduces risk of inherited assurance.
2. Avoiding inherited assurance is desirable.
3. Therefore credobaptism is biblical.

Again, practical utility does not settle exegesis.

## 4. Proper argument structure

### Exegetical/doctrinal question

Who belongs to the visible New Covenant church and who should receive baptism?

Resolve through Scripture/covenant theology.

### Pastoral-structural question

Given one’s ecclesiology, how should children be instructed, governed, admonished, protected and related to the church?

Resolve through Scripture + ecclesial consistency + wisdom.

The second question can expose **inconsistency** in practice without proving the first doctrine false.

## 5. Legitimate critique of Baptist practice

A Baptist church may be criticized if:

- its confession affirms universal moral duty and parental formation;
- Scripture directly addresses children;
- the church nevertheless behaves as though nonmember children are morally/pastorally neutral.

This criticism is internal to Baptist theology; no infant-baptism premise is required.

## 6. Legitimate critique of paedobaptist practice

A Presbyterian/Reformed church may be criticized if:

- it calls children baptized members;
- its polity promises oversight/catechesis/discipline;
- in practice it treats baptismal status as passive nominal belonging without active summons to faith and repentance.

Again, this is an internal consistency critique.

## 7. Final rule

> **Use the other tradition to reveal our blind spots, not as a shortcut around the biblical argument.**
