# Adult Child Secret Departure — Contact, Release, Non-Enabling, Household Boundaries, and Return

# 1. Concrete scenario

The target case is not an abstract “adult child disagrees with parents.”

It may involve:

- a child raised by believing parents;
- adolescence marked by discovered secrecy/double life;
- pornography, sexual messages, profanity, lying, hidden social circles or other serious sin;
- repeated conversations, exposure, promises, relapses or deliberate concealment;
- reaching civil adulthood;
- then suddenly/secretly leaving the parental home;
- perhaps continuing a respectable version of the story toward parents while living very differently elsewhere.

The pastoral question:

> What should parents do now: pursue, contact, investigate, wait, release, stop funding, invite back, impose household conditions, or some combination?

There is no single biblical pill.

---

# 2. First governing distinction: adulthood changes jurisdiction, not morality

Modern civil adulthood changes the parent's legal/household authority.

An adult child ordinarily is no longer under the same day-to-day parental government as a minor.

But adulthood does not erase:

- God's moral law;
- the fifth commandment's honor principle;
- the parent's love;
- the parent's freedom to admonish;
- the family's real relation;
- the adult child's responsibility before God;
- the parents' right to set lawful terms for their own home/property/resources.

### Required formula

> **Eighteen is not spiritual emancipation from God, but it is a real transition away from minor-child parental jurisdiction.**

Do not collapse `honor` into permanent absolute obedience.

Do not collapse `adult independence` into moral neutrality.

# 3. Puritan/Reformed historical support for differentiated adult-child authority

## William Gouge

Gouge explicitly asks what happens when children are grown and no longer under parental government.

He distinguishes forms/degrees of subjection while maintaining lifelong honor.

Source:

- https://christianreader.app/books/of-domestical-duties/07-treatise-5-duties-of-children
- primary EEBO: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A68107.0001.001?view=toc

He also argues that parents should continue fatherly concern/admonition toward grown children, even those living separately, citing Eli and Job.

Source:

- https://christianreader.app/books/of-domestical-duties/08-treatise-6-the-duties-of-parents

### Historical guard

Gouge's seventeenth-century household polity is not identical to modern civil law. His “reins” language must not be used to claim perpetual coercive jurisdiction over independent adult children.

What is useful:

- the parent/child relation is not erased by physical separation;
- manner and degree of authority change with maturity/separation;
- concern, admonition, and honor remain meaningful.

## Richard Baxter

Baxter asks directly:

> how long are children under parents' command/government?

He answers in terms of **degrees and ends** of government. As children become established in a course of life where near oversight is no longer needed, that part of government ends.

Primary EEBO:

- https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6?rgn=div1%3Bview%3Dfulltext

Children's duties:

- https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26892.0001.001/1%3A6.13?rgn=div2%3Bview%3Dfulltext

Project Gutenberg:

- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43800/43800-h/43800-h.htm

Baxter elsewhere notes that at full age a child is more at his own disposal and that childhood-level absolute obedience cannot continue unchanged into mature family/economic responsibility.

### Project conclusion

Even strongly ordered Puritan family theology does **not** require us to say:

> “An independent adult child remains under exactly the same parental command as at fourteen.”

# 4. Second governing distinction: control vs pursuit of relationship

Once the child is a safe, legally competent adult living separately, parents may have to surrender control over:

- where he sleeps;
- whom he meets;
- his phone;
- his employment;
- his schedule;
- his account passwords;
- his personal property.

That does **not** automatically mean they must surrender:

- communication;
- questions;
- invitations;
- warnings;
- gospel appeals;
- relational presence;
- prayer;
- offers of non-enabling help;
- pastoral outreach where appropriate.

### Core formula

> **You may have no right to command his apartment, yet still have every right to say, “Son, I love you, and what you are doing is sin. I want to see you. I will not lie to you about it.”**

Ligonier explicitly urges parents of prodigals of every age to continue praying, proclaiming the gospel, and pleading for repentance rather than assuming sovereignty makes means irrelevant:

- https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/hope-for-prodigal-children

# 5. Sudden secret departure: an initial clarification phase is not inherently sinful

When an adult child disappears unexpectedly after prior deception, immediate parental questions can be morally legitimate.

A proportionate first-phase checklist:

1. Is he/she physically safe?
2. Was the departure voluntary?
3. Is coercion, exploitation, trafficking, blackmail, severe intoxication, self-harm risk, or abuse involved?
4. Does the adult child have shelter/basic necessities?
5. Is this an impulsive flight or a planned relocation?
6. Is he/she willing to speak?
7. What is the truth about where and with whom he/she is living, insofar as the child voluntarily discloses?
8. Does the child want continued contact?
9. Is there a concrete request for help?
10. Would that help do good or sustain the sinful arrangement?

### Guard

This is **not** a warrant for coercive surveillance of a competent adult.

But neither is it godly wisdom to tell shocked parents:

> “Do not ask even whether your suddenly missing son is alive or safe. If you contact him, you are trying to replace God.”

No biblical text requires such passivity.

# 6. The contact matrix

## Case A — Child answers, is calm, and is willing to talk

Parents may ordinarily:

- maintain reasonable contact;
- listen for facts;
- state love;
- name sin when relevant;
- ask about safety/work/housing;
- invite a meeting;
- ask what led to the secret departure;
- acknowledge any real parental wrongs;
- distinguish apology for parental sins from approval of the child's sins;
- offer a route toward reconciliation.

Do **not** manufacture a no-contact rule merely to make the child “feel the far country.”

God can use relationship as a means too.

BCC warns parents not to give up efforts to maintain relationship simply because months/years pass:

- https://www.biblicalcounselingcoalition.org/2024/10/04/three-reminders-for-parents-of-wayward-children/

## Case B — Child answers but lies/manipulates continuously

Contact may need structure:

- fewer reactive exchanges;
- written communication for clarity;
- no money during manipulation;
- refuse endless circular argument;
- one clear statement of truth and conditions;
- avoid becoming the child's crisis-management service while he refuses honesty.

Possible line:

> “I am willing to talk. I am not willing to pretend facts are different from what they are.”

## Case C — Child is hostile but does not forbid all contact

Possible posture:

- shorter, less frequent messages;
- avoid daily moral lectures;
- keep relational door open;
- communicate major truths plainly rather than repeatedly;
- do not trade truth for access;
- do not make every contact an interrogation.

## Case D — Child explicitly asks for no contact and appears safe/competent

Parents should strongly consider respecting the boundary rather than:

- fake accounts;
- repeated calls from new numbers;
- physical following;
- pressure through every friend;
- covert digital tracking without lawful basis.

A stable, non-harassing route may remain:

- one email/letter;
- a message through a trusted pastor if appropriate;
- a clear open-door statement.

Then prayer and patience may be the main available work.

## Case E — Child is missing / acute danger is plausible

This is not primarily an “adult prodigal” counseling question.

If there are credible signs of:

- coercion;
- trafficking;
- exploitation;
- suicide/self-harm;
- violence;
- severe incapacity;
- criminal victimization,

parents should use appropriate safeguarding/emergency/legal channels.

Do not spiritualize an emergency into:

> “The father of Luke 15 waited, so we should not call anyone.”

# 7. Social media changes the mechanics, not the moral categories

Modern parents may see:

- stories;
- geotags;
- online status;
- public photos;
- friends' posts;
- relationship announcements.

This creates a new temptation for the **parent**:

> confusing digital observation with faithful parental love.

Possible legitimate uses:

- ordinary public awareness;
- safety information;
- seeing what the adult child voluntarily publishes.

Possible distortions:

- compulsive checking all day;
- alternate accounts to bypass blocks;
- interpreting every story as full truth;
- using screenshots as ammunition;
- turning prayer into surveillance-fueled anxiety.

### Parent-heart question

> Am I looking because love has a concrete duty here, or because I cannot bear not knowing what only God knows?

This is where the warning “do not try to be God” may sometimes be valid—but it must target omniscience/control fantasies, not all communication.

# 8. “Redemptive release”: useful category, dangerous slogan if absolutized

IBCD/Dave Harvey uses “rugged love” / “redemptive release” to describe allowing a wayward person to bear consequences rather than endlessly supporting the arrangements that maintain sin.

Source:

- https://ibcd.org/rugged-love-for-the-wayward-soul-transcript/

Useful insights:

- genuine love can abhor evil (Rom. 12:9);
- doing good is not identical with appeasing;
- financial support can sometimes enable;
- consequences should not be vindictive punishment inflicted by bitter relatives;
- the goal is not to make a person hurt but to stop shielding him from reality.

### Major project guard

That presentation includes examples that must **not** be universalized, especially any anecdote involving a minor being asked to leave.

Our project already locks:

- minors must not be abandoned or made homeless as discipline;
- adult-child cases require separate legal/safety analysis.

### Better formula

> **Redemptive release is not “withdraw love until repentance.” It is relinquishing illegitimate control and withdrawing forms of support that materially sustain rebellion, while continuing whatever truthful good remains wise and lawful.**

---

# 9. Doing good without enabling: concrete distinction

Parents need more than the word “boundaries.”

Ask of every requested resource:

1. What good does this provide?
2. What sinful arrangement does it materially sustain?
3. Is there a safer form of the same help?
4. Is the child demanding cash because direct aid would expose the real purpose?
5. Am I rescuing from a one-time danger or resetting the same cycle for the tenth time?
6. Are younger siblings or others harmed by this aid?
7. Are there lawful obligations I must still meet?

### Examples

Potentially non-enabling:

- meal together;
- groceries rather than unrestricted cash;
- transport to work/treatment/church;
- emergency medical care;
- helping retrieve documents;
- paying a provider directly where wise;
- temporary safe accommodation under truthful rules;
- counseling/pastoral assistance.

Potentially enabling depending on facts:

- repeated cash after known deception;
- paying rent for a cohabitation/vice arrangement indefinitely;
- replacing devices repeatedly used for illegal/abusive sexual material with no change;
- covering debts created by gambling/drugs/sexual exploitation while pattern continues;
- lying to employers/church/family to preserve child's respectable facade.

### Key principle

> **Love need not finance the lie it is begging the person to leave.**

---

# 10. What if the child left precisely to escape parental moral rules?

Adult independence may be used for righteous or sinful purposes.

The parent should not argue:

> “Leaving our home is itself always rebellion.”

An adult may rightly establish an independent household.

But neither should the child argue:

> “Because I pay my rent, fornication, pornography, lying, drunkenness, profanity, and dishonor are now morally neutral.”

The legal ability to choose is not the same as God's moral approval of the choice.

### Strong formulation

> **Adulthood enlarges the sphere in which a person bears his own decisions; it does not shrink the sphere of God's lordship.**

---

# 11. What if he leaves to continue a sexual relationship/cohabitation?

Parents must distinguish relationship from participation.

They may:

- love;
- meet;
- eat together in ordinary family context where conscience permits;
- plead;
- pray;
- refuse to call fornication marriage;
- refuse to fund or ceremonially bless what Scripture forbids.

They should not manipulate the child with:

> “If you do not obey us, we do not love you.”

Nor communicate:

> “Because we love you, your sexual arrangement no longer matters.”

Ligonier's discussion of compassion without moral affirmation is useful:

- https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/true-compassion-lgbtq-weddings

Different concrete cases need separate conscience/legal analysis.

---

# 12. What if the adult child professes Christ but lives in settled scandal?

Now church discipline may become relevant if the person is a member/professing insider.

Do not make parents privately imitate 1 Cor. 5 as though they were the church.

Formal discipline belongs to the church.

Very important 2026 9Marks resource:

- https://www.9marks.org/article/relating-to-family-members-under-church-discipline/

Its historical/Reformed conclusion:

> church discipline does not dissolve God-given family relations and duties.

It cites Owen, Gill, Edwards and older Baptist material to this effect.

### Project implication

Even if an adult child is excommunicated:

- he does not cease to be son/daughter;
- parents do not automatically owe total social shunning;
- household/family duties remain, though fellowship/affirmation must reflect the church's judgment.

This is a crucial corrective to simplistic “cut him off completely” counsel.

---

# 13. Corrupting influence on younger siblings can change co-residence decisions

An adult child may not merely be privately immoral.

He may actively:

- show pornography to siblings;
- teach them to lie;
- bring drugs/alcohol into home;
- sexualize conversations;
- recruit siblings into secret chats;
- mock parents' authority;
- threaten or harass;
- bring unsafe people into home;
- sabotage rules.

In that case the parents' duty is not only toward the prodigal adult child.

They have duties toward:

- minor children;
- spouse;
- vulnerable household members;
- guests.

9Marks explicitly discusses limiting an excommunicated adult child's presence where he endangers or influences younger siblings:

- https://www.9marks.org/article/relating-to-family-members-under-church-discipline/

### Strong conclusion

> **Parents are not required to sacrifice the moral and physical safety of the rest of the household in order to prove unconditional love to one adult child.**

---

# 14. Household residence is a privilege governed by a real moral covenant

For an adult child living at home, rules should be:

- explicit;
- known in advance;
- reasonably related to household order/holiness/safety;
- consistently enforced;
- not invented vindictively after each conflict;
- compatible with applicable civil law.

Possible core categories:

### Safety

- no violence/threats;
- no weapons misuse;
- no criminal exploitation;
- no harassment of siblings;
- no intoxicated dangerous conduct.

### Sexual holiness / protection

- no sexual partners staying overnight/using the home for fornication;
- no pornography displayed/shared with others;
- no sexual grooming or corrupting minors;
- no nonconsensual sexual material.

### Substance/legal

- no illegal drugs or illegal material;
- no using the home as criminal infrastructure.

### Truth/order

- no theft;
- no deliberate destructive deception around shared household matters;
- respect property/quiet/privacy;
- no abusive profanity directed at household members.

### Responsibility

For an able adult:

- employment/education/meaningful contribution plan;
- agreed contribution to chores/costs where appropriate;
- no indefinite subsidized idleness as an entitlement.

### Faith-specific nuance

Do **not** require false profession:

> “You must claim conversion to sleep here.”

But parents may structure a Christian home around:

- church rhythms;
- family prayer/Scripture;
- reverent conduct;
- refusal to host practices contrary to conscience.

Exactly what attendance is required of an adult household member needs prudential analysis; do not turn one family's rule into divine law.

# 16. When can asking an adult child to live elsewhere become legitimate?

This is a severe decision, not a slogan.

Possible morally weighty factors:

- repeated serious violation of explicit household rules;
- active corruption of minors;
- violence/threats;
- theft;
- illegal activity;
- sexual exploitation;
- bringing dangerous people into home;
- persistent use of parental resources to sustain gross immorality;
- total refusal of workable household order.

Before action where reasonably possible:

1. state the issue specifically;
2. distinguish moral issue from personality conflict;
3. state the required change;
4. state consequences clearly;
5. seek pastoral/wise counsel;
6. consider safety of all residents;
7. verify applicable tenancy/eviction law;
8. avoid impulsive midnight expulsion;
9. consider whether structured transition is possible.

### Guard

No universal statement like:

> “Any adult fornicator must be thrown out immediately.”

Nor:

> “A Christian parent may never require an adult child to leave.”

Both are overbroad.

# 17. Conditions for return after the child has left

There are at least three distinct returns:

## A. Relational return

> “Can we talk / eat / see each other?”

Threshold should usually be low unless safety/abuse requires distance.

Parents should not demand total sanctification before conversation.

## B. Household return

> “Can I move back home?”

This can require concrete conditions because the entire household is affected.

## C. Trust/privilege restoration

> “Can I have the same money/car/device/privacy/access as before?”

Trust may rebuild gradually.

### Critical distinction

**Receiving the person is not identical with restoring every former arrangement.**

Luke 15 cannot be used to abolish prudence toward repeated deceit or safeguarding risk.

---

# 18. What should repentance look like before/after household return?

Do not require parents to see the heart infallibly.

Look for observable fruit consistent with prior modules:

- calls sin sin;
- stops demanding that parents rename evil;
- gives truthful account beyond merely caught facts;
- willingness to end the sinful arrangement;
- accepts reasonable consequences;
- removes occasions/access;
- seeks pastoral accountability/help;
- restitution where needed;
- no recruiting/corrupting others;
- sustained change beyond immediate housing crisis.

### Guard

A person can be weak and genuinely repentant.

Do not demand:

- theatrical tears;
- perfect emotional tone;
- months of sinlessness before any help;
- certainty of regeneration unavailable to humans.

Watson module 47 remains governing for true/counterfeit repentance.

---

# 19. If he wants to return only because life became hard

Luke 15 itself begins the turn amid hunger.

Therefore hardship-motivated contact should not be dismissed:

> “You only came because you need something; go away until motives are pure.”

Instead:

- receive the opportunity for truth;
- ask what he now sees;
- distinguish rescue from restoration;
- do not instantly fund a return to the same pattern;
- set clear house conditions if co-residence is considered;
- preach grace and repentance.

A mixed initial motive may become the doorway to deeper repentance.

---

# 20. If he returns but continues deception

Parents may discover:

- secret partner visits;
- hidden pornography;
- second phone/accounts;
- continued lying;
- money theft;
- secret corruption of siblings.

Then “but he came home” is not sufficient evidence of repentance.

Possible escalation:

`clarification` → `specific confrontation` → `restricted privileges/resources` → `pastoral counsel` → `household consequence` → `possible separate residence for competent adult`.

This is not punitive theater.

It is refusal to let the house become infrastructure for the double life.

---

# 21. If parents themselves have sinned seriously

The adult child's rebellion does not erase parental sin.

Potential parental failures:

- harshness;
- hypocrisy;
- humiliating exposure;
- arbitrary rules;
- favoritism;
- violence;
- sexual misconduct;
- neglect;
- spiritual manipulation;
- gossiping child's sin throughout church;
- making reputation more important than child.

Parents should repent specifically where true.

Do not attach a manipulative condition:

> “I will repent for shouting only if you first admit pornography.”

Each sinner answers for his own sin.

But parental repentance also does not require:

> “Therefore your fornication/deception was actually justified.”

Two wrongs can be true simultaneously.

---

# 22. “If I keep calling, will I prevent the far-country lesson?”

Sometimes perhaps—if every call becomes rescue, control, bargaining, and removal of consequence.

Not necessarily—if contact is:

- truthful;
- non-financing;
- non-coercive;
- relational;
- patient;
- willing to let “no” stand;
- open to repentance.

A weekly meal can coexist with refusal to pay for vice.

A message saying “I love you; I am praying; the door to conversation is open” can coexist with refusing to lie to relatives about what happened.

### Useful modern counseling insight

Rugged love source itself distinguishes doing good from enabling and gives examples of continuing invitations/meals while not permitting residence/support in a season:

- https://ibcd.org/rugged-love-for-the-wayward-soul-transcript/

So even “letting go” literature should not be caricatured as total abandonment.

---

# 23. “If I stop contacting, maybe he will finally feel it” — danger of manipulating consequences

Parents can turn non-contact into another control technique:

> “I will disappear so that he panics and comes back.”

That is still an attempt to produce a desired internal state by engineered relational pressure.

There may be legitimate reasons for reduced/no contact:

- explicit adult request;
- abuse/threats;
- repeated manipulation;
- necessary emotional/physical safety;
- discipline boundaries in a specific context.

But if the motive is simply:

> “I know exactly what deprivation will regenerate him,”

that again assumes God's role.

### Better posture

> **Use boundaries because they are truthful and righteous, not because you possess a secret formula for controlling the child's repentance.**

---

# 24. “Maybe he needs to taste the world” — careful wording

Never counsel a child/adult:

> “Go sin more so you learn.”

Rom. 6:1–2 forbids using sin as a route to grace.

But when an adult has already chosen the far country, parents may sometimes have to stop preventing every consequence.

The distinction:

- **sending him to sin** = wrong;
- **refusing to subsidize chosen sin** = potentially wise;
- **allowing lawful consequences to stand** = potentially wise;
- **hope that God may use bitter consequences to awaken** = biblically plausible;
- **guaranteeing consequences will convert** = false.

Calvin's Luke 15 exposition is useful here:

- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom32.ii.lxi.html

---

# 25. Augustine/Monica gives a striking non-formula case

Augustine—already an adult—deceived Monica and secretly sailed away.

She:

- tried to prevent/ accompany him;
- wept;
- prayed;
- continued intercession after he escaped;
- later followed him to Milan.

Primary:

- https://ccel.org/ccel/augustine/confess/confess.vi.viii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf101/npnf101.vi.VI.I.html

Augustine later saw God's providence in the very separation his mother prayed against.

### Pastoral value

This one story holds two truths at once:

1. a mother can continue pursuing/praying rather than emotionally declaring her son dead;
2. God may use a departure the parent desperately wished to prevent.

Again: historical illustration, not inspired protocol.

---

# 26. “No contact” should not become the default modern secular reflex

Current popular culture often treats relationship cutoff as a primary solution to painful family conflict.

Even in the reverse direction (adult children considering cutting off difficult parents), conservative biblical counseling has cautioned against reflexive no-contact as a cure-all:

- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/before-cut-off-parents/

This cannot simply be reversed into a rule for parents.

But it supports the wider project caution:

> **relational severance is morally weighty and should not be prescribed casually where lesser boundaries can address the problem.**

---

# 27. Church involvement after a secret departure

Possible legitimate church roles:

- pastor checks whether member/professing child is safe;
- pastor offers meeting;
- parents receive counsel rather than acting alone in panic;
- discipline process if adult professing member persists in qualifying scandal;
- safeguarding if victims/minors involved;
- church refuses to become a secret romantic/sexual access network.

A TGC example describes a concerned father contacting a pastor near his relocated adult son:

- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/moms-wayward-children/

This is not a command, and excessive third-party pressure can be counterproductive.

But it disproves the notion that all post-departure pastoral initiative is categorically unchristian.

# 28. Parent/pastor decision tree

## Step 1 — Jurisdiction

- minor?
- competent adult?
- adult dependent because of disability/incapacity?
- married adult/new household?

## Step 2 — Safety

- ordinary moral rebellion?
- coercion?
- violence?
- exploitation?
- self-harm?
- criminal risk?

## Step 3 — Contact status

- open;
- limited;
- hostile but responsive;
- blocked;
- unknown/missing.

## Step 4 — Sin pattern

- impulsive episode;
- struggling/confessing;
- settled defended sin;
- systematic double life;
- corrupting others;
- victimization mixed with own sin.

## Step 5 — Parent action under consideration

- message;
- visit;
- money;
- food;
- transport;
- pastor contact;
- housing;
- return home;
- exclusion from home;
- restriction around siblings.

## Step 6 — Four tests

1. Is it lawful?
2. Is it truthful?
3. Does it do genuine good?
4. Does it materially sustain the rebellion or endanger others?

## Step 7 — Parent-heart test

- faith or panic?
- love or possession?
- holiness or reputation?
- mercy or enabling?
- consequence or revenge?

# 29. Suggested household-return covenant categories (research draft)

Not a universal form. Not publication-ready legal language.

Possible structure:

### Relationship

- respectful communication;
- no threats/abuse;
- family disagreements may be spoken openly.

### Truth

- no lying about matters materially affecting household safety/resources;
- no theft/fraud.

### Sexual conduct

- no sexual activity with partners in home;
- no sexualized material shown/shared to minors;
- no exploitative/nonconsensual content.

### Devices

For a previously corrupting child, conditions may include limitations on shared-network/device use if he voluntarily chooses residence; avoid covert spyware as default.

### Substances

- no illegal drugs;
- no intoxicated dangerous behavior.

### Responsibility

- work/study/contribution plan where capable.

### Church/discipleship

- parents may invite/urge church and counseling;
- whether formal attendance is a house condition for a competent adult needs careful conscience/pastoral analysis;
- never require false testimony of conversion.

### Exit consequence

- serious/repeated violation may mean residence is no longer offered, subject to law and safety.

# 30. What parents must never promise

Do not promise:

> “Tell the truth and there will be no consequences.”

Do not promise:

> “If you return, everything instantly returns to normal.”

Do not promise:

> “If we stop giving money, you will repent.”

Do not promise:

> “If we keep enough contact, you will repent.”

Do not promise:

> “If we never contact, you will repent.”

Do promise what Scripture permits:

> “We will tell you the truth, we will not call evil good, we will repent where we have sinned, we will not stop loving you, and we will seek to act before God rather than out of panic.”

# 31. Research propositions to carry into publication

1. **Adult independence is real; autonomous morality is not.**
2. **Parental authority changes in degree and form; parental love and truth need not disappear.**
3. **Luke 15 does not command zero contact.**
4. **Neither Luke 15 nor Reformed theology gives parents omniscient control over adult children.**
5. **Stopping enabling can be an act of love.**
6. **Doing good may continue even when financial/housing support stops.**
7. **Family duties do not automatically disappear even under church discipline.**
8. **Other children in the home have claims on parental protection.**
9. **Relational welcome, co-residence, financial privileges, and rebuilt trust are separate questions.**
10. **Consequences can be providential means of awakening, but parents cannot manufacture regeneration.**
11. **A parent's persistent prayer/contact can coexist with relinquishing control.**
12. **No-contact can sometimes be necessary, but should not be a reflexive pseudo-spiritual formula.**
