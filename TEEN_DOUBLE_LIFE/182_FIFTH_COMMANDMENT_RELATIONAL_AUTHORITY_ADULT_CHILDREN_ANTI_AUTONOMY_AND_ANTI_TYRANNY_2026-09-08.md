# Fifth Commandment / Relational Authority / Adult Children
## Anti-autonomy and anti-tyranny control

**Status:** RESEARCH / SOURCE-CONTROL / NOT PUBLICATION
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Depends on:** `175–181`, especially daughter/father and third-party mediation lanes.

## Research question

How should a conservative Reformed/Baptist project describe parental authority after childhood without collapsing into either:

1. modern atomistic autonomy (`I am an adult; no one has any claim on me`), or
2. unbounded domestic sovereignty (`a parent remains entitled to command every adult decision indefinitely`)?

## Primary confessional control — Westminster Larger Catechism 124–132

Source:
- https://opc.org/lc.html
- https://www.wscal.edu/westminster-larger-catechism/

The Larger Catechism treats the fifth commandment as a general doctrine of ordered human relations.

Key structure:

- Q124: father/mother includes natural parents and other God-ordained superiors;
- Q126: duties are owed according to **our several relations** as inferiors, superiors, or equals;
- Q127: inferiors owe reverence and willing obedience to **lawful** commands and counsels, according to ranks and nature of places;
- Q129: superiors act according to **the power they receive from God and the relation wherein they stand**;
- Q130: superiors sin by commanding unlawful/impossible things, encouraging evil, discouraging good, correcting unduly, provoking to wrath, or governing unjustly/indiscreetly/rigorously/remissly;
- Q131–132: Scripture-informed social ethics also recognizes relations of equals and condemns usurping preeminence among equals.

### Major synthesis

> Biblical social order is not flat equality of authority, but neither is authority generic or limitless. Authority is **relation-specific, God-derived, morally bounded, and duty-laden on both sides**.

This is a stronger project category than slogans such as `patriarchy vs feminism`.

### Application to adult children

The fifth commandment persists. What changes is the **form of relation and therefore the scope of command**.

Possible states must be distinguished:

- dependent minor in the household;
- older adolescent still under household government;
- legally adult but materially dependent and resident;
- competent adult still resident by agreement;
- competent adult living independently;
- married adult who has formed a new household;
- adult child caring for elderly parents.

No single word `adult` resolves all prudential authority questions.

## Calvin — strong subjection, real divine ceiling

Primary / accessible sources:
- https://www.ccel.org/c/calvin/comment2/eph.htm
- https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom05.ii.i.ii.html
- https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom42/calcom42.v.iv.v.html

Calvin on Eph. 6:1 and the fifth commandment is stronger than much modern evangelical counsel.

He treats obedience as a serious element of parental honour and explicitly rejects the instinct to place oneself casually on an equality footing with parents.

On Col. 3:20 he says even difficult, disagreeable, and in one sense `unreasonable` parental requirements in indifferent matters may need deference.

But Calvin simultaneously sets a hard ceiling:

> parental authority exists under God's authority; if a parent commands unrighteousness, obedience must be denied.

### Project use

Calvin supports:

- strong anti-autonomy;
- filial deference beyond mere politeness;
- parental counsel having moral weight;
- not treating inconvenience as oppression;
- refusing actual sin even when a parent demands it.

Do **not** use Calvin as a proof that every adult child, regardless of household status and new relations, remains in exactly the same command structure as a minor.

## Gill — continuity plus reduced power when grown

Source:
- https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html
- accessible mirror: https://www.joelkell.com/resources/other/gill-body-of-practical-divinity/section-4-2/

Gill explicitly states:

- children of both sexes and every age owe filial duties;
- parental power is **less when children are grown**;
- observance, gratitude and filial reverence do not cease;
- obedience never extends to sin;
- in lawful/indifferent matters real deference remains important.

### Locked synthesis

> **Reduced parental power is not dissolved filial obligation.**

This directly blocks two opposite errors:

- `18 => morally self-created autonomous unit`;
- `grown => no jurisdictional change at all`.

## Sinclair Ferguson / Ligonier — modern Reformed articulation

Source:
- https://learn.ligonier.org/podcasts/things-unseen-with-sinclair-ferguson/honoring-our-parents-for-life

Ferguson distinguishes:

- childhood honour expressed substantially through obedience;
- later adulthood honour continuing for life but changing form, especially once a new household is established.

Use as a modern Reformed witness, not as superior to Calvin/Gill/confessional controls.

## Anti-feminist use without overclaim

The project may legitimately criticize **autonomous individualism** where it teaches:

- family authority is presumptively suspect;
- adult desire is self-validating;
- parental counsel has no special moral weight;
- family involvement in courtship is inherently intrusive;
- womanhood requires practical independence from father/husband in order to possess dignity.

But do not use `feminism` as a catch-all explanation for every change in adulthood, work, education, property ownership or civil competence.

Many such changes arise from modern law/economics and can coexist with biblical filial honour.

## Anti-tyranny use

The same fifth-commandment tradition condemns parents who:

- command sin;
- claim authority they do not actually possess in the present relation;
- force what the child cannot lawfully/morally perform;
- manipulate through guilt or Scripture;
- correct disproportionately;
- expose a child to danger;
- provoke continually;
- preserve authority by rigorous or unjust conduct rather than holy example.

### Strong line

> **A biblical father is not less authoritative because his authority has limits; the limits are part of its biblical character.**

## Third-party implication

Before a pastor/friend/suitor says either:

- `you are 18; your parents have no claim`, or
- `your father said it; discussion over`,

he should ask:

1. What relation presently exists?
2. Is the young person dependent/resident/recently departed/independent/married?
3. Is the parental demand lawful and morally wise?
4. Does it belong to the parent's actual stewardship?
5. Is the child refusing because of conscience before God or because of protected desire?
6. Is the parent correcting sin or attempting domination?
7. What other God-ordained authorities (church/civil/marital) are relevant?

## Publication guard

Prefer:

> `Scripture rejects autonomous individualism and also rejects autonomous authority.`

Avoid:

> `feminism made adult daughters disobedient` as an unqualified causal thesis.

The theological argument must be established from Scripture/confession first; cultural critique comes second.
