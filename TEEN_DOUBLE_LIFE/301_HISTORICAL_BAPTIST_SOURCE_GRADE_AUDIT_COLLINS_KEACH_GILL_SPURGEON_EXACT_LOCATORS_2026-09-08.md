# 1. Hercules Collins — *An Orthodox Catechism* (1680), Preface

## Claim under audit

Collins urges Christian parents to:

- love their children’s souls;
- pray for them;
- instruct them after Abraham’s example;
- win them toward good by good example;
- use catechetical form for youth learning Christian principles;
- care seriously for their everlasting welfare.

## Source witness

Modern typeset reprint following the 1680 text:

https://www.thecalvinist.net/etc/1680%20Orthodox%20Catechism%20(Hercules%20Collins).pdf

Additional text witness:

https://anorthodoxcatechism.org/

## Exact locator

Reprint PDF:

- PDF page `11`;
- printed catechism page `6`;
- Preface.

Key sequence begins with Collins telling readers, in substance, to love their children’s souls as their own, show it by prayer and instruction, use good example, and use the catechetical handling for youth.

The same paragraph states concern for children’s everlasting welfare and parental duty.

## Verification status

## Product permission

### Safe

# 2. Hercules Collins — *Believers-baptism from heaven, and of divine institution* (1691)

## Claim under audit

Collins grants a real advantage to children of godly parents through:

- good education;
- prayer;
- good examples;

while denying that these advantages establish infant-baptism entitlement.

## Primary digital witness

University of Michigan / Early English Books Online 2 transcription:

https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo2/B20542.0001.001/1:5.5?rgn=div2;view=fulltext

## Exact locator

Printed pages `47–48`.

Page 47 contains Collins’s explicit concession that it is a great mercy for children to have godly parents, followed by the three advantages above, immediately before his denial that infant baptism was appointed for them.

Page 48 proceeds into his 1 Corinthians 7:14 legitimacy reading.

## Verification status

`DIRECT / HIGH / EXACT PRINTED PAGE CLOSED`.

## Product permission

### Safe high-value paraphrase

> Even while arguing against infant baptism, Collins explicitly calls godly parentage a great mercy because of education, prayer and example.

This is one of the strongest historical controls in the entire comparative lane.

### Exact-theology guard

Collins’s following interpretation of `1 Cor 7:14` as lawful legitimacy is historically relevant but **not** the current project’s sole exegetical owner. Route exegesis to `279`.

### Locked use

`REAL CHRISTIAN-HOUSEHOLD ADVANTAGE ≠ INFANT-BAPTISM ENTITLEMENT` is historically demonstrable in Collins.

## Verification status

`DIRECT TEXT / HIGH FOR DUTY CLAIM / CLOSED`.

## Product permission

# 6. John Gill — *A Body of Practical Divinity*, “Of the Respective Duties of Parents and Children”

## Claims under audit

Gill directly grounds:

- child obedience/honor/reverence in natural relation and Scripture;
- duties across childhood and into adulthood in a calibrated form;
- parental religious education;
- example;
- guarding corrupt company;
- early correction of vice/lying/corrupt speech;
- prayer with/for children;
- bringing children under means of grace;
- Scripture instruction according to capacity;
- teaching sin/natural misery/salvation through Christ;
- warning against educating children into first-person profession before actual knowledge/faith.

## Primary text witness

Christian Classics Ethereal Library:

https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.v.ii.html

## Exact locator

Book/section heading:

`OF THE RESPECTIVE DUTIES OF PARENTS AND CHILDREN`.

The opening child-duty section explicitly interprets Eph. 6:1 and says parental power lessens as children grow while filial observance/gratitude/reverence does not simply cease.

The parent-duty section contains the religious-training/means/prayer/Scripture/vice-control material and the warning concerning premature `I believe` instruction.

## Verification status

`DIRECT / HIGH / SECTION LOCATOR CLOSED`.

## Product permission

### Safe paraphrase

> Gill combines very strong parental religious formation and child obedience with an equally strong warning against training children to rest in an outward religious form or to speak faith they do not actually possess.

### Project synthesis — not Gill quotation

> **Teach the child Christianity before conversion; do not teach the child to counterfeit conversion.**

This formulation is excellent for Product if labeled as editorial synthesis and supported by Gill + Scripture/1689 owners.

### Guard

Do not turn Gill’s nineteenth/eighteenth-century wording into a universal definition of all Particular Baptist practice; he is a powerful representative witness, not an exhaustive sociological census.

# 7. C. H. Spurgeon — “Do Not Sin Against the Child”

## Claim under audit

Spurgeon simultaneously teaches:

- conversion of children is genuinely possible and should be expected/sought;
- church should not admit a professing child without examination;
- first terror over sin should not automatically be renamed repentance;
- first joy at Christ’s love should not automatically be renamed faith;
- child requires the same real repentance and faith in kind as an adult;
- church must not demand more than is appropriate simply because the professor is young or treat credible child profession as presumptively fraudulent.

## Exact locator

## Verification status

`DIRECT / HIGH / SERMON LOCATOR CLOSED`.

## Product permission

### Safe paraphrase

> Spurgeon neither postpones child conversion nor lowers the reality required for it: a child’s profession should be examined, but childhood itself is not a reason to demand adult-style maturity or to presume imposture.
