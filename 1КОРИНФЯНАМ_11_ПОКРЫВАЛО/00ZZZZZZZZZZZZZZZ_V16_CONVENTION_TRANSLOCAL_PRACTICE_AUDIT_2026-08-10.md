# 1 Коринфянам 11:16 — `τοιαύτην συνήθειαν` convention / translocal-practice audit

**Дата:** 2026-08-10  
**Статус:** `V16-PRIMARY-TEXT / DISCOURSE-AUDIT / RESEARCH-ONLY / PUBLICATION-HOLD`

## 0. Text

```text
Εἰ δέ τις δοκεῖ φιλόνεικος εἶναι,
ἡμεῖς τοιαύτην συνήθειαν οὐκ ἔχομεν,
οὐδὲ αἱ ἐκκλησίαι τοῦ θεοῦ.
```

Minimum transparent rendering:

> If anyone thinks/is disposed to be contentious, **we do not have such a custom/practice**, nor do the churches of God.

Important:

```text
GREEK_DOES_NOT_CONTAIN_ἄλλην = true
```

Therefore English/Russian renderings such as “we have no **other** practice” may be contextually good paraphrases but are not a one-word literal gloss.

---

# 1. `φιλόνεικος`

The adjective means fond of strife/contentious/disputatious.

Classical lexica attest both competition/emulation senses and, in relevant negative contexts, contentiousness.

In v16:

```text
τις = anyone
δοκεῖ ... εἶναι = thinks/seems/disposes himself to be
φιλόνεικος = contentious
```

Thus:

```text
V16_ADDRESSES_POTENTIAL_CONTENTIOUS_RESISTANCE = A_TEXT
```

Paul is not presenting a neutral menu of alternatives. The conditional frames continued opposition as contentious.

---

# 2. `συνήθεια`

The noun has the ordinary semantic class:

```text
habit / habituation / customary use / practice / convention
```

## 2.1. Same-letter control: 1 Cor 8:7

```text
τῇ συνηθείᾳ ... τοῦ εἰδώλου
```

Here `συνήθεια` is habituation/being accustomed to the idol.

## 2.2. NT civic control: John 18:39

```text
ἔστιν δὲ συνήθεια ὑμῖν ἵνα ...
```

Pilate speaks of a customary public practice.

Thus:

```text
SYNĒTHEIA_CUSTOM/PRACTICE = A_LEXICAL
```

The word itself does not mean:

- divine command;
- mere opinion;
- argument/contention;
- a particular garment.

---

# 3. `τοιαύτην`

`τοιοῦτος` means **such / of this kind / such as this**.

It is an anaphoric/deictic descriptor: the reader must identify **which kind of practice** is salient from the discourse.

Therefore:

```text
TOIAUTEN_REQUIRES_CONTEXTUAL_ANTECEDENT = A
EXACT_ANTECEDENT = B_C
```

This is the actual ambiguity of v16.

---

# 4. Four major referent models

## Model A — “no custom of being contentious”

Paraphrase:

> If someone wants to quarrel, we have no custom of quarrelling, nor do the churches.

### Strengths

- nearest clause contains `φιλόνεικος`;
- rhetorically plausible: Paul refuses endless dispute;
- early/modern commentators have proposed it.

### Costs

1. `φιλόνεικος` describes a **person/disposition**, while `συνήθεια` names a collective practice;
2. “the churches of God do not have a custom of contention” is semantically possible but less natural as a decisive appeal about the head-covering dispute;
3. it weakens the force of the explicit appeal to church-wide practice;
4. the sentence then gives little information about what settles the actual question.

### Grade

```text
V16_CUSTOM_OF_CONTENTION = C_VIABLE
```

Not impossible, not leading.

---

## Model B — “no such contrary head-state practice”

Paraphrase:

> If someone insists on disputing this, neither we nor the churches of God have such a practice [as the practice contrary to the order just argued for].

This can be expressed idiomatically as:

> we have no other practice

provided “other” is marked as a contextual supplement, not overt Greek.

### Strengths

1. `συνήθεια` naturally denotes an established communal practice;
2. `αἱ ἐκκλησίαι τοῦ θεοῦ` makes a corporate-practice referent especially natural;
3. Paul has spent vv4–15 specifying head-state/propriety, so this is the salient practice domain;
4. the appeal functions as a normal Pauline translocal norm-closing move.

### Cost

`τοιαύτην` does not explicitly say “contrary” or “other.” The exact negative practice must be inferred.

### Grade

```text
V16_NO_CONTRARY/ALTERNATIVE_HEAD_STATE_PRACTICE = B_LEADING
```

---

## Model C — “no such custom as the covering practice Paul just described”

Paraphrase:

> We do not have this custom of head coverings, nor do the churches.

This reading is sometimes used to make v16 cancel vv2–15.

### Formal attraction

`τοιαύτην` can in isolation point backward to a previously described practice, and `οὐκ ἔχομεν` literally says “we do not have.”

### Major discourse costs

1. Paul has just built a sustained positive argument across vv3–15;
2. vv13–15 culminate in an appeal for the readers themselves to judge propriety;
3. calling dissent `φιλόνεικος` makes little sense if Paul immediately concedes that neither he nor the churches practice what he argued;
4. the churches-of-God clause becomes a negation of the very pattern invoked to close debate;
5. v2 praises retention of `παραδόσεις`, making a final total cancellation rhetorically abrupt without an adversative explanation.

### Grade

```text
V16_CANCELS_HEAD_COVERING_PRACTICE = D_C_LOW
```

The word `τοιαύτην` alone cannot overcome the full discourse.

---

## Model D — “no such contentious contrary custom exists among churches”

This hybrid takes `τοιαύτην συνήθειαν` as the **contested practice** being defended by the contentious person rather than either pure “contention” or the positive Pauline practice.

It often produces the most natural pragmatic paraphrase:

> If anyone wants to push the contrary case, that is not our practice, nor the practice of God’s churches.

### Grade

```text
V16_HYBRID_CONTENTIOUS_CONTRARY_PRACTICE = B_HIGH_LEADING_PRAGMATIC
```

This is compatible with Model B and may be the best way to explain why the contentiousness clause and church-practice clause are both present.

# 5. Paul’s translocal-church appeal pattern inside 1 Corinthians

v16 is not an isolated rhetorical move.

## 1 Cor 4:17

Timothy will remind the Corinthians of Paul’s ways in Christ:

```text
καθὼς πανταχοῦ ἐν πάσῃ ἐκκλησίᾳ διδάσκω
```

> as I teach everywhere in every church.

## 1 Cor 7:17

```text
καὶ οὕτως ἐν ταῖς ἐκκλησίαις πάσαις διατάσσομαι
```

> and so I direct/prescribe in all the churches.

## 1 Cor 14:33

```text
ὡς ἐν πάσαις ταῖς ἐκκλησίαις τῶν ἁγίων
```

> as in all the churches of the saints.

## 1 Cor 16:1

```text
ὥσπερ διέταξα ταῖς ἐκκλησίαις τῆς Γαλατίας,
οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς ποιήσατε
```

> as I directed the churches of Galatia, so you also do.

## Result

Paul repeatedly appeals to **cross-congregational uniformity/practice** when correcting Corinth.

Therefore:

```text
V16_TRANSLOCAL_CHURCH_APPEAL = A_TEXT
V16_AS_NORMAL_PAULINE_NORMING_STRATEGY = B_HIGH
```

This strongly resists reading v16 as “there is no church-wide practice relevant here.”

---

# 6. Relation to v2 `παραδόσεις`

v2 praises the Corinthians for remembering Paul and holding the `παραδόσεις` as delivered.

v16 appeals to Paul/we + churches of God.

The lexical words differ:

```text
παράδοσις = transmitted tradition/instruction
συνήθεια = customary/habitual practice
```

Therefore they must **not** be made lexical synonyms.

But at discourse level they can form an inclusio-like institutional frame:

```text
V2: handed-on traditions
...
V16: practice of Paul/us + churches of God
```

This supports the idea that the paragraph is about implementing an apostolic/ecclesial norm rather than debating a private fashion preference.

```text
V2_V16_INSTITUTIONAL_FRAME = B_DISCOURSE
PARADOSIS_EQUALS_SYNĒTHEIA_LEXICALLY = false
```

---

# 7. E. A. Judge — convention question

E. A. Judge’s peer-reviewed chapter “The Appeal to convention in Paul” (2004) explicitly isolates the same question.

His abstract notes that Greek `συνήθεια` (like Latin `consuetudo`) emphasizes the conscious practice of a collectivity and asks **which collectivity** is in view in v16:

- circles whose practice Paul criticizes; or
- the traditions/practice shared by Paul and “the churches of God.”

This is useful because it identifies the real interpretive issue as **collective referent**, not whether `συνήθεια` means “custom” at all.

```text
JUDGE_COLLECTIVE_CONVENTION_FRAME = B_HIGH_SPECIALIST
```

The full chapter remains useful if later acquired, but the author/university abstract already closes the question architecture without user burden.

# 8. Does “custom” mean optional?

No lexical inference allows:

```text
SYNĒTHEIA = CUSTOM
THEREFORE = OPTIONAL / THEOLOGICALLY IRRELEVANT
```

Why:

1. `συνήθεια` describes **type of social practice**, not its normative force;
2. John 18:39 shows a custom can structure official/public action;
3. 1 Cor 8:7 shows a custom can be morally problematic or nonbinding;
4. therefore the noun itself does not determine whether a particular custom is endorsed, criticized or required;
5. in 11:16 the normative force comes from the **whole argument + contentiousness frame + translocal church appeal**, not from a dictionary definition of `συνήθεια`.

Thus:

```text
CUSTOM_WORD_ALONE_DOES_NOT_MEAN_OPTIONAL = A_LOGICAL/LEXICAL
V16_NORMATIVE_FORCE = B_HIGH_DISCOURSE
```

---

# 9. Does v16 prove universal identical garment form?

No.

The translocal appeal can establish a shared **practice/principle/order** without specifying that every church used:

- the same fabric;
- the same size;
- the same Roman/Greek garment;
- the same hairstyle;
- the same social symbolism in every city.

The passage itself does not name the garment.

Therefore:

```text
V16_SHARED_CHURCH_PRACTICE = A/B_HIGH
V16_IDENTICAL_GARMENT_FORM_EVERYWHERE = C_LOW/UNPROVED
```

This distinction matters for contemporary application.

---

# 10. Does v16 establish a universal head-covering command for all time?

Not by itself.

v16 strengthens the **first-century translocal** scope of the practice/order.

But the move from:

```text
Paul + churches of God shared a practice
```

to:

```text
therefore every Christian culture must use the identical physical sign forever
```

is a hermeneutical argument requiring vv3–15 and broader theology.

Conversely, the move to:

```text
it was called custom, therefore it expired with Corinthian culture
```

is equally invalid.

Thus:

```text
V16_FIRST_CENTURY_TRANSLOCALITY = B_HIGH
V16_ALONE_SETTLES_CONTEMPORARY_IDENTICAL_APPLICATION = false
```

---

# 11. Relation to Nõmmik/local-trigger models

A strong local historical trigger is **not incompatible** with v16.

If Corinth developed a local innovation (for example Nõmmik’s reconstructed sex-neutral ritual uniformity), Paul can respond:

> whatever local reasoning produced this innovation, neither we nor the churches have that contrary practice.

Indeed, v16 may fit a local-innovation model well because the **problem** can be local while the **norm used to correct it** is translocal.

```text
LOCAL_TRIGGER + TRANSLOCAL_NORM = COHERENT
```

Therefore v16 does not refute Roman-context reconstruction; it limits models that make the final standard purely local.

---

# 12. Relation to hair-only

Hair-only models can also appeal to v16 if the disputed practice concerns sex-differentiated hair presentation.

Thus v16 by itself does not distinguish:

```text
TEXTILE_COVERING
vs
HAIR_ONLY
```

That dispute is decided elsewhere by cover/uncover vocabulary, hair vocabulary and historical context.

```text
V16_MATERIAL_VS_HAIR_DISCRIMINATORY_POWER = LOW
```

The verse is stronger on **church-wide normativity** than on physical-object identity.

# 13. Model ranking for `τοιαύτην συνήθειαν`

```text
B_HIGH / B_LEADING:
  no contrary/alternative contentious practice among Paul/us and churches

C_VIABLE:
  no custom of contentiousness itself

D_C_LOW:
  no custom of the head-covering order Paul just defended; v16 cancels vv2–15
```

Exact verbal antecedent remains B/C because `τοιαύτην` does not spell out the omitted practice.

# 15. Russian research-rendering guidance

For the future analytical Russian layer, avoid silently choosing between “such” and “other.”

Literal/research rendering:

> «Если же кто считает нужным быть спорливым, **мы такого обычая/практики не имеем**, как и церкви Божии».

Then expose a disputed referent node:

```text
«такого» =
A) такого спорливого/противоположного порядка [leading]
B) обычая спорить [viable]
C) самого описанного Павлом покрытия [low, discourse tension]
```

A normal literary translation may use «иной практики у нас нет», but research UI/data must mark `иной` as an interpretive contextual supplement.
