## 2. Newberry control

Julie Newberry’s NTS article is useful because it makes the allusion proposal explicit and also **does not erase the tension**.

Her abstract says Paul brings Genesis 1, Genesis 2 and 1 Esdras 4 into conversation; she reads vv7–9 as a patriarchal hierarchy, v10 as woman’s authority, and vv11–12 as counterbalancing toward interdependence and shared origin in God.

For this project:

```text
NEWBERRY_ANALYSIS = B_HIGH_SPECIALIST_INTERTEXT_CONTROL
NEWBERRY_1_ESDRAS_ECHO = B_C_PROPOSAL
NEWBERRY_THEOLOGICAL_LABELS != PRIMARY_TEXT_FACTS
```

The article is especially helpful as an adversarial control because it recognizes both asymmetry and counterbalance rather than flattening one side.

## 3. Imago safeguard

Genesis 1:27 is the mandatory control whenever an agent writes about v7.

Allowed:

> Paul locally predicates “image and glory of God” of man and “glory of man” of woman.

Not allowed without qualification:

> Paul teaches that woman is not made in God’s image.

Reason:

```text
OMISSION_OF_EIKON_IN_SECOND_CLAUSE != EXPLICIT_NEGATION
```

## 4. Origin safeguard

Paul has an explicit origin preposition:

```text
ἐκ
```

and repeats the Genesis phrase in v12a.

Therefore origin is undeniable in vv8/12.

But:

```text
ORIGIN_IN_V8/12 != LEXICAL_DEFINITION_OF_KEPHALE_V3
```

A source reading of `κεφαλή` remains contextually possible, not proven by the existence of an `ἐκ` argument later.

---

## 5. Mutuality safeguard

The reciprocal structure is overt:

```text
woman not without man
man not without woman
woman from man
man through woman
all from God
```

Any article draft that says only “woman comes from man” while omitting the next clauses is selectively incomplete.

Conversely, saying only “men come through women” while omitting v12a is equally selective.
