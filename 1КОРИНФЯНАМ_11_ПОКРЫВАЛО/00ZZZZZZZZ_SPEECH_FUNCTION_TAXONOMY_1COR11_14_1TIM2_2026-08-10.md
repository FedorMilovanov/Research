# Speech-function taxonomy — 1 Кор 11–14 и 1 Тим 2

**Дата:** 2026-08-10  
**Статус:** `SPEECH-FUNCTION-TAXONOMY / CANONICAL-DIFFERENTIATION / RESEARCH-ONLY / PUBLICATION-HOLD`  
**Цель:** не позволять будущим агентам сводить все виды речи в собрании к одному неразличимому классу «говорить/учить».

---

## 1. Почему taxonomy необходима

Ключевая ошибка в споре 1 Кор 11:5 ↔ 14:34–35 ↔ 1 Тим 2:12 возникает, когда следующие разные действия молча приравниваются:

```text
PRAYER
PROPHECY
TONGUES
INTERPRETATION
REVELATION
WEIGHING / DISCERNMENT
TEACHING
EXHORTATION
QUESTIONING / LEARNING
GOVERNING / OVERSIGHT
```

Новый Завет не использует эти слова как простые взаимозаменяемые синонимы. Иногда функции пересекаются по содержанию или эффекту, но **лексическое и институциональное тождество должно быть доказано, а не предположено**.

---

# 2. Prayer — `προσεύχομαι`

## Direct data

1 Кор 11:4–5 параллельно говорит о мужчине и женщине, которые **молятся или пророчествуют**.

1 Кор 14:13–17 отдельно обсуждает молитву духом/умом в связи с языками и общинным `ἀμήν`.

### Calibration

```text
PRAYER_IS_SPEECH_TO_GOD = A
PRAYER_IS_PROPHECY = FALSE_BY_DEFINITION
PRAYER_IS_DIDACTIC_TEACHING_OFFICE = FALSE_BY_DEFINITION
```

Prayer can edify/hearers can participate, but direction of address distinguishes it from prophecy in normal semantic terms.

---

# 3. Prophecy — `προφητεύω / προφητεία`

## Direct Pauline data

- 1 Cor 11:4–5: both man and woman can be described as prophesying.
- 1 Cor 12:28 distinguishes `prophets` from `teachers` in the ordered gift list.
- 1 Cor 14:1–5 contrasts prophecy with tongues and says prophecy speaks to people for edification, encouragement and consolation.
- 1 Cor 14:29: two or three prophets speak and `οἱ ἄλλοι διακρινέτωσαν` — others evaluate/discern.
- 1 Cor 14:31: `πάντες` may prophesy one by one so all may learn and be encouraged.

Jill Marshall's study of Paul and Plutarch treats prophecy as divine communication through human speakers and analyzes women prophets as a genuine category rather than a hypothetical impossibility.

Source:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/paul-plutarch-and-the-gender-dynamics-of-prophecy/010C5933799D0757AAD949AB8497E3AF

### A-level boundaries

```text
PROPHECY != TONGUES
PROPHECY != PRAYER
PROPHECY != TEACHING_BY_LEXICAL_IDENTITY
PROPHECY_IS_SUBJECT_TO_EVALUATION = A
FEMALE_PROPHECY_EXISTS_IN_11_5 = A
```

### Important caution

Prophecy can contain instruction/edification and lead people to learn (14:31), but functional overlap **does not make `προφητεύω` identical to `διδάσκω`**.

---

# 4. Tongues — `γλῶσσαι / λαλεῖν γλώσσῃ`

1 Cor 14 sharply distinguishes tongues from prophecy by intelligibility and need for interpretation.

### Direct constraints

- if no interpreter, tongue speaker must `σιγάτω` in church (14:28);
- this silence command is situational, not a permanent ban on the person's future speech.

### Relevance to 14:34

This gives a crucial semantic control:

```text
SIGAO = CONTEXT-SCOPED SILENCE IN 14, NOT AUTOMATIC ABSOLUTE MUTENESS
```

That does **not** by itself tell us the scope of women's silence, but prevents defining `σιγάω` in v34 as necessarily “never vocalize anything”.

---

# 5. Interpretation — `ἑρμηνεία / διερμηνεύω`

Interpretation makes tongues intelligible to the congregation.

### Boundary

```text
INTERPRETATION_OF_TONGUES != PROPHECY_BY_DEFINITION
INTERPRETATION != PASTORAL_TEACHING_BY_DEFINITION
```

The result may instruct/edify, but lexical identity should not be assumed.

---

# 6. Revelation — `ἀποκάλυψις`

1 Cor 14:26 lists a revelation alongside teaching, tongue, interpretation and psalm/hymn. 14:30 says if a revelation comes to another seated person, the first prophet should be silent.

### Significance

The list itself is evidence that Paul can distinguish multiple contributions within one assembly.

```text
REVELATION_CONTRIBUTION != TEACHING_LEXICALLY
REVELATION_CAN_TRIGGER_PROPHETIC_TURN_TAKING = A
```

---

# 7. Weighing / discernment — `διακρίνω`

1 Cor 14:29 explicitly separates:

1. prophets speaking;
2. `the others` evaluating/discerning.

### A

Speaking prophecy and evaluating prophecy are distinguishable functions in the sentence.

### Conservative harmonization relevance

Some complementarian readings place the 14:34–35 restriction specifically on authoritative evaluation/judgment of prophecy.

This is **possible contextually** because vv29–33 concern prophetic order, but:

```text
14_34_35_DOES_NOT_LEXICALLY_SAY_DIAKRINO = TRUE
PROPHECY_JUDGING_RESTRICTION = B_C_HARMONIZATION
```

Do not promote the model to A just because it harmonizes well.

---

# 8. Teaching — `διδάσκω / διδαχή / διδασκαλία`

## 1 Corinthians

- 1 Cor 12:28 lists `teachers` after apostles and prophets.
- 1 Cor 14:26 can say `each has ... a teaching (`διδαχή`) ...` among multiple assembly contributions.

This shows teaching is a recognizable category distinguishable at least conceptually from prophecy.

## Pastoral Epistles

1 Tim repeatedly emphasizes teaching/doctrine and qualified overseer responsibility:

- 1 Tim 2:12 — `διδάσκειν` in the disputed restriction;
- 1 Tim 3:2 — overseer must be `διδακτικόν` / able to teach;
- 1 Tim 4:11,13,16 — teaching/doctrine central to Timothy's charge;
- 1 Tim 5:17 — elders laboring in word and teaching.

### Conservative synthesis

A substantial canonical case connects 1 Tim 2:12's teaching restriction with recognized governing/doctrinal teaching responsibility, especially given transition toward overseer qualifications in ch.3.

But:

```text
DIDASKEIN_v12_LEXICALLY_MEANS_ELDER_OFFICE = FALSE
ELDER_TEACHING_CONNECTION = CANONICAL_CONTEXTUAL_B
```

Robert Yarbrough's conservative exposition makes this distinction by interpreting v12 in terms of pastoral teaching/oversight while also affirming many other forms of female ministry.

Source:
https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/1-timothy-2-12-teach/

# 9. Exhortation / encouragement — `παράκλησις` family

1 Cor 14:3 says prophecy results in `οἰκοδομή`, `παράκλησις`, `παραμυθία`.

Elsewhere in NT ministry taxonomies exhortation is not simply identical to teaching.

### Boundary

A modern sermon may contain teaching **and** exhortation; this does not allow reverse inference that every exhortation in the NT is the authoritative teaching restricted in 1 Tim 2.

John Dickson's modern proposal sharply distinguishes apostolic “teaching” from contemporary exhortation/preaching. Conservative critics such as Kevin DeYoung dispute Dickson's narrow definition of `teaching`, but the debate itself demonstrates that mapping ancient speech categories to the modern sermon requires argument.

Control summary:
https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/blogs/kevin-deyoung/women-preach-churches/

### Calibration

```text
MODERN_SERMON = NOT_A_SINGLE_NT_LEXEME
SERMON_TO_DIDASKEIN_MAPPING = HERMENEUTICAL_B_C
```

# 10. Questioning / learning — `ἐπερωτάω` / `μανθάνω`

1 Cor 14:35 says if women/wives want `to learn something`, let them ask their own husbands at home.

1 Tim 2:11 strikingly begins with the positive `γυνὴ ... μανθανέτω` — let a woman learn.

### Significance

The two texts do not simply oppose female learning. Both regulate **how/where/order of learning** in contested contexts.

### Boundary

A disruption model for 1 Cor 14 has real textual support from v35's questions, but the adjective “disruptive” is reconstructed from chapter context; it is not written explicitly.

```text
QUESTIONS_PRESENT_IN_v35 = A
DISRUPTIVE_CHATTER_ONLY = C_B_RECONSTRUCTION
```

# 11. Authority — `ἐξουσία` vs `αὐθεντέω`

This distinction is especially important.

## 1 Cor 11:10 — `ἐξουσία`

- woman is grammatical subject of `ἔχειν`;
- ordinary lexical field: authority/right/power;
- exact referent on/over head is disputed.

## 1 Tim 2:12 — `αὐθεντεῖν`

Different lexeme, rare verb, disputed nuance: exercise authority, assume authority, dominate/usurp, etc.

George Knight's classic lexical study shows why the verb requires independent analysis:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/aente-in-reference-to-women-in-1-timothy-212/7B50A199BC552A29B0A9D6EA3DF650BE

Philip Payne argues `οὐκ ... οὐδὲ ... ἀλλά` can combine teaching + assuming authority into one prohibited idea:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/1-tim-212-and-the-use-of-to-combine-two-elements-to-express-a-single-idea/088810B9D987D8797FF1CB11F71F7E7D

### Negative boundary

```text
EXOUSIA_1COR11 = AUTHENTEIN_1TIM2 = FALSE_LEXICAL_EQUATION
```

The two passages can be canonically related without pretending the Greek words are identical.

# 12. Oversight / eldership — `ἐπίσκοπος / πρεσβύτερος`

1 Cor 11:5 contains none of these office terms.

1 Tim 3 gives overseer qualifications immediately after the disputed 2:11–15 paragraph; 1 Tim 5 later discusses elders, including those laboring in word/teaching.

### A negative fact

```text
ELDER / OVERSEER TERMINOLOGY_IN_1COR11_5 = ABSENT
```

### B canonical inference

The proximity of 1 Tim 2:12 to ch.3 and broader Pastoral teaching/oversight structures is an important complementarian argument that v12 concerns recognized church teaching authority.

It remains a **canonical/discourse inference**, not lexical substitution.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Oversight/eldership | governance/pastoral | yes | includes teaching | sex/qualification debate handled separately | office category itself |

# 14. Consequence for conservative reconciliation

The strongest conservative position does **not** need the implausible claim:

> “Women in 11:5 are technically speaking but somehow not really speaking.”

It can instead say:

1. women genuinely pray and prophesy;
2. Paul distinguishes several speech functions inside the gathered church;
3. 14:34–35, if authentic, restricts some female/wifely speech but exact function remains B/C;
4. 1 Tim 2 addresses teaching/authority specifically and supplies creation/fall rationale;
5. therefore female prophecy does not automatically entail elder/pastoral teaching office, and male teaching-office restriction does not automatically erase female prophecy.

This is a **functional distinction**, not a verbal trick.

# 15. Research no-overclaim rules

Do not state:

- “prophecy and teaching are the same thing”;
- “prophecy and teaching have nothing in common”;
- “judging prophecy is exactly what 14:34 says”;
- “asking questions proves only chatter was prohibited”;
- “1 Tim 2:12 literally says elder/pastor”;
- “modern preaching maps one-to-one onto one NT speech lexeme”;
- “`ἐξουσία` and `αὐθεντεῖν` are the same Greek word/concept”.
