# 1. PSI X 1115 — AD 152 marriage contract, Tephorsais

## 1.1 Direct documentary text

DDBDP/Université Grenoble legal-papyrus route:

- https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Negotia/PSI3_DDBDP.gr.html

Document metadata:

```text
PSI X 1115
Tebtynis, Arsinoite nome
28 December AD 152
marriage contract
```

The contract concerns Kronios, his sister/wife Tephorsais, and their mother Protarous. A third share of the enslaved woman Sarapias and future offspring is supplied to Tephorsais.

The crucial preserved legal sequence states:

```text
οὗ τὴν κράτησιν καὶ κυρία<ν> μένειν περὶ τὴν
Τεφορ[σ]άιν [κ]αὶ [το]ὺς παρ᾽ αὐτῆς
καὶ ἐξουσίαν ἔχειν οἰκονομεῖν περὶ αὐτοῦ
ὡς ἐὰν αἱρῆται
```

The minimum sense is:

- possession/control and ownership are to remain with Tephorsais and those deriving rights through her;
- authority/right is retained to administer/manage the property as she chooses.

Safe source-specific classification:

```text
PSI_X_1115_DATE = AD_152
TEPHORSAIS_FEMALE = A_DOCUMENT_CONTEXT
TEPHORSAIS_PROPERTY_CONTROL/OWNERSHIP = A_DOCUMENTARY
EXOUSIAN_ECHEIN_OIKONOMEIN = A_DOCUMENTARY_TEXT
PERI_AUTOU_COMPLEMENT = A_DOCUMENTARY_TEXT
HOS_EAN_HAIRETAI_CHOICE_LANGUAGE = A_DOCUMENTARY_TEXT
```

The syntax around the infinitive is coordinated with the clause placing control/ownership around Tephorsais and her successors. The safest translation is therefore not an abstract statement about women generally but:

> the document preserves/assigns Tephorsais’ side the authority/right to manage the relevant property as she chooses.

- Roman period;

# 3. P. Oxy. I 104 — AD 96, female testator, near-semantic control without `ἐξουσία`

## 3.1 Direct documentary text

Route:

- https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Negotia/Oxy3_DDBDP.gr.html

Metadata:

```text
P. Oxy. I 104
26 December AD 96
Will of Soeris, daughter of Harpocras
Oxyrhynchus
```

Soeris states:

```text
εἴη μέν μοι ὑγιαίνουσαν κυρίαν εἶναι τῶν ἐμαυτῆς,
χρᾶσθαι καὶ διοικεῖν περὶ αὐτῶν
καθ᾽ ὃν ἐὰν αἱρῶμαι τρόπον
```

In substance:

> while healthy/alive, may I remain mistress/owner of my own property, using and administering it in whatever way I choose.

This is not an `ἐξουσία` lexical parallel.

It is a **near-semantic legal control** showing that a woman in late-first-century documentary Greek can be represented as the active holder/manager of her own property with choice-language (`αἱρῶμαι`).

Therefore:

```text

EXOUSIA_LEXEME_PRESENT = FALSE
USE_AS_EXOUSIA_LEXICAL_PARALLEL = FORBIDDEN
```

Chronologically, this is very close to the New Testament period, but contextually it remains an Egyptian legal will, not Corinthian worship.
