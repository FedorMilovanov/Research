# 1. Chronology discipline

Do not flatten sources from different dates into one synchronized “Roman law.”

```text
LUCRETIUS/VIRGIL/LIVY = PRE_OR_EARLY_IMPERIAL_ROMAN_LITERARY_CONTROL
PAUL = MID_FIRST_CENTURY_CE
PLUTARCH = NEAR_POST_PAUL_ANTIQUARIAN_CONTROL
LATER_CHRISTIAN_WRITERS = RECEPTION, NOT DIRECT_CORINTH_SOCIAL_PROOF
```

A custom attested before and after Paul gains continuity plausibility, but local application still needs contextual argument.

---

# 2. Genre discipline

Each genre supports different claims.

## Poetry

Lucretius/Virgil can prove **recognizability of ritual imagery** and cultural scripts. They are weaker as frequency statistics.

## Historiography/antiquarian writing

Livy/Plutarch can attest named customs and explanatory traditions, but their accounts may be idealized/etiological.

## Sculpture/relief/portraiture

Visual evidence proves represented social/ritual possibilities and status messages. It does not tell us how every person dressed every day.

Thus:

```text
IMAGE_OF_X != POPULATION_FREQUENCY_OF_X
LITERARY_NORM != UNIVERSAL_COMPLIANCE
RITUAL_EXAMPLE != EXACT_CORINTHIAN_CAUSE
```

# 3. Primary-source impact on modern models

## Oster

Primary ledger strongly supports Oster’s core historical point that male covered-head worship was Roman and locally plausible.

## Gill

Visual ledger strongly supports Gill’s insistence on Roman-colony context and mixed portrait evidence.

## Massey

Primary historical ledger complements his lexical argument for a material object in v4–6.

## Finney

Elite sacrificial imagery makes status signaling plausible, but status motive remains reconstruction.

## Nõmmik

Primary ledger gives strong premises:

- Roman rite exists;
- male ritual covering is locally intelligible;
- prayer/divination connection exists.

It does **not** supply his distinctive Corinthian uniformity proposal.

## Callon

Plutarch’s gendered norm and mixed visual data make marriage/status questions meaningful but do not establish only-free(d)-wives automatically.

# 4. High-value negative findings

Negative findings are part of the evidence.

The inspected primary/near-primary corpus does **not** yield:

```text
A_CORINTHIAN_LAW_UNVEILED_WOMAN_EQUALS_PROSTITUTE
A_UNIVERSAL_ROMAN_RULE_ALL_WOMEN_ALWAYS_VEILED
A_UNIVERSAL_ROMAN_RULE_ALL_MALE_WORSHIP_CAPITE_VELATO
AN_EXTANT_CORINTHIAN_STATEMENT_OF_RITUAL_UNIFORMITY
A_PRIMARY_TEXT_IDENTIFYING_1COR11_4_WITH_CAPITE_VELATO_BY_NAME
```

These absences do not prove the opposite, but they block categorical rhetoric.

---

# 5. Citation hygiene for future drafts

When making a historical sentence, prefer this shape:

> Roman covered-head ritual worship is independently attested in pre-Pauline and near-Pauline literary evidence (e.g. Lucretius, Virgil, Livy, Plutarch), and published Corinthian visual evidence makes the practice locally intelligible.

Avoid:

> Roman men always covered their heads to worship, so 1 Cor 11:4 must be capite velato.

The first sentence distinguishes evidence from inference; the second collapses them.
