## Directly controlled scholarly nodes

### Curt Niccum — external evidence against easy interpolation

`The Voice of the Manuscripts on the Silence of Women: The External Evidence for 1 Cor 14.34–5`, NTS 43 (1997), 242–255.

- stresses that extant external evidence recognizes no complete omission;
- treats Western displacement as a local/common-archetype phenomenon;
- therefore interpolation cannot be declared from displacement alone.

URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/voice-of-the-manuscripts-on-the-silence-of-women-the-external-evidence-for-1-cor-14345/5045B323E5B1F82A873ED1706AD64677

### Philip B. Payne — Vaticanus / marginal-gloss case

`Vaticanus Distigme-obelos Symbols Marking Added Text, Including 1 Corinthians 14.34–5`, NTS 63 (2017).

- argues the Vaticanus marginal mark combines distigme + obelos;
- interprets this as evidence for awareness of a text in which vv.34–35 were absent/additional;
- combines this with transposition to support marginal-gloss interpolation.

URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/vaticanus-distigmeobelos-symbols-marking-added-text-including-1-corinthians-14345/A5FC01A6E14A2A1CF1F514A9BF93C581

### Richard G. Fellows 2019 — direct critique of Payne's Vaticanus mark

`Are There Distigme-Obelos Symbols in Vaticanus?`, NTS 65 (2019), 246–251.

- argues Payne's supposed obelos is indistinguishable from paragraphoi;
- specifically challenges the measurement/identification case;
- therefore Payne's Vaticanus sign cannot be treated as uncontested manuscript proof of interpolation.

URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/are-there-distigmeobelos-symbols-in-vaticanus/F2507362D31D370A04A20583EE0E1575

### Richard G. Fellows 2024 — new pro-interpolation argument

`The Interpolation of 1 Cor. 14.34–35 and the Reversal of the Name Order of Prisca and Aquila at 1 Cor. 16.19`, JSNT 47.2.

- despite the 2019 critique of Payne's Vaticanus-specific case, Fellows now argues for interpolation on different transcriptional/transposition grounds;
- therefore one must not summarize scholarly sides as fixed camps around a single siglum argument.

URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0142064X231226165

### Aļesja Lavrinoviča 2017 — v33b paragraph boundary

`1 Cor 14.34–5 without ‘in All the Churches of the Saints’: External Evidence`, NTS 63 (2017), 370–389.

- argues oldest/relevant manuscript paragraphing attaches v33b to v33a rather than vv34–35;
- warns that printed-edition punctuation linking “as in all churches” with the women clause is exegetical rather than externally manuscript-driven.

URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/1-cor-14345-without-in-all-the-churches-of-the-saints-external-evidence/D4D25E5F5D48A16F34BD806E24BE89AB

### Paul/Plutarch gender-prophecy study — authenticity control

The study treats vv34–35 as authentic and explicitly notes:

- Western displacement is real;
- Vaticanus/Fuldensis/MS88 complicate the issue;
- no manuscript completely omits vv34–35;
- internal consistency arguments often drive interpolation conclusions.

URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/paul-plutarch-and-the-gender-dynamics-of-prophecy/010C5933799D0757AAD949AB8497E3AF
