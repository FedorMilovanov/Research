## 0. Purpose

Roman literary sources are often cited as though they transmit one stable, transparent rule:

```text
MEN_UNCOVERED
WOMEN_COVERED
```

or the reverse.

This pass compares two primary antiquarian/moralizing traditions that discuss the **same famous Sulpicius Gallus divorce exemplum** and discovers that they transmit the head-covering detail in opposite directions.

This is not noise to hide. It is a direct source-critical control.

---

# 1. Plutarch, *Roman Questions* 10 — male ritual covering is explicit

Primary text/translation route:

- https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/e/roman/texts/plutarch/moralia/roman_questions%2A/a.html

In Question 10 Plutarch asks why Romans, **when they worship the gods**, cover their heads, but uncover when meeting honoured fellow humans if the toga happens to be over the head.

His discussion repeatedly treats the practice as male Roman divine-service behavior and explicitly describes pulling the toga over the ears/head during prayer.

Direct minimum:

```text

PLUTARCH_Q10_TOGA_OVER_HEAD_IN_PRAYER = A_PRIMARY_LITERARY
```

This is independent literary support for the already strong Roman `capite velato` background.

It does not prove Corinthian Christian men were performing the same rite.


# 2. Plutarch, *Roman Questions* 14 — female custom is presented as both usual and historically unstable

In Question 14 Plutarch asks why sons escort parents to burial with heads covered while daughters go uncovered with hair unbound.

One proposed explanation is inversion of ordinary custom:

> it is more usual for women to appear publicly with heads covered and men uncovered.

This is real evidence for Plutarch’s perception/representation of a normal sex-differentiated public convention.

```text
PLUTARCH_Q14_WOMEN_PUBLICLY_COVERED_AS_MORE_USUAL = A_PRIMARY_STATEMENT_BY_PLUTARCH
```

But in the **same question** he preserves a conflicting antiquarian tradition:

> formerly women were not allowed to cover the head at all.

He then gives a sequence of divorce exempla and says Sulpicius Gallus divorced his wife because he saw her **pull her cloak over her head**.

Thus Plutarch himself does not present a timeless, unchanged female rule.

```text
PLUTARCH_Q14_FEMALE_CUSTOM = HISTORICALLY_VARIABLE/ANTIQUARIANLY_COMPLEX
```

# 3. Valerius Maximus 6.3.10 — the same Sulpicius Gallus story has the opposite head state

Direct Latin control:

- PHI Latin Texts: https://latin.packhum.org/loc/1038/1/75/2386-2389%2C5666-5669%2C6383-6386%2C6603-6606%2C7122-7125%2C7162-7165

Valerius Maximus 6.3.10 states:

```text
uxorem dimisit, quod eam capite aperto foris versatam cognoverat
```

The essential meaning is unambiguous:

```text
capite aperto = with head uncovered/open
foris versatam = having been/appeared outside in public
```

So Valerius says Gallus divorced his wife because she had been outside **with her head uncovered**.

Direct minimum:

```text
VALERIUS_6_3_10_GALLUS_WIFE_UNCOVERED_IN_PUBLIC = A_PRIMARY_TEXT
```

# 4. Direct contradiction in the antiquarian tradition

The two sources transmit opposite details:

```text
PLUTARCH_Q14:
Gallus divorces wife because she pulled cloak OVER her head

VALERIUS_MAXIMUS_6_3_10:
Gallus divorces wife because she was OUTSIDE WITH HEAD UNCOVERED
```

Therefore:

```text
SULPICIUS_GALLUS_HEAD_STATE_TRADITION = INTERNALLY_CONTRADICTORY
```

This blocks treating the anecdote as a simple, independently verified fact of Republican dress law.

The contradiction may reflect:

- different source traditions;
- antiquarian misunderstanding;
- textual/source confusion;
- rhetorical reshaping by one author;
- some other transmission problem.

This dossier does **not** resolve which historical version is original.

```text
WHICH_GALLUS_VERSION_IS_HISTORICALLY_CORRECT = OPEN
```

# 7. `infula` / `vitta` terminology must remain separate from full head-cover garments

Classical Latin lexical/antiquarian evidence distinguishes sacred bands/fillets from toga/palla-over-head gestures.

A direct lexical control (BBAW Classical Language Dictionary / Lewis & Short data) defines `infula` as a woollen sacred fillet/band used on priests, victims and in consecratory contexts.

Route:

- https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/lat/infula

Thus:

```text
INFULA/VITTA = BAND/FILLET_CONSECRATORY_HEAD_ORNAMENT
TOGA_CAPITE_VELATO = GARMENT_DRAWN_OVER_HEAD
PALLA/AMICTUS_HEAD_COVER = GARMENT_COVERING_CATEGORY
```

Do not flatten all ancient “head-covering” vocabulary into one artifact.

This helps interpret Fantham/Olson references without making every `vitta`, `infula`, `palla`, `toga`, veil and hairstyle interchangeable.
