# 1 Коринфянам 11:2–16 — Greek lexical primary-corpus audit

**Дата:** 2026-08-10  
**Статус:** `LEXICAL-PRIMARY-CORPUS / RESEARCH-ONLY / FAIL-CLOSED / PUBLICATION-HOLD`

## 0. Method

This layer asks a narrower question than a commentary:

> What do the Greek words/constructions themselves permit, favor, or fail to decide before a historical/theological model is imposed?

Evidence hierarchy:

```text
A_SYNTAX/TEXT = grammatical relation or directly attested lexical fact
B_HIGH_SEMANTIC_PULL = corpus strongly favors a semantic class, but context still selects exact referent
B/C_CONTEXTUAL = word range is established but local meaning remains debated
C_VIABLE = linguistically possible serious alternative, not contextually leading
D/C_LOW = published edge proposal with weak lexical support
```

Critical anti-circularity rule:

```text
DO_NOT_DEFINE_A_WORD_FROM_THE_THEORY_THE_WORD_IS_BEING_USED_TO_PROVE
```

Lexica may cite 1 Cor 11 itself; where possible this dossier prioritizes independent Classical/LXX/Pauline parallels.

# 1. `κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων` — 11:4

Greek:

```text
πᾶς ἀνὴρ προσευχόμενος ἢ προφητεύων κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων ...
```

## 1.1. What `κατὰ κεφαλῆς` itself means

LSJ gives the genitive construction `κατὰ κεφαλῆς` as **down over/from the head**, with Homeric examples such as dust being poured down over the head.

This establishes a spatial relation:

```text
KATA_KEPHALES_SPATIAL = DOWN/OVER_FROM_THE_HEAD
```

It does **not**, by itself, lexically name:

- veil;
- toga;
- hair;
- tallit;
- hood.

The construction in 1 Cor 11:4 is elliptical in the sense that the object possessed with `ἔχων` is not separately named.

Therefore the popular claim:

> “The Greek literally says ‘wearing a veil’”

is too strong.

The safer lexical statement is:

> the man is described as **having something down/over the head**, with context determining what that something is.

## 1.2. Does the phrase favor material covering?

Yes, contextually and corpus-wise, especially when read with vv5–7 and `κατακαλύπτω`.

Preston T. Massey’s dedicated 2007 NTS study of `κατακαλύπτω` and `κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων` concludes that the covering language supports textile head coverings rather than the long-hair thesis.

But the project separates:

```text
KATA_KEPHALES_ALONE = SPATIAL, OBJECT_UNNAMED
KATA_KEPHALES_PLUS_KATAKALYPTO_CONTEXT = MATERIAL_READING_B_HIGH
```

## 1.3. Esther 6:12 caution

The LXX of Esther 6:12 has Haman returning home `λυπούμενος κατὰ κεφαλῆς`.

The Hebrew context/traditional translations concern Haman with his head covered in grief, but the Greek phrase does **not** reproduce the full Pauline `κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων` construction.

Therefore Esther is useful as evidence that `κατὰ κεφαλῆς` can occur in a head-state/shame context, but should not be presented as an exact syntactic twin proving a textile object.

```text
ESTHER_6_12_EXACT_PARALLEL = false
ESTHER_6_12_HEAD_STATE_CONTEXT = B_SUPPORTIVE
```

## 1.4. Grade

```text

V4_MATERIAL_COVERING = B_HIGH_LEADING
V4_HAIR_ONLY_FROM_KATA_KEPHALES_ALONE = C
```

# 2. `κατακαλύπτω` / `ἀκατακάλυπτος` — vv5–7, 13

## 2.1. Independent lexical corpus

LSJ defines `κατακαλύπτω` broadly as **cover up**.

Important independent examples include:

- ordinary objects being covered;
- earth/darkness/clouds covering;
- middle usage `κατὰ κρᾶτα καλυψάμενος` — **having covered his head** (Odyssey 8.92);
- veiled persons in later Classical prose.

The word is therefore not intrinsically a technical term for a particular garment. It denotes the act/state of covering.

In the LXX it is likewise a broad covering verb; context supplies what is covered and by what.

## 2.2. Negative adjective

LSJ gives `ἀκατακάλυπτος` simply as **uncovered**, with examples outside Paul as well as 1 Cor 11.

Thus:

```text
AKATAKALYPTOS = UNCOVERED
```

The lexical contrast in 11:5–7/13 is genuinely:

```text
COVERED ↔ UNCOVERED
```

not directly:

```text
SHORT_HAIR ↔ LONG_HAIR
```

## 2.3. Why this pressures hair-only models

Paul has perfectly ordinary hair vocabulary available and actually uses it in vv14–15 (`κομάω`, `κόμη`).

That makes it significant that vv5–7 use the cover/uncover lexical family instead.

This does not mathematically prove two separate physical entities; a writer can describe hair as a covering metaphorically. But a hair-only theory has to explain why Paul switches lexical fields and later explicitly names hair.

```text
LEXICAL_FIELD_V5_7 = COVER/UNCOVER
LEXICAL_FIELD_V14_15 = LONG_HAIR/HAIR
IDENTITY_OF_THE_TWO_FIELDS = NOT_LEXICALLY_GIVEN
```

## 2.4. Grade

```text
KATAKALYPTO_COVER_SEMANTICS = A_LEXICAL
EXTERNAL_MATERIAL_COVERING_IN_11_5_7 = B_HIGH_CONTEXTUAL
HAIR_ONLY = C_SERIOUS_ALTERNATIVE_WITH_HIGHER_LEXICAL_BURDEN
```

Massey’s “textile only” conclusion is strong specialist evidence, but the project retains C for hair-only because the whole passage includes explicit hair analogy and published counterarguments.

# 3. `κεφαλή` — v3 and throughout

## 3.1. Base sense

The ordinary anatomical meaning “head” is beyond dispute.

The controversy concerns metaphorical relational use in v3:

```text
κεφαλὴ ... τοῦ ἀνδρός
κεφαλὴ ... γυναικός
κεφαλὴ ... τοῦ Χριστοῦ
```

## 3.2. Primary Greek evidence against “metaphorical leadership is impossible”

The LXX contains explicit constructions where a person is made `κεφαλή` in parallel with a leadership noun.

Judges 11:11 describes Jephthah being set over the people:

```text
εἰς κεφαλὴν ... εἰς ἡγούμενον / ἀρχηγόν
```

That is direct Judeo-Greek evidence that `κεφαλή` can participate in a leader/head metaphor.

Isaiah 7:8–9 also uses a nested head relation:

```text
ἡ κεφαλὴ Αραμ Δαμασκός
ἡ κεφαλὴ Εφραιμ Σομόρων
ἡ κεφαλὴ Σομόρων υἱὸς τοῦ Ρομελιου
```

The sequence moves city/realm/person in a prominence/governance relation.

Therefore categorical claims such as:

```text
KEPHALE_NEVER_MEANS_LEADER/AUTHORITY_IN_RELEVANT_GREEK
```

are false.

## 3.3. Why this still does not make “authority over” an A lexical gloss

Semantic debate remains because a metaphorical head can combine:

- leadership;
- prominence;
- representative relation;
- source/origin associations;
- bodily-organism analogy.

The word itself does not contain an English theological phrase “authority over” in every occurrence.

Cervin/Wolters-type methodological cautions are important: lexical meaning, metaphorical mapping, Semitic/Judeo-Christian usage and local syntax must not be collapsed.

## 3.4. Source/origin

“Source” is linguistically proposed in scholarship and cannot be dismissed as impossible by fiat.

But two cautions apply:

1. the primary Judeo-Greek leadership/prominence examples show that authority/headship is a real semantic possibility;
2. in 11:8 Paul expresses origin explicitly with `ἐκ` (“woman from man”), so v8 cannot simply be back-projected as proof that the lexical meaning of `κεφαλή` in v3 is “source.”

```text
V8_ORIGIN_CONCEPT_EXISTS = A_TEXT
THEREFORE_KEPHALE_LEXICALLY_MEANS_SOURCE = INVALID_INFERENCE
```

## 3.5. Grade

```text
KEPHALE_ANATOMICAL_HEAD = A_LEXICAL
KEPHALE_CAN_FUNCTION_AS_LEADER/PROMINENCE_METAPHOR = A_CORPUS_POSSIBILITY
KEPHALE_HEADSHIP_AUTHORITY_IN_11_3 = B_LEADING_CONTEXTUAL
```

# 4. `ἀνήρ` / `γυνή` — man/husband, woman/wife

## 4.1. Lexical range is genuinely dual

LSJ directly gives:

```text
ἀνήρ = man; also husband
γυνή = woman; also wife/spouse
```

Thus the wives/all-women controversy cannot be solved by saying either sense is “not Greek.”

## 4.2. Contextual pressure

The passage repeatedly pairs `ἀνήρ` and `γυνή`, which naturally supports sex-class language.

At the same time:

- shame toward “her head” can be relational/marital;
- creation “woman from man” can shade into Adam/Eve and husband/wife paradigms;
- Callon 2024 and Ciampa’s direct summary make marital status a serious contextual issue.

The word forms themselves do not decide the scope.

## 4.3. v12 is a control

`ὥσπερ γὰρ ἡ γυνὴ ἐκ τοῦ ἀνδρός...` evokes the woman/man creation relation and makes a rigid translation “the wife from the husband” semantically awkward unless the Genesis representative sense is explained.

That is why a model can argue wives are the primary practical addressees without claiming that **every token** must lexically mean wife/husband.

# 5. `ἐξουσίαν ἔχειν` — v10

Greek:

```text
διὰ τοῦτο ὀφείλει ἡ γυνὴ ἐξουσίαν ἔχειν ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς διὰ τοὺς ἀγγέλους
```

## 5.1. Syntax

The grammatical subject of `ὀφείλει` is `ἡ γυνή`.

The infinitival complement is:

```text
ἐξουσίαν ἔχειν
```

Therefore the woman is syntactically the one who must **have** `ἐξουσία`.

```text
EXOUSIA_WOMAN_SUBJECT = A_SYNTAX
```

No translation may erase this without admitting that it is supplying an interpretive paraphrase.

## 5.2. Paul’s own `ἔχω ἐξουσίαν` usage

1 Cor 9:4:

```text
μὴ οὐκ ἔχομεν ἐξουσίαν φαγεῖν καὶ πεῖν;
```

Paul asks whether “we” have the **right/authority** to eat and drink.

1 Cor 7:37:

```text
ἐξουσίαν δὲ ἔχει περὶ τοῦ ἰδίου θελήματος
```

The subject has authority/control concerning his own will.

These are especially probative because they are Pauline and in the same letter.

## 5.3. Wider NT construction

Matthew 9:6:

```text
ἐξουσίαν ἔχει ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ...
```

Revelation 11:6:

```text
ἐξουσίαν ἔχουσιν ἐπὶ τῶν ὑδάτων ...
```

In these constructions, the grammatical subject possesses/exercises authority, and `ἐπί` specifies a domain/object over which it bears.

## 5.4. Lexical range outside NT

Classical Greek `ἐξουσία` includes:

- power/might;
- liberty/license;
- opportunity/right/faculty.

Thus the semantic core is not naturally “a visible sign that somebody else has authority over the subject.”

## 5.5. What this does to the traditional “sign of authority” translation

A paraphrase such as:

> “a sign of authority over her”

requires at least two supplied semantic steps:

1. `ἐξουσία` → “sign/symbol of authority”;
2. the authority belongs to another person rather than to the grammatical subject.

Neither “sign” nor an external possessor is overtly stated.

That does not prove Hooker/Payne/Peters-type readings of the exact referent, because `ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς` still needs explanation and clothing can function as a sign of an authorized status.

But it establishes a strong burden-of-proof asymmetry:

```text
WOMAN_HAS_AUTHORITY/RIGHT = DIRECT_SYNTAX_PLUS_NORMAL_SEMANTICS
SIGN_OF_ANOTHER_PERSONS_AUTHORITY = INTERPRETIVE_PARAPHRASE
```

## 5.6. Grade

```text
EXOUSIA_WOMAN_SUBJECT = A
EXOUSIA_NORMAL_SEMANTIC_CLASS_RIGHT/AUTHORITY/CONTROL = A/B_HIGH
V10_WOMAN_POSSESSES_OR_BEARER_OF_AUTHORITY = B_HIGH_SEMANTIC_PULL
```

# 6. `ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς` in v10

`ἐπί` with the genitive here naturally locates/references the head: authority “on/over/at the head.”

But the prepositional phrase is semantically underdetermined between possibilities such as:

- something worn on the head that expresses/embodies authority;
- authority/control concerning the head;
- authority exercised over what happens to the head;
- metonymic personal/head relation.

# 7. `διὰ τοῦτο` — v10

The connective is explicitly inferential/anaphoric: “because of this / for this reason.”

This is important for the semantic map because v10 cannot be detached from vv7–9.

```text
DIA_TOUTO_POINTS_BACK_TO_PRIOR_REASONING = A_DISCOURSE
```

# 8. `πλὴν` — v11

`πλήν` marks qualification/transition: “nevertheless / however / except that.”

The move in vv11–12 is therefore neither a mere repetition of vv7–10 nor necessarily a contradiction by a new speaker.

It naturally signals a **qualification/counterbalance** within the argument.

```text
PLEN_V11_QUALIFIES_PRIOR_ARGUMENT = A_DISCOURSE
PLEN_V11_PROVES_QUOTATION_REFUTATION = false
```

This supports the adversarial finding that vv11–12 are mandatory theological limits on simplistic unilateral hierarchy while not proving that vv3–10 are a Corinthian quotation.

# 9. `ὥσπερ ... οὕτως` — v12

Paul uses an explicit reciprocal comparative frame:

```text
ὥσπερ ... ἐκ τοῦ ἀνδρός,
οὕτως καὶ ... διὰ τῆς γυναικός
```

The syntax creates a real two-direction relation, followed by:

```text
τὰ δὲ πάντα ἐκ τοῦ θεοῦ
```

Thus:

```text
MUTUAL_INTERDEPENDENCE_11_11_12 = A_TEXT/SYNTAX
```

Any model may explain the asymmetry of `ἐκ` vs `διά`, but may not erase the reciprocal argumentative structure.

# 10. `φύσις` — v14

Greek:

```text
οὐδὲ ἡ φύσις αὐτὴ διδάσκει ὑμᾶς ...;
```

## 10.1. Broad lexical range

LSJ’s semantic range includes:

- origin/birth;
- natural qualities/constitution;
- condition;
- inherent character;
- natural order.

Paul himself uses `φύσις` and cognates flexibly.

### Romans 1:26

`παρὰ φύσιν` contrasts with `φυσικὴ χρῆσις` in a created/sexed-use argument.

### Romans 2:14

Gentiles `φύσει` do things of the law: “by nature” does not simply mean genetic biology.

### Romans 11:24

Wild/cultivated olive branches are `κατὰ φύσιν` / `παρὰ φύσιν`: the metaphor invokes ordinary kind/order but includes horticultural convention/action.

### Galatians 2:15

`ἡμεῖς φύσει Ἰουδαῖοι` — Jews “by nature/birth,” a status of natal identity, not anatomy alone.

## 10.2. Consequence for 1 Cor 11:14

Two absolutist statements are both blocked:

```text
PHYSIS_ALWAYS_MEANS_RAW_BIOLOGY = false
PHYSIS_HERE_CAN_ONLY_MEAN_ARBITRARY_SOCIAL_CUSTOM = false
```

Paul’s phrase “nature itself teaches” can invoke a perceived natural/proper order in which biological sex, embodied appearance and social recognition are not sharply separable in ancient thought.

## 10.3. Hair-growth objection

Male hair is biologically capable of growing long. Therefore v14 cannot simply mean:

> “male follicles literally cannot produce long hair.”

The verb `κομάω` means **let/wear the hair long**, an intentional/visible state, not merely possess hair follicles.

Thus the strongest contextual reading is something like:

> the recognized order/propriety associated with sexed hair presentation teaches the shame/glory contrast.

How much of that is creation-grounded versus social convention remains debated.

This is lexically important for the covering debate:

But a theory that claims Paul only ever means hairstyle must explain the lexical shift.

# 12. `περιβόλαιον` — v15

Greek:

```text
ἡ κόμη ἀντὶ περιβολαίου δέδοται [αὐτῇ]
```

## 12.1. Independent lexical data

LSJ gives `περιβόλαιον` as **that which is thrown around, a covering**.

Independent biblical examples make the garment/wrap sense exceptionally clear.

### Deuteronomy 22:12 LXX

The plural `τῶν περιβολαίων σου` refers to garments/wraps whose corners receive tassels and which a person wraps around himself.

### Psalm 101:27 LXX / Hebrews 1:12

The heavens are changed/rolled like a `περιβόλαιον`, in parallel with `ἱμάτιον` (garment).

Thus:

```text
PERIBOLAION_NORMAL_COVERING/WRAP/GARMENT_SEMANTICS = A
```

## 12.2. Does this prove an external covering in vv5–7?

Not by itself.

v15 says hair is given `ἀντὶ περιβολαίου`.

The logic depends heavily on `ἀντί` and the relation Paul is drawing.

If “hair instead of a covering” means hair replaces every external covering in the passage, hair-only gains support.

If hair functions as a natural analogue/corresponding covering that teaches the propriety of another ritual covering, textile models gain support.

The noun itself cannot decide the discourse relation.

## 12.3. Testicle/physiology theory

Ancient physiological proposals linking `περιβόλαιον` to genital/testicular ideas do not fit the normal lexical evidence recovered here.

The independent LXX/Hebrews uses are plainly wrap/garment imagery.

Thus:

```text
PERIBOLAION_TESTICLE_LEXICAL_MEANING = D_C_LOW/NOT_NORMAL_LEXEME_SENSE
```

A physiology theory would have to be a highly specialized metaphorical reconstruction, not a dictionary meaning of the word.

# 13. `ἀντί` — v15

`ἀντί` with the genitive can express:

- instead of / in place of;
- in return for;
- corresponding exchange/substitution relations.

Therefore the line is not semantically equivalent to only one English proposition.

At minimum:

```text
HAIR_STANDS_IN_A_CORRESPONDING/SUBSTITUTION_RELATION_TO_PERIBOLAION = A
```

But the preposition alone cannot decide whether Paul means:

1. hair is the **only** needed covering, replacing textile;
2. hair is a natural covering **analogous to** the ritual/social covering under discussion;
3. hair is given as a sex-differentiating counterpart that grounds propriety.

```text
ANTI_PROVES_HAIR_ONLY = false
ANTI_PROVES_TWO_COVERINGS = false
```

The whole vv4–16 discourse must decide.

# 14. Hair vs covering — lexical synthesis

The strongest lexical facts are:

```text
A. κατακαλύπτω = cover / veil
B. ἀκατακάλυπτος = uncovered
C. κομάω = wear/let hair grow long
D. κόμη = hair
E. περιβόλαιον = covering/wrap/garment
F. Paul uses A/B in vv5–7, C/D/E in vv14–15
```

Therefore:

```text
THE_TEXT_HAS_TWO_DISTINCT_LEXICAL_FIELDS = A
```

Whether they refer to one physical phenomenon or one is analogy/evidence for the other is contextual.

Project result:

```text
MATERIAL_COVERING = B_HIGH_LEADING
HAIR_ONLY = C_SERIOUS_ALTERNATIVE
```

The lexical audit strengthens the **burden** on hair-only but does not erase it by decree.
