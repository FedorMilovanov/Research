# 2. 1 Кор 14:34–35 — сначала текстология, потом гармонизация

## 2.1 Что во внешнем manuscript evidence установлено твёрдо

### A — присутствие текста в extant manuscript tradition

Нет известного сохранившегося греческого manuscript witness, который просто полностью опускает 14:34–35. Curt Niccum подчёркивает, что external evidence знает **transposition**, но не extant omission witness.

Source:
- Curt Niccum, `The Voice of the Manuscripts on the Silence of Women: The External Evidence for 1 Cor 14.34–5`, NTS 43 (1997): https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/voice-of-the-manuscripts-on-the-silence-of-women-the-external-evidence-for-1-cor-14345/5045B323E5B1F82A873ED1706AD64677

### A — transposition is real

Western witnesses place vv.34–35 after v.40 rather than after v.33. This is a genuine textual phenomenon and must not be hidden.

### A/B — paragraphing of 33b

Aļesja Lavrinoviča's manuscript study argues that `ὡς ἐν πάσαις ταῖς ἐκκλησίαις τῶν ἁγίων` belongs with v.33a rather than introducing vv.34–35; this is an important correction to arguments that directly attach “as in all churches” to the women-silence clause.

Source:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/1-cor-14345-without-in-all-the-churches-of-the-saints-external-evidence/D4D25E5F5D48A16F34BD806E24BE89AB

## 2.2 Interpolation theory — serious, contested, not removable by slogan

### Evidence used FOR interpolation

1. Western transposition after v.40.
2. Internal tension with 11:5 and repeated `all` speaking/prophecy language in ch.14.
3. Scribal marks in Vaticanus/Fuldensis/MS 88 interpreted by Payne as evidence for an earlier text lacking vv.34–35.
4. Fee's argument that such a large free transposition would be unusual and marginal-gloss insertion better explains two locations.
5. Richard Fellows 2024 develops a new transposition/interpolation argument based on broader Western textual behavior.

Sources:
- Philip Payne, Vaticanus distigme-obelos study: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/vaticanus-distigmeobelos-symbols-marking-added-text-including-1-corinthians-14345/A5FC01A6E14A2A1CF1F514A9BF93C581
- Richard G. Fellows 2024: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0142064X231226165

### Evidence AGAINST treating interpolation as settled

1. No extant manuscript omits the verses outright.
2. Niccum argues the displaced Western witnesses can be explained from a localized textual tradition/common archetype.
3. Richard Fellows 2019 directly disputes Payne's claim that the Vaticanus bar at this location is an obelos rather than an ordinary paragraphos.
4. Recent work on Paul/Plutarch prophecy explicitly treats the verses as authentic and says interpolation evidence is limited and dependent partly on perceived Pauline consistency.

Sources:
- Fellows 2019: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/are-there-distigmeobelos-symbols-in-vaticanus/F2507362D31D370A04A20583EE0E1575
- `Paul, Plutarch and the Gender Dynamics of Prophecy`: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/paul-plutarch-and-the-gender-dynamics-of-prophecy/010C5933799D0757AAD949AB8497E3AF

# 3. If 14:34–35 is authentic: what can `σιγάω` / `λαλεῖν` prohibit?

The chapter itself uses silence commands selectively:

- tongue speaker must be silent when no interpreter (14:28);
- prophet must be silent when revelation comes to another (14:30);
- women/wives are told to be silent (14:34).

Therefore `σιγάω` does not automatically mean an absolute lifetime prohibition on every vocal sound; its scope is context-dependent.

But v.35 also mentions asking questions and learning, so any narrowing must explain this clause rather than simply replace `λαλεῖν` with “chatter”.

## Model A — authoritative judging/evaluation of prophecy

### Proposal

Because vv.29–33 concern weighing prophetic speech, vv.34–35 restrict women from authoritative public evaluation/judgment of prophecies, not from prophesying itself.

### Strengths

- directly harmonizes 11:5 with 14:34–35;
- keeps both passages Pauline;
- distinguishes prophecy from authoritative evaluation/teaching;
- fits a complementarian structure where women may prophesy but not exercise governing doctrinal judgment over men.

### Pressures

- `λαλεῖν` itself is broad and v.35 explicitly mentions asking questions;
- the text does not contain a noun/verb meaning “judge prophecies” in vv.34–35 themselves;
- must derive narrower scope from immediate discourse rather than lexical meaning alone.

### Grade

`B/C — viable conservative harmonization, not A lexical fact`.

## Model B — disruptive questioning / disorder

### Proposal

Women/wives were asking disruptive questions in the assembly; Paul commands silence in that specific disorderly activity and tells them to ask husbands at home.

### Strengths

- v.35 explicitly mentions asking/learning;
- fits chapter 14's overriding concern for order;
- easily reconciles 11:5.

### Pressures

- text never says “disruptive chatter” explicitly;
- `ὑποτασσέσθωσαν` and appeal to law may indicate a broader order/submission concern;
- not all women necessarily had husbands available at home, raising scope questions if `γυναῖκες` means all women.

### Grade

`C/B depending reconstruction`; serious but historically reconstructive.

## Model C — wives specifically, not all women

### Proposal

`γυναῖκες` are wives, as suggested by “their own husbands” in v.35; married women are prohibited from a certain kind of public questioning/speaking.

### Strengths

- v.35 directly uses `τοὺς ἰδίους ἄνδρας`;
- prevents extrapolation to every unmarried woman without argument;
- creates possible continuity with wives-reading in 1 Cor 11.

### Pressure

- still must define what speaking is prohibited;
- cannot assume every occurrence of γυνή in 11 or 14 means wife.

### Grade

`B/C scope possibility`.

## Model D — broad silence / non-authoritative female speech prohibited in assembly except extraordinary prophecy

Historic conservative line: normal public teaching/speaking is restricted, while prophecy in 11:5 is an extraordinary charismatic exception or different speech category.

### Strengths

- takes v.34's surface breadth seriously;
- distinguishes gifts/offices/speech acts;
- has long reception history (e.g., Locke-like “extraordinary call” harmonizations, classic Protestant treatments).

### Pressures

- must not make 11:5 hypothetical/nonexistent;
- must explain how prophecy can be public and intelligible while “speaking” is restricted;
- terminology needs careful category distinction, not simply “prophecy does not count as speaking”.

### Grade

`B/C historic conservative harmonization`.

## Model E — quotation/refutation

vv.34–35 quote a Corinthian restriction; v.36 rebukes it.

### Strengths

- preserves transmitted text and full female speech of 11:5;
- quotation/refutation occurs elsewhere in 1 Corinthians.

### Pressures

- no explicit quotation marker;
- requires v.36 to switch speaker/stance decisively;
- early reception does not preserve clear awareness of this reading.

### Grade

`C/D — published but lower-weight than ordinary Pauline reading`.

# 4. What 1 Cor 11:5 and 14:34–35 jointly establish even before 1 Timothy

### A

1. Female prophecy/prayer is a real category in 1 Cor 11.
2. 1 Cor 14 contains a real restriction on some form of female/wifely speech **if vv.34–35 are Pauline/authentic**.
3. Therefore the canonical interpreter cannot responsibly identify `all female vocal ministry` with `the prohibited speech` without further argument.

### B

The most conservative coherent reading distinguishes **types/functions of speech**:

- prayer;
- prophecy;
- evaluation/judgment;
- teaching;
- questions/learning;
- governing authority.

They cannot be collapsed into one English category “speaking”.

### Negative boundary

# 5. 1 Тим 2:11–15 — separate but necessary control

## 5.1 Direct textual core

The paragraph contains:

- positive command/permission for woman to learn (`μανθανέτω`) in quietness/submission (v.11);
- prohibition/negative statement involving `διδάσκειν` and `αὐθεντεῖν ἀνδρός` (v.12);
- reason introduced by `γάρ`: Adam formed first, then Eve (v.13);
- second reason: deception/transgression (v.14);
- notoriously difficult childbearing/salvation clause v.15.

### A

Whatever v.12's exact scope, vv.13–14 make **creation/fall reasoning part of the author's stated rationale**. A purely local-Ephesus reading must explain why the argument appeals to Genesis rather than merely asserting local false teaching.

## 5.2 `διδάσκειν` + `αὐθεντεῖν`

### Complementarian leading model

Two related prohibited activities: authoritative doctrinal teaching / exercising authority over men in gathered-church order, often connected with elder/overseer function in chapter 3.

Robert Yarbrough's conservative exposition explicitly frames it as pastoral teaching/oversight rather than all forms of female communication or ministry.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/1-timothy-2-12-teach/

### Serious grammatical alternative

Philip Payne argues `οὐκ ... οὐδὲ ... ἀλλά` commonly joins the two verbal elements into one combined prohibited idea: teaching in combination with assuming authority over a man. This can narrow the prohibition rather than create two independent bans.

Source:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/1-tim-212-and-the-use-of-to-combine-two-elements-to-express-a-single-idea/088810B9D987D8797FF1CB11F71F7E7D

### `αὐθεντεῖν` lexical debate

The rare verb has generated a large semantic dispute: exercise authority, assume authority, dominate, usurp, etc. George Knight's classic study illustrates why the lexeme cannot be treated casually.

Source:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/aente-in-reference-to-women-in-1-timothy-212/7B50A199BC552A29B0A9D6EA3DF650BE

# 6. Local-Ephesus / false-teaching models

Serious egalitarian/contextual interpretations argue that:

- women were especially implicated in Ephesian false teaching;
- `αὐθεντεῖν` has negative/illicit-authority force;
- Genesis 2–3 corrects a local creation/Eve myth or false doctrine;
- prohibition is temporary/remedial, not sex-based permanent office rule.

The Kroeger line historically connected this to Artemis/proto-gnostic background. More recent contextual proposals refine rather than simply repeat that reconstruction.

### Conservative adversarial critique

The strongest conservative objection is not “Ephesus had no false teaching” — 1 Timothy plainly combats false teaching. The harder question is whether the text **limits the prohibition to false-teaching women** when v.12 itself does not contain a false-teaching modifier and vv.13–14 appeal to Adam/Eve.

G. K. Beale's recent response to Sandra Glahn argues that Artemis/local-background evidence does not establish temporary-only scope.

Source:
- https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/contextualizing-the-controversial-instructions-in-1-timothy-211-15-a-response-to-sandra-l-glahn-nobodys-mother/

### Calibration

`LOCAL_FALSE_TEACHING_CONTEXT = A/B contextual fact`  
`THEREFORE_v12_TEMPORARY_ONLY = C inference`  
`CREATION_ARGUMENT_HAS_NO_NORMATIVE_FORCE = weak unless separately demonstrated`.

# 7. Canonical reconciliation models

## C1 — Complementarian differentiated-speech model — current leading conservative synthesis

1. 11:5: women genuinely pray/prophesy.
2. 14:34–35: women/wives are restricted from a different speech function — likely authoritative evaluation/order-related speaking or disorderly questioning; exact subtype B/C.
3. 1 Tim 2:12: authoritative teaching/governing function over men in gathered church is restricted.
4. Prophecy ≠ elder/pastoral doctrinal teaching office.
5. Women's broad ministry/gifted speech is affirmed without equating every speech act with governing teaching authority.

### Strengths

- allows every text to speak positively rather than canceling another;
- fits diversity of speech functions in 1 Cor 12–14;
- takes Genesis rationale seriously in both 1 Cor 11 and 1 Tim 2;
- does not need interpolation to harmonize.

### Weak point

The exact definition of prohibited speech in 14:34–35 remains reconstructive; complementarian versions often overstate “judging prophecies” as though the words appeared in vv.34–35.

### Grade

`B leading canonical synthesis`, not A on every sub-detail.

## C2 — Egalitarian interpolation + contextual 1 Tim 2 model

1. 11:5 establishes female public prophecy.
2. 14:34–35 is secondary interpolation, removing contradiction.
3. 1 Tim 2 addresses local abusive/false teaching and not permanent sex-based office restriction.

### Strengths

- produces a very clean consistency model;
- draws on real textual data for 14:34–35 and real Ephesian false-teaching context.

### Pressures

- 14 interpolation has no extant omission witness and disputed sigla evidence;
- 1 Tim 2 creation/fall rationale must be localized without explicit “because these women are false teachers” wording;
- multiple independent disputed premises are required simultaneously.

### Grade

`C coherent but cumulatively assumption-heavy`.

## C3 — Authentic 14:34–35 + narrow local-disorder + narrow 1 Tim 2 abuse model

Keeps all transmitted text but narrows both restrictions to disorder/abusive authority.

### Strengths

- no textual excision;
- preserves female public gifts.

### Pressures

- requires negative modifiers/context to do substantial work not explicitly present in both passages;
- creation arguments remain a challenge for purely local application.

### Grade

`C serious model`.

## C4 — Broad silence / traditional office model

11:5 is either extraordinary prophecy or permitted non-authoritative charismatic speech; 14 and 1 Tim establish normative male public teaching/governance.

### Strengths

- long conservative reception;
- straightforwardly respects restrictions.

### Pressures

- must honor 11:5 as genuine, not pretend women never spoke in assembly;
- “broad silence” must still account for selective `σιγάω` in ch.14 and female prophetic speech.

### Grade

`B/C historic conservative family`, depending exact formulation.

# 8. Final boundary for the 1 Cor 11 project

The 1 Cor 11 research may safely say:

### A-level

- women pray/prophesy in 11:5;
- this activity is not identical by definition to pastoral teaching/elder office;
- 14:34–35 has genuine transposition evidence and no known extant complete omission witness;
- 1 Tim 2 explicitly contains teaching/authority language and creation/fall rationale.

### B-level

- a differentiated-functions reconciliation is the strongest conservative canonical synthesis;
- public assembly remains the leading context for 11:5;
- 1 Tim 2 is highly relevant to office/authoritative teaching in a way 11:5 alone is not.

### C/open

- exact restricted speech in 14:34–35;
- interpolation of 14:34–35;
- exact semantics/syntax of `αὐθεντεῖν` and `οὐδέ` in 1 Tim 2:12;
- precise boundary between prophecy, teaching, exhortation, and authoritative doctrinal judgment in first-century assemblies;
- modern office/application mapping.
