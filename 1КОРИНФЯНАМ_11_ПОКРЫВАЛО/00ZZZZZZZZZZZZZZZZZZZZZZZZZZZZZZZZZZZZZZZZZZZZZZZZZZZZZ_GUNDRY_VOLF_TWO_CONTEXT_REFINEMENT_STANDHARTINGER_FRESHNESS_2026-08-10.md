# 1. Gundry-Volf 1997 — not merely a hair-only source

Bibliographic control:

> Judith M. Gundry-Volf, “Gender and Creation in 1 Corinthians 11:2–16: A Study in Paul’s Theological Method,” in *Evangelium, Schriftauslegung, Kirche: Festschrift für Peter Stuhlmacher zum 65. Geburtstag* (1997), 151–171.

IxTheo/BILDI independently confirms author, year, volume and pp.151–171.

## 1.1 Material-practice proposal — hair/hairstyle

A page-specific quotation of Gundry-Volf's own footnote 1 on p.151 states that, in light of vv14–15 and the shame language of vv4–5, she considers it probably best to interpret the earlier covering language in terms of **hairstyles**, arguing that neither unveiled women nor men with a himation over the head carried the required first-century Roman social shame by themselves.

Thus:

```text
GUNDRY_VOLF_MATERIAL_PRACTICE = HAIR/HAIRSTYLE_PROPOSAL
GUNDRY_VOLF = NOT_SIMPLE_TEXTILE_VEIL_MODEL
```

This remains a published serious alternative, not the controlling project grade.

# 2. Her larger model — two social contexts

The most important correction is that Gundry-Volf's chapter is not primarily reducible to the object on the head.

A page-specific quotation from p.152 characterizes Paul's method through:

```text
CULTURE
CREATION
ESCHATOLOGICAL_LIFE_IN_CHRIST
```

as mutually interpretive loci.

She distinguishes:

```text
WIDER_CORINTHIAN_SOCIAL_CONTEXT
  = hierarchical honor/shame society

CULTIC_CONTEXT_OF_CHRISTIAN_WORSHIP
  = patriarchal framework partially disrupted / equality and mutuality "in the Lord"
```

Her thesis is that Paul can appeal to creation in both a hierarchical and an egalitarian direction because the two social locations create different theological pressures.

Therefore the correct source card is:

```text
GUNDRY_VOLF_MODEL =
  HAIRSTYLE
  + HONOR/SHAME_SOCIAL_RESPECTABILITY
  + TWO_SOCIAL_CONTEXTS
  + CULTURE/CREATION/ESCHATOLOGICAL_LIFE_INTERPLAY
```

not simply:

```text
GUNDRY_VOLF = HAIR_ONLY
```

# 4. Relation to the project hair/veil map

The source refinement does **not** itself strengthen `HAIR_ONLY` to leading.

Why:

- Gundry-Volf's historical premise about social shame must still be checked against the much larger Roman visual/epigraphic controls now in this repository;
- Drake 2025, Hughes 2007, Olson 2008, Stafford 2024, Andania/Lycosura and local Corinthian evidence all show that head/hair signals are context-sensitive and not reducible to one uniform Roman rule;
- Gundry-Volf's two-context theological method can remain insightful even if her precise material reconstruction is not accepted.

# 5. Important convergence with Drake 2025 without identity of conclusions

```text
GUNDRY_VOLF -> probably hairstyle/hair
```


# 7. What Standhartinger's article actually does

The direct available article is primarily a **history of feminist Pauline interpretation**, using 1 Cor 11:2–16 as a guiding test case.

It explicitly says that it will not solve all of the passage's exegetical questions and surveys competing reconstructions, including veil, hairstyle, Genesis, angelic and feminist-rhetorical approaches.

It is therefore useful for:

```text
HISTORY_OF_INTERPRETATION
SOURCE_GENEALOGY
MODEL_OWNERSHIP
```

and should not be mislabeled as:

```text
NEW_2025_SPECIALIST_BREAKTHROUGH
```

---

# 8. Source-genealogy value

Standhartinger is particularly useful because she distinguishes earlier feminist models instead of collapsing them:

- Wire's reconstruction of Corinthian women prophets;
- Gielen's hair/gender-symbolism work;
- Gundry-Volf's more traditional theological method;
- broader feminist criticism of Pauline hierarchy and attempts to identify internal counter-movements.

Future agents should use this article to **map genealogy**, not to substitute Standhartinger's survey for the primary works she discusses.
