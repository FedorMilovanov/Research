# 2. Direct author-level statement of novelty

On the official dissertation-defense page, Nõmmik personally explains what he considers new in the thesis.

In paraphrase, he says:

1. the thesis is, to his knowledge, the first comprehensive scholarly interpretation of 1 Cor 11:2–16 whose **central argument** is that Paul is addressing the Roman ritual `capite velato`;
2. it gives what he regards as the most extensive description of that ritual yet offered in biblical scholarship;
3. it attempts a new solution to the major problem facing the capite-velato hypothesis: **why Paul asks men to stop performing capite velato but does not give the same instruction to women**.

This is direct author-level evidence for the thesis architecture, not a reviewer’s inference.

# 3. Direct author-level model of the Corinthian interlocutors

The same EHS page asks how his interpretation changes the reading of 1 Corinthians.

Nõmmik says his model lets the reader hear the **voice of Paul’s Corinthian interlocutors**. He contrasts the common picture of the passage as unilateral apostolic instruction with a reconstruction in which Paul is handling strong counterarguments and new practices developed by the Corinthians in response to prior Pauline teaching.

He emphasizes that the addressees were not passive recipients; they actively reasoned about what they had learned and developed solutions for implementing teaching in a multicultural community.

This is a crucial whole-model commitment:

```text
NOMMIK_CORINTHIANS_ACTIVE_REASONING_INTERLOCUTORS = A_AUTHOR_POSITION
NOMMIK_PASSAGE_AS_RESPONSE_TO_COUNTERARGUMENTS = A_AUTHOR_POSITION
```

Project truth grade remains reconstruction-level because Paul does not preserve a transcript of the Corinthians’ prior reasoning.

# 4. Publisher-level reconstruction

Wipf & Stock supplies the model in more detail:

- Roman `capite velato` = covering the head with a garment in prayer/sacrifice/divination;
- Paul and Corinthian interlocutors are said to have known this ritual gesture;
- the conflict concerns correct procedure and effectiveness of prayer/access to divine knowledge;
- some Corinthians are reconstructed as seeking ritual uniformity;
- they therefore generalize uncovered ritual appearance to all members, including married women of multiple cultural backgrounds;
- Paul differentiates male and female ritual propriety, permitting women to retain head-coverings/hair accessories during prayer/prophecy.

This is the current direct description of the theory.

# 7. Where Nõmmik could outperform older models

A successful Nõmmik model would explain with one historical conflict:

1. why the male instruction is present at all;
2. why female instruction differs;
3. why prayer/prophecy are the relevant acts;
4. why Roman ritual context matters;
5. why Paul must defend sex-differentiated head-state;
6. why a multicultural congregation might debate a uniform rule.

This gives it more explanatory scope than reconstructions limited to:

- prostitutes;
- adulteresses;
- rebellious wives alone;
- hairstyles alone;
- elite men alone.

That is why it must receive a real stress test rather than a dismissive footnote.

# 8. Where the model is most vulnerable

The adversarial audit must attack its **distinctive links**, not merely the known Roman background.

Highest-risk links:

```text
A. capite_velato_known -> v4_exactly_capite_velato
B. Paul_prohibited_male_covered_prayer -> Corinthians_generalized_rule
C. Corinthians_generalized_rule -> married_women_pressured_to_uncover
D. prophecy/divine_knowledge -> ritual_efficacy_conflict
E. hidden_uniformity_dispute -> explains_creation/exousia/angels/physis/v16
```

A–E are not all equivalent. The first begins from strong primary evidence; B–D are increasingly reconstructive.

If the model cannot show why vv.7–16 follow naturally from the reconstructed uniformity dispute, it risks explaining vv.4–6 while merely accommodating the rest.
