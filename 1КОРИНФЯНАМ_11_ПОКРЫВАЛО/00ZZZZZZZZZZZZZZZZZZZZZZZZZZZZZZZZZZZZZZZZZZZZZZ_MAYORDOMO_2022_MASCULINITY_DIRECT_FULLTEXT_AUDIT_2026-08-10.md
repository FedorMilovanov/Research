# 1. Major methodological contribution — the man is not a disposable contrast

# 2. `κεφαλή` — source/origin can coexist with hierarchical honor-relations

Mayordomo gives a useful intervention into the polarized lexical debate.

He accepts that the metaphor need not primarily be lexical “ruler/leader” and can plausibly evoke source/origin/priority:

```text
Christ -> origin/source of man
man -> origin/source of woman in Genesis 2
God -> origin/source of Christ
```

But he argues that lexical source/origin does **not** make the discourse non-hierarchical. In vv4–5 the head-relation is activated as a relation of public honor/shame:

- male Christian conduct affects Christ’s honor;
- female Christian conduct affects the man’s honor;
- men themselves are not at the top, because Christ is their higher `head`, ultimately referring to God.

Thus:

```text
MAYORDOMO_KEPHALE_SOURCE/ORIGIN_POSSIBLE = DIRECT_BODY
MAYORDOMO_DISCOURSE_HIERARCHICAL_HONOR_FRAME = DIRECT_BODY
SOURCE_READING != NONHIERARCHICAL_DISCOURSE_AUTOMATICALLY
```

This independently supports the project’s refusal to equate:

```text
KEPHALE_SOURCE_ONLY -> NO_ORDER/ASYMMETRY
```

Current project calibration remains:

```text
KEPHALE_RELATIONAL_HEADSHIP_PROMINENCE_REPRESENTATION_11_3 = B_LEADING
KEPHALE_EXACT_NARROW_AUTHORITY_OVER_11_3 = B_C
KEPHALE_SOURCE_ONLY = C_VIABLE
CREATION_ORDER/ASYMMETRY = B_HIGH
```

Mayordomo’s argument is a contextual synthesis, not lexical proof that authority is the dictionary meaning.

# 4. Mayordomo’s own hair-model — real B1 alternative, not project promotion

Mayordomo ultimately orients his subsequent reading toward the hair/hairstyle family.

His reasons include:

- explicit `κομάω / κόμη` and `κείρασθαι` language later in the argument;
- absence of the noun `κάλυμμα` in 1 Cor 11 despite Paul using it repeatedly in 2 Cor 3;
- difficulty explaining why Paul ends with male long hair if v4 is entirely unrelated textile headwear;
- Thompson/Hughes-type visual evidence against assuming a universal local female veil practice.

He reads `κατὰ κεφαλῆς ἔχων` as allowing “something hanging down from the head,” i.e. long hair, and treats v14 as the clearer expression of the male problem.

Source status:

```text
MAYORDOMO_2022_HAIR_MODEL = DIRECT_BODY_B1_ALTERNATIVE
```

Project adversarial response:

- Massey’s lexical case for textile covering remains substantial;
- early material-covering reception remains strong;
- hair and headwear can be distinct regulatory axes in Andania/Lycosura;
- absence of a noun `κάλυμμα` does not make covering verbs cease to denote covering;
- local portraiture is not a behavioral census.

Therefore:

```text
HAIR_ONLY = C_SERIOUS_ALTERNATIVE // unchanged
MATERIAL_COVERING = B_HIGH_LEADING // unchanged
```

Mayordomo adds scholarly weight and a strong masculinity lens, not a grade reversal.

# 5. His criticism of the Roman ritual trigger must be separated from the existence of Roman `capite velato`

Mayordomo acknowledges that male Roman head-covering during prayer is abundantly attested, then asks why Paul would call such an ordinary ritual gesture shameful and notes the tension with Moses’ covering in 2 Cor 3.

This is a legitimate **fit objection** to identifying v4 exactly with Roman `capite velato`.

It does not challenge the historical existence of the Roman practice.

Therefore:

```text
ROMAN_CAPITE_VELATO_BACKGROUND = A
MAYORDOMO_V4_EXACT_ROMAN_TRIGGER_PRESSURE = REAL_B1_OBJECTION
V4_EXACT_CAPITE_VELATO = B_C // retained
```

This distinction prevents turning a model-fit objection into denial of the archaeology/literary background.

# 6. Roman/Jewish masculinity corpus — direct primary controls behind Mayordomo

Mayordomo assembles several ancient texts where hair, depilation and bodily presentation signal masculine/feminine coding. Some are independently primary-verifiable.

## Martial, *Epigrams* 10.65

Direct PHI Latin:

- https://latin.packhum.org/loc/1294/2/931/409-412

Martial addresses Charmenion, who boasts Corinthian citizenship. The satire contrasts:

- Charmenion’s carefully curled/styled hair;
- daily depilation/smoothness;
- lisping/weak speech;

with the speaker’s rough Spanish hairiness, ending in the feminizing threat to call Charmenion `soror` (“sister”).

```text
MARTIAL_CORINTHIAN_MASCULINITY_FEMINIZATION_SATIRE = A_PRIMARY_LITERARY
```

## Juvenal, *Satire* 8

Primary/legacy text route:

- https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/juvenal_satires_08.htm

Juvenal juxtaposes the “unwarlike Rhodian” and “scented Corinthian” and mocks smooth/depilated youths/legs.

## Epictetus, *Discourses* 3.1

Primary classical-text routes:

- https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0236%3Atext%3Ddisc%3Abook%3D3

# 7. `φύσις` — Mayordomo supports a convention/naturalization model but not pure arbitrariness

Mayordomo rejects a biological account of hair growth and reads Paul’s “nature” language through popular philosophical/normative reasoning in which social convention is presented/naturalized as fitting/proper.

He invokes Demosthenes, Cicero and especially ancient discourse connecting bodily/gender presentation to what is culturally perceived as natural.

This overlaps with but is not identical to the project’s broader primary-corpus synthesis.

Project remains:

```text
PHYSIS_BIOLOGY_ONLY = REJECTED
PHYSIS_MERE_ARBITRARY_CUSTOM = REJECTED
PHYSIS_SEXED_NATURALIZED_PROPRIETY = B_HIGH_LEADING
```

Mayordomo is compatible with the “naturalized propriety” half but places more weight on social construction than some other models.

# 8. vv11–12 and v10 — Mayordomo identifies internal destabilization, not simple cancellation

Mayordomo treats `πλὴν` in v11 as a limiting/corrective move. He emphasizes multiple origin narratives:

- woman from man;
- man through woman;
- all things from God.

He argues that these origins cannot simply be correlated into one unidirectional norm.

He then reads v10 actively: woman has `ἐξουσία` over her head, and v13 appeals to the community’s own judgment.

Source-specific:

```text
MAYORDOMO_V11_12_DESTABILIZE_UNILATERAL_ORDER = DIRECT_BODY
MAYORDOMO_V10_ACTIVE_WOMAN_AUTHORITY = DIRECT_BODY
```

Mayordomo goes further and calls the old passive interpretation philologically impossible.

The project should **not** simply adopt that strongest wording, because:

- woman’s grammatical/semantic agency is strong;
- but a contextual/metonymic authority-related sign interpretation can still be argued with extra semantic steps;
- ancient interpreters themselves developed sign/covering readings.

Therefore project calibration remains:

```text
EXOUSIA_WOMAN_SUBJECT = A_SYNTAX
EXOUSIA_ACTIVE_WOMAN_BEARER_PULL = B_HIGH
```

# 9. Mayordomo’s final model

His conclusion combines:

```text
external/public conformity to hegemonic gender performance
+
internal Christian interdependence and responsible moral subjecthood
```

He reads the passage as exposing a tension rather than perfectly resolving it.

This is useful as a modern synthesis but remains B1 interpretation.

It converges in part with:

- Gundry-Volf’s differentiation + mutuality pressure;
- Li Hao’s cultural adaptation + countercultural reciprocity;
- Newberry’s vv11–12 counterbalance;

while differing sharply on hair vs textile covering.
