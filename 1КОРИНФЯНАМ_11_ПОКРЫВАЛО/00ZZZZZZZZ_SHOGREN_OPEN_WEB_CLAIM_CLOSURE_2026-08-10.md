## Shogren's directly stated position

From his author article, the following position can be summarized safely:

1. In church worship **both men and women may pray aloud and prophesy**.
2. Men should do so bareheaded; women in the Corinthian/Roman setting should retain the veil/shawl they already wore publicly.
3. He treats the veil as a **real material veil/shawl**, not hair-only.
4. He connects the practice with:
   - created order / Genesis;
   - what Paul calls “natural”;
   - cultural shame/social signals.
5. His proposed Corinthian problem is that some women wanted to remove the veil, potentially signaling independence from men/husbands, rejection of cultural mores, and erasure of gender distinction.
6. He explicitly counterbalances this with men not lording over women and with male dependence on woman / Christ.
7. For modern application he does **not** require identical veil practice in cultures where the veil lacks the same social meaning; he argues Christians must preserve gospel-honoring social/gender signals appropriate to their culture.

## Historical caution

Shogren's article says respectable married/widowed Roman women commonly signaled status/fidelity through covered/bound hair and describes uncovered/unbound hair as socially “loose”. This is useful as his reconstruction, but current project controls from Gill/Callon require us **not to universalize** one dress form across every woman/status/context.
