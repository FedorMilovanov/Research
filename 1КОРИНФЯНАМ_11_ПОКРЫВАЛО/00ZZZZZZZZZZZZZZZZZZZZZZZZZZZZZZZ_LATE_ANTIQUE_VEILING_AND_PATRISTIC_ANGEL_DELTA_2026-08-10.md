Publication date: 2023-12-15.

Official route:

- https://czasopisma.kul.pl/index.php/vp/article/view/15533

License on the journal page: CC BY-ND 4.0.

## 1.2 Direct abstract-level findings

The official abstract states that patristic interpretations of the Pauline `angels` included a broad range:

- celestial/heavenly beings;
- fallen angels, with pre-Nicene links to Gen 6:1–4;
- celebrants in the liturgy;
- participants in the liturgy;
- strangers/outsiders observing the rites.

It also notes a later shift as ecclesial doctrine, social reasons and liturgical practice consolidated, and veiling became increasingly associated with positive images of women and eventually with consecrated virginity (`velatio`).

# 2. Grace Stafford 2024 — visual corpus against normative-text overreading

## 2.1 Direct Oxford control

Official Oxford Academic page verifies:

> Grace Stafford, “Veiling and Head-Covering in Late Antiquity: Between Ideology, Aesthetics and Practicality,” *Past & Present* 263.1 (May 2024): 3–46. DOI `10.1093/pastj/gtad017`.

Published 11 January 2024. Open Access, CC BY 4.0.

Route:

- https://academic.oup.com/past/article/263/1/3/7516952

## 2.2 Why it matters methodologically

Stafford explicitly distinguishes:

- churchmen’s normative insistence that modest/pious women veil;
- the much more diverse visual/material record;
- situational factors such as status, practicality, fashion and social context.

Her argument is particularly valuable for this project because 1 Cor 11 scholarship repeatedly risks treating:

```text
PRESCRIPTIVE_TEXT -> UNIVERSAL_REAL_WORLD_BEHAVIOR
```

as automatic.

Stafford’s late-antique corpus demonstrates why that inference is unsafe even in periods where Christian writers speak loudly and repeatedly about veiling.

## 2.3 Quantitative late-antique portrait control

For 150 female portrait sculptures from the late third through sixth centuries in the Last Statues of Antiquity database, Stafford reports:

```text
TIGHT_FITTING_COVER = 16 / 10.7%
LOOSE_VEIL = 17 / 11.3%
BOTH = 2 / 1.3%
TOTAL_WITH_COVER = 35 / 23.3%
NO_COVER = 110 / 73.3%
UNCLEAR = 5 / 3.3%
```

She further states that most fourth-century representations in the visual examples considered show women without head-coverings, including many explicitly Christian visual contexts.

## 2.4 Critical limitation

These numbers are **not** a first-century Corinth dataset and not a census of everyday women.

```text
STAFFORD_LATE_ANTIQUE_PERCENTAGES != 1CORINTH_C50_CE_DRESS_FREQUENCIES

```

The valid use is methodological:

# 3. Late-antique reception is development, not a frozen photograph of Paul

Stafford explicitly notes that early Christian writers took up 1 Cor 11 and extended its veiling logic beyond prayer. Paczkowski likewise traces a later consolidation of ecclesial/social/liturgical meanings around veiling and consecrated virginity.

Therefore a future reception-history chapter should distinguish:

```text
PAULINE_LITURGICAL_TEXT
-> EARLY_EXEGETICAL_ARGUMENT
-> EVERYDAY_MODESTY_EXTENSION
-> VIRGINAL/CONSECRATORY_VELATIO
```

These are related historical layers, not automatically identical practices or meanings.

This protects against a common circular reconstruction:

```text
4TH_CENTURY_CHRISTIAN_NORM
-> ASSUME_1ST_CENTURY_CORINTH_SAME_SOCIAL_RULE
-> USE_ASSUMPTION_TO_EXPLAIN_PAUL
```

That reasoning is blocked unless independently supported by first-century evidence.
