# 16. Why textual fit remains low

1. no explicit source/quotative frame at the proposed boundary;
2. advocates disagree over the span;
3. proposed block is much longer/more explanatory than the safest unmarked slogan candidates;
4. `γάρ / καὶ γάρ -> διὰ τοῦτο` strongly supports local continuity;
5. `πλὴν` need not mark a new speaker;

# 17. Publication-safe formulation

> A substantial Corinthian-quotation reading is a genuine published minority model, represented in different forms from Shoemaker to recent linguistic and ideological studies. Paul can quote or reuse Corinthian language, and proposed unmarked slogans elsewhere in the letter are real scholarly questions. However, the clearest attributed speech is explicitly framed, classic unmarked slogan candidates are short and boundary-sensitive, no close same-letter analogue was verified for a multi-verse hostile block with the same internal connective structure, advocates disagree over the quotation span, and Tertullian, Chrysostom and Ambrosiaster all read the relevant verses continuously as Paul’s argument. The model therefore belongs in a serious status quaestionis but remains low in textual fit.
