## 0. Question

The modern large quotation/refutation family assigns substantial parts of 1 Cor 11:3–10 or 11:4–9 to a Corinthian voice. This file asks a narrow historical question:

> Do early extended readers of 1 Cor 11 preserve any awareness that Paul was quoting/refuting a different speaker across vv3–10/vv4–9?

This is **reception evidence**, not direct proof of Paul’s original discourse design.

```text
EARLY_RECEPTION != ORIGINAL_AUTHORIAL_INTENT
ABSENCE_OF_EARLY_QUOTATION_READING != QUOTATION_IMPOSSIBLE
BUT
EARLY_CONTINUOUS_READING = REAL_HISTORICAL_CONTROL
```

# 1. Tertullian — c. 200: the dispute is scope, not speaker ownership

## 1.1 *De oratione* 21–22

Tertullian directly discusses 1 Cor 11:4–15 while debating whether virgins are included under Paul’s term `woman`.

Primary translation route:

- New Advent, *On Prayer*, chs.21–22: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0322.htm

His argumentative structure is unmistakable:

- opponents say the apostle named “women” but not “virgins”;
- Tertullian answers from the meaning/scope of `woman`;
- he cites `every woman praying and prophesying...`;
- he cites the male parallel `every man`;
- he cites `because of the angels`;
- he cites `nature herself` and the hair/shaving logic.

Tertullian therefore treats vv4–15 as **Paul’s own apostolic prescription/reasoning**, while disputing the class of females to whom it applies.

```text
TERTULLIAN_C200_1COR11_CONTINUOUS_PAULINE_OWNERSHIP = DIRECT_EARLY_RECEPTION
TERTULLIAN_SCOPE_DISPUTE = VIRGINS_INCLUDED_OR_NOT
TERTULLIAN_HIDDEN_CORINTHIAN_SPEAKER = NOT_ATTESTED
```

This is especially useful because Tertullian is much earlier than Chrysostom and is already aware of interpretive disputes around the passage.

## 1.2 *De virginibus velandis* ch.4 and following

Primary translation route:

- https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0403.htm
- Latin: https://www.tertullian.org/latin/de_virginibus_velandis.htm

Again Tertullian frames the question as what “the apostle” prescribed about veiling and whether the absence of the noun `virgins` exempts them.

He does not preserve a tradition in which vv4–9 or vv3–10 are Corinthian words rejected by Paul.

Safe result:

```text
EARLIEST_EXTENDED_RECEPTION_CHECKED = CONTINUOUS_PAULINE_READING
```

Do not turn this into the stronger claim that no second-century Christian ever held a quotation reading; the surviving corpus is incomplete.

# 2. John Chrysostom — Homily 26: continuous discourse, explicit Corinth reconstruction, local `διὰ τοῦτο` backlink

Primary translation:

- John Chrysostom, Homily 26 on 1 Corinthians: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/220126.htm

## 2.1 vv2–3 — continuous Pauline explanation

Chrysostom says Paul moves from the idol-food discussion to “another accusation,” begins with praise for those who kept traditions, and then gives the reason for the disputed practice at v3.

He explicitly presents v3 as Paul’s explanation:

```text
v2 tradition frame
-> v3 reason/cause
-> practical head rules
```

There is no speaker change.

## 2.2 Chrysostom’s own reconstruction of the triggering practices

Homily 26 directly reconstructs the Corinthian situation in a much more concrete way than Paul’s surviving letter itself does.

Chrysostom says in substance that:

- women prayed and prophesied unveiled / bare-headed;
- men wore long hair;
- men covered their heads when praying and prophesying;
- he calls the male long-hair / head-covering practices “Grecian custom(s)”;
- Paul had already admonished them orally while present;
- some obeyed and others disobeyed.

Direct New Advent locator:

- Homily 26, section 2, immediately before the exposition of 1 Cor 11:3.

Safe status:

```text
CHRYSOSTOM_WOMEN_UNCOVERED_AT_PRAYER_PROPHECY = DIRECT_RECEPTION_RECONSTRUCTION
CHRYSOSTOM_MEN_LONG_HAIRED = DIRECT_RECEPTION_RECONSTRUCTION
CHRYSOSTOM_MEN_COVERED_WHILE_PRAYING_PROPHECYING = DIRECT_RECEPTION_RECONSTRUCTION
CHRYSOSTOM_MALE_PRACTICES_GREEK_CUSTOM = DIRECT_RECEPTION_RECONSTRUCTION
CHRYSOSTOM_PRIOR_ORAL_ADMONITION_SOME_OBEY_SOME_DISOBEY = DIRECT_RECEPTION_RECONSTRUCTION
```

Crucial evidence boundary:

```text
CHRYSOSTOM_RECONSTRUCTION != FIRST_CENTURY_CORINTH_FACT
```

Chrysostom is a major ancient interpreter, but his statement is a late-fourth-century reconstruction of the hidden event behind Paul’s letter, not independent contemporary archaeological or documentary evidence.

This matters because later modern interpreters can inherit a concrete ancient reconstruction and accidentally present it as though Paul himself stated all of its details.

## 2.4 vv4–10 — local discourse continuity

Chrysostom proceeds continuously through:

- v4–5 male/female prayer and prophecy;
- v6 covering/shaving;
- v7 image/glory;
- v8–9 creation arguments;
- v10 authority/angels.

Most importantly for the modern Costa boundary, at v10 Chrysostom asks what `for this cause` refers to and answers that it is **because of all the things just mentioned**, then adds the angels.

Thus Chrysostom independently witnesses the ancient discourse perception:

```text
VV7_9 -> DIA_TOUTO_V10
```

rather than:

```text
V3 -> hidden quotation vv4-9 -> DIA_TOUTO back to v3
```

This is not a grammatical proof, but it is a strong early reception control for the natural local backlink already identified by the modern verse audit.

```text
CHRYSOSTOM_DIA_TOUTO_LOCAL_BACKLINK = DIRECT_EARLY_RECEPTION
COSTA_LONG_DISTANCE_BACKLINK = NO_EARLY_SUPPORT_FOUND_HERE
```

## 2.5 vv15–16

Chrysostom continues to treat the hair analogy and v16 closure as Paul’s reasoning and rebuke. He interprets v16 as rejecting contentious opposition to what Paul has just taught.

Again, no hidden multi-speaker boundary is perceived.

# 3. Ambrosiaster — late fourth century: vv3–12 treated as one continuous Pauline chain

## 3.1 Primary Latin text

Ambrosiaster, *Commentaria in Epistolam ad Corinthios Primam*.

Latin text route:

- Wikisource: https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Commentaria_in_Epistolam_ad_Corinthios_Primam_(Ambrosiaster)

Modern full English translation exists in Gerald Bray’s Ancient Christian Texts volume through IVP Academic; publication metadata route:

- https://www.ivpress.com/commentaries-on-romans-and-1-2-corinthians

## 3.2 Continuous sequence

Ambrosiaster comments:

```text
v3 head relations
v4 covered praying man
vv5-7 uncovered woman / man image and glory
vv8-10 woman from man / for man / therefore authority because of angels
v11 nevertheless mutual relation
v12 woman from man, man through woman, all from God
```

The Latin text groups vv8–10 in one continuous lemma:

```text
(Vers. 8, 9, 10.) ... Propterea debet mulier potestatem habere supra caput etiam propter angelos.
```

He then moves directly to v11 and v12.

```text
AMBROSIASTER_VV8_10_CONTINUOUS = DIRECT_LATIN_RECEPTION
AMBROSIASTER_DIA_TOUTO/PROPTEREA_CONTINUITY = DIRECT_RECEPTION

```

## 3.3 Important angel correction

Ambrosiaster genuinely says `angelos episcopos dicit` — he interprets the angels as bishops, appealing to Revelation.

This is important because a previous AI dump falsely attributed a similar “priests and bishops” reading to **Chrysostom**.

Correct attribution:

```text

CHRYSOSTOM_ANGELS_AS_PRIESTS_BISHOPS_QUOTE = FALSE_ATTRIBUTION
```

This patristic control therefore both strengthens source hygiene and preserves a real minority historical interpretation.

# 5. Separate ancient reconstruction from first-century evidence

The new Chrysostom/PG118 cross-control makes an additional source-hygiene rule necessary.

An ancient commentator may preserve an **early interpretation of the hidden event** without preserving independent access to the actual first-century event.

Therefore:

```text
ANCIENT_RECONSTRUCTION = RECEPTION_EVIDENCE
ANCIENT_RECONSTRUCTION != CONTEMPORARY_EVENT_EVIDENCE
ANCIENT_RECONSTRUCTION_REPEATED_IN_LATER_CATENA != INDEPENDENT_MULTIPLE_ATTESTATION_OF_EVENT
```

This applies especially to claims such as:

```text
CORINTHIAN_WOMEN_WERE_PRAYING_BAREHEADED = NOT_PROVED_BY_CHRYSOSTOM_ALONE
CORINTHIAN_MEN_WORE_LONG_HAIR = NOT_PROVED_BY_CHRYSOSTOM_ALONE
CORINTHIAN_MEN_COPIED_GREEK_CUSTOMS = NOT_PROVED_BY_CHRYSOSTOM_ALONE
PAUL_HAD_PREVIOUSLY_ISSUED_EXACT_HEAD_RULES_ORALLY = CHRYSOSTOM_RECONSTRUCTION
```

The project may cite these statements as **what Chrysostom understood the situation to be**, not as direct A-grade history.
