## 3. Models

### R1 — differentiated-functions complementarian

- 11:5 = genuine public female prayer/prophecy;
- 14:34–35 authentic; restriction concerns a different assembly function (authoritative evaluation/order-related speech and/or disorderly questioning);
- 1 Tim 2 restricts authoritative doctrinal teaching/governance over men;
- elder/overseer link established from broader Pastoral context, not inserted lexically into 1 Tim 2:12.

### R2 — broad traditional restriction with charismatic exception

- 11:5 = extraordinary/charismatic prophecy permitted;
- normal public teaching/governing speech restricted by 14 and 1 Tim;
- stronger continuity with classic Protestant reception.

### R3 — interpolation + local 1 Tim restriction

- 14:34–35 secondary gloss;
- 11:5 stands without conflict;
- 1 Tim 2 temporary/local false-teaching or abusive-authority restriction.

### R4 — authentic 14, disorder-only + local abuse in 1 Tim

- all texts authentic;
- 14 restriction only disruptive questioning;
- 1 Tim restriction only abusive/false teaching.

### R5 — quotation/refutation in 14 + broad female ministry

- 14:34–35 Corinthian quotation rejected by v36;
- 11:5 is normal Pauline practice;
- 1 Tim read contextually/local or disputed authorship in critical scholarship.

## 5. Result

The leading conservative synthesis is **R1**, but only in calibrated form:

- it cannot claim that `λαλεῖν` lexically means “judge prophecies”;
- it must account for v35 questioning;
- it cannot use 1 Tim 2:12 as though Greek literally says “elder/pastor”;
- it must retain female prophecy as genuine speech/ministry rather than hypothetical permission.

R2 has strong historical continuity but becomes brittle if “silence” is made absolute.

R3 is a coherent modern egalitarian package, but its cumulative case depends on two disputed moves simultaneously: interpolation of 14:34–35 and temporary/local narrowing of 1 Tim 2 despite creation rationale.

R4 avoids textual excision but places heavy weight on reconstructed disruptive/abusive context.

R5 is possible as history of interpretation but has no explicit quotation marker and therefore remains lower-weight.
