For ten years my work was text and meaning: editing, research, teaching. Since early 2026 I do the same thing with a team of AI agents — I design the architecture, write machine-checkable contracts, brief the agents, accept the work against evidence, and ship it.

In ten months that produced five live products on my own domains: a theological library with in-browser text-to-speech, a React 19 poetry platform with its own Cloudflare Worker and D1 backend, a Russian-language pastry school with 157 sourced articles, a zero-dependency commercial site with 14 geo landings, and a Telegram bot with a Mini App. Behind them sit two internal platforms — a safety-first multi-channel publishing pipeline and an evidence-based multi-agent audit system.

A significant share of the code was generated by agents under my direction. Requirements, architecture, editorial decisions, acceptance and accountability are mine — which is exactly why the verification layer is the interesting part.

## 4. Selected work

### 4.1 Theological research library — [gospod-bog.ru](https://gospod-bog.ru)

A public library built on years of exegetical research: 111 pages, 57 article routes, twelve series, ten interactive biblical atlases, genealogy, glossary, Pagefind search, an offline reader, and in-browser Russian text-to-speech with correct stress marks.

**The engineering story.** Three truths never conflated: source, build, production. Route ownership comes from machine registries rather than hand-written lists; `document-authority.json` decides which document may override anything at all; a live `release.json` pins the exact commit SHA, and a CI gate verifies that SHAs quoted in documentation match real bytes.

**Scale.** 3,308 files · ≈77k LOC · 77 workflows · 40,000 CI runs.

### 4.2 Poetry platform — [thelegendarypoet.ru](https://thelegendarypoet.ru)

React 19, TypeScript, Vite 7, Tailwind 4, Framer Motion: twelve poets, long-form documented essays, music, and a community layer implemented as a Cloudflare Worker with D1 and Turnstile — HMAC-signed requests, moderation, bot protection — deliberately separate from the static site so server secrets never reach the bundle.

**The engineering story.** `route-contract.json` is the single machine manifest for routes, redirects, sitemap, QA and budgets; `routeModules.ts` is its lazy runtime. Prerender and SEO gates run on every deploy.

**Scale.** ≈49k LOC · 23 workflows · 27,176 CI runs.

### 4.3 AI media pipeline — `mp3telegrambot`

Link in, Russian media package out: MP3 with cover art, topic, timecodes, quotes, summary, Shorts — plus translation through Yandex LiveDub with semantic QA and automatic repair of confirmed bad segments.

**The engineering story.** Fail-closed startup. If the local Bot API is unreachable, the process dies before it starts downloading a feature-length video — no silent cloud fallback that quietly changes the output. Hash-locked dependencies, secret scanning in CI.

**Scale.** 936 modules · 208,644 LOC of Python · 579 test files · 20 workflows.

### 4.4 Multi-channel publishing platform — `video-channel-manager`

Read-only audit snapshots, versioned change plans, exact remote IDs with no guessing, per-target single-writer locks, operation journals, backups, postcondition reads after every write, guarded rollback, SHA-256 media fingerprints.

**The engineering story.** The AI analyses; a deterministic executor acts. Agents never receive provider tokens. Ambiguous provider states are marked `never_replay` and require an exact read-only reconciliation before anything is retried.

**Scale.** 919 modules · 219,040 LOC of Python · 461 tests · 31 workflows.

### 4.5 Commercial site, zero dependencies — [milovicake.ru](https://milovicake.ru)

Premium site for a private pastry chef: catalogue, price calculator, delivery, reviews, PWA, video gallery, and 14 geo landings generated from a single CSV with an idempotency check.

**The engineering story.** QA is the product. `npm run qa` runs JS validation, accessibility, release-identity by SHA, Lighthouse regressions and aggregation, a security audit, suburb idempotency, and a Playwright desktop/mobile/responsive matrix. Separate contracts prove the live 404 is a real 404 and that prices, opening hours and contacts match reality.

**Scale.** 34 pages · ≈48k LOC · 111 PRs · 3,590 CI runs.

### 4.6 Editorial library, 157 sourced articles — [french.milovicake.ru](https://french.milovicake.ru)

An Astro SSG library on the French pastry tradition: chefs, techniques, recipes, market analysis, and a curated fifteen-piece collection with its own entity model and documentation timeline.

**The engineering story.** Fail-closed SEO. The build fails if titles collide, canonicals are wrong, JSON-LD disagrees with visible content, or a generated illustration lacks honest provenance. IndexNow fires only after a verified deploy.

**Scale.** 157 articles · ≈146,000 words · 7 workflows.

### 4.7 Research infrastructure — `AuditRepo` and `Research`

Two systems that exist because agents generate reports faster than humans can read them. AuditRepo: many audit passes → evidence → verification → dedup and root-cause → compact working MASTER → decision → verify the fix → retire what is solved. Research: an evidence model with source classes A1–D, independent axes for access, locator, rights and publication, and typed HOLD flags. Exactly one root-authority document per repository; authority is never selected by a date in a filename; validators are read-only.

**Scale.** 2,786 files and 120 MB of evidence · 2,076 documents and ≈3.9M words.

### 4.8 Telegram learning app — [@milovanovaibot](https://t.me/milovanovaibot)

A course and Mini App for studying 1 Peter: chapters, historical context, Greek, disputed readings, PvP battles, achievements, leaderboards. Flask and Waitress behind a signed webhook, MongoDB Atlas as durable state, separate liveness and readiness endpoints.

**Scale.** 583 modules · 89,144 LOC · 305 tests.

## 6. How I work

1. **Contract before code.** A machine-readable source of truth first — routes, documents, rights, schemas — then an implementation that must conform.
2. **Fail closed.** An unsupported claim gets removed or narrowed. A build that cannot prove itself does not ship.
3. **Separate truths.** Source ≠ build ≠ production. `merge ≠ deploy`. Green CI on an old head proves nothing about the new one.
4. **Postconditions over hope.** Read back after every write. Verify live bytes after every deploy.
5. **One owner per surface.** One branch, one PR, one accountable person. Never two agents on one file.
6. **Retire solved work.** Otherwise the backlog becomes a graveyard and the agents optimise for volume.
7. **Humans own the editorial line.** Agents do not silently fix facts, interpretations or ambiguous punctuation.

## 8. Honest FAQ

**"So an AI wrote this?"**
A large share of the code, yes — generated by agents under my direction. Mine are the requirements, architecture, contracts, editorial decisions, acceptance and accountability. That is precisely why I built a verification methodology: nobody ships at this volume without one.

**"Are you a senior software engineer?"**
No, and I do not claim to be one inside a large team. I am a product engineer and technical editor who builds end-to-end with agents, reads code, debugs production, and owns the outcome.

**"Why so many commits?"**
Because work happens in small verifiable steps under CI, not in one "final" commit. The number itself means nothing; five products running on real domains does.

**"Why is everything in Russian?"**
That is where the subject matter lies: Russian poetry, Russian church history, a Russian audience. The English portfolio and documentation are in progress; the technical base is language-agnostic.

**"What was the hardest part?"**
Stopping the agent from lying. It says "done" with complete confidence when it is not done. Everything else follows from that.
