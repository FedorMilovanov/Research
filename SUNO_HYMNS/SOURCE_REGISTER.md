## Added 2026-11-03 — PHOS-008, *Veni redemptor gentium*

- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-AUG372** — Augustine, *Sermo* 372. The decisive witness: Augustine quotes stanza 6 and names Ambrose, "in hymno quem paulo ante cantastis" — *in the hymn you sang a few moments ago*. Read in quotation with source locator, **not** from the sermon in full. Worship witness AND authorship witness in one sentence.
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-CELEST430** — Celestine I, council of Rome, 430. Quotes stanza 2 and credits Ambrose: "omnem fecisse populum una voce Deo canere" — *made all the people sing to God with one voice*. Second independent worship witness. Read in quotation.
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-FONTAINE** — Jacques Fontaine, *Ambroise de Milan: Hymnes* (Paris: Les Éditions du Cerf, 1992), pp. 266–275. B1: **not opened**; the defence of stanza 1 ("not omitted by any Milanese witness, nor by the oldest") is carried here at second hand. `ARCHIVE_HOLD`.
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-VATREG11** — Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Reg. lat. 11, fol. 233r, second half of the 8th c. **Eight stanzas**, opening "Intende, qui regis Israel". Earliest surviving witness. CATALOG_ONLY: described, facsimile not opened. `LOCATOR_HOLD`. Note: the same codex is already a Te Deum witness in `DOSSIER_PHOS-005_TE_DEUM.md` (Reg. Lat. 11, fol. 230v).
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-B3** — Universitätsbibliothek Düsseldorf, MS B-3, fol. 269r, ca. 810–829. **Seven stanzas**, opening "Veni redemptor gentium". Earliest witness of the psalm-less form. CATALOG_ONLY. `LOCATOR_HOLD`.
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-GRMS2017** — *Ambrosian Hymns: Evidence for Roman Music of Late Antiquity?*, Greek and Roman Musical Studies 5/1 (2017), 104–122. **The decisive source on the melody**: ancient notation fell out of use in the 6th c., the melodies were transmitted **orally until the 10th c.**, and the written forms are without rhythmical values and "adorned by rich melismata". Read in abstract and excerpts, not in full.
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-BNF12044** — BnF, Paris, Latin 12044, fol. 7v, 12th c. Melodic incipit: only the first three words and a melodic opening.
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-BNF15181** — BnF, Paris, Latin 15181, fol. 157r, ca. 1300. Breviary made for Notre-Dame, Paris. Seven stanzas, full version of the melody.
- **SRC-PHOS-VRG-EINS121** — Stiftsbibliothek Einsiedeln, Codex 121, ca. 1120. Earliest documentation of the plainsong melody. CATALOG_ONLY.
