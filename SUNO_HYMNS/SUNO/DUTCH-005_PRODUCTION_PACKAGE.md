**Declared form:** `ORIGINAL_RUSSIAN_LITERARY_ADAPTATION_AFTER_A_PUBLIC_DOMAIN_DUTCH_SOURCE`.

It follows the poem **stanza by stanza in order**, keeps each stanza's argument, keeps the printed internal repeats (`etc.`), and keeps the poem's spine — the tripled word that moves from **living** to **loving** to **praising**. It does not claim Dutch metre, Dutch rhyme, or the tune `Un jour &c.`, whose music is unknown.

**Rights basis for adapting at all:** the 1676 poem is public domain in substance (author d. 1677). The DBNL diplomatic transcription and the 2005 critical edition were used to locate and check readings; neither is reproduced.

## 2. The source text (1676), and its shape

Ten stanzas. Printed form of each stanza: four statement lines, then a **call** line, its repeat (abbreviated in the print as `etc.`), the **tripled word with its object**, the call again, and a closing line. The `etc.` is a repeat instruction, **not** a lacuna.

Stanza I is printed in full and is the metrical model; the rest abbreviate the repeated call. The tripled word runs: `leven` (III–VI) → `lieven` (VII–IX) → `loven` (X). The page also carries an illustration, `lode002uyt_01ill0155.gif`, which **has not been viewed** — no claim is made about what it shows.

**Two uncertainties carried into the translation, not smoothed away:**

- **I.7** — `Is uw costlijck leven, lieven, loven` has no expressed negation. Two readings are possible: (a) elliptical, the negation of line 2 carrying over — "all you see and hear is **not** your precious living, loving, praising"; (b) a pun on `costelijck` = "costly", i.e. these things **cost** you your life, love, praise. Reading (a) is adopted, because line 9 also negates and (b) would make the stanza self-contradictory; but (b) is recorded as a real secondary sense, and `costelijck` = "precious" is attested in the same poem (`'t costelijcke bloed`, `'t costelijck hert`).
- **X.8** — `hier is stoff` ("all here that is stuff/dust") echoes the dust-and-ashes motif of the same author's *Hert-sterckte* II (`Wy sijn niet dan stof en eerd`). Rendered as "вещество/прах" without importing the other poem's line.

## 5. Deviation table — every departure, with its reason

| # | Place | What was done | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D-01** | I.5–7 | The Russian supplies the negative the Dutch leaves unexpressed: *Не о том твоя жизнь, любовь и хвала* | Lines 2 and 9 both negate; a declarative reading would make the stanza contradict itself. The alternative "costs you" pun is recorded in §3 and can be restored if the owner prefers it |
| **D-02** | III–X | The tripled word is the governed noun (жизнь / любовь / хвала) in III–IX and the infinitive in X (хвалить), where Dutch triples infinitives throughout | Russian cannot triple an infinitive inside a clause without sounding like a stutter; the *device* and its position are preserved, the part of speech is not |
| **D-03** | II, I | `costelijck` → «драгоценный» | The "cost" pun is not reproducible in one Russian word; the primary sense (precious) is chosen and the loss declared |
| **D-04** | throughout | The print's `etc.` repeats are written out in full | They are repeat instructions in the 1676 print, not missing text; repeating them is fidelity, not addition |
| **D-05** | throughout | Russian contains no added refrains, no added lines, no chorus | Project rule: the source's own tripled device is the only repetition |
| **D-06** | X.5–6 | `Cijter` → «цитра» | Literal; no claim is made about which instrument Lodenstein played or had in mind |
| **D-07** | VI.5 | The Dutch `Wilt gy ten leven gaan? Wilt gy etc.` is rendered with an inline interrogative repeat, not a duplicated line | Keeps the print's repeat without inflating the stanza |
| **D-08** | all | No rhyme scheme or metre is claimed as the Dutch one | The 1665 melody is unknown; inventing a claim would be worse than declaring the absence |

## 6. Musical brief and SUNO prompts

### 6.1 The decision, and its scope

**Per-work decision for DUTCH-005:** epic sacred organ, a Georgian-flavoured male monastic choir behind one strong low male lead, syllabic declamation, ♩≈**116** in 4/4, short organ introduction, no long instrumental interludes, no stretched vowels, no guitars.

Three things about this brief must be stated plainly:

1. It is **this work's** decision. The project's standing rules leave style per work, and the no-guitar exclusion here is local — it is **not** a project rule.
2. It is close to the direction described for DUTCH-002 in earlier sessions. That is deliberate: the two poems share the same author, the same "vanity of the world → glory of heaven" argument and the same public. But it is a **newly written brief for this text**, not a reused prompt, and it will be generated (if at all) as its own item.
3. The tempo is set high enough to avoid the known failure of earlier generations — excessively slow and whispered delivery. With ten stanzas of nine lines, a slow reading would run well past seven minutes and would flatten the `leven → lieven → loven` progression.

### 6.2 Style prompt — `PROMPT-DUTCH-005-V1`

```
epic sacred organ hymn, 4/4, 116 bpm, D minor, deep pipe organ with 16-foot pedal and full plenum, Georgian-flavoured male monastic choir singing sustained parallel fifths, one strong low male lead voice declaiming syllabically in front of the choir, wide stone-church reverb, unhurried but never dragging, restrained crescendo across ten stanzas, clear diction and natural stress, no percussion, no guitars, no synthesizers
```

**Fields:**

- **Exclude:** drums, percussion, electric bass, synth pads, EDM, cinematic string swells, belting, breathy or whispered lead, excessive vibrato, auto-tune artefacts, spoken word.
- **Sections (minimal tags, per the project's tag rule):** `[Intro — organ pedal, 2 bars]` / `[Stanza 1]` … `[Stanza 10]` / `[Outro — choir alone on the last line]`.
- **Echo:** exactly one, inline, in stanza X: `хвалить, хвалить, хвалить (хвалить)`. No other echo anywhere.
- **Tempo discipline:** if the generator produces a slow, whispered reading — the known failure mode — the fix is tempo, syllabic delivery and a shorter intro, **not** cutting stanzas.

### 6.3 A documented alternative style, for comparison only — not a second version

```
solemn organ and low strings hymn, 3/4, 104 bpm, F minor, great organ foundation stop and pedal drones, cello and contrabass doubling the bass line, a quartet of low male voices entering only from stanza 4, one solo bass cantor for the call lines, plain declamation, no percussion, no guitars
```

Status of the alternative: `DOCUMENTED_OPTION`, not approved, not a version. Choosing between §6.2 and §6.3 is an owner decision; the primary prompt is the one registered in the machine-readable catalog.
