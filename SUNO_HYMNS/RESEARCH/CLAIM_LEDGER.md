## CLM-HERT-II-SEQUEL — Relation between I. and II. Hert-sterckte in Jehova

**Claim:** II. Hert-sterckte is a companion poem written one week after I: it carries no tune of its own but a printed pointer to I on p. 78, and its opening takes up the closing image of I.

**Review state:** SUPPORTED_WITH_SCOPE_LIMITS

**Source IDs:** SRC-L-1676-HERT-I-78-81, SRC-L-1676-HERT-II-82-84, SRC-L-UYT-1727, SRC-L-CRIT-2005

- The tune pointer ('als boven pag.78') is confirmed in both the 1676 and the 1727 prints and is described the same way in the 2005 apparatus.
- The verbal continuation is established by the 2005 apparatus cross-referencing I, r. 89 ('In dien Ocean') for the opening word of II; that cross-reference is B1, but the two stanzas themselves corroborate it.
- Do not describe the two poems as recensions of one text, or one as a draft of the other.

**HOLDs:** RIGHTS_HOLD

## CLM-DUTCH-004-PS131 — DUTCH-004 biblical anchor and genre

**Claim:** The poem is a poetic expansion, not a versification, of Psalm 131: it turns the psalm's assertions about a lowly heart and unlifted eyes into a request that God test the heart (stanzas I-II), and develops the weaned child of 131:2 into stanzas IV-V.

**Review state:** SUPPORTED_WITH_SCOPE_LIMITS

**Source IDs:** SRC-L-1676-DEN-NEDRIGEN, SRC-L-CRIT-2005

- The 131:1-2 correspondences are demonstrable from the 1676 text itself.
- Mapping the closing stanza to Psalm 131:3 is this project's interpretation; it is consistent with, but not established by, the 2005 commentary, which is B1 and must not be the sole support.
- Classify as BIBLICAL_CANTICLE_OR_SCRIPTURE_EXPANSION, and note that it is nevertheless printed with a tune direction, i.e. meant for singing.

**HOLDs:** RIGHTS_HOLD
