# Case — the two poems *Hert-sterckte in Jehova* (Lodenstein) and the 1664/1674 date

**Dossier ID:** DOSSIER-HERT-I-II
**Opened:** 2026-10-25 (third project pass)
**Supersedes nothing.** Earlier summary statements in `DUTCH_HYMN_CATALOG.md`, `CLAIM_LEDGER.md` and `AUTHOR_JODOCUS_VAN_LODENSTEIN.md` are corrected/expanded below; the historical rows are retained.

## Проверяемый вопрос

1. Are *I. Hert-sterckte in Jehova* and *II. Hert-sterckte in Jehova* one text or two?
2. What is the exact relation between them — shared title only, or a deliberate textual pair?
3. Which of them, if either, is the source behind Rutgers's 1806 *Zelfverloochening* and the existing DUTCH-002 track?
4. What melody belongs to each, and is either melody independently verified?
5. Does the closing date of II read 4 January **1674** or **1664**, and has the registered `EVIDENCE_HOLD` been resolved?

## Ответы, отрицания и альтернативные объяснения

### 1. They are two different poems (settled)

| | **I. Hert-sterckte in Jehova** | **II. Hert-sterckte in Jehova** |
|---|---|---|
| 1676 printed pages | 78–81 | 82–84 |
| Critical-edition item | 34 (p. 149 ff.) | 35 (p. 154 ff.) |
| Stanzas | 15 (I–XV) | 14 (I–XIV) |
| Incipit | *Soet Geselschap dat met my* | *Waar blijft daar het Vaderland?* |
| Tune direction as printed | *Te singen als Onherroepelijke Jeugd! Of: Gavotte. A la fronté.* | *Te singen als boven pag.78. Soet Geselschap dat met my, &c.* |
| Closing date line as printed | *Op 't Fort Nieuw Rees 28. in wintermaand 1673.* | *Op 't Fort Nieuw Rees 4. In Loumaand 1674.* |
| Link to Rutgers 1806 | none established | the poem in the DUTCH-002 source chain |

Both poems sit under one shared section heading (*Volgen eenige Aandagten Over sommige van 's Heeren Gods Eygenschappen, &c. Den Autheur voorgecomen geduyrende sijn verblijv in Ostagie in't Land van Cleev.*), which is why a shared title is not evidence of a shared text.

### 2. Their relation is not "same title only" — II is a deliberate sequel to I

Evidence for a textual, not merely titular, link:

- **Tune-direction dependency.** II is printed with no tune of its own: it instructs the singer to reuse I's tune by page reference ("als boven pag. 78"). The 2005 apparatus states the same ("De melodie-aanduiding verwijst naar de voorgaande tekst 34"). Confirmed in both the 1676 and the 1727 editions.
- **Verbal continuation.** II opens *Waar blijft daar het Vaderland?* The 2005 apparatus glosses that opening *daar* by pointing to I's line 89 (*"In dien Ocean"*), i.e. II takes up the image with which I closes (XV, "In dien Ocean versincken"). This is a scholarly cross-reference, class B1, but it is corroborated by the plain 1676 text of both closing and opening stanzas.
- **Chronological proximity.** I: 28 Dec 1673. II: 4 Jan 1674. Same place (Fort Nieuw Rees), one week apart.

**Permissible formulation:** "II is a companion poem written a week after I, sung to I's tune, and picking up I's closing image." **Not permissible:** calling them two recensions of one text, or treating one as a draft of the other.

### 3. DUTCH-002 follows II, not I

The existing source chain (Lodenstein → Rutgers 1806 *Zelfverloochening*) is attached to **II**. Nothing in this pass re-examined the Rutgers link itself; that claim keeps its previous status. I must never be silently substituted for II in the production record of DUTCH-002.

### 4. Melody status

- **I** names two tunes: *Onherroepelijke Jeugd* (the 2005 apparatus notes this is a forward reference to text 52, which in turn refers back to text 34) and *Gavotte. A la fronté*, whose origin the 2005 apparatus explicitly states as **not traced**.
- **II** has **no independent melody**. Its instruction is a pointer to I. Therefore the earlier catalogue wording ("melodies Onherroepelijke Jeugd or Gavotte. A la fronté" for II) is imprecise: those are I's tunes, reached through a pointer.
- Neither tune exists in this project as notation, text or recording. The critical edition introduction notes that Lodenstein drew melodies both from popular 16th/17th-century tunes and from unidentified contemporary compositions. **No melody claim is quote-safe beyond "the printed book directs the singer to these tune names."**

### 5. The date: what is now established, and what is not

Established:

- The 1676 transcription served by DBNL reads `4. In Loumaand 1674` on printed p. 84 — but that transcription is **emended**: its own colophon lists `p. 84: 1664 → 1674`.
- The 1971 Buijnsters anthology prints 1674 with the footnote *"Er staat abusievelijk: 1664."* — an independent editor saying the print reads 1664.
- The **1727 ninth edition** prints `4 in Loumaand 1674`. This is a different typesetting, 51 years later, and it is the only **printed** witness in this project that actually reads 1674.
- The emendation is **not unique to II**: the same colophon lists `p. 87: 1664 → 1674` for item 36, *'t Verheven Herte* (dated 11 January 1674), and `p. 7: 1565 → 1665` for a different text. The pattern is a compositor-level digit error (5/6 → 6/7) recurring at several dates, not a single isolated slip.
- **Chronology corroborates 1674.** Lodenstein was held as a hostage of the French in December 1673, far from Utrecht; he sent songs to the Utrecht circle, and they were printed in 1674 as *Meditatiën over eenige van Gods eygenschappen* (2005 introduction p. 646; the 1971 headnote says the same and calls them four consolation songs). The 1676 section heading for this group describes them as written during his exile in Cleves. A January 1664 date would place the poem nine years before the captivity that the collection's own heading and both modern editions associate with it.

Not established:

- **Nobody in this project has looked at the 1676 printed page.** DBNL serves page images only for pages carrying emblems; printed p. 84 has none, and the corresponding `…ill0084.gif` request returns HTTP 500. The scan bundle advertised on the DBNL record ("Scans", 18.17 MB, `https://www.dbnl.org/arch/lode002uyt_01_01/pag/lode002uyt_01_01.pdf`) was **not acquired**, and the sandbox's network policy blocks direct download from `dbnl.org`. The 1674 *Meditatien* pamphlet was likewise not acquired.
- Therefore the proposition *"the 1676 leaf reads 1664"* still rests on modern editorial testimony (B1). It cannot be promoted to a verified primary reading.

**Conclusion:** the `EVIDENCE_HOLD` on `CLM-HERT-II-DATE-1674` is **retained, but narrowed**. The *date itself* (early January 1674) now has an A1 printed witness (1727) plus independent chronological corroboration, and is safe to use with an explicit note. What remains unresolved is only the **literal reading of the 1676 impression**. The hold must not be spread to the poem's text, authorship, place or tune.

## Анализ и границы допустимой формулировки

Usable now, without further evidence:

> "Jodocus van Lodenstein wrote two poems under the title *Hert-sterckte in Jehova* on 1 Samuel 30:6 — I on 28 December 1673 and II on 4 January 1674, both at Fort Nieuw Rees during his exile. II was written a week after I and is sung to I's tune; it opens by taking up the closing image of I."

Must still be flagged:

> "(The 1676 impression is reported by two modern editions to misprint the year of II as 1664; the 1727 edition and both modern editions read 1674. The 1676 leaf itself has not been inspected in this project.)"

Forbidden:

- presenting the melody as recovered or as historically performed;
- quoting the 2005 apparatus as the poem's original wording;
- merging I and II into one catalogue entry, or into one text chain with Rutgers 1806.

## Locator warning (applies to the whole project)

The numeric slug in a DBNL `lode002uyt_01_01_00NN.php` URL is a **sequential chunk number, not a printed page number**. Observed mapping in this pass:

| URL slug | Printed pages shown |
|---|---|
| `…_0013.php` | p. 29 |
| `…_0036.php` | pp. 74–75 |
| `…_0038.php` | pp. 78–79 |
| `…_0039.php` | pp. 82–84 |
| `…_0040.php` | pp. 85–87 |
| `…_0084.php` | p. 230 |

The DBNL colophon's intervention list cites **printed** pages. Reading `p. 84` as `…_0084.php` lands on an unrelated funeral poem on p. 230. Always cite the printed page bracket `[pagina N]` and the poem title, not the slug.
