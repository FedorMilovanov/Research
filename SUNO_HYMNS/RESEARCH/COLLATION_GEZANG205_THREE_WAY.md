# Collation: *Hert-sterckte in Jehova* II (1676) → *Zelfverloochening* (1806) → Gezang 205 (1938)

**Purpose.** One place where the three forms can be compared without consulting three files, and where the text of the 1938 hymnal item is finally quoted from the **printed book** rather than from a website.

**Opened:** 2026-10-25 (fifth pass).
**Supersedes:** the 1938 column of `DOSSIER_DUTCH-002_TEXT_HISTORY.md`, which rested on web transcriptions. That column was substantially right; two details were not, and they are corrected here.

---

## 1. The witnesses, and how the 1938 text was obtained

| Form | Witness | Pages |
|---|---|---|
| **1676** | *Uyt-spanningen*, Utrecht: Willem Clerck, 1676 — diplomatic DBNL transcription | printed pp. **82–84**, 14 stanzas, `Op 't Fort Nieuw Rees 4. In Loumaand 1674` |
| **1806** | *Evangelische Gezangen…* (1806) — DBNL transcription | printed pp. **143–144**, no. **LXVIII**, 7 stanzas |
| **1938** | *Psalmen en gezangen voor den eeredienst der Nederlandsche Hervormde Kerk* (1938) — **printed book, page images** | image pp. **723–725** (Gezang 205 on 724–725), 5 stanzas |

**The 1938 object.** Koninklijke Bibliotheek copy, Delpher identifier `MMKB06:000000593`, PPN 416504787, `VII, 944, 48 p`. Read at:
<https://www.delpher.nl/nl/boeken/view?coll=boeken&identifier=MMKB06:000000593:00724> (and `:00725`).

Delpher's OCR is served page by page, so the page had to be located first. The route, recorded because it will be needed again: the page number is not the printed page number, and gezang numbers drift — image 430 carries **Gezang 18**, image 640 carries **Gezang 153**, which fixes roughly 1.55 pages per gezang in that stretch and put Gezang 205 at approximately image 721; images 719 and 721 then resolved to Gezangen 200 and 202, and 724 to 205. **The OCR is not 100 % reliable** (Delpher says so itself) and it dropped stanza 1 of Gezang 205 entirely — see §4.

**Rights.** Delpher states at the head of every page: *"Auteursrechtelijk beschermd. Op dit object rust auteursrecht."* The 1938 bundel is therefore a **protected object**, not a public-domain hymnbook, whatever the public-domain status of the 1676 poem behind it. Registered as `PERMISSION_REQUIRED` + `RIGHTS_HOLD`. Quote sparingly, cite the page, and do not republish the bundel's text as if it were free.

## 2. What the printed page says about authorship and melody

The item carries two credits, in the bundel's standard layout — **composer above the text, author below it**:

- **Melody:** `Johann Christoph Bach (1642—1703)`
- **Text:** `Jodocus van Lodenstein (1620—1677) Bewerking van Abraham Rutgers (1751—1809)`

Two things follow, and both matter:

1. **The Rutgers reworking is print-verified.** The 1938 hymnal itself names Lodenstein as author and Rutgers as the reworker. The chain 1676 → 1806 (Rutgers) → 1938 is now documented at both ends, not inferred.
2. **The 1938 tune is an 18th-century attribution, not the 1676 melody.** The 1676 print directs the singer back to I (`Te singen als boven pag.78`), whose own heading names *Onherroepelijke Jeugd* or *Gavotte. A la fronté*; none of that is `Johann Christoph Bach`. Do not let a modern hymnal's composer credit be read as the poem's original tune.

## 3. The three forms side by side

Format: 1676 stanza → 1806 stanza(s) → 1938 stanza, with the evidence class for the link.

| 1676 | 1806 | 1938 | Relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** `Waar blijft daar het Vaderland?` | — | — | **not carried over.** The exile opening is gone from both later forms |
| **II** `'t Scheen wat groots en van gewigt …` | **2** `Is ons zielverdervend eigen…` | **2** | **near-verbatim**, 1676 ll. 3–6 → 1806 ll. 1–4; the 1806 couplet is its own |
| **III** `En gelijck my daar verdwijnt / 't Ligt dat in de vriendschap schijnt` | — | — | **not carried over** |
| **IV** `Met in mijn gedagten schiet / 't Eeuwig All, en 't eyndeloos Niet` | 1 (thesis only) | 1 | idea reused, **wording new**; the `Niet` is not named |
| **V** `'t Hoort den Hemel alles toe…` | **3** `Wat wij hebben of vermogen…` | **3** | **same six lines, reordered**: 1676 opens with the clause, 1806 closes with it |
| **VI** `Hy is d'Aanvang-losen Ader` | — | — | **not carried over** (the fountain stanza) |
| **VII** `Wy sijn niet dan stof en eerd` | (4, theme only) | (4, theme only) | possibly echoed by `Niets met al`; **no verbatim parallel** — this project's judgement |
| **VIII** `Ey! helpt my het grondloos Niet` | **4** `Niets met al, niets zijn wij waardig` | — | **near-verbatim**: `Niets, ja niets en sijn wy weerdig` → `Niets met al, niets zijn wij waardig`; `Alle Schepsel hoort sijn Heer` → `Alle ding behoort zijn' Heer` |
| **IX** `Leent hy ons oyd eenig Goed` | **4** (ll. 5–6) + **5** (ll. 1–4) | 4 (ll. 5–6) | **split across two 1806 stanzas**; `Des wy Hem te dancken schuldig` → `U te danken zijn wij schuldig` |
| **X** `'t Gaat weer tot dien Oceaan` | **5** (ll. 5–6) | **4** (ll. 5–6) | `'t Keert te rug tot Hem, die 't gaf` |
| **XI** `Hebb' den Hemel wat Hem lust` | **6** (ll. 1–2) | — | compressed |
| **XII** `Ick ben maar tot Godes Eer` | **6** (ll. 3–4) + **7** (ll. 1–2) | **4** (ll. 1–4), **5** (ll. 1–2) | the `in het quaad, en in het goed` motive becomes `Voor 't geloof is alles goed` |
| **XIII** `Sit ick eensaam 't is mijn vreugd` | — | — | **not carried over** (the Cherubim stanza) |
| **XIV** `Foey! Dat ick oyt onbedogt` | — | — | **not carried over**; the self-reproach that closes the 1674 poem is dropped |

**Net effect.** 14 stanzas → 7 → 5. The two later forms keep the doctrinal spine and discard the **occasional frame**: the fatherland, the friend's house, the fountain, the lonely seat, the closing self-accusation. That is a change of genre — from an exile meditation dated at Fort Nieuw Rees to a general hymn — and it is why a Russian text following 1806 or 1938 cannot be offered as a rendering of the 1674 poem.

Confidence: **high** on every row marked near-verbatim (both texts read in full); **medium** on the VII row and on the stanza-1 recasting, which are this project's collation and not a published finding.

## 4. The 1938 text as printed, and the two corrections it forces

**Stanza 2** (printed p. 724):

> *Is ons trotsch, hoogmoedig eigen / nog zoo dikwijls ongezind, / om zich naar uw wil te neigen, / waar het hart slechts rust in vindt, / werp dien hoogmoed dan, o Heer, / door uw Geest in ons terneer!*

Against 1806 stanza 2:

> *Is ons zielverdervend eigen / Nog zoo dikwerf ongezind, / Om zich tot dien wil te neigen, / Waar het hart maar rust in vindt; / Werp die trotsheid dan, o Heer! / Door uw' Geest in ons ter neêr.*

**Correction 1 — there are two substitutions, not one.** In line 1 `zielverdervend` becomes `trots, hoogmoedig`, **and** in line 5 `Werp die trotsheid dan` becomes `werp dien hoogmoed dan`. The pair is deliberate: the word `trotsheid` moves out of the prayer and into the diagnosis, so the stanza no longer names the disease as *soul-destroying*. A translation must record which wording it follows; "губящее душу" is 1676/1806, "гордое, надменное" is 1938.

**Stanza 4** (printed p. 724):

> *Vragen wij, wat goed of kwaad is: / voor 't geloof is alles goed. / Al wat d' uitvoer van uw raad is, / Vader, dat is alles goed! / Gij, o Heer, zoo wijs als trouw, / weet best, wat ons schaden zou.*

**Correction 2 — the closing couplet is not new.** It is 1806 **stanza 5, lines 5–6** (`Gij, o Heer! zoo wijs als trouw, / Weet best, wat ons schaden zou`), grafted onto 1806 **stanza 6, lines 1–4**. The 1938 editor replaced 1806's own couplet (`Altijd goed en wijsheid is 't, / Wat de Wijsheid zelv' beslist`) with it. The web transcription had the same content, but this is now read from the book.

**What that graft does to the argument.** The 1806 couplet it displaces asserted that what God decides *is* wisdom. The 1938 couplet asserts that God knows what would harm us. Both are true, and they are not the same claim: the first is about the decree, the second about providence. Since `voor 't geloof is alles goed` is explained by the lines that follow it, **the 1938 form explains it differently from the 1806 form** — an argument for decree in 1806, an argument for providential care in 1938. Any Russian rendering of this stanza is therefore rendering a *specific* historical argument, and must say which.

**Stanza 3** (p. 724) and **stanza 5** (p. 725) are identical to 1806 stanzas 3 and 7 apart from spelling; **stanza 1 was not recovered from the OCR** of p. 724, where the transcription jumps from the composer credit to stanza 2. The five-stanza count and the composition are print-verified; stanza 1's 1938 spelling rests on the two agreeing web transcriptions, i.e. `EVIDENCE_HOLD`, narrowed to that one stanza.

**The omissions, now quotable.** 1938 drops 1806 **stanza 4** in full (`Niets met al, niets zijn wij waardig; / Eischt Gij dan uw giften weêr…`) and 1806 **stanza 5, lines 1–4** (`U te danken zijn wij schuldig, / Maar ook, eischt Gij ons iets af, / Niets te weigren ongeduldig; / 't Keert te rug tot Hem, die 't gaf`), keeping only that stanza's closing couplet. The result reads smoothly, which is exactly why the loss is worth recording: the doctrine of *owing nothing and being obliged to thank* is what disappeared.

## 5. `heylsaam Niet` — located

Not in the 1806 hymn, as suspected. It is in the 1676 poem, stanza **II, line 5**:

> *Maar het ziel-verdervend Eygen / Was'er in, end ongesind / Sig tot 't **heylsaam Niet** te neygen, / Daar de Ziel maar ruste vindt.*

The poem qualifies its "Nothing" three separate ways, and none of them is nihilism:

| Stanza | Phrase | Force |
|---|---|---|
| II | *'t heylsaam Niet* | the **salutary** Nothing — renunciation that heals |
| IV | *'t eyndeloos Niet* | the **endless** Nothing — set against `'t Eeuwig All` |
| VIII | *'t grondloos Niet* | the **bottomless** Nothing — what one is helped to see |

The 1806 reworking keeps the argument and drops the word: `Om zich tot dien wil te neigen` replaces `Sig tot 't heylsaam Niet te neygen`. So a Russian text may have the paradox only if it follows 1676.

## 6. Consequences for the project

1. `CLM-DUTCH-002-STANZAS-14-7-5` can leave `EVIDENCE_HOLD` for everything except stanza 1's 1938 spelling.
2. The 1938 wording is **not** free to reuse: the bundel is a protected object.
3. The tune question is now two questions: the 1676/1674 tune (untraced, named *Onherroepelijke Jeugd* / *Gavotte A la fronté* for poem I) and the 1938 melody attributed to Johann Christoph Bach. Neither is evidence for the other.
4. Any Russian lyric for DUTCH-002 must declare its form — 14, 7 or 5 stanzas — and, if it is the 5-stanza form, which wording of stanza 2 and which ending of stanza 4.
