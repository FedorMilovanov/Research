# Author dossier — Jodocus van Lodenstein

## Identity

- **Name forms:** Jodocus van Lodenstein; early-print spellings can include Lodensteyn; Russian project spelling: Йодокус ван Лоденштейн.
- **Life:** 1620–1677; born in Delft, died in Utrecht.
- **Role:** Dutch Reformed pastor, devotional poet and important representative of the Nadere Reformatie (“Further Reformation”).
- **Service:** pastor in Utrecht from 1653 until his death.
- **Theological context:** his writings stress lived reformation — holiness, repentance, spiritual affection and practical consequences of doctrine — rather than doctrine as a merely formal confession.

DBNL author record: https://www.dbnl.org/auteurs/auteur.php?id=lode002  
Biographical entry: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/aa__001biog13_01/aa__001biog13_01_1152.htm

## Main song collection

*Uyt-spanningen, behelsende eenige stigtelyke liederen en andere gedigten* was published in Utrecht by Willem Clerck in 1676. The title page divides the work across biblical matter and expansions of Scripture/Psalms, spiritual meditations, reflections on events and penitential poems, with an appendix. Its front matter cites Ephesians 5:19.

First-edition entrypoint: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_01_01/  
Critical-edition contents and context (2005): https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_02_01/index.php

Do not treat every numbered entry as a uniform official church hymnbook. Each text needs an individual genre and melody check.

## DUTCH-001 — *Schepselen niet en Jesus al*

- Critical-edition item 51.
- Text dated November 1661.
- Tune direction: *Singt als: Rosemont waer gy vliedt*.
- Eight stanzas of eight lines in the critical text.
- The repeated contrast places created attractions against Christ's supreme worth.
- The text does not mean creation is intrinsically evil. The question is what rules the heart and whether a created good becomes an ultimate end.

Source page: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_02_01/lode002uyt_02_01_0057.php

## DUTCH-002 source — *Hert-sterckte in Jehova*

The 1674 source poem is titled *Hert-sterckte in Jehova. Op 1 Sam. 30:6*. Its published text gives 4 January 1674 and links the poem to 1 Samuel 30:6, where David strengthens himself in the LORD his God under threat from his own people. Context pages discuss the Rees songs and Lodenstein's captivity/hostage setting.

- Source text: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002pjbu01_01/lode002pjbu01_01_0014.php

## Two separate Rees poems under the same title

The critical edition distinguishes:

- **I. Hert-sterckte in Jehova**, item 34, dated 28 December 1673 in the critical edition, with melody directions “Onherroepelijke Jeugd” or “Gavotte. A la fronté”.
- **II. Hert-sterckte in Jehova**, item 35, later linked to the seven-stanza hymn *Zelfverloochening* in *Evangelische Gezangen* (1806), no. LXVIII.

The current Russian hymn follows the later Rutgers hymnbook form. Do not silently merge the two source poems. The displayed date of II carries a date emendation caveat: the original print is reported to misprint 1664, while the digital edition corrects it to 1674. The claim remains on EVIDENCE_HOLD pending visual inspection of the original page image.

## Rutgers adaptation chain

Abraham Rutgers revised Lodenstein material for *Evangelische Gezangen* (1806), where hymn 68 is titled *Zelfverloochening*. This seven-stanza form is related to, but not textually identical with, the full 1674 source. The Russian song belongs to the later reworking lineage.
