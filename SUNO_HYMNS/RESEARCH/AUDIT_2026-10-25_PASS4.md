# Audit — 2026-10-25, fourth pass (supplementary brief)

**Status update, fifth pass (2026-10-25).** Two items in §5 are now closed and are not open any more: `heylsaam Niet` was located in the 1676 poem at stanza II line 5, and the 1938 bundel was collated, so the five-stanza composition and the stanza-2 substitutions are print-verified (stanza 1 excepted). One §5 item is now narrower: `in 't dorre stickt` has a documented gloss grounded in rhyme and etymology, though still no dictionary entry. Two items are documented dead ends rather than open questions: the WNT route and the 1630 Revius leaf. See the fifth-pass section of `REVIEW_LOG_2026-10-25.md` and `COLLATION_GEZANG205_THREE_WAY.md`. **Scope.** A check of the project against the working notes supplied on 25 October 2026, on the explicit footing that those notes are **leads from earlier chats, not facts**. Everything below either (a) restates what the repository already held, (b) records a verification performed in this pass, or (c) marks a gap. Nothing has been upgraded to "verified" without a source read in this pass.

**Headline:** the notes contained one material error (the 1806 hymn's stanza count) and one identity confusion (a poem's title mistaken for a second title). Both are corrected. Everything else in the notes held up, and two things are now established which the repository previously only suspected: `Heerlijckheyds Loff` is a real, distinct, locatable poem, and the DUTCH-001 addresses genuinely vary in the source.

**What this pass did *not* do:** it did not review any Russian lyric. None is stored in the repository. Do not let any summary of this pass be read as validating a translation.

**Mis-attribution removed.** The 1938 bundel contains **two** Lodenstein hymns — Gezang 205 (from DUTCH-002's line) and *'t Oog omhoog* (from `Heerlijckheyds Loff`). The second is a 20th-century reworking with a Bourgeois melody. Any future note that cites "the 1938 Lodenstein hymn" is ambiguous and must be disambiguated on sight.

## 4. What is verified — and to what standard

**A1, read in this pass:**

- **DUTCH-001**, 1676 print pp. 131–134: eight stanzas; tune line; date `Slagt-m. 1661`; the eight addresses; stanza IV's `Laat u min, u cruys, u croon / Mijn min my heyl'gen doen, of doon`; stanza II's `Menschen-gunst` / `Menschen-ongunst` / `Stelen 't herte weg`.
- **DUTCH-002**, 1806 pp. 143–144: seven stanzas, read in full. The line `Voor 't geloof is alles goed` sits inside an argument about God's decree and is followed by its own explanation.
- **`Heerlijckheyds Loff`**, 1676 pp. 155–158: title, incipit, ten stanzas, tune line, date 27 August 1665, item 56. Stanzas VIII–X read closely; the tripled `leven → lieven → loven` progression noted.
- **DUTCH-003**, the 1930–1935 scholarly text: fourteen lines, turn at 9, six instruments in print order, causal close; plus the apparatus's indirect report of the 1630 reading `tuyschen`.

**B1, corroborated but not quote-safe:**

- 1938 Gezang 205: five stanzas and their composition, from two agreeing web transcriptions. `EVIDENCE_HOLD`.
- 2005 apparatus: the melody of *Heerlijckheyds Loff* is of unknown origin and is **not** text 27's *Un jour*.
- Reprints of `Heerlijckheyds Loff` in 1681 and 1718 — from search-index extracts while the Database was offline. **Class C discovery leads, not evidence.**

**Not verified anywhere in this pass:** any melody as an actual tune (no notation is held for any work); any composition date; any rights position beyond "assess separately"; any publication.

## 5. What stays open

1. **`heylsaam Niet`.** The phrase from the notes is **not** in the 1806 hymn. It must belong to the 1676 poem (stanza II's argument), but the 1676 stanza was not re-read for it in this pass. Status `UNVERIFIED`. Do not quote it as established until the line is seen.
2. **The 1674 hold on DUTCH-002's date** remains narrowed to the literal reading of printed p. 84. The page image was not obtained.
3. **The eight DUTCH-001 expressions** are located but unresolved lexically; `in 't dorre stickt` and `Geven op de hand` are the weakest.
4. **DUTCH-003's Russian.** Not in the repository, not approved. The discussed turn is agentive where the original is agentive in a different way — see §4 of the sonnet dossier. A decision is needed, not a default.
5. **Two competing music directions for DUTCH-003**, neither approved.
6. **The DUTCH-002 tempo decision** (112–120 BPM) cannot be shown to describe either existing recording.
7. **Prompt duplication risk:** the same epic-organ and male-ensemble brief appears under both DUTCH-002 and `DUTCH-CAND-021`. With no verbatim prompts held, it is not determinable whether one prompt was reused for two poems.
8. **The Dutch Song Database** was unreachable (HTTP 403) throughout; every melody statement in this project remains provisional until it is consulted.
9. **PR #236 → #237:** the owner still needs to decide which of the two stays.

## 6. Method notes worth keeping

- **A working note is a lead.** Three of the notes' four claims about DUTCH-002 were right and one was wrong; the count of "how many stanzas" was the wrong one, and it was the claim most likely to be repeated because it is the easiest to state.
- **Read the stanza around the line.** `Voor 't geloof is alles goed` survives as a slogan only if its next three lines are not read. The same is true of DUTCH-003's final line and of `zielverdervend eigen`, whose whole force is destroyed by the 1938 substitution.
- **Two hymnals, one author, two hymns.** The 1938 bundel is the most likely source of a modern mis-attribution for both of the Lodenstein lines in this project.
- **Nothing here was invented to fill a gap.** Six `NOT_CAPTURED` / `NOT_IN_REPOSITORY` markers remain where a prompt or a lyric would be.
