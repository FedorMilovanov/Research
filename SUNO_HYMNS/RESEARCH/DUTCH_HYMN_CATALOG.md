# Dutch hymn / devotional-song catalog

The status below records the project state known from prior conversation, not public-release status.

## DUTCH-001 — *Schepselen niet en Jesus al*

- **Russian release title:** «Всё сотворённое — ничто, Иисус — всё».
- **Author:** Jodocus van Lodenstein (1620–1677).
- **Text category:** stanzaic Dutch Reformed devotional song with printed melody direction.
- **Critical-edition item:** no. 51.
- **Date claim:** The 2005 critical edition gives November 1661. The exact item locator is currently in a copyrighted critical edition; inspect the original 1676 page image before a quote-sensitive claim.
- **First reliable publication anchor:** *Uyt-spanningen*, Utrecht, Willem Clerck, 1676.
- **Tune direction:** *Singt als: Rosemont waar gy vliedt* — the 1676 print reads **waar**; the critical edition normalises it to *waer*. The melody is untraced.
- **Structure:** eight stanzas, eight lines per stanza. **Verified from the print itself** (printed pp. 131–134, `SRC-L-1676-SCHEPSELEN-131-134`), not from the critical edition.
- **Stanza architecture (verified):** each stanza is a statement of four lines, then a Christological address with its own verb of attraction, then a closing couplet. The addresses **vary**: *soet* / *hoogste*, *lief* / *hoogste*, *schoon* / *schoonste*, *lief* / *minlijckst*, *schoon* / *cierlickst*, *lief* / *soetste*, *soet* / *soetste*, *lief* / *waardigst*. There is **no refrain**; a Russian text that holds one address constant across all eight stanzas is a documented adaptation, not a translation.
- **Theological boundary:** do not imply created things are intrinsically evil; the text contrasts Christ's supreme worth with created things when they compete for the heart or become ultimate ends.
- **Translation decisions:** the eight points raised on 2026-10-25 (reciprocal vow, favour/disfavour, the sanctify-or-kill couplet, and the archaic lexis) are recorded in `DOSSIER_DUTCH-001_TRANSLATION_DECISIONS.md`, with a confidence rating per item and `PENDING_LEXICAL_CHECK` status for the expressions that still need a dictionary.
- **Production:** one audio version and YouTube/Telegram derivatives were reported prepared.
- **Publication status:** unknown/not verified.
- **References:** https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_01_01/ ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_02_01/lode002uyt_02_01_0057.php

## DUTCH-002 — *II. Hert-sterckte in Jehova* → *Zelfverloochening*

- **Russian release title:** «Блаженно, блаженно — быть ничем».
- **Source author:** Jodocus van Lodenstein.
- **Later reworker:** Abraham Rutgers (1751–1809).
- **Source text:** *II. Hert-sterckte in Jehova. Op 1 Sam. 30:6*, printed in the 1676 first edition on **pp. 82–84** (14 stanzas); critical-edition item 35, p. 154 ff. The poem stands on its own in the 1676 print — it does not need the critical edition to be read.
- **Date:** 4 January 1674, with a *narrowed* caveat. The 1727 ninth edition prints 1674 (an independent typesetting); the 1971 anthology prints 1674 and states that the print reads 1664; the DBNL transcription of the 1676 print is emended at printed p. 84 from 1664 to 1674. The 1676 leaf itself has **not** been inspected, so `CLM-HERT-II-DATE-1674` keeps `EVIDENCE_HOLD` — but the hold now covers only the literal reading of the first impression, not the date's use. The same misprint-and-correction recurs at p. 7 (1565→1665) and p. 87 (item 36), which is a compositor-level fault, not an isolated slip.
- **Melody:** **no independent tune.** The print directs the singer back to I on p. 78 (`Te singen als boven pag.78. Soet Geselschap dat met my, &c.`), whose own heading names *Onherroepelijke Jeugd* or *Gavotte. A la fronté*. Neither tune is acquired; the recorded modern setting is not either of them.
- **Later hymnbook forms — three counts, do not merge them:**
  - **1676** original: **14 stanzas**.
  - **1806** *Evangelische Gezangen*, no. **LXVIII** *Zelfverloochening*: **seven stanzas** (printed pp. 143–144, read in full 2026-10-25). An earlier project statement that 1806 has five stanzas was **wrong**.
  - **1938** *Hervormde Bundel*, **Gezang 205**: **five stanzas**. It omits 1806 stanzas **4 and 5**, keeps the last couplet of stanza 5 as the close of its own fourth stanza, and replaces `zielverdervend eigen` with `trots, hoogmoedig eigen`.
  - The 1806 form is a **reworking**, not a stanza-subset: it drops the autobiographical exile frame of 1676 stanzas I, III and XIII. Full collation table in `DOSSIER_DUTCH-002_TEXT_HISTORY.md`.
- **1938 wording not printed-verified:** Gezang 205 has been read only through web transcriptions; the printed bundel has not been collated (`EVIDENCE_HOLD`).
- **Do not conflate:** a separate *I. Hert-sterckte in Jehova* stands in the same 1676 volume on **pp. 78–81** (15 stanzas, critical-edition item 34, dated 28 December 1673 in both the print and the critical edition), with its own tune names. II is its sequel, not its duplicate. See `DOSSIER_HERT_STERCKTE_I_II.md`.
- **Modern transmission:** Afrikaans Lied 276, plus another Lodenstein-derived text listed as Lied 271.
- **Textual boundary:** the 1674 source poem and 1806 hymn are related but not identical. The Russian lyric follows the later hymn tradition; do not call it a word-for-word translation of the full 1674 text.
- **Production:** two audio versions and four derivatives (two YouTube MP4s, two Telegram MP3s) were reported prepared.
- **Publication status:** unknown/not verified.
- **References:** https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_01_01/lode002uyt_01_01_0039.php (1676 print, pp. 82–84) ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_02_01/lode002uyt_02_01_0040.php (critical edition, item 35) ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_03_01/lode002uyt_03_01_0040.php (1727 edition) ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002pjbu01_01/lode002pjbu01_01_0013.php ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002pjbu01_01/lode002pjbu01_01_0014.php ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_01_01/colofon.php ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_02_01/lode002uyt_02_01_0147.php ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_eva005evan01_01/_eva005evan01_01_0070.php ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/aa__001nieu03_01/aa__001nieu03_01_0176.php ; https://missio.org.za/songs/lied-276/ ; https://missio.org.za/songs/lied-271/

## DUTCH-003 — *Hy droech onse smerten*

- **Russian release title:** «Он понёс наши скорби».
- **Author:** Jacobus Revius (1586–1658), name variant Jacob Reefsen.
- **Text category:** fourteen-line Passion sonnet; no original tune direction verified in the current record.
- **Title / biblical anchor:** echoes Isaiah 53:4, “He bore our sorrows”.
- **First publication:** *Over-Ysselsche sangen en dichten*, Deventer, 1630.
- **Composition date:** not established; do not infer it from publication year.
- **Composition (verified line by line 2026-10-25):** lines 1–4 negate the Jewish participants, 5–8 negate the soldiers; **line 9 is the turn** — `Ick bent, ô Heer, ick bent die u dit heb gedaen`; lines 10–13 make the speaker the instruments in this order: the tree/cross (`den swaren boom`, glossed *het kruis* by the edition), the rope, the nail, the spear, the scourge, the blood-dropped crown; **line 14** — `Want dit is al geschiet, eylaes! om mijne sonden` — assigns the event to the speaker's sins. The movement from *onse* to *mijne* is the sonnet's own design, not an interpretation. See `DOSSIER_DUTCH-003_REVIUS_SONNET.md`.
- **Russian text:** the version discussed in earlier sessions is **not in this repository and is not approved**. No translation claim is made.
- **Music direction:** two options were discussed (atmospheric guitar and cello; or a cappella growing into organ, cello and male ensemble after the turn). **Neither is approved**, and no style prompt exists in the repository.
- **Production:** MP3 filename is known; tag readback and final artwork remain open.
- **Publication status:** unknown/not verified.
- **References:** https://www.dbnl.org/auteurs/auteur.php?id=revi001 ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/revi001over01_01/ ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/revi001over01_01/revi001over01_01_0327.php ; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_taa008197601_01/_taa008197601_01_0008.php

## DUTCH-004 — *Den nedrigen christen, Op Psalm 131*

- **Working Russian title:** «Смиренный христианин» (Psalm 131) — **not final**; the owner has not approved a title.
- **Author:** Jodocus van Lodenstein (1620–1677). Attribution certain: printed in his own collection.
- **Text category:** `BIBLICAL_CANTICLE_OR_SCRIPTURE_EXPANSION` — a poetic **expansion**, not a versification, of Psalm 131, printed with a tune direction.
- **Printed place:** *Uyt-spanningen*, Utrecht, Willem Clerck, 1676, **pp. 74–75**; 7 stanzas, 8 printed lines per stanza forming 7 sung lines (the second divided).
- **Date of writing:** **unknown**. Neither the 1676 nor the 1727 printing carries a date line. Do not infer one from the publication year, and do not borrow the 1673–74 dating of the neighbouring Rees group.
- **First publication:** 1676.
- **Transmission:** reprinted in the 9th edition (1727) on the same pages with only orthographic variation; stanza IV switches from exclamation to question mark there. Full variant table in the dossier.
- **Biblical anchor:** Psalm 131. The 131:1–2 correspondences are demonstrable from the text; the mapping of the closing stanza to 131:3 is this project's interpretation.
- **Melody:** `Singt als Prendete la: siet boven pag.29.` The pointer resolves to *Simeons Lof-sang* on printed p. 29, whose heading is `Te singen Als: Prendete la.` The tune's origin is **untraced**; no notation is held; the Dutch Song Database is offline. No melody claim beyond the printed instruction.
- **Rights:** the 1676 poem is public domain in substance and may be adapted; the 2005 critical edition, the 1971 anthology and the DBNL transcription are separate modern objects and must not be reproduced.
- **Production:** `LYRIC_DRAFT`. A Russian adaptation draft v0.1 and a production package exist in `SUNO/`. **Nothing has been generated, exported, covered or published.**
- **Publication status:** unknown / not verified — nothing exists to publish yet.
- **Dossier:** `CANDIDATE_DUTCH-004_DEN_NEDRIGEN_CHRISTEN.md` ; package: `../SUNO/DUTCH-004_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE.md`.

## Identified but **not** catalogued — *Heerlijckheyds Loff*

- **Not a track.** Registered as backlog candidate `DUTCH-CAND-021`; it has no work ID, no lyric and no prompt.
- **Identity resolved 2026-10-25:** a distinct ten-stanza poem by Lodenstein, 1676 print, **pp. 155–158**, incipit `HOog! om hoog! mijn siel, na boven!`, tune line `Voyse: Un jour &c.`, dated **27 August 1665**, critical-edition item 56.
- **The earlier confusion:** the working notes paired `Heerlijckheyds Loff` with `Hoog, omhoog…` as if they were two titles or two works. The second is the poem's first line.
- **A trap for attribution:** the 1938 Hervormde Bundel carries a **modernised reworking** of this text, *'t Oog omhoog, het hart naar boven!*, set to a melody attributed to **Loys Bourgeois**. That is a 20th-century hymnal object, not the 1665 poem and not the 1665 tune. The 1938 bundel therefore contains **two** distinct Lodenstein hymns (Gezang 205 from DUTCH-002's line, and this one); they must never be conflated.
- **Melody:** the 2005 apparatus says the melody's origin is unknown and that it is **not** the *Un jour* of text 27, so the printed tune name cannot be resolved to a tune. No notation, no recording.
- **Dossier:** `CANDIDATE_DUTCH-CAND-021_HEERLIJCKHEYDS_LOFF.md`.

## Status separation — mandatory per work

Every work keeps these seven states **apart**, and no state may be inferred from a neighbour:

1. **Confirmed source text** — read in the original printing, with the printed page recorded.
2. **Verified translation** — the actual text exists in the repository *and* has been collated against the source.
3. **Working draft** — exists, unreviewed.
4. **Musical prompt** — the verbatim text submitted to the generator.
5. **Generation output** — a file, with metadata read from the file rather than from memory.
6. **User-approved version** — the owner has said so explicitly, and the approval is dated.
7. **Unverified history** — claims that rest on working notes from earlier sessions.

Two rules follow, and both have already been broken once in this project's short history:

- **A prompt, and even a good-sounding generation, is not evidence that a translation is accurate.** Accuracy is established only by collation (state 2).
- **A draft discussed earlier is not approved.** State 6 requires an explicit, dated owner decision.

Current state across the catalog: no Russian text is stored in the repository for any of the four works, so **state 2 is empty for all of them**, and all prompts are `NOT_CAPTURED`.

## Classification policy

Keep these labels separate: metrical psalm/psalm versification; biblical canticle or expanded biblical text; devotional song with melody direction; devotional poem without tune instruction; sonnet or other literary poem; later hymnbook reworking; translation/adaptation; modern musical setting.

Do not assume original genre is “hymn” merely because a modern Russian adaptation is presented as a hymn on the channel.


## Primary-witness and date safeguards

- The DBNL first-edition transcription for Uyt-spanningen is described as diplomatic but not complete: it records omissions and editorial corrections.
- The 2005 Ros/Strengholt critical edition is marked copyright-protected by DBNL. Item pages are valuable locators, but the modern edited text and apparatus should not be reproduced in full without permission.
- The date of II. Hert-sterckte is not treated as settled at source level: the online presentation corrects a reported 1664 printing error to 1674. Keep EVIDENCE_HOLD until the original page image is inspected and the visual evidence recorded.
- *Hy droech onse smerten* is a sonnet first published in 1630. The current exact online text is in a 1930–1935 scholarly edition; the original 1630 leaf is not acquired in this project.
- *Heerlijckheyds Loff* was identified in the fourth pass (1676, pp. 155–158). Its full text has not been collated; the incipit, tune line, date, stanza count and the tripled figures are verified, and stanzas VIII–X were read closely.
- The 1938 Hervormde Bundel has **not** been collated in this project. Gezang 205 is known only through agreeing web transcriptions; treat its wording as `EVIDENCE_HOLD` until the printed bundel is checked.
