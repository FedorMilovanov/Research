# DUTCH-001 — translation decisions requiring collation

**Work:** `DUTCH-001`, *Schepselen Niet en Iesus Al* (Jodocus van Lodenstein)
**Dossier opened:** 2026-10-25 (fourth pass), from working notes carried over from earlier sessions.
**Status of the Russian text:** the Russian lyric is **NOT IN THIS REPOSITORY**. Everything below is therefore a register of *decisions to be applied to a text that is not held here* — not a record of a translation that can be checked. Do not read a decision as an accomplished translation.

**Primary text verified in this pass:** *Uyt-spanningen* (Utrecht: Willem Clerck, 1676), **printed pp. 131–134**, 8 stanzas, closing date `Slagt-m. 1661` (= November 1661), tune line `Singt als: Rosemont waar gy vliedt.`
Locator: <https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_01_01/lode002uyt_01_01_0056.php>

Note a variant now confirmed against the print: the 1676 tune line reads **`Rosemont waar gy vliedt`**, while the registry previously carried the critical edition's `waer`. Both are recorded; the print reading is authoritative for this work.

## Why this dossier exists

Five distinct failure modes were flagged in earlier sessions, and the 1676 text confirms all five are live risks:

1. a reciprocal vow formula rendered as a one-sided wish;
2. "human favour / human disfavour" reduced to "other people's opinion";
3. the stanzas' **eight different Christological addresses** flattened into one universal refrain;
4. one stanza's sanctify-or-kill antithesis weakened;
5. archaic concrete images paraphrased into abstractions.

## 1. `Iesu! wilt gy my` — reciprocal vow, not a wish

**Locators:** I.7 (`Iesu! wilt gy my, ick wil u, wil u alleen, / En segg tot al de schepsels: Neen.`), repeated in II–VIII with a varying second half.

**Confirmed printed wording (stanza I):** *Iesu! wilt gy my, ick wil u, wil u alleen, / En segg tot al de schepsels: Neen.*

**Reading to adopt:** the clause is the first half of a **reciprocal formula** — "wilt Thou be mine, I will be Thine" — the betrothal/covenant exchange familiar from the Song of Songs, not a statement about Christ's desire in the abstract. Hence the working sense «Иисус, если Ты меня приемлешь — я Твой, Твой один» is closer than «если Ты хочешь меня», because the second half (`ick wil u, wil u alleen`) answers it as a vow and the following line renounces every creature.

**Confidence:** **medium-high**, on internal evidence only — the reciprocal structure is visible in the printed text and recurs eight times. No lexical authority was consulted in this pass.

**Decision status:** `ADOPTED_AS_WORKING_DECISION`, pending one dictionary check. **Next check:** *WNT* s.v. **willen** (the formulaic `wilt gij mij` / `ik wil u` construction) and a comparison with the same formula in Lodenstein's Song-of-Songs poems (e.g. items 4, 21, 42 of the 1676 collection).

## 2. `Menschen-gunst` / `Menschen-ongunst` — disposition, not reputation

**Locator:** stanza II, lines 1–4: *Menschen-gunst, maar een blick / Menschen-ongunst, maar een schrick, / Stelen 't herte weg; nog ist haat nog gunst, / Maar niet dan der schepslen tover-cunst.*

**What the text says:** human **favour** and human **disfavour** each steal the heart — favour by a glance, disfavour by a terror. The stanza's argument is that it is neither hatred nor favour as such but the **creatures' magic** that captivates. So the field is affection and aversion that take the heart captive, not public opinion or reputation.

**Decision status:** `ADOPTED_AS_WORKING_DECISION`. Any Russian rendering that says only «мнение людей» is **wrong on this stanza**. Confidence: **high** — the printed text names both favour and disfavour as the pair, and the stanza's conclusion is about enchantment, not standing.

## 3. The eight Christological addresses must stay eight

This is the single most destructive simplification available to a translator, and the print confirms the variation is systematic, not accidental:

| St. | Address as printed (1676) | The "all that can…" clause |
|---|---|---|
| I | `Iesu! Iesu soet! Iesu! hoogste van` | `Al wat my lusten can` |
| II | `Iesu! Iesu lief! Iesu 't hoogste van` | `Al wat my trecken can` |
| III | `Iesu, Iesu schoon! Iesu 't schoonste van` | `Wat my behagen can` |
| IV | `Iesu! Iesu lief! Iesu 't minlijckst van` | `Al wat ick minnen can` |
| V | `Iesu! Iesu schoon! Iesu cierlijckst van` | `Wat my vermaken can` |
| VI | `Iesu! Iesu lief! Iesu 't soetste van` | `Wat my vermaken can` |
| VII | `Iesu! Iesu soet! Iesu 't soetste van` | `Al wat my smaken can` |
| VIII | `Iesu! Iesu lief! Iesu 't waardigst van` | `Al wat my trecken can` |

Eight addresses (**soet, lief, schoon, minlijckst, cierlijckst, soetste, waardigst**, with `hoogste` twice) and five different verbs of attraction (**lusten, trecken, behagen, minnen, vermaken, smaken**). The adjectives track the stanza's subject: beauty in III and V, loveliness in IV, sweetness in VI–VII (taste, stanza VII is about the tongue and gluttony), worthiness in VIII (the closing turn to the invisible).

**Decision status:** `ADOPTED_AS_WORKING_DECISION` — **no single refrain across the eight stanzas**. A Russian version may hold the address constant only if the owner explicitly decides to do so, and that decision must be logged as a deviation. Confidence: **high**.

## 4. Stanza IV — the sanctify-or-kill antithesis

**Locator:** stanza IV, lines 7–8: *Iesu mint gy my, Laat u min, u cruys, u croon / Mijn min my heyl'gen doen, of doon.*

**What the text says:** "Jesus, if Thou lovest me, let Thy love, Thy cross, Thy crown make my love holy — **or kill it**." Three instruments (love, cross, crown) act on one object (my love) with a binary outcome: sanctify or put to death.

**Decision status:** `ADOPTED_AS_WORKING_DECISION`. The Russian must preserve **both** the threefold instrument and the either/or. Collapsing this to "освяти мою любовь" deletes the mortification half of the line and with it the stanza's argument. Confidence: **high** — the printed line is unambiguous.

## 5. Archaic concrete images — decisions pending lexical check

All eight expressions were located in the 1676 print. What is *not* yet done is the dictionary work; a literal-modern gloss is offered, with the check each one needs.

| Expression | Stanza / printed context | Literal sense offered | What could go wrong | Check needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `costelijck kuys` | V: `D'yd'le pragt, 't costelijck kuys / Van mijn cleed, mijn hoff, mijn huys` | costly **finery/neatness** — the substantivised *kuis* ("clean, neat, chaste") used as a noun for elegant, spotless condition | misread as "chastity", which is a different word-field and would wreck the stanza | WNT s.v. **kuis** (II), and the 2005 apparatus for this line (copyrighted: consult, do not quote) |
| `Reyn` | V: `Draagt de naam van Reyn, dat het oog verquickt, / Als, helas! het hert in 't dorre stickt` | **clean/pure** as a *name* that things carry — outward purity that refreshes the eye | rendering it as moral purity turns an aesthetic contrast into a moral one | WNT s.v. **rein**; confirm capitalisation is the print's emphasis, not a personification |
| `in 't dorre stickt` | V, same couplet | the heart **sticks/sti(c)ks fast in the dry** — an image of being caught in aridity | the verb is ambiguous between "stick, adhere" and "stifle, smother"; the Russian must choose, and the choice changes the image | WNT s.v. **stikken** (both senses) — this is the least settled item in the list |
| `Leckre tong` | VII: `Leckre tong, gulsicheyd / Heeft my dickmaals stil misleydt` | the **licking/sweet-toothed tongue**, i.e. appetite for delicate taste | "лакомый язык" sounds comic if unhandled; "избалованный вкус" loses the body part | WNT s.v. **lekker**; decide whether to keep the metonymy |
| `schemer-ligt` | VIII: `Ah! betovrend schemer-ligt!` | **dusk-light, twilight** — the half-light that enchants | modern Russian "сумерки" is neutral; the line needs the *enchantment* | no lexical risk; a poetic decision |
| `Geven op de hand` | VIII: `Geven op de hand! Tegenwoordicheyd!` | giving **into the hand**, i.e. hand-to-hand immediacy, present possession | may be an idiom of payment/delivery rather than of presence; the pair with `Tegenwoordicheyd` suggests immediacy | WNT s.v. **hand** (op de hand geven); confirm the idiom |
| `Tegenwoordicheyd` | VIII, same line | **presence, immediacy** — what is present to the senses | easily flattened to abstract "присутствие" and lose the contrast with the next line's `d'onsigtbaarheden` | none beyond the pairing |
| `d'onsigtbaarheden` | VIII: `Doet my door 't gelove sien / Wat heyl d'onsigtbaarheden bien` | **the invisibilities** — the plural substantive for unseen things, offering salvation | "невидимое" (singular/neuter) is acceptable; "невидимые вещи" is clumsy | decide singular/plural deliberately; the print is plural |

### 5a. What the fifth pass could and could not settle

**Dictionaries were not reachable, and that is now recorded rather than assumed.** The *Woordenboek der Nederlandsche Taal* is served by the Geïntegreerde Taalbank at `gtb.ivdnt.org`, which returned only its JavaScript search shell — no article text is retrievable from this environment. `ensie.nl`, which hosts the 1898 *Groot woordenboek der Nederlandsche taal*, is behind a paywall. **No WNT sense has been consulted for any of the eight expressions.** They therefore stay `PENDING_LEXICAL_CHECK`.

**One item moved anyway, on internal evidence.** `in 't dorre stickt` sits in a rhyme:

> *Draagt de naam van Reyn, dat het oog verquickt, / Als, helas! het hert in 't dorre stickt*

`stickt` rhymes with `verquickt` ("revives, refreshes"), and the line sets the two against each other. The etymological record supports reading them as a pair rather than as an accidental rhyme: *stikken* in the sense "smother, be choked" is, per the *Etymologisch woordenboek* (Van Veen/Van der Sijs, via `etymologiebank.nl`), the same word as *stikken* "to stitch" — an intensivum of *steken*, glossed there as "**blijven steken, niet verder kunnen**". The two senses the dossier had opposed are historically one word, and the rhyme selects the "stuck, unable to go further" sense.

**Narrowed gloss:** *the heart is left stuck fast — choked — in the dry*, not "the heart adheres to dryness". Confidence moves from **low** to **medium**; the status stays `PENDING_LEXICAL_CHECK` until WNT is read.

**`Geven op de hand` remains the weakest item in the file.** No dictionary was consulted and the idiom was not found in a corroborating text. What can be said from context alone: it stands in a pair with `Tegenwoordicheyd!` in an exclamatory list of what enchants (`Ah! betovrend schemer-ligt! / Geven op de hand! Tegenwoordicheyd!`), which points to **hand-to-hand immediacy, tangibility, present possession** rather than to a payment formula. That reading is a hypothesis, and it is labelled as one.

**`wilt gy my`** also remains unchecked against WNT. The reciprocal-vow reading rests on the printed formula alone — `Iesu! wilt gy my, ick wil u, wil u alleen` — which is strong, but it is internal evidence.

**Decision status for all eight:** `PENDING_LEXICAL_CHECK`. None is approved. Confidence in the literal glosses: **medium**, **low for `in 't dorre stickt` and `Geven op de hand`**.

## 6. Stanza structure to preserve

The print gives 8 stanzas of 8 lines. Each stanza is built as: four lines of statement, then the **address line + its "all that can…" clause**, then two closing lines that carry the stanza's application. The address pair is not a chorus inserted between stanzas — it is **inside** the stanza, and the stanza's argument continues after it. Any musical setting that treats the address as a detachable refrain will break the argument of the stanza it sits in.
