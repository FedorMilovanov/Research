# Tune directions in *Uyt-spanningen* — what the 2005 melody register does and does not prove

**Opened:** 2026-10-25. **Trigger:** the register had never been read, and four separate open items in this project were variations of the same question — "what tune is this poem sung to, and is the tune the same as that other poem's?"
**Locator:** *Uyt-spanningen* (2005), **Melodie-register**, printed pp. 663–664, DBNL `lode002uyt_02_01_0151.php` — page opened and read 2026-10-25.

**Rights.** The register is part of a copyrighted edition (`SRC-L-CRIT-2005`, `PERMISSION_REQUIRED`, `RIGHTS_HOLD`). It is an **index of tune directions**: names and text numbers. Only the entries this project actually uses are transcribed below, as bibliographic facts, with the analysis in this project's own words. Do not reproduce the register wholesale.

## 1. What the register is

For every tune direction printed in the collection it lists the **numbers of the texts** sung to it — the 2005 numbering, which is not the 1676 order. Its own rubric says the numbers after each tune designation refer to the texts the melodies belong to. It is a finding aid for the edition, not a melody catalogue: it identifies **which poems the print sends to the same direction**, and says nothing about what the melody was.

That distinction is the whole value of this file. Three questions in this project were being answered as if the register were a melody catalogue, and at least one earlier reading of the apparatus looked like a contradiction with it.

## 3. Three findings

### 3.1 DUTCH-005 — the register does not say the tune was text 27's

The 2005 apparatus note on item 56 reads, in full: *Melodie: Herkomst onbekend, anders dan Un jour &c, van tekst 27* — origin unknown, **different from** the *Un jour* of text 27. The register lists 27 and 56 under one heading.

Read as a catalogue of melodies, those two statements contradict each other. Read as what they are — an **index of direction strings** and an **editorial note about melodies** — they say something more useful: the print used the same *words* for two different tunes, and the modern editor could tell that the melody behind our poem was not the Grouzy chanson sung to text 27.

**Rule for copy.** The register may be cited to show that two poems were sent to the same direction. It may **not** be cited to show that they shared a melody. For DUTCH-005 the operative sentence stays what it already was: the tune is indicated only by a reference, its origin is unknown, and it is not the *Un jour* of text 27.

### 3.2 DUTCH-004 — two keys, one tune, and the trap that was already sprung once

Item 9, *Simeons lof-sang*, printed p. 29, is headed `Te singen als: Prendete la.`, and its own first line is `Gy laat/ dien gy vind rede staan/`. Item 32 (DUTCH-004) is headed `Singt als Prendete la: siet boven pag. 29.` The register therefore lists the pair **twice**: under the tune name `Prendete la`, and under item 9's incipit `Gy laat, dien gy vind rede staan`.

So the second entry is not a second tune. It is the collection's tune `Prendete la`, keyed by the incipit of the poem that first uses it — the same double keying that `Nerea` / `Doet yemant sorg` / `Tien Geboden` / `Reveillez vous` shows for the group 26, 29, 50, 65, 87, 88.

This is the **`Bourgeois` / `psalm 42` trap in a new place**. There, one melody under two names was read as two rival melodies and cost a correction. Here it would cost the same: «напев `Prendete la` или напев „Gy laat…”» is a false dichotomy, and both strings point at one tune whose origin the edition calls unknown. For DUTCH-004 nothing in the copy changes — the tune is still untraced — but its dossier now records *why* there appear to be two names, and the next agent will not open the question again.

### 3.3 Candidate DUTCH-CAND-006 — the first tune in this collection with a name that can be chased

Item 57, *'t Cruyce van een christen*, is headed `Toon: 't En is niet alle tijd, &c. Siet boven pag. 100.` The register sends the same direction to text 41, *Jesus lijdende liefde* (printed p. 100), which is what the page reference means. The 2005 headnote adds the one thing no other tune direction in this project has offered: the incipit is the opening line of **Camphuysen's song *Christelijcke Gelatenheydt***.

That is a named, searchable artefact outside Lodenstein — a seventeenth-century Dutch song whose text and tune have their own transmission. It does not mean the melody is "found": the direction names a song, not a notation we hold, and Liederenbank is still offline. It does mean that for candidate 006 the next step is a Camphuysen enquiry rather than another dead end in an unknown tune name. Recorded in `CANDIDATE_DUTCH-006_CRUYCE_VAN_EEN_CHRISTEN.md`.

### 3.4 A side result: the appendix poem has a real tune

Text 131, *'s Heeren heerlijckheyts loff*, is directed to **Psalm 6**. Unlike the tunes that occupy this project's attention, the Genevan psalm 6 melody has a documented transmission with notation. If that appendix poem is ever produced, it is the one Lodenstein text in this collection that could be sung to a period melody a listener can compare against a printed source — with the caution that "directed to Psalm 6" is a statement about the 1676 collection, not about what any congregation actually sang.

## 4. How to use this file

1. Before opening a tune question, check this register: the answer may be "the print sends two poems to one direction", which is cheaper than collating tune names.
2. Never convert "same direction" into "same melody". The editor's notes are where melody identity lives, and they are per text.
3. When a direction has two strings (`Prendete la` / `Gy laat…`), look for the poem whose incipit the second string quotes. That is a second key, not a second tune.
4. When a direction quotes a line from another poet (Camphuysen, Datheen, a French chanson), that poet's song is the searchable object, not Lodenstein's poem.

## 5. What is still open

- **Liederenbank** (Meertens/KNAW) returns 403, re-checked 2026-10-25. Every melody identification in this collection would normally be confirmed there; until it returns, nothing here goes beyond what a printed page says.
- **No notation** of `Prendete la`, of `'t En is niet alle tijd`, of the 1665 `Un jour` or of most other directions is held by this project. The register does not change that.
- The register's 2005 numbering had to be mapped onto the 1676 order by hand for every entry above; the mapping is by printed page, not by an index in the edition, and is therefore only as good as the pages listed here.
