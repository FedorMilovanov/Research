# DUTCH-005 — rhyme: what the Dutch does, and what a rhymed Russian would cost

**Status:** analysis + two worked stanzas. **No change to the lyric text.** The text of record stays `DUTCH-005-RU v0.6`, which is *unrhymed on purpose*, because rhyme in Russian cannot be added without re-composing the line — this file exists so that decision is made with the numbers in front of it.

## 1. The Dutch is rhymed, and in a strict pattern

Read from the 1676 print (`TRANSCRIPTION_DUTCH-005_1676.md`), stanza by stanza. Nine printed lines; the pattern is the same everywhere:

| St. | 1 & 3 | 2 & 4 | 5, 6, 8 | 7 (tripled) | 9 (close) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | boven / loven | niet / siet | aard / aard / aard | loven (rhymes with 1, 3) | waard (rhymes with 5–8) |
| II | schijnen / verdwijnen | costelijck / slijck | bloed / bloed / bloet | loven | doet (rhymes with 5–8) |
| III | Heerlijck / begeerlijck | (crijgelijck) / onsigtbaarlijck | oog / oog / oog | leven | hoog (rhymes with 5–8) |
| IV | leven / beven | pijn / quijn | bedenckt / leven / bedenckt | leven | schenckt (rhymes with 5–8) |
| V | deugden / vreugden | Heerlijckheyd / Heylicheyd | vereent / leven / vereent | leven | verleent (rhymes with 5–8) |
| VI | snooden / (dood, en) | leeft / cleeft | gaan / leven / gaan | leven | aan (rhymes with 5–8) |
| VII | weerdig / veerdig | min / in | dan / lieven / dat | lieven | can (rhymes with 5–8) |
| VIII | Schoonheyd / gewoonheyd | schijnt / verdwijnt | aan / lieven / aan | lieven | gaan (rhymes with 5–8) |
| IX | Bevallijckheden / seden | can / van | al / lieven / al | lieven | sal (rhymes with 5–8) |
| X | tongen / songen | wackerheyd / Heerlijckheyd | hand / want / stoff | loven | loff (rhymes with 5–8) |

Three consequences that matter for any Russian version:

1. **The load-bearing rhyme is the last one.** In every stanza the closing line rhymes with the call line, which has just been sung twice and will be sung a third time. That is the rhyme the ear waits for, and it is the one worth paying for.
2. **Lines 5, 6 and 8 are the same line repeated** — their "rhyme" is automatic. Only line 9 is real work.
3. **The tripled word is a fixed element**: `leven` / `lieven` / `loven`. In stanzas III–VII it rhymes with nothing — it is the poem's spine, not its rhyme scheme.

## 2. What Russian cannot carry over

- **Dutch rhyme here is mostly morphological**: `-lijck`, `-heyd`, `-aen`, `-oog`. Russian has `-ость`, `-ание`, `-ой`, but the words that carry the *meaning* of these lines do not fall on those endings.
- **Line 3 is the problem line.** In the Dutch, line 3 rhymes with line 1 while carrying the fourth item of a list; in Russian the list items (`жизнь, любовь, хвала` / `болезнь, трепет, истома`) end where they end, and bending them to a rhyme changes what the line says.
- **Rhyme costs syllables.** Russian rhyme endings are longer than Dutch ones (`-ение`, `-ание`), and the text is strictly syllabic. Every rhyme pair added pushes against the measured counts.

So: a rhymed version is not a patch on v0.6. It is a second composition, and it will be **less literal** — that trade has to be chosen, not assumed.

## 3. Two stanzas worked out, as evidence rather than promise

Kept close to the Dutch, with the Dutch scheme (1 & 3, 2 & 4), so the cost can be seen.

### Stanza II — done, and it costs almost nothing

```
Блеск и пышность — только тень,        (7)   a
Всё, что ценно, — суета,                (7)   b
Гаснет радость в тот же день,          (7)   a
Злато, серебро — тщета.                (7)   b
Там драгоценная кровь,                 (7)   c
Там драгоценная кровь                  (7)   c
Стоит всей жизни, любви и хвалы:       (10)  d
Там драгоценная кровь,                 (7)   c
Сердцу всех выше эта кровь.            (8)   c
```

Two trades, both small and declarable: `Lusten met 't gebruyck verdwijnen` → «Гаснет радость в тот же день» (the same sense of "used and gone", but with a day where the Dutch has none), and the close repeats «кровь», an identical rhyme where the Dutch has the true rhyme `bloed`/`doet` — Russian has no verb that will rhyme here without padding.

### Stanza VIII — done, and it costs more

```
Прекраснейшая Красота,                 (8)   a
Причина красы, что мнится,             (8)   b
С годами, с привычкой — тщета,         (9)   a
Слабеет — и в тень обратится.          (9)   b
Тебе, Красота, поклоняюсь,             (9)   c
Тебе, Красота, поклоняюсь,             (9)   c
Коль что любить — любить, любить, любить; (10) d
Тебе, Красота, поклоняюсь:             (9)   c
Туда моё сердце стремится.             (8)   b
```

Here the rhyme forces «тщета» into line 3, where the Dutch has simply "pleases less". It is defensible — vanity is the poem's own word in stanza II — but it is a word the Dutch does not have in that line. **This is what rhyme costs, and it is the reason to decide before I do nine more.**

## 4. Three standards, and what each costs

| Standard | What it means | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Blank verse (v0.6, current)** | No rhyme; every word answerable to the Dutch | Rhyme absent where the Dutch has it | The honest default while the text is still being verified |
| **B. Cadence rhyme** | Lines 2 & 4 rhyme; the closing line rhymes with the call line; lines 1 & 3 free | The rhyme the ear actually waits for; small semantic trades | **Recommended.** Gets most of the musical effect for the least distortion |
| **C. Full Dutch scheme** | 1 & 3, 2 & 4, close with the call | Two or three lines per stanza drift from the Dutch | Only if the rhyme matters more than the letter |

## 5. What I will not do

- Ship a text where four stanzas rhyme and six do not — that sounds worse than consistent blank verse, and it hides the defect.
- Add rhyme by padding ("— вновь и вновь", "— вот", "— и вот") or by inventing a chorus or a refrain. The Dutch has none.
- Present a rhymed adaptation as a translation. Rhyme is authorial work: it belongs in the adaptation layer and must be declared as such.
