# DUTCH-003 — *Hy droech onse smerten*: the sonnet's dramaturgy, and what is not approved

**Work:** `DUTCH-003`, Russian release title «Он понёс наши скорби»
**Author:** Jacobus Revius (1586–1658)
**Dossier opened:** 2026-10-25 (fourth pass)
**Status of the Russian text:** the text discussed in earlier sessions is **NOT IN THIS REPOSITORY** and is **NOT an approved translation**. Nothing below approves it.

## 1. Witness and its limits

The text read in this pass is the poem page of the **1930–1935 scholarly edition** (W.A.P. Smit), `SRC-R-HY-DROEG`:
<https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/revi001over01_01/revi001over01_01_0327.php>

**The 1630 first edition has still not been inspected.** The catalogue's caution stands: a citation to this page is not evidence that the 1630 leaf was found and checked. What the scholarly page *does* give us is a documented collation witness, in its own apparatus:

> *Hy droech onse smerten,* 8. **A** heeft een drukfout: **tuyschen**.

Here **A** is the editor's siglum for the first edition, so this single note establishes two things: the 1630 print reads `tuyschen` at line 8 where the scholarly text prints `tuyschten`, and the modern text is a collation against that print. One documented 1630 reading is therefore known **indirectly**; the leaf itself is still not held. Composition date and any original melody remain **unestablished**.

Status of the 1630 object: `NOT_ACQUIRED` / `EVIDENCE_HOLD` / `LOCATOR_HOLD` — unchanged by this pass.

## 2. Genre: a religious sonnet, not a hymn

14 lines. No melody direction is recorded for it. It belongs to a collection (*Over-Ysselsche sangen en dichten*, Deventer 1630) whose title speaks of "sangen", so the word "song" in the book's title must not be read as a tune instruction for this poem. Treat it as a **sonnet** — a literary form with a turn — and any music as a modern setting.

The title alludes to **Isaiah 53:4** ("Hy droech onse smerten" — he bore our sorrows). The allusion is to the title, and the poem's argument is the application of that verse to the speaker.

## 3. The dramaturgy, verified line by line

The structure noted in earlier sessions is **confirmed exactly** by the printed text.

| Lines | Function | Printed text |
|---|---|---|
| **1–4** | The first group of actors, named and then negated | *T'en zijn de Joden niet, Heer Jesu, die u cruysten, / Noch die verradelijck u togen voort gericht, / Noch die versmadelijck u spogen int gesicht, / Noch die u knevelden, en stieten u vol puysten* |
| **5–8** | The second group — the soldiers — likewise negated | *T'en sijn de crijchs-luy niet die met haer felle vuysten / Den rietstock hebben of den hamer opgelicht, / Of het vervloecte hout op Golgotha gesticht, / Of over uwen rock tsaem dobbelden en tuyschten* |
| **9** | **The turn** — from third person to first | *Ick bent, ô Heer, ick bent die u dit heb gedaen* |
| **10–13** | The speaker becomes the instruments | *Ick ben den swaren boom die u had overlaen, / Ick ben de taeye streng daermee ghy ginct gebonden, / De nagel, en de speer, de geessel die u sloech, / De bloet-bedropen croon die uwen schedel droech* |
| **14** | Causal close: personal guilt | *Want dit is al geschiet, eylaes! om mijne sonden.* |

**The turn is line 9**, precisely as recorded. It is the first person's arrival, and it is doubled — `Ick bent … ick bent` — an emphatic repetition that a translation must not smooth away.

**The instruments, in the original's order:** cross/tree (`den swaren boom`, glossed by the apparatus as *het kruis*), rope (`de taeye streng`), nail (`de nagel`), spear (`de speer`), scourge (`de geessel`), blood-dropped crown (`de bloet-bedropen croon`). **Six**, and the order is cross → rope → nail → spear → scourge → crown. The working note listed the same six in the same order; that is confirmed.

Two details worth carrying into any translation:

- The first eight lines work by **negation of named agents** — not the Jews, not the soldiers — and each clause is concrete (spitting in the face, binding, bruising, the reed-staff and hammer, the setting-up of the wood, dicing for the robe). A translation that generalises them into "люди" or "грешники" destroys the poem's method: it names, then removes, before it turns.
- `puysten` is glossed *builen* (bruises/boils) — physical, not metaphorical.

## 4. The last line, and why it must not be softened

*Want dit is al geschiet, eylaes! om mijne sonden* — "for all this has happened, alas! **because of my sins**".

Three components, all load-bearing: the totality (`dit is al` — *all* of this), the lament (`eylaes!`), and the **cause** (`om mijne sonden`). The line does not say "for my salvation" or "for my sake" in a beneficent sense; it assigns the Passion's occurrence to the speaker's sins as its ground. Reducing it to a general mention of salvation removes the poem's verdict and leaves the turn (line 9) without its explanation.

## 5. The discussed Russian turn — a working option, with a semantic warning

The variant noted from earlier sessions:

> «Я — тот, о Господи! Я — виновник мук Твоих!»

**This is a working option, not an approved formulation**, and it has a measurable weakness. The Dutch says **"I am he who *did this to Thee*"** — the speaker claims the **act**. The Russian variant claims **causation/guilt** ("виновник"). Those are not the same claim: agentive ("я это сделал") is stronger and more concrete, and it is what the doubled `ick bent … ick bent die u dit heb gedaen` actually says. The causal force belongs to line 14 (`om mijne sonden`), which the original keeps separate from line 9.

**Recommendation to the owner, not a decision:** preserve the division of labour — line 9 agentive, line 14 causal. Merging both into line 9 flattens the sonnet's two-stage ending. Either way, the choice must be recorded as a documented deviation with reasoning.

## 6. Music directions — neither approved

Two directions were discussed:

1. atmospheric guitar and cello;
2. a cappella opening, with organ, cello and a male ensemble entering gradually **after the semantic turn**.

The second has a real structural argument in its favour: the poem itself turns at line 9, so an arrangement that changes at the turn would be following the text rather than decorating it. That is an observation, not a decision.

**Status of both: `DISCUSSED_NOT_APPROVED`.** Per the project's own rule, style is decided per work, and no style prompt for DUTCH-003 exists in this repository (the verbatim prompt remains `NOT_CAPTURED`). A note of caution on direction 1: the project's per-work exclusion of guitar applies to the epic Lodenstein setting only, so guitar is not globally excluded — but it is also not thereby approved for this poem.

## 7. What must happen before any Russian text is called a translation

1. Inspect the **1630 leaf** (or a digitised copy of it) and collate at least lines 1, 8, 9, 13, 14; the `tuyschen/tuyschten` variant is the known test case.
2. Obtain the actual Russian draft from the owner; it is not in the repository, so no accuracy claim is currently possible.
3. Check the six instruments one by one against the Dutch, in order.
4. Verify that the turn falls on line 9 in the Russian and that line 14 remains causal.
5. Record every deviation with a reason.
