## The `etc.` is a repeat instruction, not a lacuna

Stanza I is printed in full and is the metrical model. In III–VII the call line's repeat is abbreviated `etc.`, and the line is then printed again in full lower down the stanza. Stanza VIII prints no `etc.`: it repeats the call in full instead. Reading the `etc.` as missing text is the one mistake that would corrupt the whole reconstruction.

## Variants between 1676 and 1727

| Place | 1676 | 1727 |
|---|---|---|
| I.3 | `'t Regte leven, lieven, loven` | `'t Rechte leven, leven, loven` |
| III–VII | call line + `etc.` | the full call line printed twice, plus `etc.` |
| VII.8 | `Mijn siel na boven dat` | `... dan` — and `dan` matches line 5, so the 1676 `dat` looks like a slip |
| VIII | 9 lines; call line + repeat | the call line is **omitted**, 8 lines only |
| VIII.2 | `Oorsaack van wat hier schoon schijnt` | `Oorsaack van **al** wat hier schoon schijnt` |
| IX.3 | `menschen seden` | `menschen seden,` (comma added) |
| VIII.7 vs IX.7 | `Wil ick oyt yets lieven` | `Wil ick oyt yets lieven` |

Everything else is spelling (`c`/`k`, `u`/`v`) and does not affect reading.

## Three places where the print decides a disputed reading

1. **VIII.3–4 reads `nog`, and `Min vermaackt` is affirmative.** There is no negation in the clause, so "neither through years nor through custom does it fade" is not in the text; a Russian version that inserts «не померкнет» adds a negation the book does not have.
2. **VIII.5 `U, Schoonheyd, bid ick aan`** — the addressed Beauty is adored, therefore `Die` in line 3 cannot be that Beauty; it reaches back to `wat hier schoon schijnt`.
3. **VII.6 vs VII.8** — `Mijn siel na boven **dan**` / `na boven **dat**`: the print contradicts itself inside one stanza, and 1727 resolves it in favour of `dan`.

## A second hymn with almost the same title — checked in the 1676 table of contents

The contents page of the 1676 edition (`https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lode002uyt_01_01/`) lists both:

| DBNL file | Printed title | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `..._0061.php` | **`Heerlijckheyds Loff.`** | **this work** — pp. 155–158, dated 27 Aug 1665, `Voyse: Un jour &c.` |
| `..._0142.php` | **`'s Heeren Heerlijckheyts Loff.`** | a **different** poem, in the appendix section |

The poem names its own subject pair in stanza 10: `Iesus lof en Heerlijckheyd` (10.4), `Singt sonder eynden Iesus loff` (10.9) — `lof` = praise, `Heerlijckheyd` = glory, the same two words the title is made of. So `Heerlijckheyds Loff` = «Хвала славе»; «Слава Господня» drops the praise and adds a `Heere` absent from this title.

So the working Russian title «Слава Господня» sits closer to the *other* hymn's title («Хвала Господней славе») than to ours («Хвала славе»). The two must not be merged or confused, and any rename is the owner's decision.

**Correction to a claim that circulated in review:** our hymn is **not** № 56 and the other is **not** № 131. Those numbers are DBNL file numbers, and file `0056` is `Schepselen Niet en Iesus Al` — which is **DUTCH-001** — while `0131` is `Den Medicijn-meester Duc-d'Alba`. The warning about the title collision is right; the numbers attached to it are wrong, and using them would have transcribed the wrong poem.
