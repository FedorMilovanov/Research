# Genre and classification policy

The historical object, its later hymnbook adaptation and the modern recording each need their own classification. This catalog is deliberately not a simple list of “hymns”, because the source corpus contains several literary and liturgical forms.

## Controlled project classifications

- METRICAL_PSALM — metrical rendering of a biblical Psalm, such as the Datheen or Revius psalter tradition. Keep separate from freely composed hymn texts.
- BIBLICAL_CANTICLE_OR_SCRIPTURE_EXPANSION — a canticle or a poetic expansion/meditation on a specific biblical passage.
- DEVOTIONAL_SONG_WITH_TUNE_DIRECTION — a text for which a historical source prints a melody instruction.
- DEVOTIONAL_POEM_OR_MEDITATION — a spiritual poem without a verified tune direction or without sufficient evidence that it was sung.
- DIALOGUE_OR_PASTOREL — a staged poetic exchange or pastoral dialogue; do not automatically classify it as congregational song.
- SONNET_OR_LITERARY_POEM — a literary poem such as Revius's Hy droech onse smerten.
- LATER_HYMNBOOK_REWORKING — an adapted/revised form printed in a later hymnbook, such as Rutgers's Zelfverloochening (1806).
- TRANSLATION_OR_LITERARY_ADAPTATION — a later text in another language; document its source relationship instead of calling it the original.
- MODERN_MUSICAL_SETTING — present-day composition/arrangement, including SUNO output. Not evidence of a historical melody or instrumentation.

## Classification rules

1. Record source-text genre and recording/production genre separately.
2. A printed melody direction proves an instruction in that source, not the original melody's exact sound or a performance at a specific service.
3. A hymnbook's existence or title-page invocation of Ephesians 5:19 does not prove every included item was used uniformly across churches.
4. The 1676 Uyt-spanningen describes itself as containing edifying songs and other poems and is organized into several kinds of writing. Each entry must be classified from its own text and source notes.
5. Keep songbook item numbers distinct from individual text counts: the 2005 critical edition groups some texts under combined item numbers and occasionally supplies repeated portions for melody.
6. Distinguish source author, later reviser, translator, literary adaptor, musical composer/arranger and singer/performer.
7. When genre or tune is not supported by a primary source, use UNKNOWN / NOT VERIFIED rather than inventing an attribution.
